# Source List with Links

## Project: Navratri — The Nine Nights of the Divine Feminine

---

## Tier 1 — Primary Sources (Scriptural)

| # | Source | Type | Description | Credibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Devī Māhātmya (also Durgā Saptashatī / Chandi Patha) | Sacred text | 700 verses in 13 chapters, embedded in Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa. Core scripture of Navratri. Contains the Mahishasura-Madhu-Kaitabha and Mahishasura-Madhini narratives. | Primary — foundational text |
| 2 | Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa | Sacred text | One of the 18 major Puranas. Contains Devī Māhātmya (Chapters 78-90 of 137 total chapters). Attributed to sage Markandeya. | Primary — Puranic source |
| 3 | Kālikā Purāṇa | Sacred text | Dedicated to Goddess Kali/Durga. Contains details on Navratri rituals, animal sacrifice (historical), and Kumari Puja. | Primary — Tantric/Puranic source |
| 4 | Skanda Purāṇa | Sacred text | Contains references to Navaratri and Skandamata (the 5th form of Durga) | Primary — Puranic source |
| 5 | Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇa | Sacred text | Presents Devi as the supreme reality. Important for understanding the Shakta theological framework. | Primary — Puranic source |
| 6 | Rigveda — Devi Suktam (RV 10.125) | Sacred text | Earliest Vedic hymn to the Goddess (~1500 BCE). Describes the Goddess as the supreme power behind all creation. | Primary — Vedic source |
| 7 | Atharvaveda — Sri Suktam | Sacred text | Hymn to Sri/Lakshmi as the Devi. Part of the Vedic Devi worship tradition. | Primary — Vedic source |

## Conflicting Sources and Resolution

| Conflict | Source A | Source B | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date of Devi Mahatmya composition | 5th century CE (some scholars) | 7th century CE (other scholars) | State as "5th-7th century CE" range |
| Origin of Navadurga system | 7th century CE | 10th century CE | State as "7th-10th century CE" range |
| Animal sacrifice practice | Still practiced in some temples | Largely banned or symbolic in most temples | Present both: "historically practiced; increasingly replaced with symbolic offerings in modern times" |
