## Project: Navratri — The Nine Nights of the Divine Feminine

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Navratri celebrates the victory of Goddess Durga over the demon Mahishasura across nine nights. Each night honors one of the nine forms of Durga (Navadurga). The festival is celebrated twice yearly, with Sharad Navratri (autumn) being the most prominent. It is deeply rooted in the Devī Māhātmya and represents the power of Śhakti — the Divine Feminine.

---

## 2. Key Facts

### Mythological Core
- Mahishasura obtained a boon: no man/god could kill him
- Devas combined energies to create Durga
- 9-day battle; Durga beheads Mahishasura on Day 10 (Vijayadashami/Dussehra)
- Durga rides a lion/tiger, armed with weapons from all gods

### The 9 Forms (Navadurga)
1. Shailaputri — Daughter of the Mountain
2. Brahmacharini — The Ascetic
3. Chandraghanta — Bell of the Moon
4. Kushmanda — Creator of the Universe
5. Skandamata — Mother of Skanda/Kartikeya
6. Katyayani — The Warrior
7. Kalaratri — Dark Night of Destruction
8. Mahagauri — The Pure One
9. Siddhidatri — Giver of Perfection

### Regional Variations
- **Bengal:** Durga Puja — pandals, elaborate idols, community feasts
- **Gujarat:** Garba/Dandiya Raas — circle dances with sticks
- **South India:** Golu/Kolu — doll displays in stepped arrangements
- **North India:** Fasting, Jaagran, Ramlila performances
- **Mysore:** Mysore Dasara — state festival since 1610 CE

### Scriptural Sources
- Devī Māhātmya (Markandeya Purana) — primary
- Kalika Purana — rituals and historical practices
- Devi Bhagavata Purana — theological framework
- Rigveda Devi Suktam (RV 10.125) — earliest Vedic reference
