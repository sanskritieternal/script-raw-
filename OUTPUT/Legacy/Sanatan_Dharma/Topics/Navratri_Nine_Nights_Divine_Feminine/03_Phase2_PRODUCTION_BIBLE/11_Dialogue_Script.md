# Dialogue/Script Guide

## Project: Navratri — The Nine Nights of the Divine Feminine

---

## Narration Perspective

**Third-person documentary storytelling with devotional reverence**

- Narrator is an informed guide, telling the story of the Goddess
- Not first-person (no "I believe" or "we celebrate")
- Not in-character as a deity or mythological figure
- Maintains distance between narrator and subject while conveying reverence

---

## Tense Rules

| Content Type | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mythological narrative | Past tense | "देवी ने महिषासुर का वध किया" (The Goddess killed Mahishasura) |
| Descriptions of deities | Present tense (eternal) | "देवी दुर्गा सदैव रक्षक हैं" (Goddess Durga is ever-protective) |
| Historical events | Past tense | "१९९० में पहली बार सामुदायिक दुर्गा पूजा हुई" |
| Modern celebrations | Present tense | "आज नवरात्रि पूरी दुनिया में मनाया जाता है" |
| Philosophical concepts | Present tense | "शक्ति सृष्टि की सक्रिय ऊर्जा है" |
| Ritual descriptions | Present tense (ongoing tradition) | "नवरात्रि में कर्तव्य पूजा की जाती है" |

---

## Language Rules

### Primary Language: Hindi (Devanagari)

- All narration is in Hindi (Devanagari)
- No English words mixed into narration
- Sanskrit terms handled per Voiceover Guide protocol

### Devanagari Text in Script

All narration text is written in Devanagari script with transliteration for reference.

Example format:
```
नवरात्रि (Navratri) — नौ रातें जब देवी शक्ति अवतरित होती है
```

### Numbers and Dates

| Type | Format |
|---|---|
| Hindi numbers in narration | Devanagari numerals (१, २, ३...) |
| Years | Devanagari with Saka/Vikram context: "सातवीं सदी ईस्वी" |
| Dates (calendar) | Hindi calendar names: प्रतिपदा, द्वितीया, etc. |

---

## Citation Style in Narration

| Type | Format (Devanagari) | English equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Scriptural reference | "देवीमाहात्म्य में कहा गया है कि..." | "As stated in the Devi Mahatmya..." |
| Scholarly reference | "विद्वानों के अनुसार..." | "According to scholars..." |
| Historical reference | "इतिहास के अनुसार..." | "According to history..." |
| Direct quote | Full Devanagari + attribution | Full quote + attribution |

---

## Profanity Policy

**Zero tolerance.** No profanity, crude language, or disrespectful references.

---

## Character Dialogue Rules

| Character Type | How Handled |
|---|---|
| Devas (gods) | Referenced by narrator, not directly speaking in narration |
| Mahishasura | Referenced by narrator — no dialogue for the demon |
| Durga | Referenced by narrator with devotional tone |
| Sage Markandeya | May be referenced as the source of the narrative |
| Modern celebrants | Shown through real footage, no scripted dialogue |

**No character dialogue in narration.** All content is narrator-driven.

## Number, Date, Name Handling

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Numbers in narration | Devanagari numerals |
| Sanskrit/Devanagari names | Always Devanagari + transliteration on first mention |
| Place names | Standard Hindi: कोलकाता, गुजरात, मैसूर, दक्षिण भारत |
| Festival names | Always Devanagari: नवरात्रि, दुर्गा पूजा, विजयादशमी |

## Script Structure

### Opening (Scenes 1-5)
- Hook the viewer immediately with the cosmic battle concept
- Establish the stakes: the entire universe under threat
- Introduce the question: How does a demon become invincible?

### Act 1 — Cosmic Battle (Scenes 6-20)
- Build tension with Mahishasura's conquest
- Introduce the Devas' desperation
- Build toward the creation of Durga
- Climax: Durga's arrival and the first confrontation

### Act 2 — Nine Nights (Scenes 21-60)
- Methodical coverage of each of the 9 nights
- Each form gets 4-5 scenes:
  1. Introduction of the form
  2. Visual showcase (animated)
  3. Mantra + meaning
  4. Spiritual significance
- Build momentum through the 9 nights
- Peak: Kalaratri (most intense form) on Saptami
- Resolution: Mahagauri (return to purity) on Ashtami
- Transcendence: Siddhidatri (perfection) on Navami

### Act 3 — Regional Celebrations (Scenes 61-80)
- Shift from mythological to real-world
- Each region gets 4-5 scenes
- Build a sense of national (and global) celebration
- Include sensory details: sounds of dandiya, colors of pandals

### Act 4 — Deeper Symbolism (Scenes 81-100)
- Slow down the pacing
- More contemplative tone
- Connect mythology to personal transformation
- Expand to global context

### Act 5 — Closing (Scenes 101-108)
- Peak devotional moment: Aarti/Jai Mata Di
- Call to personal reflection
- Fade out on a note of peace and empowerment
