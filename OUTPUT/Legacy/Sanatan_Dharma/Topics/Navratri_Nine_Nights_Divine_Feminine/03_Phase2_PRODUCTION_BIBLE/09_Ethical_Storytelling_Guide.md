# Ethical Storytelling Guide

## Project: Navratri — The Nine Nights of the Divine Feminine

---

## Representation Rules

### Who Must Be Represented Fairly

| Group/Entity | Representation Rule |
|---|---|
| Goddess Durga/Devi | Represented with utmost respect, traditional iconography, devotional tone |
| Mahishasura | Presented as mythological antagonist — not a reference to any real community or group |
| Devas (Vishnu, Shiva, etc.) | Mentioned respectfully as part of the narrative |
| Regional communities (Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, etc.) | Celebrated equally — no hierarchy of "correct" way to celebrate |
| Shakta scholars/practitioners | Acknowledged as sources of knowledge |

### What NOT to Do

1. **DO NOT** present Mahishasura as representing any real ethnic or religious group — he is a purely mythological figure
2. **DO NOT** privilege one regional celebration over others (e.g., don't present Durga Puja as "the real" Navratri)
3. **DO NOT** mock, diminish, or show disrespect to any deity or religious figure
4. **DO NOT** present animal sacrifice as "backward" or "primitive" — present it historically and note modern evolution
5. **DO NOT** impose a feminist or secular lens that diminishes the devotional significance of the content

---

## Balance Requirements

| Topic | Balance Rule |
|---|---|
| Mahishasura story | Present as sacred mythology AND as allegory for inner demons — both levels are valid |
| Navadurga system | Acknowledge that the 9-forms system is a later development (not in the original Devi Mahatmya) |
| Regional variations | Give equal weight to each major tradition (Bengal, Gujarat, South, North) |
| Historical dating | Acknowledge uncertainty — use ranges, not single dates |
| Shakti philosophy | Present both Shakta (Goddess as supreme) and non-Shakta perspectives |

## Sensitive Topic Handling

### Animal Sacrifice

**Rule:** Present historically and with context.

> "कुछ शाक्त परंपराओं में पशुबलिदान की पारंपरिक प्रथा रही है, जिसकी उल्लेख कालिका पुराण में भी है। हालांकि, आज के समय में अधिकांश मंदिरों में यह पद्धति प्रतीकात्मक बलिदान या फल-फूलों से बदल गई है।"

> (Some Shakta traditions have a traditional practice of animal sacrifice, mentioned even in the Kalika Purana. However, in modern times, most temples have replaced this with symbolic offerings.)

### Different Interpretations

**Rule:** Acknowledge that Navratri means different things to different people.

- For devotees: Literal worship of the Goddess
- For scholars: Historical development of Goddess worship
- For spiritual seekers: Inner transformation
- For cultural participants: Festival and community

All perspectives are valid and should be acknowledged.

## Specific Rules for This Topic

1. **No commercialization of sacred content:** Do not frame Navratri primarily as a "trending topic" or "viral potential." Present it as a sacred tradition with relevance.

2. **Avoid festival commercialism:** Don't present Navratri as primarily a shopping season or entertainment event. The core is spiritual.

3. **Gender sensitivity:** Navratri celebrates the Divine Feminine. Frame it positively as empowerment and spiritual power, not as "women-only" or exclusionary.

4. **Cultural respect:** When showing images of celebrations, ensure they are authentic and respectfully presented. No staged or exploitative footage.

5. **Inclusivity:** Navratri is celebrated by Hindus primarily, but its message of inner transformation has universal appeal. Present it as such without appropriating from or diminishing the source tradition.

## Bias Checks

| Check | Pass Criteria |
|---|---|
| Is one regional tradition given more weight than others? | No — all major traditions (Bengal, Gujarat, South, North) get equal coverage |
| Are mythological elements presented as "superstition"? | No — presented with respect and explained both literally and symbolically |
| Is the tone devotional or dismissive? | Devotional and respectful throughout |
| Are all 9 forms given equal detail? | Yes — each gets a dedicated scene |
| Is Shakti presented only as "feminine power" or also as cosmic energy? | Both — spiritual/philosophical AND empowerment framing |
