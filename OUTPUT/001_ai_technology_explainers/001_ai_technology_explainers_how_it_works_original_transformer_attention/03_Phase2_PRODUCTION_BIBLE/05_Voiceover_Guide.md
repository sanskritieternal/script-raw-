# Voiceover — Engaging Storytelling
One calm EN narrator. Explain rather than announce a revelation. Emphasize “invented,” “within the studied systems,” and “before normalization.” Read the script exactly within its evidence scope; do not improvise product examples or claims.

## Timing and delivery
75 cues occupy planned eight-second slots. See 11_Dialogue_Script.md for spoken text and PRODUCTION_MANIFEST.json (narration_plan) for its machine-readable equivalent. Plan around 150–175 words per minute, with shorter cues leaving reading space. Equations and names require actual rehearsal; word count is not a recorded-duration measurement. Aim to finish each cue within its slot and use remaining time for a visual hold.

If a cue overruns, first reduce wording while preserving claim scope and arithmetic. Log the change in both script records, retain eight-second scene duration, then repeat narration review. Do not speed the entire explanation merely to fit a dense passage.

## Pronunciation and accessibility
Read NLP as individual letters; arXiv as “archive”; ln as “natural log”; q as “query” when introducing notation. Use “one half,” “one quarter,” “two thirds” and “four thirds.” Verify author-name pronunciation before recording; do not claim a pronunciation was approved.

A licensed voice or the user's own voice may be used. Named researchers are never impersonated. Provide an accurate transcript and synchronized captions after generation. The visual description must remain understandable without relying exclusively on color or movement.
