## Editorial scope

**Focus / example question:** What competing explanations remain for a claim's persistence after correction?

**Coverage:** Investigate why one claim remained persuasive after a correction using available audience and distribution evidence; avoid assuming a single psychological cause.

**Evidence to seek:** Compare competing explanations and specify missing discriminating evidence. Case records: original claims and records, dated redistribution traces, lesson designs and original learning evaluations. Starting framework: UNESCO media and information literacy curriculum (https://www.unesco.org/mil4teachers/en/introduction). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting.

**Limits:** Check original evidence and assess learning, not just confidence. Niche boundary: Claim checking, media interpretation and evaluated learning; media history, account security and general education have separate homes.

Before Phase 1, narrow this guidance to a specific case, source or work, and the relevant place, population, version or period. Record that choice in the episode's story question and research summary.
