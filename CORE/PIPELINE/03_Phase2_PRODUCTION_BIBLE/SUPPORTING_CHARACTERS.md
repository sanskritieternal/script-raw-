# Supporting character dossiers

## Character profiles

Read `CORE/PIPELINE/03_Phase2_PRODUCTION_BIBLE/PRODUCTION_DETAIL_STANDARD.md`. Repeat the full dossier structure below for every relevant individual. Fill it with concrete episode decisions and descriptions; do not replace fields with a paragraph about continuity precautions. Tables may summarize an index, but must not substitute for individual profiles.

### Character: <stable ID and name>

#### Identity, role and appearance age

Record name/aliases, stable ID, narrative importance, story function, title/status where supported, current depiction/voice use, actual scene ranges and design version. Distinguish source-backed age from proposed adult/child visual presentation; do not invent historical dates or exact ages.

#### Physical description

Individually specify relevant face/cheeks/jaw, eyes, eyebrows, nose/muzzle, mouth, ears, hair/fur/facial hair, body build/proportions, posture, hands/feet, skin/material treatment, distinctive features and nonhuman anatomy where applicable. Supply chosen proposed design values, not merely phrases such as keep the face consistent. Separate documented appearance from creative reconstruction. Do not assign stereotyped morality to complexion, species, sex or accent.

#### Clothing

Describe the actual proposed outfit pieces, silhouette, layering, material, colours, folds/attachment, appropriate variants and scene/event ranges. Link authoritative costume IDs. State what changes and what remains the same. Do not invent a wardrobe for an unrelated scene or imply historical authentication from an art prompt.

#### Accessories, weapons and jewellery

Give individual appearance/function/state/owner and scene use for relevant items; link prop IDs. Explicitly say none assigned or not depicted when appropriate. Distinguish permanent ornaments from transferred story objects. Do not use blank category labels or generic warnings instead of the actual episode decision.

#### Personality and observable behaviour

Describe individual characteristics through supported actions and proposed performance choices, including gestures, posture, attention, speech and interactions. Label interpretation or fictional invention. Do not assert private psychology or an unsourced biography as verified fact.

#### Emotional and dramatic arc

Map the character's role at actual story stages to scene regions, actions, expression and performance direction. A mention-only character can have an audience-information progression without an invented performed arc. Distinguish that from an on-screen emotional transformation.

#### Expression and gesture sheet

Specify individual neutral, questioning/considering, explaining/listening, emotional and action expressions as appropriate. Give concrete face/body changes and relevant scene uses. Define poses/contact studies and identity constraints. Do not use one universal negative prompt that forbids required emotion or movement.

#### Voice, speech and pronunciation

Specify narrator versus performed dialogue, language, delivery qualities, pronunciation-review needs, speaking/quiet regions and actual recording status. A proposed voice direction is not a real recording or authorization to imitate a living performer.

#### Relationships and scene states

Record the relevant relationship network, interactions, entry/exit, key decisions and persistent costume/location/prop states. Link chronology and profiles by ID instead of creating conflicting duplicate biographies.

#### Character consistency prompt

Provide one complete reusable, character-specific reference prompt with the chosen face/body/hair/costume/expression/style values. Specify neutral/reference layout and distinguish it from a scene-action prompt. Actual reference IDs/paths and approval status remain explicit. A list saying write a prompt is not a finished prompt.

#### Negative prompt / exclusion brief

Provide individual identity, anatomy, costume, prop, style and unwanted-content exclusions. Distinguish creative exclusions from generator-specific supported syntax. Do not claim a model supports a particular input/control before verification.

#### Reference assets and acceptance

Specify needed views, expression/pose/scale sheets, costume/prop contacts, asset IDs, actual/planned state and concrete acceptance/rejection examples. Real reviewer/date/version evidence is required for approval; proposed designs remain proposed.

## Appearance and voice

Keep physical and voice descriptions inside each individual dossier so a reader can find a complete character specification in one place. The appearance-lock and voiceover documents remain authoritative for version/review and recording decisions; they complement, not replace, the dossier detail.

## Evidence and depiction

Link actual Phase 1 claims/source locators for narrative assertions. Clearly distinguish source facts, interpretation and proposed visual invention. An invented art detail can be deliberately proposed without being falsely presented as textual evidence. No fabricated actual file, source, age, voice permission or approval.

## Scene and reference links

Fill actual episode-relative links to the relevant appearance, costume/prop, timeline, scene and asset records. Preserve stable IDs and one authoritative profile per character; keep main/supporting importance separate from protagonist/opponent/ally function.

## Mention-only supporting figures

Do not stop the dossier at mention-only when the user requests a complete character concept bible. State current scene status, then provide concrete optional reference-only physical/outfit/expression/prompt proposals as clearly labelled creative concepts where useful. Keep these separate from active visual locks, assets and generation requests. They do not add a new scene, voice or cast member without an explicit creative revision. If a proposed field is genuinely unnecessary or inappropriate, explain the actual reason rather than filling it with false source claims.

## File-specific completion evidence

Individual dossiers with story function and source scope; concrete optional visual/voice/prompts when requested, clearly separate inactive concepts from active cast.

Record actual section/example/reference evidence in the per-file editorial depth review before marking complete. A word count, present heading or generic continuity warning is not proof that the decisions above have been supplied.
