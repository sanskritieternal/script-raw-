## PRODUCTION RULES (13 CATEGORIES)

### Rule 1: Factual Accuracy — MANDATORY
- Every claim must be VERIFIABLE (not opinion).
- Financial figures must be CORRECT (math errors destroy credibility).
- Dates and timelines must be ACCURATE (chronology matters).
- Company names spelled correctly (no shortcuts).
- If uncertain, cite source and let viewers verify.

### Rule 2: Source Documentation & Attribution
- Every significant claim needs a SOURCE.
- Sources must be CREDIBLE (SEC filings, news sources, academic).
- Sources listed publicly (description links).
- Quotes include attribution.
- Numbers include source year and original company.

### Rule 3: Financial Precision & Accuracy
- Dollar amounts must be exact (round to nearest $100M+ only if stated).
- Math must be correct (if claiming $X profit, verify math).
- Comparisons must be adjusted for inflation (1970s $X ≠ 2024 $X).
- Financial concepts explained correctly (don't confuse revenue/profit).
- Valuation methods transparent (how was unicorn valued at $1B).

### Rule 4: Legal Liability Prevention
- No false statements about companies/people.
- No accusations without evidence.
- Speculation clearly marked as speculation ("some argue", "theory suggests").
- Defamatory language avoided.
- When in doubt: legal review before publishing.

### Rule 5: Bias Prevention & Fair Representation
- If criticizing company, acknowledge successes too.
- If praising founder, acknowledge failures/controversies.
- Multiple perspectives shown (if story is controversial).
- No unfair advantage given to competitors.
- Acknowledge conflicts of interest.

### Rule 6: Context & Nuance (Avoid Oversimplification)
- The subject is complex; avoid "X caused failure" (usually multiple factors).
- Acknowledge competing theories of what happened.
- Show nuance (not everything is binary good/bad).
- Market forces, timing, luck all matter (credit appropriately).
- Avoid false causation (correlation ≠ causation).

### Rule 7: Ethical Storytelling (Don't Exploit Failure)
- Failed subjects: Lessons learned focus (not mockery).
- Fraud cases: Victim impact acknowledged.
- Founder failures: Humanize (don't just embarrass).
- Layoffs/crises: Employee impact considered.
- Bankruptcies: Financial consequences for real people.

### Rule 8: Transparency & Disclosure
- Sponsored content clearly labeled.
- Affiliate links disclosed.
- Conflicts of interest revealed.
- If you own stock in company, disclose.
- Patreon members thanked by name.
- Bias acknowledged where present.

### Rule 9: Investment Disclaimer
- Videos are NOT investment advice.
- Disclaimer in description: "Not financial advice".
- No "buy/sell/hold" recommendations.
- No prediction of stock price.
- Educational only; viewers make own decisions.
- "See video for entertainment/education purposes only".

### Rule 10: Controversial Topics — Extra Care
- Multiple perspectives included.
- Controversial claims flagged.
- Sources for controversial claims provided.
- Acknowledge legitimate debate.
- Don't present opinion as fact.

### Rule 11: Continuous Verification
- Errors corrected (video update/description edit).
- New information incorporated (if major facts change).
- Comments monitored for fact-checking.
- Corrections acknowledged publicly.
- Willingness to be wrong earns trust.

### Rule 12: Industry Respect & Expert Consultation
- Complex financial topics: consult expert.
- Emerging industries: research thoroughly.
- Regulatory changes: verify current status.
- Technical accuracy matters.
- Accountants, lawyers, economists in video = credibility.

### Rule 13: AI Safety & Content Filter Rules
- Prevent accidental inclusion of harmful, illegal, defamatory, or platform-policy-violating content.
- Run the safety checklist on all AI-generated scenes before production proceeds.
- See full section below.

## TIMING SPECIFICATIONS & AUDIO BLOCK MAPPING

### Scene Duration Standards

**Default (FIXED 8-SECOND mode): every scene is exactly 8 seconds.** When Q42 = VARIABLE, use the per-content-type table below.

| Scene Type | Duration | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Standard scene | 8s | Most scenes across all content types (FIXED default) |
| Biographical deep dive | 8–10s | Founder profiles, personal journeys (VARIABLE only) |
| Quick hook / data reveal | 5–6s | Short-form reels, fast-paced openings (VARIABLE only) |
| Data visualization reveal | 8s | Charts, graphs, financial figures (FIXED default) |

### Video Generation Tool Timing Constraints

**Veo3 (and similar AI video generators):**
- **Hard clip limit: 8 seconds per generated clip.**
- Scenes longer than 8s must be produced as two or more clips stitched together in post.
- Plan shot structure accordingly: each 8s clip should contain one complete visual idea.

**Standard Animation (2D/3D, pre-rendered):**
- No hard duration limit. Full 6–10s range applies freely.

**Live Action:**
- No hard clip limit. Scene length determined by creative and narrative needs.
- B-roll coverage: shoot 2–3x intended scene duration for editorial flexibility.

**Data Visualization + Interview Clips:**
- Chart/graph reveals: allow 7–8s for viewer to absorb data.
- Interview soundbites: 15–45 seconds per clip, trimmed to key statement.
- Composite scenes: data viz lead-in (3–4s) → interview reaction (8–12s) → return to data (3–4s).

### Audio Block Mapping (Narration)

**Target narration speed:** 140–160 WPM (Words Per Minute)
- This is the professional documentary standard.
- Slightly slower than entertainment (170–190 WPM) to allow complex concepts to land.
- Slightly faster than academic lecture (120–140 WPM) to maintain engagement.

**Audio Block Template per Scene:**
- **Opening hook line** (1–2s): Provocative statement or question
- **Core narration** (4–6s): Fact delivery, data context, narrative progression
- **Closing signal** (1–2s): Transition cue, emotional beat, or data reveal lead-out
- **Total narration per scene:** ~18–22 words (at 140–160 WPM for an 8-second scene)
- **Silence / data absorption time:** 0–2s per scene (chart on screen, no narration)

### Script Delivery Template

Use this template as the header for every master script and scene batch document:

```
SCRIPT DELIVERY HEADER
======================
Total Scenes: [count]
Total Duration: [MM:SS]
Content Domain: [domain name]
Target Audience: [General / Professionals / Entrepreneurs / Investors / Students]
Average Scene Duration: [X.X]s
Technical Info:
  - Video Method: [AI Video / Animation / Live Action / Data Viz + Interviews]
  - Primary Tool: [Veo3 / Seedance / Kling / Custom Animation / etc.]
  - Audio: 140–160 WPM narration, [voice profile]
  - Safety Level: [Standard / Strict / Platform-specific]
File Naming: [domain]-[title]-v[version].md
Legal Review Required: [Yes / No] — Reason: [brief]
```

### File Naming Convention (detailed)

- Default pattern: `{domain-slug}-{content-title}-v{version}.md`
  - Example: `company-rise-fall-kodak-demise-v1.md`
  - Example: `startup-stories-airbnb-unicorn-v2.md`
  - Example: `financial-crises-2008-crash-v1.md`
- Batch files: append batch number — `{domain}-{title}-v1-batch-01.md`
- Version increments: v1 → v2 when substantive narrative or data changes. Minor edits do not require version bump.
- Scene files within a batch: `scene-001.md`, `scene-002.md`, etc.

## VISUAL STYLES CATALOG

### 1. Stock Footage + Graphics
**Description:** Professional news-style; stock footage interspersed with animated graphics/overlays.
**Best For:** General stories, market news, company updates.
**Technical:** Quality stock footage (Getty Images, Unsplash) + overlaid text/logos.
**Color Grading:** Professional, neutral, corporate (blues, grays, whites).
**Pacing:** Moderate, matches voiceover.
**Examples:** CNBC, Bloomberg, Financial Times videos.
**Impact:** Credible, professional, trustworthy.

### 2. Data Visualization Heavy
**Description:** Charts, graphs, animations dominate; visual data tells story.
**Best For:** Economic analysis, financial crises, market history, comparisons.
**Technical:** Professional charts (animated), data graphics, infographics.
**Color Grading:** Color-coded (red = loss, green = profit; or per data type).
**Typography:** Professional, readable, not cluttered.
**Examples:** 3Blue1Brown economics videos, FT DataViz.
**Impact:** Extremely credible, data-driven, educational.

### 3. Archive Footage
**Description:** Historical footage; time-machine aesthetic; old news reels, historical recordings.
**Best For:** Company histories, industry evolution, luxury brands, fashion.
**Technical:** Real historical footage (public domain, licensed).
**Color Grading:** Faded, aged, period-appropriate (black/white or faded colors).
**Pacing:** Slower, contemplative, respects historical weight.
**Examples:** Documentaries, History Channel-style.
**Impact:** Authentic, nostalgic, educational context.

### 4. Documentary Realism
**Description:** On-location filming; interviews; real environments; journalistic.
**Best For:** Corporate scandals, investigations, people-focused stories.
**Technical:** Professional documentary crew; interviews with talking heads.
**Color Grading:** Natural, realistic, professional lighting.
**Examples:** Netflix documentaries, news investigations.
**Impact:** Extremely credible, adds depth, humanizes story.

### 5. Animated Explainers
**Description:** Simple 2D/3D animations teaching concepts; characters explaining.
**Best For:** Marketing psychology, supply chain, models, financial concepts.
**Technical:** Professional animation; clear, simple visuals.
**Pacing:** Matches teaching pace (slower for complex topics).
**Examples:** CrashCourse, CGP Grey economics.
**Impact:** Engaging, accessible, educational.

### 6. Timeline Visualization
**Description:** Evolution over time; company history laid out chronologically; visual progression.
**Best For:** Company rise/fall, startup stories, industry evolution, IPO journeys.
**Technical:** Animated timeline; key events marked; visual milestones.
**Color Grading:** Professional, highlighting key moments.
**Examples:** Company evolution videos, decade-by-decade histories.
**Impact:** Shows trajectory clearly, easy to follow narrative.

### 7. Infographics + Text Overlay
**Description:** Educational style; text-based, clean aesthetics; statistical focus.
**Best For:** Statistical analysis, marketing psychology, economic comparisons.
**Technical:** Professional infographics; minimal motion; focus on information.
**Color Grading:** Clean, minimal, corporate colors.
**Pacing:** Slower (viewer reads text).
**Examples:** Educational YouTube channels, data journalism.
**Impact:** Authoritative, data-heavy, professional.

### 8. Documentary + Interview Hybrid
**Description:** Interview-driven narrative; expert commentary + visuals; talking heads central.
**Best For:** Billionaire stories, founder stories, corporate culture, investigations.
**Technical:** Professional interviews; talking heads prominent; supporting footage.
**Pacing:** Driven by interview pacing (natural, conversational).
**Examples:** Netflix documentaries, YouTube interviews.
**Impact:** Personal, credible, adds dimension.

### 9. Animation + Data Visualization Hybrid
**Description:** Animated data graphics; numbers brought to life; interactive-feeling.
**Best For:** Financial analysis, economic history, market movements.
**Technical:** Animated charts; data-driven animations; seamless transitions.
**Color Grading:** Data-driven (colors mean something; consistent palette).
**Examples:** FT graphics, 3b1b data visualization.
**Impact:** Engaging + credible; makes data digestible.

### 10. Minimalist Aesthetic
**Description:** Clean, professional, corporate look; less is more; focus on content.
**Best For:** Serious analysis, corporate news, professional audience.
**Technical:** Minimal animations; clean typography; white space respected.
**Color Grading:** 1-2 accent colors; mostly white/black/gray.
**Examples:** Financial channels, corporate videos.
**Impact:** Professional, serious, trustworthy.

### YouTube Algorithm Advantages for Category Content

**Advantage 1: NO DEMONETIZATION**
- Category content welcome (not like horror).
- Monetizes normally (good CPM rates).
- No age restrictions (reach full audience).
- Sponsors like category content (high-value ads).

**Advantage 2: SERIES ALGORITHM BOOST**
- Category audiences binge (watch multiple).
- Playlists organize naturally (by company/topic).
- Higher watch time = better ranking.
- Algorithm rewards series completions.

**Advantage 3: EVERGREEN + CURRENT**
- Historical content stays relevant (no expiration).
- New information adds freshness.
- Can update old videos (evergreen content).
- Consistent views over long period.

### Thumbnail Strategy

**Color Palette:**
- Success: Greens, blues (growth, achievement, trust)
- Failure: Reds, oranges (danger, caution)
- Neutral: Grays, blacks (professional, serious)
- Accent: Gold, yellows (luxury, premium)

**Numbers & Data Elements:**
- Large percentage increases (99%, 1000% growth)
- Dollar amounts ($1B, $50M loss)
- Charts showing trend (📈 📉)

**Faces & Authority:**
- Key figure portraits (recognizable faces)
- Authority figures (add credibility)
- Expressive faces (adds intrigue)

**Text Approach:**
- Curiosity gaps: "This is why X failed"
- Shock value: "$1 BILLION LOSS"
- Mystery: "The secret behind X"
- Achievement: "How they built $100B"
- All caps keywords (easy to read at small size)

### Thumbnail Examples by Category

**Company Rise/Fall:** Company logo + growth/decline arrow, founder face + $ symbol, text: "[Company] EXPOSED" / "How they FELL".
**Figure Success:** Key figure portrait (serious expression), currency/growth symbols, text: "[Name]'s RISE" / "How they succeeded".
**Product Launch:** Product + growth arrow, founder smiling + financial imagery, text: "$1B PRODUCT" / "Their SUCCESS".
**Scandal/Fraud:** Key figure face (serious look), red X or caution symbol, text: "EXPOSED" / "THE FRAUD".
**Market/Trends:** Stock chart, financial visuals, big numbers, text: "CRASH" / "BOOM" / Numbers.

## VOICEOVER RULES

### Voiceover Tone by Category Sub-Genre

**Company Rise/Fall:**
- Tone: Narrative storytelling, engaging
- Pace: 160-180 WPM
- Delivery: Respectful of both success and failure
- "Apple went from garage startup to $3 trillion..."

**Billionaire Stories:**
- Tone: Inspiring, occasionally critical
- Pace: 160-180 WPM
- Delivery: Humanize (not just hero worship)
- "He was just like everyone else, until one decision changed everything..."

**Scandal/Fraud:**
- Tone: Serious, investigative, slightly accusatory
- Pace: 140-160 WPM (slower, let gravity sink in)
- Delivery: Documentary authority
- "What happened next shocked the financial world..."

**Startup Growth:**
- Tone: Enthusiastic, energetic, optimistic
- Pace: 170-190 WPM (faster, excited)
- Delivery: Celebration of innovation
- "Then something incredible happened..."

**Economic Analysis:**
- Tone: Expert, educational, neutral
- Pace: 150-170 WPM (measured, clear)
- Delivery: Explain concepts carefully
- "This phenomenon is called..."

**Innovation Focus:**
- Tone: Excited, wonder-struck, educational
- Pace: 160-180 WPM
- Delivery: Celebrate breakthrough
- "This technology would change everything..."

**Marketing/Psychology:**
- Tone: Knowledgeable, slightly playful
- Pace: 160-175 WPM
- Delivery: Reveal "tricks" of marketing
- "And that's exactly what they want you to think..."

**Investment/VC:**
- Tone: Professional, analytical
- Pace: 160-175 WPM
- Delivery: Financial knowledge
- "The numbers simply didn't add up..."

### Voiceover Technical Specifications

**Audio Quality:**
- Sample rate: 48kHz
- Bit depth: 24-bit
- Format: WAV or AIFF (lossless)
- No compression (compress at mastering)

**Voiceover Duration Per Scene:**
- Content needs clarity (not silence like horror).
- 60-80% voiceover, 20-40% visual/data time.
- Why: Audiences want explanation + visuals work together.

### Multi-Language Voiceover (if Q19 ≠ None)
- Generate a `VOICE_SCRIPT_<lang>.md` file per additional language at the end of Phase 3.
- Localize speaker labels into the target language (e.g., NARRATOR → कथावाचक in Hindi).
- Keep primary scene breakdown in English for production cross-reference.
- Transliterate character/company names when the target language uses a different script.
- Translate parenthetical directions (e.g., "(excited)") into the target language.

## DATA VISUALIZATION & INFOGRAPHICS GUIDELINES

### When to Use Data Visualization

**Use Charts When:**
- Comparing numbers (Company A revenue vs B revenue)
- Showing trends over time (stock price, growth trajectory)
- Displaying percentages or parts of whole
- Breaking down complex data
- Teaching financial concepts

**Avoid Charts When:**
- Single static number (just say it in voiceover)
- Too complex to animate clearly
- Data is outdated (updates constantly)
- Chart would be tiny/unreadable

### Chart Types & Best Uses

**Line Graph:** Trends over time (stock price, company growth).
**Bar Chart:** Comparisons between items (tech CEO net worth comparison).
**Pie Chart:** Parts of a whole (market share).
**Stacked Bar:** Composition over time (GDP by sector).
**Scatter Plot:** Correlation/relationship between variables.
**Infographic/Icon-Based:** Educational, non-mathematical data (5 reasons company failed).

### Data Visualization Best Practices

**Color Coding:**
- Red = Loss, decline, bad, warning
- Green = Profit, growth, good, success
- Blue = Neutral, professional, trust
- Gold = Premium, luxury, wealth
- Consistent throughout video

**Animation Strategy:**

## AI SAFETY & CONTENT FILTER RULES

### Purpose
Prevent accidental inclusion of harmful, illegal, defamatory, or platform-policy-violating content in storytelling. All AI-generated scenes (AI Video, animation scripts, narration drafts) must pass this filter before production proceeds.

### 13.1 Mandatory Removal Rules

The following content categories must be **REMOVED** from all scenes and scripts. Zero exceptions unless explicitly noted.

- **Sexual/Suggestive Content:** REMOVE entirely. storytelling has no place for sexual content, suggestive imagery, or innuendo. If a historical source contains such references, paraphrase generically without explicit detail.

- **Violence / Injury / Death / Weapons:** REMOVE. content has no place for graphic violence, injury descriptions, death imagery, or weapon references. *Exception:* Historical war-related economic impacts (e.g., WWII industrial mobilization) may reference conflict in strictly factual, non-graphic, educational framing. No dramatization of violence.

- **Child Harm / Suffering:** REMOVE entirely. No exceptions. Any scene touching on child labor, child exploitation, or harm to minors must be handled with extreme sensitivity, described factually at the macro level (e.g., "labor practices in this factory drew criticism"), and never depict or describe specific harm to children.

- **Crime / Abuse / Illegal Activity:** REMOVE, with one qualified exception. General criminal content does not belong in storytelling. *Exception:* When documenting legitimate, well-documented corporate scandals (Enron, Wirecard, Theranos, FTX), factual descriptions of criminal conduct are permissible only when:
  1. The conduct has been established by a court, regulatory body, or credible investigative journalism.
  2. The framing is non-exploitative and educational, not sensational.
  3. All claims remain compliant with legal liability prevention guidelines.
  4. Defamatory risk is assessed and flagged before publishing.

- **Hate Speech / Politics / Extremism:** REMOVE entirely. Political bias, extremist views, hate speech, or discriminatory content have no place in professional category content. When covering political-economic topics (trade wars, regulation), maintain neutral, factual framing without endorsing or condemning political positions.

- **Real People / Celebrities (Non-Public-Domain):** REMOVE from AI-generated content. Living key figures, celebrities, and public personalities must be described generically ("the company's CEO," "a prominent investor") unless they are explicitly in the public domain through verified, citable sources. *Exception:* Public figures whose public statements and actions are documented in primary sources (SEC filings, press conferences, published interviews) may be referenced by name with proper attribution.

- **Negative Environment / Self-Harm / Suicide References:** REMOVE entirely. No references to self-harm, suicide, or related mental health crises in contexts unless the topic is directly relevant (e.g., workplace mental health crisis), in which case handle with extreme care, factual framing, and sensitivity to affected individuals.

### Safety Review Checklist

Run this checklist before finalizing any scene or script:

- [ ] **Sexual/Suggestive:** No explicit or implied sexual content present
- [ ] **Violence/Injury/Death/Weapons:** No graphic descriptions; historical war context uses neutral, factual framing only
- [ ] **Child Harm:** Zero content depicting or describing harm to minors
- [ ] **Crime/Abuse/Illegal:** All criminal references sourced from verified primary documents; scandal content follows exception rules
- [ ] **Hate/Politics/Extremism:** No partisan political language, hate speech, or extremist framing
- [ ] **Real People/Celebrities:** Living individuals described generically unless public-domain-sourced with attribution
- [ ] **Negative Environment:** No self-harm, suicide, or related references
- [ ] **Defamation Risk:** All factual claims sourced; no unverified accusations against named individuals/companies
- [ ] **Corporate Scandal Compliance:** If scandal content is present, legal review confirmation recorded
