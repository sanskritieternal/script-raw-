## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Wood Carving across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Wood Carving Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Wood Carving.
- **Tier 2: Wood Carving Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Wood Carving.
- **Tier 3: Wood Carving Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Wood Carving.
- **Tier 4: Wood Carving Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Wood Carving.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Wood Carving: A Wood-Carving Form's Record**
   - *Question:* What dated objects show one workshop tradition?
   - *Key Focus:* Name place and distinguish surviving evidence from origin. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
2. **Wood Carving: A Carved Interior or Object**
   - *Question:* What does it reveal about function and craft?
   - *Key Focus:* Check maker, wood, patron, site, and provenance. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
3. **Wood Carving: Forest to Finished Object**
   - *Question:* Whose growing, cutting, drying, and finishing labor is omitted?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit suppliers and artisans with consent. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
4. **Wood Carving: Reading a Wood Carving**
   - *Question:* How do grain, joinery, motif, and use fit together?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one named object and maker explanation. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
5. **Wood Carving: Design Around Grain**
   - *Question:* How do species and moisture shape form and durability?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented material choices and outcomes. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Wood Carving: A Carving's Unknown Workshop**
   - *Question:* What could identify maker or date?
   - *Key Focus:* Check marks, joinery, wood analysis, records, and gaps. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
7. **Wood Carving: A New Tool in the Workshop**
   - *Question:* Did it change labor, detail, price, or authorship?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated processes and artisan views. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
8. **Wood Carving: An Artisan-Teacher's Legacy**
   - *Question:* Who learned from one named carver?
   - *Key Focus:* Use workshop records and consented apprentices' accounts. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
9. **Wood Carving: A Material Supply Shock**
   - *Question:* How did a change in timber access affect craft?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace species, cost, substitutions, and local livelihoods. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
10. **Wood Carving: From Timber to Carving**
   - *Question:* Which stages link drying, design, cutting, joinery, and finish?
   - *Key Focus:* Document approved process, not unsafe tool instruction. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Wood Carving: Orders for Carved Wood**
   - *Question:* What explains a documented market shift?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare housing, tourism, substitutes, prices, and definitions. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
12. **Wood Carving: One Workshop Across Generations**
   - *Question:* How did products, tools, and buyers change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated objects and practitioner accounts. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
13. **Wood Carving: Sustaining a Wood Craft**
   - *Question:* What do makers need for viable practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare legal timber, training, fair pay, and demand. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
14. **Wood Carving: Handmade or Machine-Finished?**
   - *Question:* What does a product claim actually establish?
   - *Key Focus:* Check maker records, visible evidence, and definition. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
15. **Wood Carving: One Carved Object's Journey**
   - *Question:* How was it made, sold, used, repaired, and displayed?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace provenance and maker consent. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Wood Carving: A Carved Furniture Commission**
   - *Question:* Which client and use requirements shaped the work?
   - *Key Focus:* Record design, dimensions, material, budget, and revisions. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
17. **Wood Carving: Selecting Wood**
   - *Question:* Why choose one species or reclaimed stock?
   - *Key Focus:* Check origin, workability, durability, and supply claims. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
18. **Wood Carving: Workshop Decisions**
   - *Question:* Which steps require hand judgment?
   - *Key Focus:* Observe with permission and credit assistants. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
19. **Wood Carving: Pattern on Wood**
   - *Question:* How do motif, grain, and object function interact?
   - *Key Focus:* Ask maker about meaning before interpretation. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
20. **Wood Carving: Workshop Credit**
   - *Question:* Who designed, prepared, carved, assembled, and finished?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace wages, credits, and decision rights. Evidence base: provenanced objects, timber and workshop records, material analysis, conservation reports, and consented artisan and user accounts.
