# Selected primary-text source note — storytelling

Inspected 2026-10-24 for `BANK-372-043`. Compare only the specified accessible English editions, not an alleged earliest or definitive Cinderella.

- [Charles Perrault, *The Tales of Mother Goose*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17208/17208-h/17208-h.htm), Charles Welsh English translation published by D. C. Heath in 1901, Gutenberg transcription: the Cinderella text uses a fairy godmother, pumpkin coach, midnight limit and glass slipper. The edition's title says “first collected” in 1696; that editorial statement is not an inspected first printing.
- [Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, *Grimms' Fairy Tales*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2591/2591-h/2591-h.htm), Gutenberg edition based on translations by Edgar Taylor and Marian Edwardes: “Ashputtel” uses a hazel tree and bird, repeated festivities, a golden slipper and deception exposed in the shoe test. The electronic edition does not itself resolve which German printing underlies every passage.

Both texts were accessible. They are primary narratives in later English translations, not direct witnesses to oral origins, chronological influence, authorial intent or all Cinderella variants. A script should cite exact edition and translator and avoid copying long passages; legal reuse rights depend on the production jurisdiction and edition.
