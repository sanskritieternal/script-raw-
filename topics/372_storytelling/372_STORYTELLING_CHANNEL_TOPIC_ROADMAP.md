## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Storytelling across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Storytelling Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Storytelling.
- **Tier 2: Storytelling Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Storytelling.
- **Tier 3: Storytelling Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Storytelling.
- **Tier 4: Storytelling Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Storytelling.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Storytelling: A Story Form in Context**
   - *Question:* Which documented community or medium shaped a particular narrative form?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated accounts and avoid claiming a single universal origin. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
2. **Storytelling: A Story Retold Many Ways**
   - *Question:* Which elements remain and change across documented tellings?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versions and their makers, dates, and audiences. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
3. **Storytelling: Whose Version Survived?**
   - *Question:* Which narrators or communities were excluded from an archive or canon?
   - *Key Focus:* Check collection history and living stakeholder accounts. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
4. **Storytelling: How a Story Holds Together**
   - *Question:* How do character desire, conflict, and consequence connect in one tale?
   - *Key Focus:* Map specific events without prescribing one formula. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
5. **Storytelling: Time and Point of View**
   - *Question:* What changes when the same events are told out of order or through another narrator?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare bounded versions and information available to audiences. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Storytelling: An Uncertain Story Source**
   - *Question:* Which parts of a tale's attribution or transmission remain unresolved?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate earliest record from speculative ancestry. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
7. **Storytelling: A New Medium for a Story**
   - *Question:* How did a recording or interactive format change teller and audience choices?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versions and production constraints. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
8. **Storytelling: A Teller's Documented Choices**
   - *Question:* Which changes can be attributed to a named storyteller?
   - *Key Focus:* Use versions, interviews, and collaborator testimony. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
9. **Storytelling: A Story Enters Public Memory**
   - *Question:* What dated event changed circulation of a particular narrative?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace publication, performance, translation, and reception. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
10. **Storytelling: A Reveal's Information Path**
   - *Question:* What does the audience know at each step of one story?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a scene or beat map and test alternatives. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Storytelling: A Tale's Changing Popularity**
   - *Question:* Which evidence shows circulation or interpretation changed?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated editions, performances, or audience records. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
12. **Storytelling: One Tale in Two Periods**
   - *Question:* What social or formal differences shape two retellings?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versions with their audiences and contexts. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
13. **Storytelling: in a New Format**
   - *Question:* What possibilities and limits does an emerging format introduce?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe documented experiments and avoid predicting adoption. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
14. **Storytelling: Every Story Has One Structure?**
   - *Question:* How do counterexamples challenge a universal narrative template?
   - *Key Focus:* Define template and test diverse bounded works. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
15. **Storytelling: Tracing a Story's Journey**
   - *Question:* How did a particular narrative move between tellers and media?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow versioned sources with attribution gaps marked. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Storytelling: A Story for a Particular Audience**
   - *Question:* What was the teller or production asked to communicate?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, version, and actual reception. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
17. **Storytelling: The Medium Shapes the Tale**
   - *Question:* How do voice, text, image, and interaction carry different information?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare a bounded narrative across media. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
18. **Storytelling: From Idea to Told Story**
   - *Question:* Which documented revisions changed a story's causal chain?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow outline, draft, rehearsal, and release. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
19. **Storytelling: Choosing a Narrator**
   - *Question:* What becomes visible or hidden through one point of view?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare alternative narrations of the same event. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
20. **Storytelling: A Story Made Together**
   - *Question:* Who contributed to a particular version and who is credited?
   - *Key Focus:* Use production or oral-history records and local attribution norms. Evidence base: dated story versions, recordings, oral-history and archive provenance, production notes, rights and consent records, and situated audience testimony.
