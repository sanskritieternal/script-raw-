# Geometry primary-source follow-up — 2026-10-24

## BANK-364-041 — Euclid, Elements I.1

- Primary text in David E. Joyce's Clark University English edition, *Elements*, Book I, Proposition 1: https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/java/elements/bookI/propI1.html .
- Euclid constructs an equilateral triangle by taking the intersection of two equal-radius circles and then inferring equal sides. The editor's separate guide flags that the stated postulates do not themselves establish the intersection point's existence.
- Limit: distinguish Euclid's translated proposition from Joyce's modern critique. The argument is a construction under specified geometric assumptions; a rendered picture is not independent proof of an unstated existence axiom. Translation, diagrams and later formal axiomatizations may vary.

## BANK-364-042 — Hilbert, Foundations of Geometry

- Primary historical text: David Hilbert, *The Foundations of Geometry*, E. J. Townsend authorized English translation (1902; the accessible reprint is 1950), Project Gutenberg ebook: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17384 ; text PDF: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17384/17384-pdf.pdf .
- The edition lays out groups of axioms for connection, order, parallels, congruence and continuity and examines their compatibility and independence. The translator states that the 1899 German address preceded additions incorporated from the French edition.
- Limit: specify this translated/reprinted edition and its amendments; do not claim every 1899, 1902 or later axiom list is identical. A formal derivation is relative to its chosen axioms and logic, not a measurement proving which physical space we occupy.
