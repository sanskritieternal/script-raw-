# BANK-127-047: Jurassic Park's Dinosaur Input Device

Research checked: 2026-10-24. Status: selected premise research, not script or idea approval.

[Industrial Light & Magic's Jurassic Park project account](https://www.ilm.com/vfx/jurassic-park/) says the Dinosaur Input Device was made with Phil Tippett's team to bridge stop-motion animation and CG. Its [production retrospective](https://www.ilm.com/creating-dinosaurs-jurassic-park/) describes an armature with motion encoders: moves could be recorded and applied to CG models. ILM says the final film combined roughly six minutes of CG dinosaurs with nine minutes of practical puppets. These are first-party production descriptions, not an independent shot audit.

**Claim boundary:** The initial stop-motion plan changed for this film, and stop-motion artists contributed to its CG workflow. The records do not show that Spielberg fired an entire department or that CGI ended stop-motion everywhere. The archived trending pitch says four minutes of CGI; ILM's retrospective says six, so do not repeat the four-minute figure without a defined cut and measurement method. The project page reports fewer than 60 fully CG dinosaur shots, but shots and screen time are different units.
