## BANK-387-043: the 2015 Smiler collision

- [The 2016 Crown Court sentencing remarks](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/r-hse-v-merlin-attractions-operations-ltd-sentencing-remarks.pdf) are the primary judicial account of the 2 June 2015 collision at Alton Towers. The judge identified failures in risk assessment and a structured work system, addressing the controlling-computer reset and override sequence, and criticized emergency access. The [HSE case record](https://resources.hse.gov.uk/convictions-history/case/case_details.asp?SV=4444325) confirms the regulatory case.
- The judgment expressly rejected an early individual “human error” explanation as the underlying cause. Report the court's findings with attribution; do not infer motives, reproduce graphic injury detail unnecessarily, or extrapolate one failure to all coasters. Inspect technical exhibits before describing exact logic or block-section states beyond the judgment.
- This case gives the active block-control and inspection questions a bounded systems example. It does not measure current safety of a ride.

## BANK-387-044: Matterhorn Bobsleds opening

- [Walt Disney Imagineering's history](https://sites.disney.com/waltdisneyimagineering/our-story/) dates the Matterhorn Bobsleds opening to June 1959 and describes a tubular steel-track coaster as part of Disneyland's first major expansion. This is an operator account and supports the dated opening and its own technology claim.
- A “world's first” claim needs comparison with independent engineering and historical records. The page does not document every wheel, control or evacuation design detail; seek drawings, patents and operating records before assigning those features to this ride.
- This named design transition is separate from the Smiler operating-system failure and from 386's theme-park expansion case.
