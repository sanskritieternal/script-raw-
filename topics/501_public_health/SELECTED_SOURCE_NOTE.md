# Selected source note — CDC National Wastewater Surveillance System

Reviewed 2026-10-24 for BANK-501-043. Proposed question only; no approved IDEA, individual diagnosis or medical guidance.

## Primary agency research and documentation

- CDC authors, *Progress and Expansion of the National Wastewater Surveillance System, United States, 2020–2024*, Emerging Infectious Diseases (August 2026): https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/13/26-1118_article . It retrospectively describes growth in NWSS testing coverage, pathogen targets, analytics and public displays over the first four years.
- CDC, *About CDC's Wastewater Monitoring Program*: https://www.cdc.gov/nwss/wastewater-surveillance.html . The current program page describes participating sites and pathogens; its displayed site and population counts are dynamic and must be dated at scripting.
- CDC, *About Wastewater Data*: https://www.cdc.gov/nwss/about-data.html . CDC warns that municipal-sewer sampling can omit households on septic or other nonsewer systems, overrepresent urban/suburban populations, and present preliminary data that later change.

## Medical, denominator and outcome limits

Wastewater viral signals reflect sampled sewersheds; they are not individual cases, prevalence, a diagnosis, or an estimate of clinical severity. Growth in the number of sites or pathogens monitored is a surveillance-system outcome, not proof the program reduced infections or improved health. Coverage counts describe represented sewer service populations, not every U.S. resident. Pathogen-specific interpretation and site participation vary by date. Do not identify small communities or infer a person's infection from aggregate data. Paraphrase CDC research and check chart/data reuse terms.
