# BANK-083-053 — Hope Diamond material and provenance

**Selected question:** What can the Smithsonian establish about the Hope Diamond's material and ownership trail?

**Primary institutional records inspected (2026-10-24):**

- The [Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History object record](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/object/nmnhmineralsciences_1001299) identifies a 45.52-carat dark gray-blue diamond, specimen NMNH G3551-00, in a Cartier necklace setting. The museum catalogue is an object-identification source; its locality field alone does not document every transfer in the stone's history.
- The museum's [detailed history](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/explore/collections/hope-diamond-history) traces the French Blue and later Hope provenance through dated inventories and accounts. It acknowledges gaps in when and where Henry Philip Hope acquired the diamond and discusses the emergence of a curse narrative as part of the stone's public history.
- The Smithsonian's [material and display history](https://avpreservation.si.edu/spotlight/hope-diamond/history) describes the type IIb classification, boron-linked blue coloration and red phosphorescence after ultraviolet exposure. Its provenance account notes the 1958 donation and the distinction between an older approximate weight and measured 45.52 carats.

**Bounded finding:** The current museum object and material characteristics are documented. Boron is linked to the blue color; UV-stimulated red phosphorescence is a separately observed phenomenon. The “curse” is a reported legend whose historical spread can be investigated, not an explanation for owners' misfortunes. The specific archived ocean-floor boron origin is not established by these object records.

**Before production:** Check the technical spectroscopy and geological literature before explaining boron incorporation or the phosphorescence mechanism in detail. Separate Tavernier/French Blue attribution, documented transfers and later reconstructions, and cite the museum's provenance gaps.
