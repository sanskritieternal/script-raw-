## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Writing across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Writing Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Writing.
- **Tier 2: Writing Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Writing.
- **Tier 3: Writing Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Writing.
- **Tier 4: Writing Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Writing.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Writing: A Writing Practice Emerges**
   - *Question:* Which audience or medium shaped one writing convention?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated examples and guides. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
2. **Writing: A Draft and Its Final Form**
   - *Question:* What did revision improve or change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare authorized drafts and intended audience. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
3. **Writing: Editors and Writing Partners**
   - *Question:* Whose feedback or labor shaped a finished piece?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credited records and interviews. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
4. **Writing: From Purpose to Paragraph**
   - *Question:* How can a writer make one point clear for one reader?
   - *Key Focus:* Show brief, draft, and revision. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
5. **Writing: Structure and Reader Inference**
   - *Question:* How does ordering evidence change a reader's understanding?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare alternative drafts and reader tests. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Writing: A Difficult Attribution**
   - *Question:* Which evidence can identify who wrote or revised a text?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate style inference from records. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
7. **Writing: A Writing Tool Tested**
   - *Question:* Does a tool improve clarity or merely speed?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts, errors, and writer control. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
8. **Writing: A Writer's Revision Habit**
   - *Question:* What does a named writer's archive reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts and correspondence, not universal lessons. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
9. **Writing: A Text Gets Rewritten**
   - *Question:* Which editor or event prompted a documented revision?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace version and feedback. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
10. **Writing: as Iteration**
   - *Question:* How do planning, drafting, checking, and revision interact?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one bounded piece. Evidence base: consented versioned drafts, editorial notes, source and rights records, published text, reader feedback, and writing-instruction evidence.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
