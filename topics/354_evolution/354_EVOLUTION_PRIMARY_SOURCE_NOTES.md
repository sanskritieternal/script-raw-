# Evolution primary-source follow-up — 2026-10-24

## BANK-354-041 — Long-term E. coli experiment

- Primary paper: Blount, Borland and Lenski, *Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli*, *PNAS* 105 (10 June 2008): https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105 . Author-hosted paper: https://lenski.mmg.msu.edu/lenski/pdf/2008%2C%20PNAS%2C%20Blount%20et%20al.pdf .
- The researchers followed 12 initially identical glucose-limited populations and replayed frozen lineages. A citrate-using phenotype appeared after more than 30,000 generations in one population; later frozen clones showed greater propensity for the trait than earlier ones under the tested conditions.
- Limit: the publisher DOI redirected to an inaccessible page in this review; its indexed primary-paper abstract and author-hosted PDF were available. This is a specified laboratory environment and phenotype, not a claim that all evolutionary innovations require the same path or that evolution has a goal.

## BANK-354-042 — Stickleback armor and Eda

- Primary paper: Colosimo et al., *Widespread parallel evolution in sticklebacks by repeated fixation of Ectodysplasin alleles*, *Science* 307 (25 March 2005), full paper hosted by the US National Human Genome Research Institute: https://www.genome.gov/sites/default/files/genome-old/pages/About/NACHGR/Feb2006NACHGRAgenda/ScienceKingsley.pdf . DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1107239 .
- Mapping, sequencing and transgenic tests implicated the Eda pathway in reduced lateral armor plates in sampled freshwater threespine stickleback populations; the authors identify repeated use of related low-plate alleles, with exceptions.
- Limit: the paper's sampled populations and trait do not prove that all traits, freshwater populations or evolutionary histories follow the same genetic route. A candidate pathway and population association are not a complete ecological account of every selective pressure.
