## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Translation across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Translation Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Translation.
- **Tier 2: Translation Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Translation.
- **Tier 3: Translation Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Translation.
- **Tier 4: Translation Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Translation.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Translation: How Translation Developed**
   - *Question:* How did dated texts and translator accounts shape a translation tradition?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one translation tradition or technique through dated texts and translator accounts; distinguish later reputation from original context. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: identified source editions, authorized parallel passages, drafts, translator notes and attributed reader studies. Starting framework: International Federation of Translators charter (https://library.fit-ift.org/public/Resources/Documents/FIT_Translators_Charter_1994.pdf). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Translation: A Documented Translation Case**
   - *Question:* How does an influential translation relate to its source edition and reception?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare one influential translation with its identified source edition and reception; state language pair, version and limits of comparison. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: identified source editions, authorized parallel passages, drafts, translator notes and attributed reader studies. Starting framework: International Federation of Translators charter (https://library.fit-ift.org/public/Resources/Documents/FIT_Translators_Charter_1994.pdf). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
3. **Translation: People behind Translation**
   - *Question:* Whose editing, interpreting or language-consulting work is overlooked?
   - *Key Focus:* Document overlooked translators, interpreters, editors or language consultants in one publication; credit their roles using attributable records. Evidence base: Use attributable contribution records and consented participant accounts. Case records: identified source editions, authorized parallel passages, drafts, translator notes and attributed reader studies. Starting framework: International Federation of Translators charter (https://library.fit-ift.org/public/Resources/Documents/FIT_Translators_Charter_1994.pdf). Case records: identified source editions, authorized parallel passages, drafts, translator notes and attributed reader studies.
