## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Traditional Instruments across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Traditional Instruments Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Traditional Instruments.
- **Tier 2: Traditional Instruments Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Traditional Instruments.
- **Tier 3: Traditional Instruments Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Traditional Instruments.
- **Tier 4: Traditional Instruments Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Traditional Instruments.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Traditional Instruments: An Instrument's Early Record**
   - *Question:* What dated object or text documents one instrument form?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish earliest surviving example from invention. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
2. **Traditional Instruments: A Celebrated Instrument in Use**
   - *Question:* What can one performance show about its sound and role?
   - *Key Focus:* Name performer, repertoire, setting, and instrument variant. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
3. **Traditional Instruments: The Instrument Makers**
   - *Question:* Whose material knowledge and labor are overlooked?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit craft, repair, supply, and teaching with consent. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
4. **Traditional Instruments: How One Instrument Works**
   - *Question:* How do its materials and playing method produce sound?
   - *Key Focus:* Use a named local form and qualified maker explanation. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
5. **Traditional Instruments: Sound, Design, and Context**
   - *Question:* How do construction choices affect timbre and tuning?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare measured examples without reducing quality to one metric. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Traditional Instruments: An Uncertain Instrument Attribution**
   - *Question:* Which evidence might identify its maker or date?
   - *Key Focus:* Check materials, marks, provenance, and archive gaps. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
7. **Traditional Instruments: A New Material in an Old Form**
   - *Question:* What changed in sound, cost, repair, or access?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare maker and performer accounts with dated specifications. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
8. **Traditional Instruments: A Maker-Teacher's Legacy**
   - *Question:* How did one person transmit craft or performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace apprentices and objects without sole-inventor claims. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
9. **Traditional Instruments: Instrument Making After a Shock**
   - *Question:* How did one supply or policy change alter craft?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated material, price, and maker records. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
10. **Traditional Instruments: From Raw Material to Music**
   - *Question:* Which choices connect making, tuning, and performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one consented workshop and performance. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Traditional Instruments: Demand for an Instrument**
   - *Question:* What explains a reported change in making or playing?
   - *Key Focus:* Check buyers, teaching, materials, and survey coverage. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
12. **Traditional Instruments: One Instrument Across Generations**
   - *Question:* How did build, sound, and repertoire change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated objects and practitioner accounts. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
13. **Traditional Instruments: Keeping Makers and Players Connected**
   - *Question:* What resources do practitioners need?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare apprenticeships, material access, pay, and venues. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
14. **Traditional Instruments: One Unchanging Instrument?**
   - *Question:* Which features vary by place, maker, or era?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare provenanced objects and current practice. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
15. **Traditional Instruments: A Working Instrument's Life**
   - *Question:* How was it made, played, repaired, and passed on?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace ownership with consent and avoid unsupported sound claims. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Traditional Instruments: Making for a Named Performer**
   - *Question:* Which musical and practical needs shape an instrument?
   - *Key Focus:* Record performer preferences, setting, materials, and maker decisions. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
17. **Traditional Instruments: Choosing Sound Materials**
   - *Question:* Why does a maker select one wood, skin, metal, or reed?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace availability, properties, sustainability, and local alternatives. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
18. **Traditional Instruments: Inside the Workshop**
   - *Question:* Which stages require judgment and skill?
   - *Key Focus:* Observe construction and tuning with maker permission. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
19. **Traditional Instruments: Shape and Sonic Aim**
   - *Question:* How do dimensions and decoration reflect design choices?
   - *Key Focus:* Ask the maker; separate acoustics from symbolic claims. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
20. **Traditional Instruments: Maker and Performer Together**
   - *Question:* Who shaped the final sound and design?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit maker, player, teacher, and inherited methods. Evidence base: dated instruments with provenance, maker and performance records, acoustic comparisons, and consented practitioner accounts.
