## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Capitals across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Capitals Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Capitals.
- **Tier 2: Capitals Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Capitals.
- **Tier 3: Capitals Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Capitals.
- **Tier 4: Capitals Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Capitals.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Capitals: How a Seat of Government Emerged**
   - *Question:* Why did a particular city first become a seat of government?
   - *Key Focus:* Choose one polity and period; separate its initial selection from later stories about national origins. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
2. **Capitals: A Capital That Changed the Model**
   - *Question:* What does one widely cited capital actually reveal about state power?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one capital's institutions, design, and reception; test whether it is representative. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
3. **Capitals: Who Built the Capital?**
   - *Question:* Whose labor and displacement are absent from a capital's official story?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace documented builders, landholders, and affected residents in one city. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
4. **Capitals: What Makes a Capital a Capital?**
   - *Question:* How is a capital designated, and what functions can sit elsewhere?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one constitution or official designation to distinguish legal status, legislative seat, executive seat, and largest city. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
5. **Capitals: Capital Status and Sovereignty**
   - *Question:* How can rival claims to a capital expose a deeper dispute over sovereignty?
   - *Key Focus:* Bound the case by jurisdiction and date; distinguish legal claims, effective control, and diplomatic practice. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Capitals: Why Was This Site Chosen?**
   - *Question:* Which reasons for a capital's location are documented, and which remain uncertain?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare contemporary decision records with later explanations for one selection. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
7. **Capitals: Infrastructure for Governing**
   - *Question:* Which communication or transport change altered where a government could operate?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow a dated technology through the practical constraints of one governing center. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
8. **Capitals: The Capital Decision Makers**
   - *Question:* Who had authority to choose a capital, and whose interests did the decision serve?
   - *Key Focus:* Reconstruct a named decision using records of the competing proposals and affected communities. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
9. **Capitals: When a Capital Moved**
   - *Question:* What event triggered one move of the seat of government?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare the government's stated reasons with the documented sequence of relocation. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
10. **Capitals: How a Government Uses Its Capital**
   - *Question:* Which institutions must be in a capital, and which can function elsewhere?
   - *Key Focus:* Map the actual locations and duties of institutions for one polity at a fixed date. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Capitals: Losing Capital Status**
   - *Question:* What changed for a city after it ceased to be a capital?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented administrative, economic, and cultural effects before and after one change. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
12. **Capitals: A Capital Across Two Periods**
   - *Question:* How did the role of one capital change between two dated periods?
   - *Key Focus:* Use the same criteria for institutions, population, boundaries, and access in both periods. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
13. **Capitals: Proposals for a New Capital**
   - *Question:* What constraints would determine whether a proposed capital move can happen?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine a current proposal as a conditional scenario; date each claim and avoid predicting adoption. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
14. **Capitals: Testing a Capital Origin Story**
   - *Question:* Does a popular account of why a capital was chosen match the contemporary record?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify the earliest version of the story and compare it with decision documents. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
15. **Capitals: One Capital Decision in Documents**
   - *Question:* How did one capital designation proceed from proposal to implementation?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a sourced timeline of a single decision, including dissent and administrative follow-through. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Capitals: Capital on the Map and in Practice**
   - *Question:* Does the officially mapped capital match where government actually works?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare legal designation, institutional addresses, and dated records of actual proceedings. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
17. **Capitals: Why This Location?**
   - *Question:* How did access between regions shape the choice of one capital site?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare rival candidate sites with period-appropriate routes, travel times, and political constraints. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
18. **Capitals: Seasonal Seats of Government**
   - *Question:* Why did a government operate from different cities in different seasons?
   - *Key Focus:* Investigate a documented seasonal arrangement and its administrative and climatic reasons. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
19. **Capitals: Residents and the Capital Project**
   - *Question:* How did residents describe the effects of a new capital on their community?
   - *Key Focus:* Use attributed local accounts alongside land, housing, and public-service records. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
20. **Capitals: Terrain and Capital Design**
   - *Question:* How did terrain or water shape the layout of a chosen capital?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare planning documents and physical constraints for one city; avoid geographic determinism. Evidence base: dated government records, legal texts, historical maps, planning documents, and attributed local accounts as relevant.
