## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Woodworking across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Woodworking Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Woodworking.
- **Tier 2: Woodworking Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Woodworking.
- **Tier 3: Woodworking Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Woodworking.
- **Tier 4: Woodworking Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Woodworking.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Woodworking: A Joinery Tradition Traced**
   - *Question:* Which dated artifact or manual documents a named wood joint?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify wood species, region, tool, and period. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
2. **Woodworking: A Wooden Object under Load**
   - *Question:* What design and material choices explain its observed performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dimensions, construction record, and condition report. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
3. **Woodworking: Woodworkers behind an Object**
   - *Question:* Whose milling, joinery, carving, finishing, or repair work is documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Check workshop records and provenance. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
4. **Woodworking: Wood Moves with Conditions**
   - *Question:* How does moisture change dimensions in a specific object?
   - *Key Focus:* Use species data and measured conditions without design certification. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
5. **Woodworking: Strength in the Joint**
   - *Question:* How do grain orientation and joint geometry affect a bounded load case?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare qualified tests and stated assumptions. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Woodworking: An Unknown Workshop Method**
   - *Question:* What tool marks or records can establish how an object was made?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate observed marks from inferred technique. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
7. **Woodworking: A Machine Changes a Cut**
   - *Question:* Which operation became repeatable after a documented tool change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare output and work process, including guarding. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
8. **Woodworking: A Maker's Design Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a joiner alter a form or joint?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drawings, prototypes, and attributed accounts. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
9. **Woodworking: A Material Supply Shift**
   - *Question:* How did a dated supply change alter species or construction choice?
   - *Key Focus:* Check trade and workshop records. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
10. **Woodworking: From Board to Object**
   - *Question:* How do stock selection, layout, joinery, assembly, and finishing connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one documented build without unsafe machine instructions. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Woodworking: A Joint Replaced**
   - *Question:* Which cost, material, or performance evidence drove substitution?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated shop practice and surviving objects. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
12. **Woodworking: A Wooden Piece after Years**
   - *Question:* Which movement, wear, or repair appears over time?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare condition reports and environment. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
13. **Woodworking: A New Wood Composite**
   - *Question:* Which claims about strength or impact still need testing?
   - *Key Focus:* Use standards and measured product data. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
14. **Woodworking: One Wood Is Best?**
   - *Question:* How do species, grain, moisture, and use change the answer?
   - *Key Focus:* Define task and test conditions. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
15. **Woodworking: Making One Wooden Object**
   - *Question:* How did plan, stock, joinery, and finish evolve?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated workshop artifacts and credits. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Woodworking: The Object's Required Use**
   - *Question:* What dimensions, load, appearance, and maintenance were specified?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief with finished object. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
17. **Woodworking: Boards and Blades**
   - *Question:* Which material properties and tool limits shaped a documented operation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use manufacturer and shop records; avoid bypassing safeguards. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
18. **Woodworking: A Joinery Trial**
   - *Question:* Which test or mockup led to a revised joint?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare specimens and failure observations. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
19. **Woodworking: Grain as Design**
   - *Question:* How does visible grain or seam placement affect appearance and performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Use actual boards and joint layout. Evidence base: dated workshop plans and objects, measured wood and condition data, maker records, controlled tests, manufacturer instructions, and OSHA woodworking guidance.
20. **Woodworking: The Workshop Team**
   - *Question:* Who designed, milled, assembled, finished, and installed the piece?
