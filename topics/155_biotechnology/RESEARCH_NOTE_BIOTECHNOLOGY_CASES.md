# Selected primary-source research: biotechnology cases

Checked 2026-10-24. These three checks bound selected proposals. They do not verify other archived claims about cures, prices, clinical failure rates, future species or laboratory capability; they provide no treatment or engineering protocol.

## Casgevy approval — BANK-155-044

- Primary regulator records: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, [8 December 2023 approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies-treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease) and [regulatory action summary](https://www.fda.gov/media/175179/download?attachment=).
- FDA approved Casgevy, an edited autologous cell therapy, for sickle cell disease in patients aged 12 or older with recurrent vaso-occlusive crises. The summary describes the studied population, endpoints and follow-up expectations.
- Limit: The approval is U.S., product, indication and date specific. It is not an unconditional cure for all sickle cell disease, a direct in-body edit, or a claim that all recipients avoid every future crisis. The regulator’s evidence is not a substitute for individualized clinical eligibility or long-term outcome data.

## March 2024 pig-kidney transplant — BANK-155-045

- Primary treating-institution record: Massachusetts General Hospital, [21 March 2024 transplant announcement](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/press-release/worlds-first-genetically-edited-pig-kidney-transplant-into-living-recipient) and [kidney xenotransplant FAQ](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/kidney-xenotransplant-faqs).
- The hospital reported a genetically edited pig kidney transplanted into a living recipient on 16 March 2024 and described its investigational oversight. The announcement states 69 genomic edits for this donor organ.
- Limit: These were contemporaneous institutional accounts of one early procedure. “Successful transplant” at announcement means the operation and initial function reported then, not proof of long-term graft survival, broad safety, routine approval or resolution of the organ shortage. Avoid patient-specific advice and update any later outcome claim from dated primary follow-up.

## RFdiffusion protein-design research — BANK-155-046

- Original study: Watson et al., [De novo design of protein structure and function with RFdiffusion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06415-8), *Nature* 620 (2023), 1089–1100; [article PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06415-8.pdf).
- The authors report a generative model and experimental characterization of selected designed assemblies, binders and other proteins. This is evidence for particular design tasks and validation assays, not for universal prediction or clinical deployment.
- Limit: Structure prediction, design proposal and measured function are different steps. Some candidates fail; a tested binder or scaffold is not an approved therapy or an end to trial and error. Keep claims tied to the paper’s specific designs, assays and selection process.
