# BANK-019-051 — U.S. AI-film submission evidence

**Proposal:** What evidence should an AI short-film team assemble before a U.S. festival submission?  
**State:** bounded U.S. source review, 2026-10-24. This is a proposal note, not clearance, eligibility advice, an IDEA readiness decision or a festival application.

## Primary sources inspected

| Source | Checked material | Bounded finding | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| [U.S. Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence*, Part 2 (2025)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf) | Executive summary and the discussion of human expressive control in AI-assisted outputs. | The Office distinguishes protectable human-authored expression, arrangement or modification from purely AI-generated material; it assesses contribution case by case. | An Office report does not decide ownership of a particular film, its inputs, a vendor's terms or festival eligibility. The report is specific to U.S. law and Office practice. |
| [U.S. Copyright Office AI registration guidance (2023)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf) | Registration instructions for works containing AI-generated material. | Applicants are instructed to identify human authorship and disclose more than de minimis AI-generated content in a registration application. | Registration practice is not an asset-license or fair-use determination; guidance may be updated before an actual filing. |
| [17 U.S.C. § 107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html) and [Copyright Office explanation](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/more-info.html) | Statutory four factors and the Office's fact-specific explanation. | A third-party clip, score, image or excerpt calls for its own use analysis; no fixed percentage or edit amount automatically establishes fair use. | These sources cannot pre-clear a particular use. Festival terms, venue rules and other jurisdictions are separate. |

## Editorial decision

The preserved pitch combines copyright, fair use, training data and festival acceptance as if one answer could clear an AI film. Separate the team's **authorship evidence** (script, edit decisions, performed or photographed material, generated-output selection and modification), **input and output permissions** (music, voices, actors, source images, model and service terms), **third-party use analysis**, and **festival-specific submission rules**. A production log can preserve evidence, but a log alone does not confer rights or eligibility.

The current question is a U.S. submission-evidence checklist to investigate, not a judgment that any named film qualifies. Select an actual festival and edition, read its current rules, identify every submitted asset and jurisdiction, and obtain legal review where a material rights question remains. The neighboring [documentary fair-use question](../../005_documentary_storytelling/IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-005-124) concerns a selected excerpt; this proposal concerns an AI short film's whole submission dossier.
