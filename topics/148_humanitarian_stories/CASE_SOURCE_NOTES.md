# Selected humanitarian case-source notes

Inspected 2026-10-24. These bounded premise checks use public institutional records and aggregate claims. They do not authorize identification of recipients, patients, survivors or workers; production research must preserve consent, privacy and agency. Proposed topics retain empty `verified_sources` fields.

## Gaza access in March 2024 — BANK-148-045

- The [UN OCHA March 2024 humanitarian-access snapshot](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-access-snapshot-gaza-strip-1-31-march-2024) describes damaged routes, movement and security constraints and states that airdrops and maritime alternatives could not replace land crossings and internal routes for aid at scale. The [dated snapshot PDF](https://www.ochaopt.org/sites/default/files/Humanitarian%20access_March_2024.pdf) preserves its time-bound map and text.
- This is an access and logistics assessment for March 2024, not a present-day route recommendation, military assessment or proof of how much food reached a particular family. Keep entry, safe distribution and household outcomes separate; avoid sensational images or details that expose people needing aid.

## WHO psychological first aid — BANK-148-046

- The [WHO, War Trauma Foundation and World Vision 2011 field-worker guide](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241548205) describes psychological first aid as humane, supportive and practical help that respects dignity, culture and abilities. The [guide PDF](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/44615/9789241548205_eng.pdf) explicitly distinguishes it from psychological debriefing and calls for local adaptation.
- The guide is a framework and consensus document, not evidence that every deployment improves a clinical endpoint or that a scripted intervention is appropriate for an individual. The existing BANK-148-024 concerns aid-worker mental health; this case concerns support offered to people affected by crises. Do not turn it into treatment instructions.

## Turkmenistan's 2024 statelessness statement — BANK-148-047

- [UNHCR's Turkmenistan country profile](https://www.unhcr.org/where-we-work/countries/turkmenistan) says that in October 2024 the country announced resolution of **all known** statelessness cases. [UNHCR Global Trends 2024](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/global-trends-report-2024.pdf) explains wider statelessness reporting and estimates about 4.4 million people reported globally at end-2024, with incomplete country coverage.
- “All known” is an administrative and reporting boundary, not proof that nobody lacks nationality or documentation. An individual status story would require informed consent, protected records and local legal expertise. The proposed question uses aggregate and policy evidence rather than exposing a person's identity.
