# Selected radio and audio case-source notes

Inspected 2026-10-24. These premise checks support proposed questions; they are not a complete historical, legal, hearing-health or production source review. Proposed topics keep `verified_sources` empty pending episode research.

## Radio Caroline and the 1967 UK law — BANK-124-047

- The [enacted Marine, &c., Broadcasting (Offences) Act 1967](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/41/pdfs/ukpga_19670041_en.pdf) defines prohibited acts connected with broadcasts from ships and aircraft, including specified UK-linked participation, supply and advertising circumstances. [Section 5 as enacted](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/41/section/5/enacted) sets out the coverage conditions rather than a blanket declaration that every ship in international waters was illegal.
- The [legislation history page](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/41/contents) marks the Act repealed. A historical episode must identify the rule in force at the event date and compare it with dated Radio Caroline operating evidence. The law alone does not prove that the BBC changed policy because of one ship, nor what individual advertisers or suppliers did.

## Radio loudness measurement — BANK-124-048

- The [European Broadcasting Union R 128 recommendation](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128), version 5.0 dated November 2023, specifies a −23 LUFS programme-loudness target and separately describes loudness range and maximum true peak. [R 128 supplement 3, Loudness in Radio](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128s3.pdf), and [EBU Tech 3401 radio-production guidance](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/tech3401) distinguish programme production from distribution paths including FM, DAB and streaming.
- These primary technical documents define a broadcaster workflow, not an empirical history of commercial music mastering or evidence that louder 1990s albums damaged hearing. Such claims need dated master comparisons, platform practice and hearing-health research. Do not apply one distribution target universally to every consumer platform or recording.
