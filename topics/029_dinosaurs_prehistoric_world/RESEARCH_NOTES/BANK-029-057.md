# BANK-029-057 — Nanotyrannus specimen assignments

**Question:** How do growth evidence and named specimens bear on the *Nanotyrannus* versus juvenile *Tyrannosaurus* debate?  
**Review date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** Bounded premise review only; the BANK topic remains `proposed`, with no script or production approval.

## Primary sources inspected

1. Woodward et al., [“Growing up *Tyrannosaurus rex*: Osteohistology refutes the pygmy ‘*Nanotyrannus*’…”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6938697/), *Science Advances* 6, eaax6250 (2020), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aax6250. Femur and tibia sections of two half-grown specimens support their interpretation as immature *T. rex*. This study did **not** directly section every fossil assigned to *Nanotyrannus*.
2. Longrich et al., [“*Nanotyrannus* and *Tyrannosaurus* coexisted at the close of the Cretaceous”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09801-6), *Nature* 648, 357–367 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09801-6. The authors use near-mature NCSM 40000, comparative anatomy and growth models to argue for a distinct genus and two proposed species. Check its [2026 publisher correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10185-4) against any figure or specimen wording before quotation.
3. Griffin et al., [“A diminutive tyrannosaur lived alongside *Tyrannosaurus rex*”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343602/), *Science* 391, 300–305 (2026 print; first online December 2025), DOI 10.1126/science.adx8706. The authors test maturity using ceratobranchial (“hyoid”) histology of the isolated skull holotype CMNH 7541 and comparative archosaur material, supporting a mature small tyrannosaur. The PubMed abstract and institutional [Princeton account](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2026/01/09/princeton-paleontologist-yes-tiny-tyrannosaurs-lived-alongside-t-rex) were inspected; the full *Science* methods and supplementary data still need claim-level inspection.

## Claim boundary

- The archived “2024–2026 growth ring studies reignite the controversy” headline is stale. A 2020 limb-histology study supported the juvenile-*T. rex* interpretation, while 2025–2026 papers give specimen-specific evidence for a distinct small tyrannosaur. They do not make every historical assignment, proposed second species or growth trajectory automatically settled.
- A growth ring count is not by itself a species test. Maturity, anatomy, specimen identity, sampling location and comparative model all matter. The NCSM 40000 skeleton and CMNH 7541 holotype provide different evidence; do not merge them into a single fossil.
- The “Dueling Dinosaurs” acquisition and supposed death struggle are a separate provenance and taphonomy question (`BANK-029-060`), not proof of this taxonomic classification. General fossil identification and skeletal-growth methods remain in `BANK-029-014`, `BANK-029-017`, `BANK-029-020` and neighboring niche 355.
