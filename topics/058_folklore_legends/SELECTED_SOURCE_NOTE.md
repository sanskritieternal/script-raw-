# 058 selected source inspection — 2026-10-24

These are bounded source leads for proposed episodes, not proof of supernatural events or readiness to publish a cultural tradition.

## Hamelin — BANK-058-053

The [City of Hamelin's old-town guide](https://www.hameln.de/de/tourismus/altstadt-und-weserbergland/altstadt-und-stadtgeschichte/altstadtrundgang) reproduces a dated local inscription about a piper and 130 children. The city also presents the [Brothers Grimm's later legend text](https://www.hameln.de/en/thepiedpiper/thepiedpiper/brothers-grimm-pied-piper). The inscription is evidence for a commemorated local account; the Grimm version is a later literary witness. Neither independently proves a mass disappearance, an exact count of victims or a single historical explanation. Date the inscription and each retelling separately.

## Grimm's *Aschenputtel* — BANK-058-054

The digitized [1812 first edition page](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Seite%3AKinder_und_Hausm%C3%A4rchen_%28Grimm%29_1812_I_098.jpg) and the [edition index spanning 1812 to 1857](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Kinder_und_Hausm%C3%A4rchen) provide traceable primary text witnesses. Compare actual passages across chosen editions before calling any one text the “original” or saying all revisions only sanitized violence. Identify edition, translator and collector; an English adaptation is another witness, not the oral source itself.

## Black Shuck and the 1577 pamphlet — BANK-058-056

[Abraham Fleming's 1577 pamphlet as reproduced in a digitized early print](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_straunge_and_terrible_wunder_wrought_v.html?id=dBtcAAAAQAAJ) describes a black dog amid a church storm. The pamphlet's wording and later regional name “Black Shuck” need separate dating; a sensational report is evidence of a telling and its religious frame, not a supernatural event. Verify the digitized edition or surviving copy before quoting.

## La Llorona in performed tradition — BANK-058-057

The [Library of Congress's recorded storytelling episode](https://www.loc.gov/item/podcasts/2021-10-27/la-llorona-looking-at-a-ghost-story-for-dia-de-muertos-and-halloween/) includes a voiced variant and discussion of transmission. It demonstrates that a teller, performance setting and language matter. Do not make one Latin American origin story definitive; record the teller, location and version permissions for any new performance.

## Digital folklore and Slender Man — BANK-058-063

The [Library of Congress's digital-folklore discussion](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2015/12/hipster-st-george-and-the-unicorn-a-web-archiving-mumming/) dates the online image creation to 2009 and notes later collaborative stories and a 2014 attack. A born-digital provenance question is different from explaining violence through one fictional character. If the incident is discussed, verify it with court or official records and avoid a causal shortcut or spectacle around minors.
