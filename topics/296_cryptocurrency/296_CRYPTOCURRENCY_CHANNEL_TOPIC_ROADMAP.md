## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Cryptocurrency across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Cryptocurrency Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Cryptocurrency.
- **Tier 2: Cryptocurrency Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Cryptocurrency.
- **Tier 3: Cryptocurrency Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Cryptocurrency.
- **Tier 4: Cryptocurrency Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Cryptocurrency.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Cryptocurrency: From Digital Cash Ideas to Crypto Networks**
   - *Question:* Which design problem did an early crypto network set out to solve?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace a dated proposal, launch, and actual design choices. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
2. **Cryptocurrency: A Crypto Project Behind the Headline**
   - *Question:* What did one prominent network or token demonstrate in practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate its stated purpose from measured use and failures. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
3. **Cryptocurrency: Who Maintains the Network?**
   - *Question:* Whose technical and governance labor is missing from a project's public story?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace named contributors, funding, and decision processes. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
4. **Cryptocurrency: Token, Network, and Custody**
   - *Question:* What does a user actually own or control in one crypto arrangement?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish the token, ledger, wallet keys, and intermediary claim. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
5. **Cryptocurrency: Consensus and Finality**
   - *Question:* When is a transaction considered final on a named network?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain the rule, assumptions, delays, and failure modes. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Cryptocurrency: An Unexplained Market Move**
   - *Question:* Which explanations for a documented crypto-market event remain unproven?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated trades, disclosures, and missing data without assigning motive. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
7. **Cryptocurrency: A Claimed Protocol Advance Tested**
   - *Question:* Which constraint did a protocol change measurably address?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare throughput, security, cost, and decentralization before and after. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
8. **Cryptocurrency: Governance Beyond the Founder**
   - *Question:* Who can change a crypto protocol or service?
   - *Key Focus:* Map maintainers, validators, issuers, users, and formal control. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
9. **Cryptocurrency: A Network Disruption Reconstructed**
   - *Question:* What failed during one documented outage or exploit?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public incident reports and consequences; omit actionable attack steps. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
10. **Cryptocurrency: A Crypto Transfer End to End**
   - *Question:* What happens from signing a transfer to settlement or redemption?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace wallet, network, intermediary, fees, and finality for one asset. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Cryptocurrency: A Project's Growth and Collapse**
   - *Question:* Which documented dependencies failed when a crypto project contracted?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare claims, reserves or funding, governance, and dated outcomes. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
12. **Cryptocurrency: One Network Across Two Versions**
   - *Question:* Which material properties changed after a protocol upgrade?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare compatible metrics and user effects at two dates. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
13. **Cryptocurrency: A Conditional Crypto Use Case**
   - *Question:* What must change before a proposed crypto use case works at scale?
   - *Key Focus:* State technical, legal, cost, and adoption assumptions. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
14. **Cryptocurrency: Testing a Crypto Promise**
   - *Question:* Does a common promise about anonymity or decentralization hold for one system?
   - *Key Focus:* Define the claim and inspect architecture and real-world operation. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
15. **Cryptocurrency: One Token's Paper Trail**
   - *Question:* What do issue documents and transaction records show about one token?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace issuance, governance, distribution, and later changes. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Cryptocurrency: The Claim Behind a Stablecoin**
   - *Question:* What exactly can a holder redeem, from whom, and under which terms?
   - *Key Focus:* Read issuer terms and dated reserve disclosures for one product. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
17. **Cryptocurrency: Payment Use Versus Price Exposure**
   - *Question:* What costs and volatility arise when a household uses one crypto payment route?
   - *Key Focus:* Use a labeled hypothetical payment and dated fees. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
18. **Cryptocurrency: Risks Before and After Settlement**
   - *Question:* Which risks change while a crypto transaction is pending or held?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate network, custody, issuer, and market risks by stage. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
19. **Cryptocurrency: The Real Cost of a Crypto Transfer**
   - *Question:* Which network and intermediary charges apply to one transfer?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated fee schedules and a transparent scenario. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
20. **Cryptocurrency: What Protection Actually Applies**
   - *Question:* Which protections cover a user of one crypto service in a named jurisdiction?
   - *Key Focus:* Check legal classification, registration, custody terms, and exclusions. Evidence base: protocol documents, dated transaction or market data, issuer terms, incident reports, and regulator publications.
