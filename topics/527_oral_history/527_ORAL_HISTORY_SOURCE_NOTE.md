# Oral history source follow-up - 2026-10-24

## BANK-527-043 - Library of Congress Veterans History Project rights and access

- Primary custodial policy: Library of Congress American Folklife Center, Veterans History Project (VHP), *Rights & Access*, https://www.loc.gov/collections/veterans-history-project-collection/about-this-collection/rights-and-access/ (inspected 2026-10-24). It says interviewees and interviewers retain any copyright they hold; publication or exhibition requires permission from relevant rights holders. The Library cannot grant permission merely because materials are available for research.
- Primary process/access sources: Library of Congress, VHP *Frequently Asked Questions*, https://wwws.loc.gov/vets/vets-questions.html ; *Using the Collection*, https://www.loc.gov/collections/veterans-history-project-collection/about-this-collection/using-the-collection/ ; and *Step 4: Access*, https://www.loc.gov/programs/veterans-history-project/how-to-participate/step-4-access/ (inspected 2026-10-24). The FAQ says required forms with personal identifiers are held separately and not generally public; an online descriptive record need not include a digitized interview.
- Access and dignity limits: This is the U.S. VHP collection's published policy, not general copyright advice or a guarantee that every participant approved every later use. Individual collections may carry donor, privacy, copyright or next-of-kin restrictions. Before quoting or showing an interview, inspect its own rights statement and seek the required permissions; publicly viewable does not mean unrestricted reuse. Trauma, family references and service experiences warrant care even where legal reuse might be possible. Avoid using any participant's testimony in the proposed topic until those checks are done.

## Scope and overlap

BANK-527-043 concerns one named archive's access and republication rules. BANK-527-034 covers consent over time generally; BANK-526-038 concerns Indigenous source-community publication authority, a distinct governance relationship.
