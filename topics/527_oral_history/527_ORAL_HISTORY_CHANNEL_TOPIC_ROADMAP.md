## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Oral History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Oral History Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Oral History.
- **Tier 2: Oral History Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Oral History.
- **Tier 3: Oral History Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Oral History.
- **Tier 4: Oral History Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Oral History.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Oral History: How Oral History Developed**
   - *Question:* How did an oral-history project's original questions shape its collection?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one oral-history project from its founding questions through collection and interpretation; distinguish its aims from later uses of the recordings. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: authorized recordings, consent records, interview guides, transcripts and corroborating primary records. Starting framework: Oral History Association core principles (https://oralhistory.org/oha-core-principles/). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Oral History: A Documented Oral History Case**
   - *Question:* Whose experiences can a documented interview collection represent?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one documented interview collection using participant selection, consent and context; assess whose experiences it can and cannot represent. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: authorized recordings, consent records, interview guides, transcripts and corroborating primary records. Starting framework: Oral History Association core principles (https://oralhistory.org/oha-core-principles/). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
3. **Oral History: People behind Oral History**

   - *Key Focus:* Document overlooked narrators, interviewers, translators or archive workers in one project; credit their roles and respect permissions. Case records: authorized recordings, consent records, interview guides, transcripts and corroborating primary records. Evidence base: Compare competing explanations and specify missing discriminating evidence. Case records: authorized recordings, consent records, interview guides, transcripts and corroborating primary records.
