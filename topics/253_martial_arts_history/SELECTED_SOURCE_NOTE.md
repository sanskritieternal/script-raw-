# Selected primary-source note: martial-arts history

Checked 2026-10-24. Proposed cases BANK-253-042 and BANK-253-043.

The J. Paul Getty Museum catalogues *Il Fior di Battaglia*, Ms. Ludwig XV 13 (83.MR.183), as an illuminated manuscript around 1410 associated with Fiore Furlan dei Liberi: https://www.getty.edu/cona/CONAFullSubject.aspx?subid=700002326 . Its record describes 47 folios and hundreds of pen drawings. This dates a surviving instructional witness and its represented repertoire. It cannot establish that Fiore invented the illustrated techniques, that the manuscript is the earliest manual anywhere, or how an entire school trained. Use collection images only within their stated rights.

UNESCO Intergovernmental Committee decision 9.COM 10.8 (2014) records Brazil's Capoeira circle nomination and describes master-to-disciple transmission, the circle's music and practice, and bearer participation/consent in the nomination: https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/9.COM/10.8 . It is primary evidence for what the committee evaluated and inscribed in 2014. It is not independent proof of one historical origin, universal school practice, or every practitioner's agreement. Credit Afro-Brazilian communities and avoid treating inscription as an ownership claim.
