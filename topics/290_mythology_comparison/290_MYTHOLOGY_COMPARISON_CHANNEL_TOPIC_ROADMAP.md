## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Mythology Comparison: How a Comparison Became Popular**
   - *Question:* When did scholars or storytellers first pair two named narratives?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace publications, translations, and earlier community contexts. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
2. **Mythology Comparison: Two Similar Stories in Context**
   - *Question:* What is actually alike and different in two widely compared narratives?
   - *Key Focus:* Read complete source versions, genre, audience, and local meaning. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
3. **Mythology Comparison: Narrators Left Out of Comparison**
   - *Question:* Whose version or performance is missing from a familiar comparison?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented community voices and trace archival selection. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
4. **Mythology Comparison: What Counts as a Fair Comparison?**
   - *Question:* Which features can be compared without erasing each story's setting?
   - *Key Focus:* Define source, version, genre, date, and community before motifs. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
5. **Mythology Comparison: Resemblance Versus Transmission**
   - *Question:* What evidence would show contact rather than independent similarity?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare chronology, routes, wording, and alternative explanations. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Mythology Comparison: A Shared Motif with Unknown Path**
   - *Question:* Why can a parallel between two stories not yet be explained?
   - *Key Focus:* State source gaps and distinguish resemblance from proven borrowing. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
7. **Mythology Comparison: Searching a Story Corpus**
   - *Question:* Can digital motif searches reveal useful parallels without false matches?
   - *Key Focus:* Check metadata, translations, oral variants, and algorithmic selection. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
8. **Mythology Comparison: A Collector Shapes the Record**
   - *Question:* How did one translator or collector affect later comparisons?
   - *Key Focus:* Read field notes, edition choices, permissions, and critiques. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
9. **Mythology Comparison: A Story Enters New Circulation**
   - *Question:* What changed after one publication, translation, or performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare earlier local use with new audience and interpretation. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
10. **Mythology Comparison: Comparing Two Versions Step by Step**
   - *Question:* Which source features must be matched before drawing a conclusion?
   - *Key Focus:* Align versions, speakers, dates, genre, and community role. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Mythology Comparison: A Comparison's Reputation**
   - *Question:* Why did one proposed connection gain or lose scholarly support?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow evidence, criticism, new sources, and methodological changes. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
12. **Mythology Comparison: One Story's Changing Performance**
   - *Question:* How did a named community tell a story in two periods?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated recordings and consented present voices; avoid a frozen tradition. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
13. **Mythology Comparison: An Open Comparison Question**
   - *Question:* Which additional source could test a claimed connection?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify needed provenance, language, or contact evidence. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
14. **Mythology Comparison: Myth as Falsehood?**
   - *Question:* What does one community mean by a narrative that outsiders label a myth?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute local terms and distinguish sacred meaning from historical verification. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
15. **Mythology Comparison: A Motif Through Archives**
   - *Question:* How did one story move from oral performance into comparative scholarship?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace collectors, recordings, translations, and community response. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Mythology Comparison: A Narrative in Its Whole Source**
   - *Question:* What does a cited episode mean within the full text or performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Check edition, narrator, neighboring material, and translation. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
17. **Mythology Comparison: Different Readings of One Tale**
   - *Question:* How do two communities interpret a similar narrative differently?
   - *Key Focus:* Represent each in its own terms before comparing. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
18. **Mythology Comparison: A Story in Community Life**
   - *Question:* When and why is one narrative told today?
   - *Key Focus:* Use permission and explain occasion, audience, and restrictions. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
19. **Mythology Comparison: A Story Within an Observance**
   - *Question:* Where does narration occur in one documented rite?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe local public context without reproducing restricted material. Evidence base: specified primary texts and editions, consented oral recordings and performer accounts, translation notes, manuscript provenance, and dated contact records.
20. **Mythology Comparison: A False Parallel from Translation**
