# BANK-091-071 — washing raw chicken

Checked 2026-10-24. Editorial evidence for a proposed explanation; this is not a production or recipe approval.

## Claim and source inspection

- The archived trending pitch says washing chicken creates a “cross-contamination bacteria blast.” The [FDA Food Safety at Home page](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/womens-health-topics/food-safety-home), inspected in full, says not to wash meat, poultry, fish or eggs because sink water can splash bacteria. It separately recommends keeping raw foods apart, cooking chicken to 165 °F and using a thermometer rather than appearance. This supports a splash and handling-risk explanation, not a quantified blast or a claim that every wash contaminates every surface.
- The [FoodSafety.gov food-type guidance](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/food-safety-by-type-food), inspected in full, likewise says never to wash raw poultry and to cook it to the proper temperature. Its page shows a 2025 review date. It does not report an effect size for washing in a particular kitchen.
- The [USDA FSIS washing-food guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/washing-food-does-it-promote-food) appeared in search results with a summary of USDA kitchen research, but direct page access returned 403. Its study design, numbers and precise causal wording were **not inspected**, so no numerical result from that source should be scripted from this note.

## Boundaries and next check

Frame this as current U.S. food-safety guidance for raw poultry and a plausible splash/cross-contact pathway. Do not portray washing as sterilization, assert a universal exposure rate, or imply that a cooked item cannot be contaminated later. If a script uses kitchen-study percentages or compares cultural handling methods, obtain the primary USDA report and its protocol and review the wording with a food-safety specialist.
