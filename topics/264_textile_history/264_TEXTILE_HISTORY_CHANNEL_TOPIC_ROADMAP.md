## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Textile History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Textile History Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Textile History.
- **Tier 2: Textile History Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Textile History.
- **Tier 3: Textile History Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Textile History.
- **Tier 4: Textile History Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Textile History.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Textile History: An Early Textile Record**
   - *Question:* What dated fabric or document establishes one local textile form?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish survival from absolute beginning. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
2. **Textile History: A Celebrated Cloth Reexamined**
   - *Question:* What does one textile reveal about makers and use?
   - *Key Focus:* Check fiber, technique, owner, occasion, and provenance. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
3. **Textile History: Labor Behind a Textile**
   - *Question:* Whose growing, spinning, dyeing, weaving, and selling is uncredited?
   - *Key Focus:* Use archival and consented maker accounts. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
4. **Textile History: Reading a Historic Cloth**
   - *Question:* How do fiber, structure, pattern, and use fit together?
   - *Key Focus:* Define object, place, period, and source limits. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
5. **Textile History: Trade Versus Local Making**
   - *Question:* Can a textile's material and technique locate its production?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare analysis, trade records, and maker knowledge. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Textile History: An Unknown Textile Workshop**
   - *Question:* Which evidence could identify maker or date?
   - *Key Focus:* Check weave, dyes, labels, provenance, and gaps. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
7. **Textile History: A Loom or Dye Transition**
   - *Question:* How did one change affect work and cloth?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare productivity, wages, design, and access. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
8. **Textile History: A Textile Organizer's Role**
   - *Question:* How did one maker or merchant change production?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated records and account for collective labor. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
9. **Textile History: A Trade Shock in Cloth**
   - *Question:* How did one policy or disruption alter supply?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace prices, output, workers, and substitutes. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
10. **Textile History: Tracing a Textile Chain**
   - *Question:* How did fiber become fabric and reach users?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow growing, spinning, dyeing, weaving, transport, and sale. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Textile History: A Fabric's Changing Market**
   - *Question:* What explains a documented production trend?
   - *Key Focus:* Check demand, imports, machines, labor, and definitions. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
12. **Textile History: One Textile Across Eras**
   - *Question:* How did material, making, and meaning change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare provenanced objects and living practice. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
13. **Textile History: What Evidence Is Still Missing**
   - *Question:* Which archives or objects could revise this history?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify access, permissions, and analytical limits. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
14. **Textile History: A Cloth from One Place Only?**
   - *Question:* Does a claimed geographic origin fit the evidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate motif resemblance from provenanced manufacture. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
15. **Textile History: One Cloth's Recorded Journey**
   - *Question:* How did it move from maker to current holder?
   - *Key Focus:* Build an ownership and use timeline. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Textile History: A Historic Commission**
   - *Question:* What did patron, function, and maker negotiate?
   - *Key Focus:* Use contracts, sketches, and finished object where available. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
17. **Textile History: Fiber and Dye in Context**
   - *Question:* Why were particular inputs available?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace ecology, cultivation, trade, and labor. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
18. **Textile History: Reconstructing Making Steps**
   - *Question:* Which steps are evidenced and which inferred?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare object analysis with maker testimony. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
19. **Textile History: Pattern with a Purpose**
   - *Question:* Why might a layout fit its intended use?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute period and practitioner explanations; mark inference. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
20. **Textile History: Who Made the Cloth**
   - *Question:* How were design, yarn, weaving, and finishing divided?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace credits and wages, including invisible work. Evidence base: dated textiles with ownership records, material analysis, trade and labor archives, and attributed maker and community accounts.
