# Selected beverage source checks — 24 October 2026

These checks bound three proposed cases. Recipe, producer label, survey and public-health explanations answer different questions. Remaining archive claims about flavor, health, prices and regulation need market- and product-specific review.

## BANK-092-043 — espresso pressure

- The [Specialty Coffee Association’s published barista survey](https://sca.coffee/sca-news/25-magazine/issue-3/defining-ever-changing-espresso-25-magazine-issue-3-zyx36) reported average extraction pressure of 8.5 bar among respondents, with 9 bar the most frequent response. The article presents typical dose, yield and time ranges but calls espresso an evolving practice. A convenience survey is not a universal engineering standard or proof of best taste.
- **Editorial bound:** Specify machine, pressure measurement point, recipe, coffee, grind and sampled baristas. Do not infer a fixed extraction yield, physiological effect or sensory outcome from pressure alone.

## BANK-092-064 — Red Bull ingredient claim

- The [manufacturer’s UK ingredient page](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/energydrink/products/red-bull-energy-drink-ingredients-list) lists caffeine, taurine, B-group vitamins, sugars and water and states **80 mg caffeine per 250 ml** for that product and market. This is a label claim, not evidence that the ingredients create a special combined effect or that every can size contains 80 mg.
- The existing [BANK-092-028](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md) addresses caffeine variability by serving and preparation. The named product case needs a dated label, serving size and regulator evidence before any health or performance conclusion.
- **Editorial bound:** Do not translate population caffeine studies into advice for an individual or repeat the archive’s “hit so hard” mechanism as fact.

## BANK-092-065 — drinking seawater

- [NOAA’s public explanation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/drinksw.html), updated September 2026, describes why eliminating seawater’s salt load requires more water than seawater provides when normal human kidneys excrete it. This supports a general hydration-risk explanation, not a personalized clinical forecast.
- [WHO’s desalinated drinking-water guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-HSE-WSH-11.03) treats desalination as a distinct route to safe drinking water requiring risk management; it does not imply untreated seawater is potable.
- **Editorial bound:** Separate the chemistry of saline water from medical triage or survival instructions. Do not sensationalize the archive’s cellular imagery or promise a fixed outcome for an individual exposure.
