# Selected primary-source note: basketry

Checked 2026-10-24. Proposed cases BANK-267-038 and BANK-267-039.

Smithsonian NMNH sweetgrass specimen E203654-0, collected in 1899, preserves an original collector-card quotation and later catalog notes naming Abenaki basketmaker Caroline Masta, black ash preparation in Canada, and relatives who gathered and braided sweetgrass: https://collections.si.edu/search/detail/edanmdm:nmnhanthropology_8356188 . The record documents what a collector and subsequent catalogers wrote, not the relatives' own complete accounts or a representative Abenaki production model. Avoid repeating the card's dated terminology as contemporary language; seek community perspectives and image-use terms before publication.

The Smithsonian's artist-attributed account of Kelly Church's *Sustaining Traditions-Digital Memories* (2018) describes its black ash and sweetgrass, included flash drive of oral histories and harvesting descriptions, and an emerald ash borer vial: https://resilience.si.edu/story/kelly-church-sustaining-traditions-basketry . The page credits Church as Ottawa/Pottawatomi and identifies museum accession 2020.49A-B. It is direct institutional/artist evidence about this artwork and its intended message, not a measured trend in ash availability or a claim for all Anishinaabe basket makers. Copyright is marked to Church; do not reuse images or recordings without checking permissions and community protocols.

Neighbor review: BANK-095-028/055 cover general fiber choice and a willow tradition; BANK-141-030 covers readiness judgment; 265 covers woven cloth structures; 264 textile history. These cases document named basket makers, divided labor and an artwork's transmission purpose rather than generic fiber selection.
