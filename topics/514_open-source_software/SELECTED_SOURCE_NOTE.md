## Primary project records

- Python PEP 8001, ?Python Governance Voting Process? (created 24 August 2018): https://peps.python.org/pep-8001/ . Defines the core-developer vote after Guido van Rossum stepped down, the candidate PEPs, voting timetable and election method.
- PEP 8016, ?The Steering Council Model? (created 1 November 2018): https://peps.python.org/pep-8016/ . Historical proposal, marked accepted by core-developer vote on 17 December 2018; explicitly preserved for history.
- PEP 13, ?Python Language Governance? (created 16 December 2018): https://peps.python.org/pep-0013/ . The live formal governance specification, including a five-person steering council, powers, constraints, core-team election and amendment process.

## Bounded premise

The Python core development community chose the steering council model in December 2018 after the founder relinquished the BDFL role. PEP 8016 documents the proposal and acceptance, while PEP 13 defines the governing process. This is a concrete instance of an open-source project formalizing succession and decision rights.

## Claim and access limits

PEP 13 is a living document with later amendments, including changes in 2024?2025. Do not present its present-day text as an exact frozen 2018 rulebook; compare archived revisions before claiming a particular clause existed at transition. A documented governance design does not prove practical inclusiveness, workload distribution or project success. The PEP vote was by CPython core developers, not every Python user. No universal claim about open-source projects follows from this one project.
