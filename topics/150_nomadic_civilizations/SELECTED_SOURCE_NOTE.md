## BANK-150-047 - Afar salt-route work

- Till Jakob Frederik Trojer, [*Arho - The Afar Salt Trade of Northeastern Ethiopia*](https://boap.uib.no/index.php/jaf/article/view/3146) (Journal of Anthropological Films, 2021, DOI 10.15845/jaf.v5i01.3146) describes a film following one caravan, based on 13 months of fieldwork in 2017-2018. Its abstract reports truck competition and participant-selected Afar music. The film page is firsthand project evidence for that period and the participants represented, not a census of all trade.
- Helena S. Woldekiros, [*The Route Most Traveled: The Afar Salt Trail, North Ethiopia*](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?lng=en&pid=S0717-73562019005000502&script=sci_arttext) (Chungara 51, 2019), reports mapped routes, field observations and interviews. It separates Afar lowland caravanners and Tigray highland caravanners, notes other workers and women's roles, and locates water sources and trade nodes. Its sample and study date do not establish that today's routes or livelihoods are identical.
- **Bound:** "Last traders," fixed temperature, inevitable disappearance and all-Afar ownership of every caravan are not established. Attribute work to named Afar and Tigray participants and seek community review or additional voices before writing a present-day account. Film imagery/music require rights review; citation does not grant reuse.

## BANK-150-048 - Khazar manuscript claim

- Princeton Geniza Project, [*Letter: T-S Misc. 35.38*](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/36496/) (Cambridge University Library, Taylor-Schechter collection) provides images, Hebrew transcription and English translation of a **copy of a letter supposedly from a Khazar Jew** to Hasdai ibn Shaprut. The text narrates conversion and Khazar rulers. The catalog's "supposedly" and the manuscript's gaps matter: the text directly verifies a transmitted claim, not the event or population-wide adherence.
- **Bound:** Compare date, provenance, textual reconstruction and independent material/chronicle evidence before asserting who converted and when. Neither "Jewish steppe empire" nor a uniform religious identity follows from this single document.
