# Research note: dated sociology cases

Checked 2026-10-24. These are bounded source-access notes for proposed BANK questions, not verified_sources entries or IDEA approval.

## BANK-168-045 — May 2021 American Perspectives Survey

- Survey Center on American Life, [May 2021 American Perspectives Survey download and methods](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/download/may-2021-american-perspectives-survey/): interviews May 14-23, 2021, with 2,019 U.S. adults in the probability-based Ipsos KnowledgePanel.
- Survey Center on American Life, [topline questionnaire](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/May-2021-APS-Topline-Questionnaire.pdf): question 13 asks for close friends excluding relatives; the overall response for none is 12 percent.
- Survey Center on American Life, [State of American Friendship report](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/research/the-state-of-american-friendship-change-challenges-and-loss/), June 8, 2021, reports subgroup estimates and survey methodology. Its [men's friendship summary](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/why-mens-social-circles-are-shrinking/) reports 15 percent of men with no close friends and 28 percent of men under 30 with no close social connections. These are different measures and populations.

**Claim limit:** The archived headline that 30 percent of young men have zero close friends is not established by the inspected wording. Do not substitute 28 percent "no close social connections" for a differently worded close-friends question. These self-reports are a 2021 U.S. survey, not a universal or causal account of loneliness. Inspect subgroup denominator, confidence intervals and comparability with the 1990 question before trend claims.

## BANK-168-046 — Opportunity Atlas

- Chetty, Hendren, Friedman, Jones and Porter, [Opportunity Atlas research page](https://opportunityinsights.org/paper/the-opportunity-atlas/), released October 2018. The page describes tract-level adult outcomes for children grouped by parent income, race and gender using linked U.S. administrative records.
- Authors, [Atlas paper](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/atlas_paper.pdf), methods for the 1978-1983 birth cohorts and assignment to childhood tracts. The present research page also identifies a later journal publication; use the version date explicitly.
- Authors, [Publicly Available Estimates methods](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Atlas_methods.pdf): estimates with 20 or fewer children are suppressed and privacy noise is added; selected small subgroup estimates can be imprecise.

**Claim limit:** Tract-level associations and the paper's bounded analyses do not mean birthplace dictates any individual's lifetime wealth. The atlas is U.S. cohort-specific and privacy-protected; do not identify people or treat suppressed cells as zero. Any causal neighborhood claim needs the separate study design and its assumptions.

## Representation

Avoid portraying survey subgroups or neighborhoods as deficient people. Describe measures, institutional context and uncertainty. Do not collect identifiable local participant accounts for these cases without informed consent and a plan for privacy and reuse.
