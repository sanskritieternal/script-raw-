## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Sociology across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Foundational Sociological Paradigms (Structural Functionalism, Conflict Theory, Symbolic Interactionism) (Beginner / Scratch)** — The sociological imagination (C. Wright Mills), Émile Durkheim's social facts and suicide study, Karl Marx's class conflict, Max Weber's rationalization and Protestant ethic, and quantitative survey vs qualitative ethnographic methodology.
- **Tier 2: Social Stratification, Class Mobility, Social Capital & Bureaucratic Iron Cages (Intermediate)** — Pierre Bourdieu's cultural capital (habitus), Robert Putnam's Bowling Alone social capital decline, Gini coefficient income inequality, total institutions (Erving Goffman's Asylums), and bureaucratic McDonaldization (Ritzer).
- **Tier 3: Deviance, Criminological Theories (Labeling, Broken Windows), Gender Roles & Urban Ecology (Upper Intermediate)** — Howard Becker's labeling theory of deviance, Broken Windows policing controversies, Judith Butler's gender performativity, Chicago School urban concentric zones, and institutional racism/systemic discrimination.
- **Tier 4: Postmodern Liquid Modernity (Bauman), Surveillance Societies (Foucault) & Algorithmic Social Contracts (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Michel Foucault's Panopticon and biopolitics, Zygmunt Bauman's liquid modernity and precarious labor, digital algorithmic sorting of human populations, and reimagining the 21st-century social contract.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Stanford Prison Experiment: The Deconstruction of a 50-Year Sociological Myth**
   - *Question:* Did Philip Zimbardo's 1971 basement experiment truly prove that ordinary people turn into sadistic monsters, or was it a scripted performance pushed by researchers?
   - *Key Focus:* Demand characteristics, leaked audio recordings coaching guards how to behave, participant Carlo Prescott's revelations, failure of replications, and the modern replication crisis in social psychology.
2. **The Sociological Imagination: How Personal Troubles Are Actually Public Issues**
   - *Question:* Why is being unemployed, in debt, or lonely not just a personal failure, but the reflection of massive macroeconomic and institutional structures?
   - *Key Focus:* C. Wright Mills' 1959 framework, separating individual agency from structural constraints, viewing personal biographies through the lens of historical epochs, and developing critical social awareness.
3. **The Big Three: Durkheim, Marx, and Weber Explained in Plain English**
   - *Question:* How do three 19th-century European thinkers provide completely different lenses to understand inequality, religion, and modern work?
   - *Key Focus:* Émile Durkheim (social cohesion, anomie, mechanical vs organic solidarity), Karl Marx (historical materialism, bourgeoisie vs proletariat, alienation of labor), and Max Weber (rationalization, the Protestant work ethic, the bureaucratic iron cage).
4. **Durkheim's Suicide Study: How the Most Private Act is Governed by Social Facts**
   - *Question:* How did a French sociologist prove that taking one's own life follows predictable mathematical patterns based on social integration and regulation?
   - *Key Focus:* The four types of suicide: Egoistic (lack of integration), Altruistic (excessive integration), Anomic (breakdown of social rules during rapid change), and Fatalistic (over-regulation); and the concept of 'social facts'.
5. **Pierre Bourdieu and Cultural Capital: Why Wealth Isn't Just in Your Bank Account**
   - *Question:* Why do elite accents, knowing which fork to use, and understanding classical art guarantee corporate promotions more than high IQ scores?
   - *Key Focus:* Economic, cultural (embodied, objectified, institutionalized), and social capital; 'Habitus' as internalized class disposition; and how education systems subtly reproduce class privilege without looking rigged.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **Bowling Alone: The 30-Year Collapse of Social Capital and Community in America**
   - *Question:* Why did Americans stop joining bowling leagues, rotary clubs, and neighborhood barbecues starting in the 1970s, creating the modern loneliness crisis?
   - *Key Focus:* Robert Putnam's landmark data, bridging vs bonding social capital, suburban car dependency, television and screen privatization of leisure, and the decline of societal generalized trust.
7. **Erving Goffman's Dramaturgy: Life as a Theater Stage and the Presentation of Self**
   - *Question:* Are we ever our true selves, or are we constantly performing tailored roles depending on whether we are 'front stage' or 'back stage'?
   - *Key Focus:* The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1956), impression management, costume and props, back stage relaxation and boundary dropping, and how social media turned all of life into an inescapable front stage performance.
8. **The McDonaldization of Society: Why Modern Life Feels Predictable, Efficient, and Soul-Crushing**
   - *Question:* How did fast-food operating principles take over hospitals, universities, tourism, and dating?
   - *Key Focus:* George Ritzer's four dimensions: Efficiency, Calculability, Predictability, and Control through non-human technology; and the 'irrationality of rationality' leading to dehumanizing homogenization.
9. **Labeling Theory and Deviance: How Society Creates the Criminals It Punishes**
   - *Question:* Is an act inherently criminal, or does an act only become deviant when society successfully sticks a public label onto the perpetrator?
   - *Key Focus:* Howard Becker's Outsiders (1963), primary deviance (rule-breaking without self-identity change) vs secondary deviance (embracing the deviant master status due to social stigma), and self-fulfilling prophecies in the justice system.
10. **Total Institutions: Inside the Isolated Worlds of Prisons, Asylums, and Boot Camps**
   - *Question:* What happens to human personality when every aspect of sleep, play, and work is conducted in the same place under a single rigid authority?
   - *Key Focus:* Goffman's Asylums (1961), the 'mortification of self' stripping away clothes, names, and personal belongings, standardized institutional routines, and the psychological difficulty of re-entering civilian society.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **The Broken Windows Theory: The Subway Experiment That Reshaped Global Policing**
   - *Question:* Did cracking down on graffiti and broken windows really reduce homicides in 1990s New York, or did demographic and economic shifts do the work?
   - *Key Focus:* James Q. Wilson and George Kelling's 1982 thesis, signaling of disorder inviting serious crime, transition into aggressive 'Stop-and-Frisk' policing, racial disparities, and modern sociological critiques.
12. **Foucault's Panopticon: How Surveillance Controls Us Without Guards in the Tower**
   - *Question:* Why do people follow rules when no one is watching, and how did Jeremy Bentham's 18th-century prison blueprint become the architecture of the modern surveillance state?
   - *Key Focus:* Discipline and Punish (1975), the transition from public torture to internalized psychological self-policing, the permanent visibility illusion, and modern algorithmic corporate and state surveillance.
13. **Judith Butler and Gender Performativity: Deconstructing the Binary**
   - *Question:* What does it mean to say that gender is not something you are, but something you repeatedly perform through bodily acts and language?
   - *Key Focus:* Gender Trouble (1990), distinction between biological sex and socially constructed gender roles, the ritualized repetition of norms, subverting societal expectations, and queer theory foundational concepts.
14. **The Chicago School and Urban Ecology: Why Crime Clusters in Specific City Zones**
   - *Question:* Why did certain immigrant neighborhoods in Chicago maintain high crime rates for 50 years even when the entire ethnic population turned over three times?
   - *Key Focus:* Park and Burgess' Concentric Zone Model (Zone in Transition), Shaw and McKay's Social Disorganization Theory, spatial urban ecology, residential turnover eroding community cohesion, and urban sociology fieldwork.
15. **Liquid Modernity: Zygmunt Bauman on Precarious Jobs, Fleeting Romance, and Anxiety**
   - *Question:* Why does life in the 21st century feel constantly slippery, unstable, and anxiety-ridden compared to our grandparents' world?
   - *Key Focus:* Solid modernity (lifelong jobs, durable marriages, stable factory towns) dissolving into liquid modernity (gig work, disposable consumer goods, swiping dating apps, hyper-mobility), and the burden of individual risk.
16. **The Matthew Effect: Why Success Multiplies and Failure Compounds in Science and Society**
   - *Question:* Why do famous scientists get all the credit for discoveries made by junior lab assistants, and why does wealth naturally concentrate into fewer hands?
   - *Key Focus:* Robert K. Merton's 1968 sociology of science paper, 'For to every one who has will more be given', cumulative advantage dynamics, algorithmic search amplification, and breaking structural inequality loops.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **The Strength of Weak Ties: Why Acquaintances Get You Better Jobs Than Close Friends**
   - *Question:* How did Mark Granovetter prove that distant acquaintances are vastly more valuable for finding a dream career than your best friends and family?
   - *Key Focus:* Social network analysis, dense bonding networks having redundant information vs weak ties acting as bridges across disparate social clusters, LinkedIn network dynamics, and career mobility.
18. **Algorithmic Sorting: How Code is Quietly Encoding New Social Castes**
   - *Question:* When credit scores, hiring algorithms, and predictive bail software score humans automatically, are they creating an inescapable digital underclass?
   - *Key Focus:* Cathy O'Neil's Weapons of Math Destruction, automated discrimination in resume screening, zip code redlining encoded into insurance algorithms, lack of algorithmic due process, and regulatory AI audits.
19. **The Cost of Poverty: The Financial Penalties of Having an Empty Bank Account**
   - *Question:* Why does it cost significantly more money to be poor than to be wealthy in modern capitalist societies?
   - *Key Focus:* The 'Poverty Premium', 400% APR payday loans, $35 overdraft fees, buying small packages at high unit costs, subprime auto lending rates, lack of preventative dental care resulting in emergency surgeries, and municipal fines.
20. **The Masterclass on Sociological Research: Designing Rigorous Qualitative and Quantitative Studies**
   - *Question:* How does a professional sociologist design an ethnographic field immersion or national survey that uncovers unvarnished social truths without researcher bias?
   - *Key Focus:* Participant observation ethics, managing the observer effect, representative probability sampling, designing neutral non-leading survey questions, coding qualitative interview transcripts with NVivo, and IRB institutional ethics approval.
