## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Metalworking across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Metalworking Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Metalworking.
- **Tier 2: Metalworking Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Metalworking.
- **Tier 3: Metalworking Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Metalworking.
- **Tier 4: Metalworking Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Metalworking.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Metalworking: A Metalworking Method in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated artifact or manual documents a forming or joining method?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify alloy, workshop, region, and period. Evidence base: revision-controlled drawings, material certificates, process and inspection records, controlled tests, maker archives, and current OSHA welding guidance.
2. **Metalworking: A Metal Object Examined**
   - *Question:* Which process evidence explains its shape and condition?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drawings, material analysis, and provenance. Evidence base: revision-controlled drawings, material certificates, process and inspection records, controlled tests, maker archives, and current OSHA welding guidance.
3. **Metalworking: Hands behind the Metal**
   - *Question:* Who prepared stock, formed, joined, finished, and inspected it?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace shop records and maker marks. Evidence base: revision-controlled drawings, material certificates, process and inspection records, controlled tests, maker archives, and current OSHA welding guidance.
4. **Metalworking: From Stock to Form**
   - *Question:* How do cutting, bending, joining, and finishing differ?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain one object's stages without unsafe operating steps. Evidence base: revision-controlled drawings, material certificates, process and inspection records, controlled tests, maker archives, and current OSHA welding guidance.
5. **Metalworking: Properties after Processing**
   - *Question:* How do heat and deformation alter a named alloy's properties?
   - *Key Focus:* Use material certificates and controlled tests. Evidence base: revision-controlled drawings, material certificates, process and inspection records, controlled tests, maker archives, and current OSHA welding guidance.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Metalworking: An Unknown Fabrication Route**

7. **Metalworking: A Tool Changes Fabrication**

9. **Metalworking: A Standard or Alloy Change**

10. **Metalworking: From Specification to Inspection**
   - *Question:* Which steps connect material, process, joining, and acceptance?
