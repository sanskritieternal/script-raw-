# BANK-079-024 — Natural and synthetic ultramarine

**Selected premise:** The archived pitch says real ultramarine cannot be bought and was once worth more than pure gold. This local materials question asks how lightfastness, opacity and composition should be checked separately when choosing paint. The named Renaissance commission and economic history have a primary home at `BANK-042-055`.

**Primary institutional sources inspected (2026-10-24):**

- The [National Gallery technical bulletin on Northern European painting, 1400–1550](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/media/15643/methods_and_materials1997.pdf) identifies lazurite in lapis lazuli as natural ultramarine's blue component, describes extraction grades and gives a specific 1521 Dürer purchase comparison. This is object/material history, not a current market price list.
- The [National Gallery's *Making Colour* exhibition record](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/exhibitions/past/making-colour/journey-through-colour) distinguishes lapis-derived ultramarine from the artificial French ultramarine developed in the nineteenth century.
- The [National Gallery technical analysis of Monet's palette](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/upload/pdf/Roy_2007.pdf) identifies synthetic French ultramarine in specific later paintings and dates its availability to the late 1820s.

**Bounded finding:** Natural ultramarine and synthetic ultramarine are distinct material and historical categories. Natural lapis-derived pigment was costly in named historical settings; synthetic ultramarine became a much cheaper option. The records do not establish that genuine natural ultramarine is impossible to buy today or that every historical sample exceeded gold's value by weight. Those claims need a dated supplier listing and matched historical currency/weight context if a script uses them.

**Before production:** Inspect paint labels or material-safety sheets for a specified present-day product; do not infer composition from the word “ultramarine” alone. Check exact object-specific pigment analyses before claiming which blue a painter used.
