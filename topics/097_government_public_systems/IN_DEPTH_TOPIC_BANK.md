### pending_topics/1 — The Social Contract: Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau on Why Humans Obey the State

**Original question:** Why do millions of free human beings surrender their natural liberty to pay taxes and obey traffic lights under the threat of state violence?

**Original planning scope:** Thomas Hobbes' brutal State of Nature ('solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short') demanding an absolute Leviathan sovereign, John Locke's inalienable natural rights to life, liberty, and property justifying rebellion, and Jean-Jacques Rousseau's 'General Will'.

**Editorial disposition:** out_of_scope. Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau are political-philosophy texts, not a named public-system implementation case.

### pending_topics/2 — Separation of Powers: Montesquieu's Tripartite Shield Against Tyranny

**Original question:** How did Montesquieu and James Madison design a constitutional system where 'ambition is made to counteract ambition' so that no single tyrant could seize total control?

**Original planning scope:** Tripartite division (Legislative writing law, Executive enforcing law, Judicial interpreting law), checks and balances (vetoes, judicial review, impeachment), Federalist No. 51, and the dangerous creep of executive orders.

**Editorial disposition:** new_case. United States v. Nixon is a dated separation-of-powers dispute; legal holdings require a jurisdictional limit.

**Scoped proposals:** [BANK-097-055](#bank-097-055).

### pending_topics/3 — Parliamentary vs. Presidential Systems: Westminster Fusion vs. American Division

**Original question:** Why can a British Prime Minister pass an entire legislative manifesto in weeks while an American President faces years of paralyzed legislative gridlock?

**Original planning scope:** Westminster system fusion of legislative and executive branches, the vote of no confidence toppling governments in 24 hours, coalition building vs. the American fixed-term four-year executive, and Juan Linz's famous peril of presidentialism thesis.

**Editorial disposition:** new_case. The U.K. 1979 no-confidence vote and U.S. impeachment make a bounded constitutional comparison.

**Scoped proposals:** [BANK-097-056](#bank-097-056).

### pending_topics/4 — The Rule of Law: Why Even Kings and Presidents Must Stand Under the Law

**Original question:** What is the single difference between a civilized constitutional state and a mafia protection racket? The 800-year evolution of the Rule of Law.

**Original planning scope:** Magna Carta (1215) Clause 39 guaranteeing trial by jury of peers, A.V. Dicey's three principles of the Rule of Law (no arbitrary punishment, equality before the law, constitutional rights), and independent judiciaries resisting executive overreach.
