# Selected case: JFK8 representation election

**Proposed question:** `BANK-516-044` — What did the NLRB's 2022 JFK8 election record establish about worker representation?

## Primary records inspected

- [NLRB case 29-RC-288020](https://www.nlrb.gov/case/29-RC-288020), public case record. Search-index extraction showed the April 1, 2022 tally, 8,325 eligible voters, 2,654 for and 2,131 against, 67 challenged ballots, the defined JFK8 employee unit, and closure as certification of representative. The direct page fetch returned a maintenance shell during the October 2026 site-maintenance window; re-open the live docket and underlying certification before a script quotes exact figures or procedural dates.
- [NLRB Region 29 election-count announcement](https://www.nlrb.gov/news-outreach/region-29-brooklyn/region-29-brooklyn-announces-results-of-jfk8-amazon-ballot-count), dated April 1, 2022. The search-index extraction corroborated the named facility, union and date; direct fetch timed out.
- [NLRB election procedure](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/what-we-do/conduct-elections) explains the distinction among tally, objections and certification. This general procedure does not establish what happened in every later JFK8 dispute.

## Bounded premise and gaps

The public record supports a JFK8 bargaining-unit election result and a certification record. It does not, on its own, show a negotiated contract, improvements in pay or safety, workers' individual motivations, or representation across all Amazon sites. The unit expressly excludes other job categories. The case docket lists later filings, so legal and bargaining status must be checked again at script time. No worker interview, contract or subsequent employment-outcome record was inspected here.

## Coverage and overlap decision

Archive pitches about a documented case, collective bargaining, a turning point and an evidence trail route to this bounded question. Generic status, pay, worktime, subcontracting and grievance pitches route to existing `BANK-516-*` foundations. Biographical, tool-effectiveness, future-outcome and superlative claims are marked out of scope pending their own evidence. `BANK-516-033` explains bargaining generally; `BANK-516-044` is confined to what this NLRB election record establishes. Workplace safety issues at JFK8 belong in a separate evidenced `517` question, and the DuPont case in `517` concerns process-safety investigation rather than the election.
