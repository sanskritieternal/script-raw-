## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Borders across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Borders Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Borders.
- **Tier 2: Borders Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Borders.
- **Tier 3: Borders Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Borders.
- **Tier 4: Borders Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Borders.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Borders: How a Boundary Was Drawn**
   - *Question:* Which dated agreement or decision established one border segment?
   - *Key Focus:* Read the instrument, maps, and implementation records; distinguish delimitation from demarcation. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
2. **Borders: A Famous Border in Its Legal Context**
   - *Question:* What do the governing documents say about one widely discussed boundary?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare treaty language, later agreements, and actual administration at a fixed date. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
3. **Borders: Residents of a Border Change**
   - *Question:* Whose experiences are missing from the official account of a boundary shift?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented testimony and records of residence, services, and movement. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
4. **Borders: A Border Is More Than a Line**
   - *Question:* What is the difference between a claimed boundary and an administered crossing?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain legal line, physical markers, controls, and local use in one place. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
5. **Borders: Competing Boundary Evidence**
   - *Question:* How do a treaty, map, and long practice bear on one dispute?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe their different legal and historical roles without deciding the case. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Borders: A Segment Still in Dispute**
   - *Question:* Which boundary question remains unresolved in official records?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify parties' positions, available evidence, and procedural status by date. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
7. **Borders: Mapping a Border with New Data**
   - *Question:* Does a new survey resolve a defined boundary uncertainty?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare measurement precision with the governing legal instrument and local realities. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
8. **Borders: Negotiators Behind a Boundary**
   - *Question:* What did named negotiators decide, and what remained for later implementation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use minutes, drafts, final text, and subsequent records. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
9. **Borders: A Boundary Agreement Takes Effect**
   - *Question:* What changed on the ground after a dated border agreement?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare markers, crossings, administration, and resident rights before and after. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
10. **Borders: From Treaty to Border Marker**
   - *Question:* How does a legal boundary become a marked and managed border?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace negotiation, ratification, delimitation, demarcation, and maintenance in one case. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Borders: A Crossing Opens and Closes**
   - *Question:* Why did use of one border crossing change over time?
   - *Key Focus:* Check rules, trade, transport, conflict, public health, and counting methods. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
12. **Borders: Life Across a Border in Two Periods**
   - *Question:* How did a defined community's movement change after one policy or line change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare equivalent groups, routes, documents, and service access. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
13. **Borders: A Proposed Border Arrangement**
   - *Question:* What would a pending boundary or crossing proposal have to resolve?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify legal authority, implementation costs, and resident effects without predicting settlement. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
14. **Borders: A Map Claim Under Examination**
   - *Question:* Does a popular map accurately show the legal and administered boundary?
   - *Key Focus:* Check date, projection, source, contested status, and official instruments. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
15. **Borders: A Border Dispute's Paper Trail**
   - *Question:* How did a specific border claim travel through negotiation or adjudication?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow filings, maps, decisions, and implementation records. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Borders: A Resident Near the Line**
   - *Question:* What does one consented account reveal about everyday border administration?
   - *Key Focus:* Pair experience with dated rules and broader evidence. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
17. **Borders: Who Manages This Border?**
   - *Question:* Which agencies set the line, manage a crossing, and hear appeals?
   - *Key Focus:* Name the jurisdiction and distinguish legal mandates from practice. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
18. **Borders: The Incentives Behind a Crossing Rule**
   - *Question:* How did revenue, security, or service pressures shape one rule?
   - *Key Focus:* Use official records and measured outcomes; test alternative explanations. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
19. **Borders: Who Cannot Cross Easily?**
   - *Question:* Which documentation, cost, disability, or language barrier affects a defined group?
   - *Key Focus:* Use rights standards, local rules, and disaggregated evidence. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
20. **Borders: Two Communities on One Border**
   - *Question:* Why do nearby communities experience the same line differently?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare crossing locations, family ties, livelihoods, and service access. Evidence base: dated treaties and statutes, official boundary maps, court decisions, crossing rules, oversight reports, and consented resident accounts.
