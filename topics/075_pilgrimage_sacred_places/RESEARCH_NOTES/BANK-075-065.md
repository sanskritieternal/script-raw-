# BANK-075-065 — Lalibela's living rock-hewn churches

**Selected question:** How do Lalibela's rock-hewn churches function as both living pilgrimage and conservation site?

**Primary institutional records inspected (2026-10-24):**

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre property record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/18/) identifies eleven medieval monolithic rock-hewn churches in Lalibela, grouped around a trench/passage system, and describes the site as a continuing place of Ethiopian Christian pilgrimage and devotion. It attributes the building program to King Lalibela while describing the material architecture and conservation pressures separately.
- [UNESCO's Lalibela conservation activity](https://whc.unesco.org/en/activities/160/) describes the rock, interconnected passages and weathering/erosion concerns. It says the construction is attributed to King Lalibela rather than treating every component's date as directly documented.
- [2018 World Heritage advisory mission report](https://whc.unesco.org/document/169702) describes active church and pilgrimage use as part of the site's authenticity and identifies the roles of church, state and local community in stewardship. Its findings are dated; current physical or security conditions require a newer site-specific check.

**Bounded finding:** Eleven rock-hewn churches form a living pilgrimage complex and a heritage-conservation concern. The archived “carved straight down into volcanic rock” captures only part of the construction process: UNESCO describes hewing monolithic blocks, chiselling interiors and connecting trenches, passages and drainage. Attribution to one ruler and a single construction period remain historical interpretations requiring more precise archaeological sources.

**Before production:** Inspect site plans, phase analyses, church-custodian accounts and the latest conservation report before claiming construction sequence, safety, access or a current restoration state. Obtain permission for filming worshippers and protected interiors. This note verifies only a selected premise boundary; other pilgrimage, health, access and historical claims remain research pending.
