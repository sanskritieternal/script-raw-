# Selected source note — JCVI-syn3.0 minimal bacterial cell

Reviewed 2026-10-24 for BANK-483-043. Proposed question only; no approved IDEA, field-use readiness or biosafety clearance.

## Primary research and institutional records

- Hutchison et al., *Design and synthesis of a minimal bacterial genome* (Science, 2016), indexed by NIST with DOI: https://www.nist.gov/publications/design-and-synthesis-minimal-bacterial-genome . The record describes iterative design, synthesis and testing that produced JCVI-syn3.0 with 531 kilobase pairs and 473 genes.
- J. Craig Venter Institute, *First Minimal Synthetic Bacterial Cell Designed and Constructed* (2016-03-24): https://www.jcvi.org/media-center/first-minimal-synthetic-bacterial-cell-designed-and-constructed-scientists-venter . The originating institute explains that syn3.0 was developed from the earlier syn1.0 genome through design-build-test work; it reports 149 genes of then-unknown biological function.
- Original paper DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad6253 . Consult the paper for exact methods and growth conditions before a script makes mechanistic claims.

## Capability and biosafety limits

This was a genome designed and synthesized using prior living-cell information and transplanted into existing cellular machinery. It was not creation of life from nonliving chemicals, a universal minimum gene count, or proof that every gene's function was understood. The viable laboratory organism says nothing by itself about environmental release, ecological safety or commercial scale. Do not include procedural construction steps. Recheck later gene-function studies and strain variants before present-tense claims. Paraphrase institutional and paper text; check figure/data rights.
