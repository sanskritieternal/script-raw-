## BANK-178-044 — 1911 Boy Scouts handbook

- Boy Scouts of America, [The Official Handbook for Boys, first edition, 1911](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/29558/pg29558.txt), a digitized copy of the institution's own period text. The title page identifies the 1911 edition. Its preface states that the National Council sought uniform guidance for patrols and names character, citizenship, service and outdoor practice among its aims. Project Gutenberg released the transcription in 2009 and last updated it in 2021.
- Scouting America, [First BSA Handbook](https://oa-scouting.org/history/first-bsa-handbook), institutional chronology distinguishes the 1910 handbook from the June 1911 Official Handbook.

**Claim limit:** A handbook shows what leaders prescribed, not how every boy, family or troop behaved. The text reflects its own era's gender, citizenship and imperial assumptions. Attribute institutional ideals and compare them with participant or local records before claiming outcomes. Do not reproduce outdated hazardous field instructions.

## BANK-178-045 — Goolwa community shed

- Australian Men's Shed Association, [Shed Life 2018-2019 biennial report](https://mensshed.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/AMSA-Shed-Life-Biennial-Report-2018-2019.pdf), p. 5. Its retrospective account dates the Goolwa community shed for men to February 24, 1993 and distinguishes the first group called a "Men's Shed" at Tongala in July 1998.
- Australian Men's Shed Association, [About AMSA](https://mensshed.org/about-amsa/), says the national association formed in 2007 and describes its support and social-inclusion aims.

**Claim limit:** Association history is a participant account of dates and organizational purpose, not a controlled measure of lower suicide rates or superiority to therapy. The original active BANK question's claimed mental-health effect needs independent evidence. A U.S. "boom" requires a dated U.S. count. If obtaining member accounts, seek informed consent, allow review of sensitive personal details and avoid depicting men who seek clinical help as failures.
