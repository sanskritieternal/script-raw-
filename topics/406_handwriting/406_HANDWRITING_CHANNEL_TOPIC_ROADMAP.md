## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Handwriting across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Handwriting Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Handwriting.
- **Tier 2: Handwriting Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Handwriting.
- **Tier 3: Handwriting Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Handwriting.
- **Tier 4: Handwriting Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Handwriting.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Handwriting: A Handwriting Practice Changes**
   - *Question:* Which dated school, office, or family practice shaped a script form?
   - *Key Focus:* Use manuals and actual documents in context. Evidence base: dated original manuscripts, copybooks, archive provenance, transcription records, imaging methods, material analysis, and bounded legibility tests.
2. **Handwriting: A Letter as Written Object**
   - *Question:* What can layout, corrections, and ink reveal about its production?
   - *Key Focus:* Read the specific document with provenance. Evidence base: dated original manuscripts, copybooks, archive provenance, transcription records, imaging methods, material analysis, and bounded legibility tests.
3. **Handwriting: Whose Hand Is in the Record?**
   - *Question:* Which clerk, scribe, or family member wrote a document attributed to another?
   - *Key Focus:* Check archival and authorship evidence. Evidence base: dated original manuscripts, copybooks, archive provenance, transcription records, imaging methods, material analysis, and bounded legibility tests.
4. **Handwriting: Reading an Unfamiliar Hand**
   - *Question:* How can letter forms and context guide transcription?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare several words and mark uncertain readings. Evidence base: dated original manuscripts, copybooks, archive provenance, transcription records, imaging methods, material analysis, and bounded legibility tests.
5. **Handwriting: and Identity**
   - *Question:* What can a sample support about writer attribution, and what cannot it prove?
   - *Key Focus:* Use provenance and qualified methods; avoid personality claims. Evidence base: dated original manuscripts, copybooks, archive provenance, transcription records, imaging methods, material analysis, and bounded legibility tests.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Handwriting: An Illegible Word**
   - *Question:* Which reading fits letter forms and surrounding text?

7. **Handwriting: Pen, Paper, and Keyboard**

8. **Handwriting: A Writer's Changing Hand**

9. **Handwriting: A Script Taught in Schools**

10. **Handwriting: Transcribing a Manuscript**
