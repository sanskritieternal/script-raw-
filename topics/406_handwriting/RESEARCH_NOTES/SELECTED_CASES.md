## BANK-406-044: 2024 digital-pen and keyboard EEG comparison

- [Van der Weel and Van der Meer's original paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1219945/full) reports high-density EEG from 36 university students during prompted word writing with a digital pen and keyboard typing. Its primary result concerns task-related theta/alpha connectivity patterns in the specified analysis, not a direct test of school achievement or memory retention.
- The study's discussion interprets connectivity using prior literature. Before a production claim, inspect trial exclusions, typing method, statistical comparisons, task duration and later commentary or replication. Do not infer that pen-and-paper, every writing system, all age groups or disability accommodations yield the same result. Faster typing and accessible keyboards may serve different needs.
- This case narrows 406's existing handwriting-versus-typing question to one measured protocol. It differs from 405's lettering art, 363's general reproducibility question and 406's graphology myth topic.
