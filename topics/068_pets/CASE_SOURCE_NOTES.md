## BANK-068-059 — Flat-faced dog breathing welfare (archive pending 10 and trending 26)

- The Royal Veterinary College’s [VetCompass research account](https://www.rvc.ac.uk/vetcompass/news/new-publication-on-canine-brachycephaly-and-upper-respiratory-disease) compares recorded upper-respiratory disorders in selected flat-faced breeds and other breeds. RVC’s [welfare account](https://www.rvc.ac.uk/research/facilities-and-resources/animal-welfare-science-and-ethics/news/international-experts-warn-noisy-breathing-could-signal-suffering-in-popular-flat-faced-dogs) explains why noisy breathing should not be normalized as a breed trait.
- Editorial boundary: report the study population, outcomes and welfare implications. Do not infer the health of a particular dog or repeat the archive’s claim that Europeans stopped buying pugs or that one Europe-wide breeding ban exists. Any legal comparison requires named jurisdictions and current official law.

## BANK-068-064 — Dog Aging Project rapamycin trial (archive pending 15)

- The investigators’ [TRIAD trial design paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12181551/) describes a randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled multicenter study of rapamycin in healthy middle-aged dogs. The project’s [trial explanation](https://dogagingproject.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500001690382-What-is-the-TRIAD-Cohort) defines its aims, and its [owner guidance](https://dogagingproject.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4416990542103-Can-I-give-my-dog-rapamycin-on-my-own) says it cannot recommend use for aging-related decline outside the monitored trial.
- Editorial boundary: distinguish a designed trial and its endpoints from a demonstrated four-year life extension. This note supplies no dose, treatment suggestion or prediction for an individual animal.

## BANK-068-070 — Xylitol in dogs (archive trending 21)

- The US FDA’s [xylitol consumer guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/paws-xylitol-its-dangerous-dogs) describes reports of canine poisoning, including exposures involving chewing gum, and explains why dogs can be vulnerable. Its [animal-health warning](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/paws-xylitol-toxic-dogs) notes that xylitol can appear in several products and names veterinary or animal-poison support as the immediate contact for suspected ingestion.
- Editorial boundary: the ingredient, quantity and species matter; not every gum contains xylitol and cats are not equivalent to dogs. The proposed episode is a source-based hazard explainer, not home treatment advice.
