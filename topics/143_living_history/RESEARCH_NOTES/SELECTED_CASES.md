# Selected living-history interpretation cases

Research checked: 2026-10-24. Status: bounded premise research, not script or idea approval.

## BANK-143-046: Monticello descendant testimony

[Monticello's Getting Word project](https://www.monticello.org/exhibits/getting-word-oral-history-project) says it began recording descendants of the site's enslaved community in 1993. Monticello's [memoir and oral-history index](https://www.monticello.org/slavery/enslaved-at-monticello/memoirs-oral-histories-by-members-of-monticellos-enslaved-community) exposes selected first-person and descendant sources, and its [research-and-interpretation timeline](https://www.monticello.org/milestones-in-the-research-and-interpretation-of-slavery-at-monticello) shows institutional changes over time.

**Limit:** These records establish the site's own oral-history and interpretation work. A descendant recollection needs its speaker, recording date, family relation and historical context; it does not become a verbatim reconstruction of every enslaved person's experience. The site's account is not an independent impact evaluation of what visitors learned. Use descendant voices with attribution and rights review.

## BANK-143-047: British Museum object M.574

The [British Museum catalogue entry for M.574](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_M-574) labels this iron chastity-belt object a forgery, gives a tentative eighteenth- or nineteenth-century production date, and explains that much of the period-use evidence is anecdotal or satirical. It also records uncertainty around the object's own date.

**Limit:** The catalogue supports a case about a particular museum object's changing attribution. It does not show that all such objects originated with Victorians, that medieval sources never mentioned the device, or that a museum deliberately deceived visitors. Check display and catalogue history before narrating institutional motive.
