## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Living History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Material Authenticity, Period Clothing & Daily Domestic Routines (Beginner / Scratch)** — Stitch-for-stitch hand-sewn historical clothing (linen/wool), natural plant dyes, cooking over wood fires in cast iron/earthenware, and authentic period hygiene.
- **Tier 2: Open-Hearth Cooking, Historic Weapon Drills & Historical Agriculture (Intermediate)** — Flintlock musket manual of arms, Roman legionary shield wall maneuvers (testudo), open-hearth baking with Dutch ovens, and historic crop rotation.
- **Tier 3: Living Museum Interpretation, Battle Reenactments & First-Person Empathy (Upper Intermediate)** — First-person vs third-person costumed interpretation, Gettysburg and Waterloo mega-reenactments, deconstructive experimental archaeology, and historical trauma sensitivity.
- **Tier 4: Experimental Archaeology, Medieval Construction (Guédelon) & Living History Stewardship (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Building a full 13th-century castle from scratch (Guédelon), sailing authentic replica caravels across oceans, historic breed livestock preservation, and living history pedagogy.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **Guédelon Castle: Building a 13th-Century Fortress Using Only Medieval Tools**
   - *Question:* How are stonemasons and carpenters in France spending 30 years building an authentic 1228 fortress using only limestone, oxen, and human treadwheels?
   - *Key Focus:* 13-knot rope geometry, human squirrel-cage cranes, quarrying sandstone with iron wedges, lime mortar slaking, and answering unsolved medieval architecture mysteries.
2. **The Stitch-Counters: The Obsession with Authentic Historic Clothing**
   - *Question:* Why do hardcore living historians reject sewing machines, synthetic polyester threads, and modern chemical dyes?
   - *Key Focus:* Hand-waxed linen thread, period seam-finishing techniques, madder and woad natural dyes, wool broadcloth weights, and wearing authentic undergarments (stays/corsets).
3. **The 18th-Century Hearth: Cooking Authentic Dinners with Townends**
   - *Question:* How did a family business selling historical reenactment gear turn cooking 1700s fried chicken and portable soup into a 2-million-subscriber sensation?
   - *Key Focus:* Dutch oven baking in hearth coals, salt pork and hardtack staples, historical recipe translations, nutmeg obsession, and the sensory intimacy of vintage foodways.
4. **The Roman Legionary: Marching 20 Miles with 60 Pounds of Lorica Segmentata**
   - *Question:* What is it actually like to wear steel plate armor, carry a wooden scutum shield, and march across rugged terrain for days?
   - *Key Focus:* Lorica segmentata leather strapping flexibility, pilum bendable armor-piercing javelins, hobnailed caligae sandals, and experimental reenactment test marches.
5. **First-Person vs Third-Person: The Acting Dilemma in Living Museums**
   - *Question:* Should a costumed docent pretend it is 1840 and act confused by smartphones, or speak as a modern historian explaining the past?
   - *Key Focus:* Plimoth Patuxet first-person method, suspension of disbelief, handling anachronistic tourist questions, visitor intimidation, and third-person educational clarity.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **The Flintlock Musket Manual of Arms: 15 Steps to Fire 3 Rounds a Minute**
   - *Question:* Why did 18th-century soldiers bite paper gunpowder cartridges and practice rigid drills until they could reload under cannon fire?
   - *Key Focus:* Bite ball, prime pan, cast about, charge with cartridge, ram down ball, half-cock safety, and British Army volley fire discipline on Napoleonic battlefields.
7. **Colonial Williamsburg: How Rockefeller Rebuilt America's Colonial Capital**
   - *Question:* How did John D. Rockefeller Jr. spend millions buying 500 historic buildings to create the world's largest living history museum in Virginia?
   - *Key Focus:* Historic architectural preservation, archaeological excavations, trade shop apprenticeships (joinery/gunsmithing/apothecary), and evolving historical narratives.
8. **Living on a Victorian Farm: The BBC Historical Farming Experiments**
   - *Question:* What happened when Ruth Goodman, Peter Ginn, and Alex Langlands spent a full year living under Victorian, Edwardian, and Wartime farm conditions?
   - *Key Focus:* Coal stove maintenance, horse-drawn hay harvesting, sheep dipping, period laundry physical exhaustion, and discovering lost domestic skills.
9. **Waterloo 200: Mobilizing 6,000 Reenactors on the Sacred Battlefield**
   - *Question:* How did thousands of historical reenactors from 50 nations recreate the epic clash of Napoleon and Wellington with 300 horses and 100 cannons?
   - *Key Focus:* Bivouac camp life, cavalry charge safety coordination, black powder smoke fog-of-war, authentic tactical maneuvering, and historical spectacle.
10. **The Myth of the Dirty Middle Ages: Historic Hygiene and Washing Realities**
   - *Question:* Did medieval people really never bathe and smell like sewer trenches, or have Hollywood movies lied to us for decades?
   - *Key Focus:* Linen undergarments absorbing sweat and oil, public bathhouses in medieval cities, washing hands before meals, herbal mouthwashes, and Victorian slander.
### Tier 3: Strategic & Advanced Architectures
11. **Sailing the Mayflower II: Crossing the Atlantic in a 17th-Century Wooden Caravel**
   - *Question:* What is it like to steer a blunt, pitching 100-foot square-rigged merchant ship across storm-tossed seas using an archaic wooden whipstaff?
   - *Key Focus:* Square-rig sail handling, cramped dark tween-decks conditions, scurvy prevention, ballast stability, and experimental maritime archaeology at sea.
12. **Historic Pigment Chemistry: Grinding Lapis Lazuli, Cochineal, and Lead White**
   - *Question:* How did Renaissance masters grind Afghan gemstones and crush Mexican cactus beetles to create brilliant blue and crimson oil paints?
   - *Key Focus:* Ultramarine extraction from lapis lazuli, toxic flake lead white corrosion, crushed cochineal scale insects, walnut oil binders, and illuminating manuscripts.
13. **Heritage Livestock Breeds: Resurrecting the Animals Our Ancestors Actually Farmed**
   - *Question:* Why do modern factory farm chickens and hogs bear zero resemblance to the lean, hardy, disease-resistant animals of the 18th century?
   - *Key Focus:* Tamworth hogs, Jacob multi-horned sheep, Devon milking cattle, loss of maternal brooding instincts in modern breeds, and living museum gene banks.
14. **The Hardtack Challenge: Tasting the Break-Your-Teeth Rations of the Civil War**
   - *Question:* Why did soldiers call baked flour-and-water biscuits 'sheet-iron crackers' and tap them on tables to shake out weevils before eating?
   - *Key Focus:* Baking hardtack to zero moisture for indefinite shelf life, boiling in salt pork fat to soften, weevil infestations ('worm castles'), and soldier caloric deficits.
15. **Confronting Slavery in Living History: Interpreting Enslaved Lives at Historic Sites**
   - *Question:* How do historic plantations like Monticello and Mount Vernon move past romanticized 'Gone with the Wind' myths to honor enslaved human beings?
   - *Key Focus:* Direct descendant oral histories, slave cabin archaeology, emotional labor of Black living historians, confronting defensive tourists, and ethical accuracy.
### Tier 4: Future Horizons & Masterclasses
16. **Medieval Medicine in Practice: Leeches, Trepanning, and Herbal Apothecaries**
   - *Question:* Was medieval medicine pure superstitious barbarism, or did willow bark teas and honey wound dressings contain genuine pharmaceutical science?
   - *Key Focus:* Four humors balance (blood, yellow bile, black bile, phlegm), willow bark salicylic acid (aspirin precursor), surgical cautery irons, and bald's eyesalve MRSA bacteria breakthrough.
17. **The Blacksmith of Guédelon: Forging 300,000 Nails and Sharpening Stone Chisels**
   - *Question:* How does a single on-site castle blacksmith forge every iron door hinge, pickaxe head, and square-headed nail for a 30-year fortress build?
   - *Key Focus:* Charcoal hearth maintenance on site, forging pointed nails from iron bars in 30 seconds, case-hardening stonemason chisels, and iron recycling in medieval Europe.
18. **The Farbfeld and Authentic Natural Dyes: Madder Red, Woad Blue, and Weld Yellow**
   - *Question:* How did medieval dye vats ferment woad leaves in stale urine to produce the deep indigo blues of royal tapestries?
   - *Key Focus:* Mordanting wool with alum, indigo reduction vats, alizarin dye extraction from madder roots, lightfastness tests, and recreating historic heraldry.
19. **The Philosophy of Historical Immersion: Can We Truly Know the Past?**
   - *Question:* Can wearing wool and eating hardtack ever let us experience how a 17th-century peasant actually felt, or is it an impossible modern fantasy?
   - *Key Focus:* Hermeneutic circle of historical understanding, modern psychological privilege, embodied muscle memory insights, and historical empathy as a civic virtue.
20. **The Master Living History Architecture: Designing World-Class Experiential Programs**
   - *Question:* What comprehensive institutional framework unites material culture research, period livestock breeding, staff historical pedagogy, and visitor safety?
   - *Key Focus:* Documentary evidence vetting, period-accurate safety protocols (fire/black powder), educational curriculum alignment, interpretive staff training, and long-term site preservation.
