# Selected source note — CALERIE and epigenetic measures

Reviewed 2026-10-24 for BANK-472-044. This is a proposed research question, not health advice, approval or production readiness.

## Primary research and agency records

- Belsky et al., *Effect of long-term caloric restriction on DNA methylation measures of biological aging in healthy adults from the CALERIE trial* (Nature Aging, 2023; full text): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10148951/ . The post hoc analysis used blood samples from the two-year randomized CALERIE trial (220 adults without obesity randomized to a prescribed 25% restriction or ad libitum control). It reports an effect on DunedinPACE but no significant effect on the PhenoAge or GrimAge estimates.
- U.S. National Institute on Aging, CALERIE study resource: https://www.nia.nih.gov/research/resource/comprehensive-assessment-long-term-effects-reducing-intake-energy-calerie . The agency describes Phase 2 as a three-site, two-year randomized trial in healthy, non-obese young and middle-aged adults.
- NIA AgingResearchBiobank study record: https://agingresearchbiobank.nia.nih.gov/studies/calerie/details . It reports that average sustained restriction was about 12%, below the assigned 25% target.

## Medical and claim limits

The methylation analysis was post hoc and measures a biomarker, not observed lifespan, reduced disease incidence or a safe intervention for an individual. Different clocks did not agree; the study itself calls for long-term clinical outcomes. Two years and a selected healthy adult sample cannot establish life extension for older, ill or general populations. No diet, calorie target or treatment recommendation follows from this note. Recheck sample exclusions, statistical tables and later follow-up before scripting a numerical effect. Paraphrase article text and check figure rights.
