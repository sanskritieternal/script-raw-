## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Literature across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Literature Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Literature.
- **Tier 2: Literature Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Literature.
- **Tier 3: Literature Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Literature.
- **Tier 4: Literature Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Literature.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Literature: A Work's First Context**
   - *Question:* What publication and social setting shaped one literary work?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated edition, correspondence, and contemporary response. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
2. **Literature: A Canonical Text Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What does the text itself support beyond its reputation?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify edition and passage. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
3. **Literature: Editors and Translators**
   - *Question:* Whose work changed how a literary work reached readers?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace proofs, translation, and credit. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
4. **Literature: Read a Passage Closely**
   - *Question:* How do speaker, form, and context shape meaning?
   - *Key Focus:* Use a short quoted passage within rights limits. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
5. **Literature: Narrator and Reliability**
   - *Question:* Which textual signals complicate a narrator's account?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare scenes and alternative readings. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Literature: An Unsettled Attribution**
   - *Question:* Which manuscript or publication evidence supports rival claims?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate consensus from conjecture. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
7. **Literature: A New Reading Medium**
   - *Question:* How did format change a work's circulation or reception?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated editions and reader evidence. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
8. **Literature: Author and Collaborators**
   - *Question:* Which decisions can be attributed to whom?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts, letters, editorial records. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
9. **Literature: A Work's Publication**
   - *Question:* What changed between manuscript, first edition, and later versions?
   - *Key Focus:* Document text variants. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
10. **Literature: Text to Edition**
   - *Question:* How do drafting, editing, printing, and preservation affect a work?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one documented edition. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Literature: A Work's Changing Reputation**
   - *Question:* Which institutions and readers altered its status?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare reviews, syllabi, and editions across time. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
12. **Literature: Two Readings across Time**
   - *Question:* How did reception of one passage change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated, situated critical sources. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
13. **Literature: A New Edition or Adaptation**
   - *Question:* Which editorial choices require fresh evidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Treat proposal as conditional. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
14. **Literature: An Author Legend Tested**
   - *Question:* Does a popular biographical story match records?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace earliest claim and archive. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
15. **Literature: Manuscript to Reader**
   - *Question:* How did one text pass through drafts, publication, and later use?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a dated source trail. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Literature: What Was the Work Asked to Do?**
   - *Question:* Which commission or writer aim is documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish stated intent from interpretation. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
17. **Literature: Writing Materials**
   - *Question:* How did notebook, typewriter, or printing limits shape text?
   - *Key Focus:* Use surviving artifacts and drafts. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
18. **Literature: Revision Visible**
   - *Question:* What changed between two authorized versions?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare textual variants, not imagined process. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
19. **Literature: Form and Meaning**
   - *Question:* Why does a structural or stylistic choice matter in a passage?
   - *Key Focus:* Test with close reading and context. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
20. **Literature: Many Hands in a Text**
   - *Question:* How did editor, translator, or performer alter the work?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute changes from records. Evidence base: dated editions and manuscripts, archival catalogues, author and editor correspondence, contemporary reviews, translation records, and rights documentation.
