## BANK-056-059 — British Bombe (archive pending 12)

- Bletchley Park’s [teaching notes](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/teachers-notes-ks2-2019.pdf) describe the Bombe as a machine designed by Alan Turing and Gordon Welchman to speed work on Enigma settings. The [Computer History Museum timeline](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1941/) also places the machine in a larger codebreaking process.
- Editorial boundary: illustrate constraint elimination and later human checking. Do not imply that pressing a button automatically yielded plaintext, or credit one person for all wartime Enigma work.

## BANK-056-065 — Zero-knowledge proofs (archive pending 20)

- Goldwasser, Micali and Rackoff’s [original research paper](https://doi.org/10.1137/0218012) develops knowledge complexity in interactive proof systems. MIT CSAIL’s [research account](https://toc.csail.mit.edu/node/218) identifies this as the foundational zero-knowledge work.
- Editorial boundary: explain completeness, soundness and the zero-knowledge property through one stated protocol. “Prove you know a secret without revealing it” is a useful intuition, but privacy and security of a real implementation depend on the actual protocol and assumptions.
