## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Embroidery across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Embroidery Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Embroidery.
- **Tier 2: Embroidery Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Embroidery.
- **Tier 3: Embroidery Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Embroidery.
- **Tier 4: Embroidery Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Embroidery.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Embroidery: An Embroidered Form's Early Record**
   - *Question:* What dated cloth shows one local stitch practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate earliest example from absolute beginning. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
2. **Embroidery: A Celebrated Embroidered Garment**
   - *Question:* What does it reveal about maker, wearer, and occasion?
   - *Key Focus:* Check provenance, stitches, materials, and use. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
3. **Embroidery: Uncredited Stitchers**
   - *Question:* Whose hours and teaching are hidden behind a finished piece?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented maker accounts and pay context. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
4. **Embroidery: Reading Embroidery**
   - *Question:* How do ground cloth, thread, stitch, and motif work together?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one named community practice. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
5. **Embroidery: Pattern and Variation**
   - *Question:* How much can stitchers adapt within an inherited design?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated work and maker explanations. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Embroidery: An Embroidery's Unknown Maker**

9. **Embroidery: After Migration**

10. **Embroidery: From Cloth to Embroidered Work**

### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Embroidery: Demand for Embroidery**
   - *Question:* What explains a measured change in orders?
   - *Key Focus:* Check fashion, ceremonial use, prices, imports, and data limits. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
12. **Embroidery: One Motif Across Generations**
   - *Question:* How did stitch, color, and use change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use provenanced pieces and maker accounts. Evidence base: provenanced embroidered objects, dated workshop and market records, material examination, and consented maker and wearer accounts.
13. **Embroidery: Supporting Stitchers**
   - *Question:* What do makers need for paid and voluntary practice?

16. **Embroidery: for a Named Use**

18. **Embroidery: Stitching a Motif**
   - *Question:* Which stages require counting, tension, and correction?

19. **Embroidery: Placing a Pattern**
   - *Question:* Why does a maker put a motif on a border or field?
