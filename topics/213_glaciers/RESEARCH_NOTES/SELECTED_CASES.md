# Selected glacier-case source checks

All linked topics remain **proposed**. Research here bounds a case question; it does not approve a production script or hazard advice.

## BANK-213-037 — Thwaites

The [International Thwaites Glacier Collaboration findings](https://thwaitesglacier.org/findings) distinguish field observations, ocean forcing, shelf condition and model projections. Its findings do not supply a fixed collapse date. Ice-shelf weakening is not identical to instantaneous loss of grounded ice or an immediate global sea-level jump. Review individual papers before quoting rates or projection ranges.

## BANK-213-038 — Ladakh ice stupas

An [original artificial-ice-reservoir modeling and measurement study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.771342/full) compares the ice-volume evolution of a Ladakh structure with two Swiss structures. A [socio-hydrology field study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10113-018-1372-0) describes local irrigation context. A constructed seasonal reservoir is not a natural glacier; measured stored water does not by itself prove regional farm recovery.

## BANK-213-039 — Chamoli 2021

The [original multinational reconstruction](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70221399) identifies an initiating rock-and-ice avalanche and downstream cascading flow. The archived label of a glacial-lake outburst is inaccurate for this event. Preserve the distinction between the remote-sensing reconstruction, any field evidence and specific consequences before scripting a timeline.

## BANK-213-040 — Larsen B 2002

The [NSIDC March 2002 report](https://nsidc.org/news-analyses/news-stories/antarctic-ice-shelf-collapses-largest-event-last-30-years) estimated about 3,250 square kilometers of shelf disintegration over a 35-day window. A [later NSIDC explainer](https://nsidc.org/learn/ask-scientist/what-happened-larsen-ice-shelf) gives a different 2,717-square-kilometer description for that rapid phase. Do not quote one as an exact uncontested area without reconciling imagery boundaries and dates. Floating shelf loss has no direct displacement-based sea-level rise, though it can affect grounded ice flow.
