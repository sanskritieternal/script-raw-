## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Minimalism across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Minimalism Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Minimalism.
- **Tier 2: Minimalism Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Minimalism.
- **Tier 3: Minimalism Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Minimalism.
- **Tier 4: Minimalism Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Minimalism.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Minimalism: A Simplicity Movement in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated social or artistic practice shaped a named minimalist approach?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute the specific tradition and avoid one origin. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
2. **Minimalism: A Minimal Home in Use**
   - *Question:* What does a photographed sparse home reveal and conceal about daily life?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare inventory, storage, and occupant accounts. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
3. **Minimalism: The Labor behind Less**
   - *Question:* Who shops, repairs, rents, stores, or cleans for a minimalist household?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace externalized work and cost. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
4. **Minimalism: Define Less of What**
   - *Question:* Does a minimalist practice reduce possessions, purchases, space, or obligations?
   - *Key Focus:* Choose a measure before judging results. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
5. **Minimalism: Less Stuff, Same Service?**
   - *Question:* Does a low-consumption choice maintain a household's needed function?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare functional units and equity effects. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Minimalism: An Unmeasured Benefit Claim**
   - *Question:* Which evidence is missing for a claim about wellbeing or footprint?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify comparison group, outcome, and confounders. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
7. **Minimalism: Digital Substitution**
   - *Question:* Does replacing physical goods with subscriptions reduce cost or impact?
   - *Key Focus:* Count devices, energy, fees, and service access. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
8. **Minimalism: Why One Household Downsized**
   - *Question:* What documented changes in need or values led to fewer possessions?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented accounts, not personality diagnosis. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
9. **Minimalism: A Move to Less Space**
   - *Question:* What changed in purchases, routines, and costs after downsizing?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated records and household composition. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
10. **Minimalism: A Purchase Decision**
   - *Question:* How does a person decide to keep, borrow, repair, or buy?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace actual task and available alternatives. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Minimalism: A Minimalist Rule Abandoned**
   - *Question:* Which unmet need or hidden cost led to revision?
   - *Key Focus:* Use household follow-up, not ideology. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
12. **Minimalism: A Household before and after Decluttering**
   - *Question:* Which inventory, spending, and use patterns changed?
   - *Key Focus:* Measure over time, not just a single photograph. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
13. **Minimalism: A Low-Consumption Scenario**
   - *Question:* What could change if access to repair or sharing improves?
   - *Key Focus:* State assumptions and distributional effects. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
14. **Minimalism: Less Always Greener?**
   - *Question:* When do travel, replacement, or outsourcing offset fewer possessions?
   - *Key Focus:* Use full life-cycle boundary. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
15. **Minimalism: A Year of Keeping Less**
   - *Question:* How did a household test and revise its own rules?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated choices, costs, and lived experience. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Minimalism: Living with Fewer Things**
   - *Question:* Which tasks become easier or harder for a specific household?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented accounts over a defined period. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
17. **Minimalism: Services that Make Less Possible**
   - *Question:* Which libraries, rentals, repairs, or public spaces substitute for ownership?
   - *Key Focus:* Map access and eligibility. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
18. **Minimalism: Why Minimalism Appeals**
   - *Question:* Which cost, aesthetic, time, or environmental motive is documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Use participants' own words and avoid assumed virtue. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
19. **Minimalism: Who Cannot Own Less Easily?**
   - *Question:* Which disability, caregiving, work, or rural need changes the tradeoff?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare actual access and alternatives. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
20. **Minimalism: Different Forms of Simplicity**
   - *Question:* How do low-consumption practices vary by household and place?
   - *Key Focus:* Avoid treating one lifestyle as universal. Evidence base: dated inventories and spending logs, consented resident accounts, access and service records, life-cycle assessments, and original low-consumption research.
