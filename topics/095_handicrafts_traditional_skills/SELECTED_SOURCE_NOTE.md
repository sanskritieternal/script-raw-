# Selected craft-premise source checks — 24 October 2026

These notes support two bounded proposal questions. They do not verify all 61 active topics, make a craft safe to demonstrate, or provide filming and reproduction rights.

## BANK-095-068 — Japan's recognized craft holders

The [Agency for Cultural Affairs' Intangible Cultural Properties explanation](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/cultural_properties/introduction/intangible/) was inspected directly. The government designates Important Intangible Cultural Properties and recognizes holders through individual, collective or group routes. It describes a ¥2 million annual grant for recognized **individual** holders, and separate subsidies for training or public performance by recognized groups and others. “Living National Treasure” is a popular label for such an individual holder; the source does not say every craft master receives a salary. A production case should identify one dated designation and holder, confirm current amounts and use conditions, and obtain permission for maker footage.

## BANK-095-073 — Ecuadorian toquilla hat weaving

The [UNESCO 2012 listing and decision](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-weaving-of-the-ecuadorian-toquilla-straw-hat-00729) appeared in indexed primary-source text. It identifies Ecuadorian coastal toquilla fiber, farming and weaving roles and a range from one day to eight months depending on fineness; Pile is named for extra-fine hats. Direct access to the UNESCO page and decision returned a human-verification screen, so the full nomination and periodic report have **not** been inspected. This is enough to reject the archived absolute “six months under moonlight” as an established universal rule, but not enough to price a Montecristi hat or describe an individual maker's labor. Obtain a specific maker's record, consent and a dated sale or price source before a script makes those claims. Do not use the “Panama hat” nickname to relocate the craft to Panama.
