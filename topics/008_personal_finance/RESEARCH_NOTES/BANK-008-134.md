# BANK-008-134 — 50/30/20 rule as a household test

Status: bounded source review for a topic proposal, not a verified script or personal financial advice. Inspected 2026-10-24.

## Primary sources inspected

- [CFPB, *My spending rule to live by*](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_worksheet_my-spending-rule-to-live-by.pdf): presents the 50/20/30 allocation as a tip and explicitly invites a household to choose a personal rule that fits its circumstances. Its savings/debt bucket is 20%; the order in the label differs from the common 50/30/20 phrasing.
- [CFPB, *Analyzing budgets* student worksheet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_building_block_activities_analyzing-budgets_worksheet.pdf): asks learners to classify expenses using monthly net income, compare the resulting shares with 50/30/20, and explain their classification.
- [CFPB, *Assess your spending*](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/): recommends comparing a realistic budget with actual bank balances and including less frequent expenses.

## Editorial boundary

The older queue asks whether the rule “still works with today's housing prices.” The inspected CFPB materials establish a teaching heuristic, not a current housing affordability test or a universal target. A future episode should first choose a jurisdiction, household type, location, date and actual income/expense data; classify ambiguous costs transparently; include irregular expenses; then calculate whether the heuristic fits the example. No current rent series, Elizabeth Warren attribution, or household outcome claim has been checked here. Keep the 50/30/20 percentages and all claimed impacts out of a script until the chosen data and assumptions are documented.
