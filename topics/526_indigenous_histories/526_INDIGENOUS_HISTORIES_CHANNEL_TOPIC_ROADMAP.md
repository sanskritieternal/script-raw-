## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Indigenous Histories across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Indigenous Histories Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Indigenous Histories.
- **Tier 2: Indigenous Histories Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Indigenous Histories.
- **Tier 3: Indigenous Histories Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Indigenous Histories.
- **Tier 4: Indigenous Histories Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Indigenous Histories.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Indigenous Histories: How Indigenous Histories Developed**
   - *Question:* How does a community's authorized account explain a historical development?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one community's account of a historical development using community-authorized sources and dated records; avoid imposing a single origin narrative. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: community-authorized historical sources, attributed oral accounts, provenance and dated external records. Starting framework: Smithsonian Native Knowledge 360 (https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Indigenous Histories: A Documented Indigenous Histories Case**
   - *Question:* What does one Indigenous historical case establish within its own place and period?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one documented Indigenous historical case in its own community, place and period; distinguish its meaning from broad claims about all peoples. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: community-authorized historical sources, attributed oral accounts, provenance and dated external records. Starting framework: Smithsonian Native Knowledge 360 (https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Case records: community-authorized historical sources, attributed oral accounts, provenance and dated external records.
