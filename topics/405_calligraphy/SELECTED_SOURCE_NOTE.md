# Selected source and boundary note — 405 Calligraphy

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-405-043** concerns a named album, not an entire calligraphic tradition.

## Primary collection record

The Metropolitan Museum of Art, [*Pilgrim's Visiting Album*, object 94.18.5](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/75964), dates this Japanese item to the eighteenth century and identifies it as one of four albums. The museum says an adherent of the Pure Land School compiled slips brushed with the name of Amida during temple visits. Signed and sealed slips indicate that **most** calligraphers were abbots of Pure Land temples in the greater Edo area. The record identifies gilt paper and paper, ink and color paintings, and embroidered silk covers. The Met labels this object image public domain and the object not on view as inspected; check current access and image-specific metadata before publication.

## Limits, attribution and overlap

Signatures and seals support item-level attribution where legible and verified, not a blanket claim that every slip has an identifiable hand. The museum description alone does not establish the order of temple visits, the date each slip was written, or that this practice represents all Buddhist or Japanese calligraphy. Consult script readers and tradition-specific scholarship before translating, interpreting devotional meaning, or comparing styles. **BANK-405-034** addresses broad historical letterform study and **BANK-405-039** page layout; this case follows one album's signed contributions and collection form. **BANK-406-043** concerns limits of handwriting inference about a person's diagnosis or character; this case does not attempt such inference.
