## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Calligraphy across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Calligraphy Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Calligraphy.
- **Tier 2: Calligraphy Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Calligraphy.
- **Tier 3: Calligraphy Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Calligraphy.
- **Tier 4: Calligraphy Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Calligraphy.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Calligraphy: A Script Style in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated work documents a specific calligraphic convention?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify language, place, material, and provenance. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
2. **Calligraphy: Reading a Calligraphic Work**
   - *Question:* How do spacing, line, and ink shape a named composition?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine original or high-quality record with translation where needed. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
3. **Calligraphy: Makers of a Manuscript**
   - *Question:* Whose preparation, copying, illumination, or mounting is documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Check colophon, collection history, and conservation records. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
4. **Calligraphy: Writing as Visual Form**
   - *Question:* What distinguishes a script's letter forms from its layout and message?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one identified work and language expertise. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
5. **Calligraphy: Script and Social Meaning**
   - *Question:* How do form, patronage, and use change interpretation of a work?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated context and alternative readings. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Calligraphy: An Uncertain Attribution**
   - *Question:* Which features and records support or weaken a maker claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate handwriting comparison from proof of identity. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
7. **Calligraphy: A Tool Changes Script**
   - *Question:* How did a brush, pen, paper, or digital tool alter a named practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare works and makers' accounts. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
8. **Calligraphy: A Calligrapher's Choice**
   - *Question:* Which documented revision or departure distinguishes an artist?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated works and attributed commentary. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
9. **Calligraphy: A Manuscript Enters a Collection**
   - *Question:* What changed in access or interpretation after acquisition?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace provenance and catalog revisions. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
10. **Calligraphy: From Stroke to Page**
   - *Question:* How do tool, sequence, spacing, and support interact in one work?
   - *Key Focus:* Use visible evidence and authorized demonstration. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Calligraphy: A Script's Changing Use**
   - *Question:* Which administrative or artistic practice changed a style's role?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated documents, not an assumed decline. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
12. **Calligraphy: A Script across Periods**
   - *Question:* What formal features changed and which persisted?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare works in the same language and function. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
13. **Calligraphy: A Digital Calligraphic Practice**
   - *Question:* Which released project changes creation or access?
   - *Key Focus:* Document maker control and material differences. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
14. **Calligraphy: Beautiful Writing Is Legible?**
   - *Question:* How can visual beauty and reading ease diverge for a particular audience?
   - *Key Focus:* Test script, language knowledge, and use context. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
15. **Calligraphy: A Manuscript's Material Journey**
   - *Question:* How was a written work made, moved, altered, and conserved?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated object and collection records. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Calligraphy: Writing for a Purpose**
   - *Question:* What message, format, and audience did a commission require?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, inscription, and use. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
17. **Calligraphy: Ink and Support**
   - *Question:* How do brush or pen, ink, and paper affect visible marks?
   - *Key Focus:* Use object analysis and conservation records. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
18. **Calligraphy: A Page's Making**
   - *Question:* Which preparation, writing, correction, and mounting stages are documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify evidence for each stage. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
19. **Calligraphy: Spacing and Rhythm**
   - *Question:* How do line length and empty space guide reading?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze a specified sheet and translation. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
20. **Calligraphy: Text and Ornament**
   - *Question:* Who wrote, decorated, bound, and commissioned the work?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute roles from surviving evidence. Evidence base: identified works and inscriptions, language-specific scholarship, collection provenance, maker records, material analysis, and conservation reports.
