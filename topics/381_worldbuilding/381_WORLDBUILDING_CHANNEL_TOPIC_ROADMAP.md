## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Worldbuilding across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Worldbuilding Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Worldbuilding.
- **Tier 2: Worldbuilding Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Worldbuilding.
- **Tier 3: Worldbuilding Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Worldbuilding.
- **Tier 4: Worldbuilding Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Worldbuilding.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Worldbuilding: An Invented World's Sources**
   - *Question:* Which documented historical or artistic references shaped a setting?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute sources and distinguish inspiration from direct copying. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
2. **Worldbuilding: A World under Scrutiny**
   - *Question:* What makes a named fictional world coherent for its story?
   - *Key Focus:* Test selected rules against events, not an imagined complete encyclopedia. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
3. **Worldbuilding: Who Built the World?**
   - *Question:* Which writers, designers, cartographers, and consultants shaped it?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits, drafts, and asset provenance. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
4. **Worldbuilding: Rules, Places, and People**
   - *Question:* What minimum facts must an audience learn to follow one scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Map the scene's required knowledge and reveal order. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
5. **Worldbuilding: Institutions in an Invented World**
   - *Question:* How do a stated resource and authority rule shape daily life?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace access, labor, and conflict within the work. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Worldbuilding: An Unanswered World Detail**
   - *Question:* Is an apparent gap deliberate, contradictory, or version-dependent?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare identified editions and creator records. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
7. **Worldbuilding: in a Game Engine**
   - *Question:* Which choices become testable when a world is interactive?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze a released build and its constraints. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
8. **Worldbuilding: A Creator Changes the Setting**
   - *Question:* What documented revision altered an invented society?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated drafts and attributed accounts. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
9. **Worldbuilding: A World Expanded across Works**
   - *Question:* How did a sequel or adaptation revise established rules?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare publication order and authorized versions. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
10. **Worldbuilding: A Rule's Consequences**
   - *Question:* What follows when one world rule interacts with a character goal?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace setup, available options, and outcome. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Worldbuilding: A World Rule Retconned**
   - *Question:* Why was a rule changed or ignored in a later version?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare texts and production notes; mark unknown intent. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
12. **Worldbuilding: A Setting across Editions**
   - *Question:* Which institutions or geography changed between versions?
   - *Key Focus:* Use version-specific maps and texts. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
13. **Worldbuilding: Expanding a Fictional World**
   - *Question:* What unresolved design decision does a planned release face?
   - *Key Focus:* State documented plans without predicting canon. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
14. **Worldbuilding: Needs Total Detail?**
   - *Question:* How much setting knowledge is actually required for one narrative?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare scene function with background notes. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
15. **Worldbuilding: A World's Design Trail**
   - *Question:* How did a setting evolve from outline to released work?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow drafts, maps, and design approvals. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Worldbuilding: The Setting's Job in the Story**
   - *Question:* Which conflict or mood did the world brief need to support?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, designs, and finished scene. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
17. **Worldbuilding: Maps, Timelines, and Bibles**
   - *Question:* What do planning artifacts establish about a fictional world?
   - *Key Focus:* Check authorship, version, and whether the detail appears in the work. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
18. **Worldbuilding: Revising a World Rule**
   - *Question:* Which contradiction or story need prompted a change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts and downstream consequences. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
19. **Worldbuilding: Revealing the World through Action**
   - *Question:* What does a character encounter before the rules are explained?
   - *Key Focus:* Track audience knowledge in a bounded sequence. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
20. **Worldbuilding: A Shared Setting**
   - *Question:* Which contributions came from writers, artists, game designers, and consultants?
   - *Key Focus:* Use records and relevant credit rules. Evidence base: identified editions and builds, dated drafts, maps and bibles, production assets, credited contributions, rights records, and situated audience responses.
