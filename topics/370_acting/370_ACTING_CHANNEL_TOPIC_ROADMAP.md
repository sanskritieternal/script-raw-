## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Acting across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Acting Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Acting.
- **Tier 2: Acting Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Acting.
- **Tier 3: Acting Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Acting.
- **Tier 4: Acting Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Acting.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Acting: A Performance Tradition Traced**
   - *Question:* Which documented training or staging practice shaped a particular role?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated scripts, rehearsal records, and practitioner accounts. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
2. **Acting: A Celebrated Performance Revisited**
   - *Question:* What observable choices make a named performance distinctive?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze recorded work and situated criticism without mind-reading. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
3. **Acting: The Ensemble's Contribution**
   - *Question:* Who helped create a remembered scene beyond its star?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credits, rehearsals, blocking, and collaborator testimony. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
4. **Acting: Choices in a Scene**
   - *Question:* How do objective, timing, listening, and movement affect a short exchange?
   - *Key Focus:* Show a bounded scene and compare alternatives. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
5. **Acting: Across Takes**
   - *Question:* How can a performance remain coherent when scenes are shot out of order?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace continuity notes and available takes. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Acting: A Disputed Performance Credit**
   - *Question:* What evidence can establish who performed or devised a moment?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate contracts, credits, footage, and later recollection. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
7. **Acting: Performance Capture and Acting**
   - *Question:* Which actor choices are preserved or altered in a captured performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare recorded action, animation, and credited collaborators. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
8. **Acting: A Role Built with a Director**
   - *Question:* Which decisions can be attributed to actor, director, or editor?
   - *Key Focus:* Use rehearsal and production records rather than inferred intent. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
9. **Acting: A Staging Change**
   - *Question:* How did a documented production decision change a role's delivery?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated versions and contemporary accounts. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
10. **Acting: Listening on Camera**
   - *Question:* How does a reaction shot shape the scene's meaning?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare performance, framing, and cut order. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Acting: A Performance Reassessed**
   - *Question:* Why did critical response to a role change over time?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated reviews and changed viewing context. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
12. **Acting: One Role, Different Productions**
   - *Question:* Which choices change when performers restage the same text?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare performance records within their media limits. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
13. **Acting: Changing Actor Workflows**
   - *Question:* Which emerging production practice changes preparation or credit?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify contract, production type, and documented effects. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
14. **Acting: A Single Acting Method?**
   - *Question:* Does a performer use one fixed technique for every role?
   - *Key Focus:* Check training and production accounts against work. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
15. **Acting: Building a Role**
   - *Question:* How did documented rehearsal and production choices shape a performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow drafts, coaching, takes, and final edit. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Acting: What Was the Actor Asked to Do?**
   - *Question:* How did script and direction frame a role at a given stage?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, rehearsal notes, and finished work. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
17. **Acting: The Actor's Working Tools**
   - *Question:* How do costume, prop, set, and microphone affect delivery?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify actual equipment and performance constraints. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
18. **Acting: From Audition to Final Cut**
   - *Question:* What changes between casting, rehearsal, shooting, and edit?
   - *Key Focus:* Track versioned evidence and who made each choice. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
19. **Acting: Body in the Frame**
   - *Question:* How does position and movement change a character relationship?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze blocking, camera placement, and alternatives. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
20. **Acting: Making a Performance Together**
   - *Question:* How do partner, director, editor, and crew shape a role?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit documented contributions without erasing actor agency. Evidence base: recorded performances, scripts, rehearsal and continuity notes, credits, contracts where available, actor and collaborator interviews, and dated criticism.
