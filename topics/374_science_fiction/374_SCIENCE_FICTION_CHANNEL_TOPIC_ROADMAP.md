## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Science Fiction across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Science Fiction Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Science Fiction.
- **Tier 2: Science Fiction Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Science Fiction.
- **Tier 3: Science Fiction Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Science Fiction.
- **Tier 4: Science Fiction Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Science Fiction.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Science Fiction: A Speculative Convention Emerges**
   - *Question:* Which dated scientific or publishing context shaped one science-fiction device?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare original work and contemporary sources without claiming one origin. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
2. **Science Fiction: A Speculative Technology Examined**
   - *Question:* What premise does a named work actually require?
   - *Key Focus:* Read its stated mechanism and narrative consequences. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
3. **Science Fiction: Beyond the Canon**
   - *Question:* Whose speculative work was missed by a narrow genre history?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated publications, translations, and reception. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
4. **Science Fiction: The Counterfactual in Science Fiction**
   - *Question:* Which one change organizes a story's imagined world?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify premise, baseline, and consequences. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
5. **Science Fiction: A Model of Society in Fiction**
   - *Question:* What institutional effects follow from a speculative technology?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace access, power, constraints, and dissent in the text. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Science Fiction: An Unexplained Future Device**
   - *Question:* Which mechanism does the story explain and which does it leave open?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate textual fact from fan speculation. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
7. **Science Fiction: A Real Tool and Its Fictional Double**
   - *Question:* How does a fictional device depart from contemporary science?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated scientific sources with the work, not later inventions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
8. **Science Fiction: A Writer's Speculative Revision**
   - *Question:* How did a draft change the scientific premise?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts, letters, and attributed interviews. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
9. **Science Fiction: A Science-Fiction Debate in Print**
   - *Question:* Which editorial or publishing decision shifted a theme's circulation?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated magazines, books, and responses. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
10. **Science Fiction: A Premise Generates Plot**
   - *Question:* What consequence follows from one speculative assumption?
   - *Key Focus:* Map causal steps and note where the work skips them. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Science Fiction: An Old Future Revisited**
   - *Question:* What does an obsolete forecast reveal about its original context?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare period expectations with later evidence without grading prophecy. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
12. **Science Fiction: A Device Across Two Eras**
   - *Question:* How did two works imagine the same technological problem differently?
   - *Key Focus:* Match premise and compare social assumptions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
13. **Science Fiction: A Future Story as Scenario**
   - *Question:* Which present concern does a near-future narrative explore?
   - *Key Focus:* Treat story as thought experiment, not prediction. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
14. **Science Fiction: Predicted This?**
   - *Question:* What did a specific work describe before a real technology existed?
   - *Key Focus:* Date publication, define similarity, and avoid causal claims. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
15. **Science Fiction: Tracing a Speculative Idea**
   - *Question:* How did one premise move through drafts, publication, and adaptation?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow attributed, dated artifacts. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Science Fiction: The Thought Experiment's Boundary**
   - *Question:* What rule makes this imagined future distinct?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare author brief and the finished work's exceptions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
17. **Science Fiction: Research Behind a Future World**
   - *Question:* Which sources informed a fictional device or society?
   - *Key Focus:* Verify cited research and date it to composition. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
18. **Science Fiction: Revising a Speculative Premise**
   - *Question:* What changed between an outline and published version?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versioned text and editorial notes. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
19. **Science Fiction: Explaining Without Lecturing**
   - *Question:* How does a scene disclose an unfamiliar technology?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze action, dialogue, and reader knowledge. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
20. **Science Fiction: A Shared Future**
   - *Question:* Who developed scientific advice, writing, visual design, and adaptation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits and documented contributions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, dated scientific context, author drafts, publication and adaptation records, and situated criticism.
