## BANK-374-045 — The Machine Stops

- Primary text: E. M. Forster, *The Machine Stops*, in the 1928 collection *The Eternal Moment and Other Stories*, digitized by Project Gutenberg from HathiTrust images: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/72890/pg72890-images.html . The story first appeared in 1909; this inspected text is the 1928 collection edition.
- In the story, Vashti and Kuno disagree over mediated contact and direct experience while daily provision and social activity depend on the Machine. This supports analysis of how one speculative system creates character conflict.
- Limit: the 1928 collected text was inspected, not a 1909 facsimile or draft. Textual differences across editions and authorial revision require separate collation. Similarity to later communications technology is an interpretive comparison, not proof of technical prediction. Do not reproduce long passages; rights vary by jurisdiction and edition.

## BANK-374-046 — The War of the Worlds

- Primary text: H. G. Wells, *The War of the Worlds*, Project Gutenberg text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36/36-h/36-h.htm . The novel was published in 1898; this digital edition was inspected rather than a first-print facsimile.
- The narrator explicitly compares Martian violence with European destruction of Indigenous Tasmanians near the opening, making an imperial reversal available for close reading alongside later invasion scenes.
- Limit: this is a literary interpretation of a period text; the narrator's racist language and historical claim should be contextualized and checked against Indigenous-led historical sources before any factual account of Tasmanian history. The novel provides no scientific evidence about life on Mars or predictions of real invasions. Avoid long reproduction; check rights for specific editions and markets.
