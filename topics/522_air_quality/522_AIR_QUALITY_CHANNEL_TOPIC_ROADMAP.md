## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Air Quality across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Air Quality Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Air Quality.
- **Tier 2: Air Quality Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Air Quality.
- **Tier 3: Air Quality Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Air Quality.
- **Tier 4: Air Quality Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Air Quality.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Air Quality: How Air Quality Developed**
   - *Question:* Which pollution problem shaped an air-quality measure or policy?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one air-quality measure or policy through the pollution problem and monitoring methods that shaped it; name place and period. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: calibrated monitor series, averaging definitions, emissions inventories and original exposure studies. Starting framework: WHO global air quality guidelines (https://www.who.int/Publications/i/Item/9789240034228). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Air Quality: A Documented Air Quality Case**
   - *Question:* Did a clean-air intervention change emissions, concentration or actual exposure?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one documented clean-air intervention using pollutant, monitoring coverage and comparison design; separate emissions changes from exposure changes. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: calibrated monitor series, averaging definitions, emissions inventories and original exposure studies. Starting framework: WHO global air quality guidelines (https://www.who.int/Publications/i/Item/9789240034228). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
3. **Air Quality: People behind Air Quality**
   - *Question:* Whose monitoring or community-reporting work is missing from an air-quality account?
   - *Key Focus:* Document overlooked monitoring, maintenance or community reporting in one air-quality case; use attributed accounts and protect private health information. Evidence base: Use attributable contribution records and consented participant accounts. Case records: calibrated monitor series, averaging definitions, emissions inventories and original exposure studies. Starting framework: WHO global air quality guidelines (https://www.who.int/Publications/i/Item/9789240034228). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
4. **Air Quality: Understanding Air Quality**
   - *Question:* How do emissions, concentrations and exposure differ for one pollutant?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain emissions, ambient concentration and personal exposure through one pollutant and location; distinguish short peaks from long-term averages. Evidence base: Use primary definitions and a documented end-to-end example. Case records: calibrated monitor series, averaging definitions, emissions inventories and original exposure studies. Starting framework: WHO global air quality guidelines (https://www.who.int/Publications/i/Item/9789240034228). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
5. **Air Quality: Tradeoffs in Air Quality**
   - *Question:* Which monitoring and modeling choices change an exposure estimate?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare methods for estimating one pollution exposure or effect; examine monitor placement, calibration, weather and uncertainty. Evidence base: Compare original methods, assumptions, measures and uncertainty. Case records: calibrated monitor series, averaging definitions, emissions inventories and original exposure studies. Starting framework: WHO global air quality guidelines (https://www.who.int/Publications/i/Item/9789240034228). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
