# BANK-004-034 — Nataraja image and CERN reception case

**Proposal:** [What does Nataraja imagery express in its historical and devotional settings?](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-004-034)  
**State:** bounded case and source-access note, 2026-10-24. This remains a topic proposal, not a script, doctrinal verdict or production approval.

## Separate the three questions

1. What can be described on an identified historical Nataraja object?
2. How does an attributed institution interpret that object's imagery?
3. Why did CERN display a modern Nataraja statue in 2004, and what did its public statement mean by a science metaphor?

These are related but not interchangeable. The CERN installation does not establish that an older sculpture or Hindu text contained a particle-physics theory. The existing [evidence-standard proposal](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-004-064) can explain why a metaphor and an experimental claim require different tests.

## Editorial disposition

The archived local queue record asks why a Nataraja statue is at CERN, but its title and scope leap to “mathematics and physics encoded” and subatomic parallels. Keep that wording visible as historical planning material. Map its Nataraja image subject to `BANK-004-034` and its metaphor-versus-experiment problem to `BANK-004-064`. A separate BANK proposal would repeat those scopes.

**Claim candidates for later review:** CERN's 2004 gift and stated diplomatic context; the specified museum objects' cataloged period, place and attributes. A script must attribute each institutional interpretation. **Open:** inspect the CERN plaque/bulletin directly, select a named Shaiva textual or living interpretive source, check translation and image rights, and seek an informed tradition-specific review before making theological claims. No claim that the icon predicts modern physics is supported here.
