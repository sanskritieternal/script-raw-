# BANK-006-135 — Delayed-choice quantum eraser counts

[Return to the main niche bank](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-006-135).

This is a bounded source note for an experimental topic proposal, not a complete script or a claim to settle every quantum interpretation. The preserved queue asks whether the future can change the past; the experiment's reported count structure does not itself establish that conclusion.

## Inspected research

- [Kim, Yu, Kulik, Shih and Scully, “A Delayed ‘Choice’ Quantum Eraser,” *Physical Review Letters* 84 (2000), author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903047) describes entangled signal and idler photons, detector `D0` on the signal side and `D1`–`D4` on the idler side. The chosen optical path made idler registration about 8 ns later than signal registration. The reported observable curves are **joint detection rates**: `R01` and `R02` show fringes shifted by π relative to one another, while the which-path subsets `R03` and `R04` do not show interference. The authors use quantum-eraser language for these correlations.
- [R. E. Kastner, “The ‘Delayed Choice Quantum Eraser’ Neither Erases Nor Delays,” *Foundations of Physics* 49 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-019-00278-8) gives a later theoretical analysis arguing against a retrocausal reading and against taking “erasure” literally. It is an interpretation and critique of the experiment, not a replacement measurement.

## Claim boundary and next research

An episode can diagram the detector paths and explicitly compare each coincidence subset with the unsorted `D0` distribution. Oppositely phased fringes cannot simply be presented as a fringe pattern in the unsorted record. A later match of detector timestamps changes which previously recorded events are included in a plotted subset; it does not show that a past detector entry was rewritten or that usable information travelled backward in time. Before scripting, inspect the original figures and counting definitions, derive the marginal count prediction from the stated model, and compare additional experimental and theoretical analyses. Keep the observed correlations separate from claims about what a photon “really did.”
