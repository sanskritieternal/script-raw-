# Selected source and boundary note — 385 Circus History

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed case: **BANK-385-045**, a 1943 Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey poster.

## Primary object and catalog

- Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Division, [*Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus. The greatest show on earth*](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2019635304/), identifies an offset poster dated c1943, call number **POS - CIRCUS - Ring. Bros. Bar. & Bai., 1943 no. 2a**, showing a woman and elephant marching. The item record reports a 1945 gift from Ringling Bros. and says rights status has not been evaluated. The item page was search-indexed but direct page opening returned HTTP 403 during this review; treat catalog metadata as inspected through search, and inspect the object before any close visual interpretation.
- The Library’s [circus, carnival, and rodeo poster collection guide](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2005675800/) describes about 500 posters across companies, mostly 1870–1910. That collection context does not prove a specific 1943 act or itinerary.

## Editorial limits and overlap

The poster establishes a dated publicity depiction, not whether a particular woman or elephant appeared in a live procession, how animals were treated, or what audiences saw. Such claims would need programs, contracts, route books, performance records or reliable eyewitness documentation. Reproduction permission must be assessed per item; the Library does not evaluate this item’s rights. Existing **BANK-385-037** covers the broad promise of circus posters, **BANK-385-033** performer identity in publicity, and **BANK-385-039** animal-performance debates. This case stays with one named 1943 object and the evidentiary divide between advertising and performance. Adjacent performing arts archive **BANK-081-050** concerns recording preservation rather than this poster. Record-extremes framing is unsupported without a defined measure.
