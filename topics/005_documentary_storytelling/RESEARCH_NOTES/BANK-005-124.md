# BANK-005-124 — U.S. fair-use excerpt review

[Return to the main niche bank](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-005-124).

This note bounds a documentary topic proposal to U.S. law. It does not decide whether a particular clip is fair use, clear rights, or give legal advice. The preserved legacy queue title promises use “without getting sued”; that outcome cannot be promised.

## Inspected primary text

- [17 U.S.C. § 107, as published by the U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html) gives examples of purposes that may qualify and requires consideration of purpose and character, nature of the copyrighted work, amount and substantiality, and effect on the potential market in each particular case. It does not create a categorical documentary exception.
- The [U.S. Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/) explains those factors and collects judicial decisions; the page states that its index does not substitute for legal advice. An index entry is a lead to an opinion, not an automatic rule for a different clip or edit.

## Proposed episode workflow and limits

Choose one specified excerpt and identify the exact source work, owner or rights contact if known, timecodes, reason it is quoted, length and qualitative importance, relation to the documentary's argument, and every intended version or platform. Record the four questions prompted by § 107 and competing considerations; ask qualified U.S. counsel to review the actual proposed use before presenting a clearance conclusion. Separately track music, underlying works, privacy, publicity, platform and contract questions where applicable. A license review in [BANK-005-107](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-005-107) is a different workflow from evaluating a possible statutory fair use.

The cited statute and Office guide support the legal framework only. No clip, agreement, court opinion for an analogous fact pattern, release plan or distribution contract has been reviewed for this proposal. Recheck current law and relevant jurisdiction before scripting.
