# BANK-005-125 — Early NFB direct-cinema comparison

[Return to the main niche bank](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-005-125).

This is a source-access note for a proposed film-history comparison, not a completed viewing log or script. The preserved queue describes cinéma vérité as a revolution that removed narrators and scripts. The inspected NFB records show why that is too broad.

## Inspected primary collection records

- The [National Film Board of Canada's *Candid Eye* collection record](https://collection.nfb.ca/collection/candid-eye-television-series) identifies a 1958 series described as unscripted and unrehearsed. This supports the collection's characterization, not a claim that every episode had identical methods.
- An [NFB curator's account of *Candid Eye*](https://blog.nfb.ca/blog/2018/04/24/candid-eye-series/) describes portable filming and the series' development, and explicitly describes unobtrusive narration in *Blood and Fire*. That is enough to reject the universal “stripped of narrators” shorthand, but the episode itself still needs to be viewed before analyzing its narration.
- The [NFB record for *Les raquetteurs*](https://collection.nfb.ca/film/les_raquetteurs) identifies the 1958 short, its directors Gilles Groulx and Michel Brault, and its sound and editing credits. The NFB calls it a precursor of direct cinema. Its catalog description does not by itself settle how much filming was prompted, staged or selected in the edit.

## Comparison to complete before scripting

View an identified *Candid Eye* episode and *Les raquetteurs* in authorized versions. Log exact sequences, spoken narration, synchronous location sound, interview prompts, visible filmmaker participation, camera mobility, and any missing production records. Distinguish a film's visible method from later retrospective labels. The evidence here supports a narrower comparison of two NFB contexts; it does not establish a universal account of French, Canadian and U.S. movements or a single technology that caused all changes.
