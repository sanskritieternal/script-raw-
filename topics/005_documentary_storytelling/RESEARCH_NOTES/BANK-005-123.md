# BANK-005-123 — Apollo 11 archive paths for a documentary case

[Return to the main niche bank](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-005-123).

This is a bounded source-access note for a proposed documentary-craft episode, not a researched script or a production approval. The older local queue title, “The Lost Footage of Apollo 11: How NASA Cleaned and Restored the Original Moon Tapes,” conflates two different projects. Its original wording remains in `topics_status.json` for provenance.

## What the inspected primary records support

- [NASA's July 2009 restoration announcement](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-11/nasa-releases-restored-apollo-11-moonwalk-video/) says the released moonwalk video came from the best available **broadcast-format** sources, including a Sydney switching-center tape, CBS broadcast tapes and kinescopes. NASA described a three-year search for the original one-inch telemetry backup tapes as unsuccessful. This record supports a distinction between restored broadcast images and recovered original telemetry; it does not say that the missing tapes were restored.
- The [National Archives' March 2019 account of the film partnership](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/apollo-11-footage-debuts-in-new-documentary) describes preservation and digitization of previously unprocessed large-format NASA film holdings for the *Apollo 11* documentary, alongside access to mission audio. That partnership concerns archival motion-picture film and audio, not the recovery of the moonwalk telemetry backup tapes.
- The [National Archives' moving-image inventory](https://www.archives.gov/research/motion-pictures/apollo-av) identifies individual Apollo 11-related film records and gauges. For example, local identifier `255-PV-75` is listed as “Apollo 11 Activities During Countdown and Launch” on 65 mm film. This is a catalog-level example; it has not been matched shot by shot to the finished documentary.

## Editorial boundary and next research

The case should show two archive workflows side by side: NASA's 2009 search and restoration of broadcast copies, and the National Archives' later scanning of surviving film holdings for a documentary. Use “lost footage” only with a named medium and custody path. The records above do not establish that a particular shot in the 2019 film came from a particular reel, or that every mission audio segment used in the film has the same provenance. Before writing those claims, inspect the film itself, production credits, shot/asset logs where available, and the referenced archive item. Do not imply that large-format film depicts the lunar surface television feed.
