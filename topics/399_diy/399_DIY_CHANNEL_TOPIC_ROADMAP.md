## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of DIY across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: DIY Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in DIY.
- **Tier 2: DIY Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of DIY.
- **Tier 3: DIY Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in DIY.
- **Tier 4: DIY Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for DIY.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of DIY: A Home-Making Practice in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated tool, material, or housing change made one DIY task common?
   - *Key Focus:* Use catalogs, manuals, and local building history. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
2. **DIY: A DIY Project after Use**
   - *Question:* Did a celebrated project solve its stated household problem?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, measured result, cost, and maintenance. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
3. **DIY: Who Helped Make It?**
   - *Question:* Whose trade knowledge, help, or inspection is omitted from a solo-build story?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits, invoices, and participant accounts. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
4. **DIY: Define the Project Boundary**
   - *Question:* Which parts of a proposed repair are within a novice's competence?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify task, materials, hazards, and when licensed work applies. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
5. **DIY: Repair or Replace?**
   - *Question:* How do service life, cost, safety, and material impact change the choice?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented options and local requirements. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **DIY: Why Did a Repair Fail?**
   - *Question:* Which measurement or inspection can distinguish possible causes?
   - *Key Focus:* Use product and site records; do not diagnose a structure remotely. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
7. **DIY: A New Home Tool**
   - *Question:* What documented task does the tool make easier or more precise?

8. **DIY: A Maker's Revision**
   - *Question:* What evidence explains a changed design or material?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated sketches and build notes. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
9. **DIY: A Code or Product Change**
   - *Question:* What changed for a particular home project after a dated rule or recall?
   - *Key Focus:* Check jurisdiction and official notice. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
10. **DIY: From Need to Finished Repair**
   - *Question:* What planning, assessment, build, and inspection stages are documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Map accountable steps without substituting for qualified instruction. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **DIY: A DIY Method Reconsidered**
   - *Question:* Which failures or new evidence caused a common technique to decline?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated manuals and incident records. Evidence base: dated plans and receipts, product data, manufacturer instructions, CPSC safety guidance, current local permit information, inspections, and consented household feedback.
12. **DIY: A Repair after One Year**
   - *Question:* What wore, moved, leaked, or stayed functional?
