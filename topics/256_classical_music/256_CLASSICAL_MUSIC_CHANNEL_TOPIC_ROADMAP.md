## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Classical Music across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Classical Music Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Classical Music.
- **Tier 2: Classical Music Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Classical Music.
- **Tier 3: Classical Music Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Classical Music.
- **Tier 4: Classical Music Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Classical Music.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Classical Music: How a Repertoire Formed**
   - *Question:* What sources document the emergence of one classical music tradition?
   - *Key Focus:* Define region, institutions, genres, and dated evidence. Evidence base: dated scores and editions where applicable, oral lineage records, recordings, programs, contracts, and attributed artist and audience accounts.
2. **Classical Music: A Canonical Work Reheard**
   - *Question:* Why did one work enter a canon and what is omitted?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare score or oral source, performances, and institutions. Evidence base: dated scores and editions where applicable, oral lineage records, recordings, programs, contracts, and attributed artist and audience accounts.
3. **Classical Music: Labor Behind a Concert**
   - *Question:* Which teachers, accompanists, copyists, and technicians are uncredited?
   - *Key Focus:* Use programs, contracts, and consented accounts. Evidence base: dated scores and editions where applicable, oral lineage records, recordings, programs, contracts, and attributed artist and audience accounts.
4. **Classical Music: Listening to One Form**
   - *Question:* What pattern helps a newcomer follow a performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Use tradition-specific terms and a named recorded example. Evidence base: dated scores and editions where applicable, oral lineage records, recordings, programs, contracts, and attributed artist and audience accounts.
5. **Classical Music: Variation Within a Framework**
   - *Question:* What choices can performers make within one form?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare multiple performances with practitioner explanations. Evidence base: dated scores and editions where applicable, oral lineage records, recordings, programs, contracts, and attributed artist and audience accounts.

6. **Classical Music: A Disputed Attribution**
