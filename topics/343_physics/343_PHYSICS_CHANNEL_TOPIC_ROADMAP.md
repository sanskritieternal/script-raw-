## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Physics across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Physics Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Physics.
- **Tier 2: Physics Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Physics.
- **Tier 3: Physics Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Physics.
- **Tier 4: Physics Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Physics.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Physics: A Physical Idea Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which observation and experiment led to a named physical model?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated papers and distinguish proposal from later confirmation. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
2. **Physics: A Landmark Experiment Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What did its apparatus actually measure?
   - *Key Focus:* Read original method, uncertainty, and later replication. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
3. **Physics: People Behind a Measurement**
   - *Question:* Who built instruments, calibrated them, and analyzed records?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit technicians and collaborators from source records. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
4. **Physics: From Quantity to Physical Claim**
   - *Question:* How do measurement, units, and a model answer one question?
   - *Key Focus:* Use a bounded experiment and explain uncertainty. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
5. **Physics: When a Model Breaks Down**
   - *Question:* Which conditions exceed a theory's tested range?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare predictions with independent measurements. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Physics: An Anomalous Result**
   - *Question:* Which error, background, or new effect could explain it?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate reported data from speculative interpretation. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
7. **Physics: An Instrument Changes Physics**
   - *Question:* What newly measurable quantity did it reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare sensitivity, calibration, and scientific result. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
8. **Physics: A Physics Dispute in Evidence**
   - *Question:* How did researchers test competing explanations?
   - *Key Focus:* Read proposals, critiques, and experiments. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
9. **Physics: A Confirmed Physical Result**
   - *Question:* What sequence led from first report to independent confirmation?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a dated experimental timeline. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
10. **Physics: A Physical Mechanism**
   - *Question:* How does one model connect cause and observed effect?
   - *Key Focus:* State assumptions, variables, equations, and limits. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Physics: A Theory's Changing Standing**
   - *Question:* Which observations strengthened or constrained it?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace evidence without calling a model simply true or false. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
12. **Physics: One Quantity Measured Two Ways**
   - *Question:* How did precision and uncertainty change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use the same measurand and comparable units. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
13. **Physics: A Proposed Experiment**
   - *Question:* What result would distinguish competing predictions?
   - *Key Focus:* Define sensitivity, controls, and feasible measurement. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
14. **Physics: A Physics Claim in Headlines**
   - *Question:* Does the experiment support the broad interpretation?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare result, confidence, and scope. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
15. **Physics: From Apparatus to Paper**
   - *Question:* What work produced one published measurement?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow design, calibration, data, analysis, and review. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Physics: A Signal Becomes a Theory Test**
   - *Question:* Which inference links raw detector output to a physical claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace calibration, background, and model. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
17. **Physics: How a Quantity Is Measured**
   - *Question:* What instrument and unit define a reported value?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify measurand, traceability, and uncertainty budget. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
18. **Physics: Across Scales**
   - *Question:* When does a useful approximation stop working?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare length, energy, and time ranges. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
19. **Physics: Controlled and Natural Conditions**
   - *Question:* Does a lab-tested effect appear in an uncontrolled setting?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare environment and confounders. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
20. **Physics: Repeat Measurements**
   - *Question:* How much variation comes from noise or changing conditions?
   - *Key Focus:* Use repeated trials and statistical reporting. Evidence base: original papers, experiment descriptions, calibration and uncertainty budgets, NIST metrology guidance, and independent replications.
