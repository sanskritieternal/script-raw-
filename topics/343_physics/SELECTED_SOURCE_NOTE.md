## GW150914 / LIGO ? BANK-343-043
The LIGO collaboration?s detection paper states that the two observatories observed a transient on 2015-09-14; its matched-filter analysis reported a false-alarm rate below one event per 203,000 years and significance above 5.1 sigma. The paper is the primary claim, published in 2016. Detector calibration and transient-noise companion records are listed by the collaboration. This supports a measurement-to-inference question. It does not mean every inference is model-free, that one event tests all of general relativity, or that the inferred source was imaged.

Primary records: https://dcc.ligo.org/P150914/public ; https://ligo.org/detections/gw150914/ . The significance refers to this search and analysis, not a general probability that Einstein?s theory is true. Astronomy source-population and counterpart questions belong in neighboring banks.

## 2012 Higgs discovery / CERN ? BANK-343-044
CERN explains that 2012 ATLAS and CMS analyses looked for statistical excesses in reconstructed invariant mass and announced a new particle consistent with the Higgs boson on 2012-07-04. This supports a case about event statistics, backgrounds and discovery thresholds. The 2012 statement should not be retroactively described as having measured all later properties of the particle.

Primary collaboration papers: https://cds.cern.ch/record/1471031/files/plb-716-1.pdf (ATLAS) ; https://cds.cern.ch/record/1471016 (CMS). Institutional explanation: https://home.cern/science/physics/higgs-boson/how/ . The later 2012 papers include analysis beyond the July 4 announcements; distinguish announcement-era and publication-era numerical claims.
