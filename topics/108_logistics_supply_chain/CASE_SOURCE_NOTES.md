# Selected logistics case-source notes

Inspected 2026-10-24. These notes support bounded questions, not production-ready scripts. Proposed topics retain empty `verified_sources` until each episode's full source review.

## Red Sea route choices — BANK-108-054

- The [International Maritime Organization Red Sea incident page](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/red-sea.aspx) reports 57 incidents notified to and confirmed by IMO from 10 January 2024 through 31 December 2025; its live page reported 61 through the inspection date. It identifies the reporting mandate and its own confirmation boundary.
- The [IMO Maritime Safety Committee resolution announcement](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/pressbriefings/pages/imo-msc-resolution-red-sea.aspx) describes attacks as a threat to navigation and a disruption to trade. These records establish the security premise, not a precise delay or cost for every vessel. A script must pair dated carrier routing and trade-flow records with these incidents before attributing a network-wide change.

## Panama Canal drought limits — BANK-108-055

- The [Panama Canal Authority FY2024 results](https://pancanal.com/en/the-canals-fy-2024-financial-results-reaffirm-its-focus-on-sustainability-and-vision-for-the-future/) record 9,944 deep-draft transits, 21% below FY2023, and attribute the decline to temporary water-saving measures during drought conditions. The [authority's January 2024 slot notice](https://pancanal.com/en/panama-canal-to-increase-daily-transits-to-24-starting-in-january/) documents the dated transit limit and subsequent planned increase.
- The archive's "$4 million" queue-skipping claim is not established by these aggregate records. Any auction-price example needs its specific transaction, date and distinction between bid, fees and total voyage cost. FY2024 figures do not describe the current canal operating regime.

## Dali and Key Bridge — BANK-108-058

- The [NTSB final investigation page](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA24MM031.aspx/) and [final report MIR-25-40](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2540.pdf) concern the 26 March 2024 contact. The Board found a loose signal-wire connection from improper wire-label banding caused a blackout and loss of propulsion and steering near the bridge. It also identified bridge vulnerability and ineffective immediate warning to highway workers as contributors to collapse and loss of life. Six highway workers died.
- The investigation establishes an accident sequence and safety findings. It does not itself prove a single port-wide economic loss or that the ship's size alone caused the collapse. Any port-flow analysis needs separately dated port and transport records.
