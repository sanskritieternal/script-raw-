## BnF Curie notebooks

The Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF), which holds the Curie papers, describes contaminated laboratory notebooks in [“Un héritage qui rayonne. Les carnets de Marie Curie”](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/un-heritage-qui-rayonne-les-carnets-de-marie-curie) (2026). According to the holding institution, contamination was addressed during the proposed 1967 donation with measurements, decontamination and microfilming; the collection entered the national library in 1970. Particularly contaminated sheets were encapsulated, some covers protected, and use of originals was limited. BnF reports review campaigns in 1996 and from 2020 onward, with protection updated afterward. The Musée Curie's [1898 notebook record](https://curiotheque.musee.curie.fr/s/fr/item/20029) is a collection-level lead for identifying a specific object before any illustration or quotation.

**Boundaries:** The BnF article supports a question about its own Curie paper collection and preservation decisions. It does not establish that all Marie Curie belongings remain radioactive, that every item sits in a lead box, a universal access rule, a measured radiation dose, or a causal claim about Curie's illness. Describe protective practices in the publication's time and confirm current access directly with BnF before filming. Do not provide informal handling advice. Images and manuscript quotations need separate rights and provenance review.
