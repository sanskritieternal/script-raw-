### Growth, wood and seasonal change — foundation

Subcategory `CATALOG-202-R01` · foundation.

<a id="bank-202-010"></a>
#### BANK-202-010 — The Hydraulic Ceiling: Why Trees Cannot Grow Taller Than 426 Feet

**Video question:** What fundamental physical and biological limits prevent even the mightiest giant redwoods from growing 500 feet into the air?

**Individual coverage scope:** Xylem water tension pulling against gravity, embolism and cavitation bubble risks at negative 2.0 MPa pressure, leaf turgor pressure drop, and stomatal shutdown at the tree crown.

<a id="bank-202-011"></a>
#### BANK-202-011 — Wood Anatomy: The Vascular Architecture of Xylem Vessels, Tracheids, and Heartwood

**Video question:** How does living vascular sapwood transform into dead, rock-hard heartwood packed with antimicrobial tannins to support massive trunks?

**Individual coverage scope:** Lignified cellulose cell walls, wide vessel elements in hardwoods vs narrow safety-valved tracheids in conifers, vascular rays transporting nutrients horizontally, and tyloses plugging old pipes.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-202-012"></a>
#### BANK-202-012 — The Bark Armor: Cork Cambium, Suberin Wax, and Defending Against Insect Borers

**Video question:** How does a tree continuously expand its waterproof, fire-resistant outer armor without splitting its own living tissues?

**Individual coverage scope:** Phellogen (cork cambium) producing dead phellem cells saturated with suberin wax, lenticels allowing gas exchange through bark, resin canals pitching out bark beetles, and rhytidome shedding.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-202-013"></a>
#### BANK-202-013 — Dendrochronology: Reading Droughts, Volcanic Eruptions, and Sunspots in Rings

**Video question:** How do climate scientists match overlapping tree ring patterns across thousands of years to reconstruct Earth's temperature history to the exact year?

**Individual coverage scope:** A.E. Douglass and cross-dating principles, earlywood spring growth vs dense latewood autumn bands, frost rings matching distant supervolcanoes (e.g. 1815 Tambora), and carbon-14 spikes.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-202-014"></a>
#### BANK-202-014 — Gymnosperms vs Angiosperms: Hardwoods vs Softwoods, Deciduous Abscission, and Evergreens

**Video question:** Why is balsa wood biologically a 'hardwood' while dense Douglas fir is a 'softwood', and why do broadleaf trees drop their leaves?

**Individual coverage scope:** Angiosperm vessel elements defining hardwoods vs gymnosperm naked tracheids defining softwoods, deciduous leaf abscission layer formation to prevent winter water loss, and evergreen needle waxy cones.
