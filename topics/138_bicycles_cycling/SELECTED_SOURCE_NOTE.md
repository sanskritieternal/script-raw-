Checked 2026-10-24. This bounds proposed BANK-138-045 and does not approve a script or validate inherited technical and health claims.

## Unit Pack Power battery warning

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's [April 15, 2024 product safety warning](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Stop-Using-Unit-Pack-Power-UPP-E-bike-Batteries-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-and-Death) identifies Unit Pack Power conversion batteries with model numbers **U004** and **U004-1**, reports 13 U.S. overheating incidents including seven fires with substantial property damage, states that the batteries had not been accredited-laboratory certified to the applicable UL standard, and records the manufacturer's refusal to conduct an acceptable recall. This is a CPSC warning about identified products, not a recall of all UPP products or all e-bikes.

For context, the FDNY's [lithium-ion battery safety page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/fdny/codes/reference/lithium-ion-battery-safety.page) reports 268 New York City lithium-ion-battery fires in 2023, many involving e-bikes. Its [2024 report](https://www.nyc.gov/site/fdny/news/03-25/fdny-commissioner-robert-s-tucker-significant-progress-the-battle-against-lithium-ion) reports 277 lithium-ion-battery fires in 2024, including non-structural fires. Those city totals are **not** counts of UPP model fires or a risk rate per battery sold. The two sources use different case definitions and geographic scopes.

**Limits:** Do not infer that price caused the identified failures, that any specific UPP incident burned an apartment, or that all lower-cost e-bikes are unsafe. The warning establishes reported incidents and agency findings at publication time; the incident count, product status and local rules can change. A script should date every statistic and, if describing an individual fire, use its fire-investigation report. Avoid amateur battery handling or firefighting instructions; direct consumer action to current agency notices.
