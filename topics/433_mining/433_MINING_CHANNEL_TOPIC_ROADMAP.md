## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Mining across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Mining Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Mining.
- **Tier 2: Mining Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Mining.
- **Tier 3: Mining Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Mining.
- **Tier 4: Mining Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Mining.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Mining: A Mine Opens**
   - *Question:* Which deposit, demand, and land decisions led to one mine?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated maps, permits, and community records. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
2. **Mining: A Mine beyond Its Output**
   - *Question:* What do production, safety, and restoration records show?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify mine, commodity, period, and ownership. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
3. **Mining: Workers and Neighbors**
   - *Question:* Whose labor or land experience is absent from a mine's story?
   - *Key Focus:* Use attributed accounts and public records. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
4. **Mining: From Deposit to Product**
   - *Question:* How are resource, ore, waste, and saleable product distinguished?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one commodity and facility. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
5. **Mining: Ore Grade and Recovery**
   - *Question:* How do grade, processing losses, and price shape a mine plan?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated reserve and process assumptions. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Mining: Unexpected Water Quality**
   - *Question:* Which samples could distinguish mine drainage from natural background?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare upstream, downstream, and seasonal data. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
7. **Mining: A Mining Method Change**
   - *Question:* Did a new method improve recovery, safety, or disturbance?
   - *Key Focus:* Use matched production and monitoring data. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
8. **Mining: A Mining Decision**
   - *Question:* Who approved expansion and who contested it?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace hearings, technical reports, and consent claims. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
9. **Mining: A Mine Incident**
   - *Question:* What did an official investigation establish and remedy?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate finding from initial report. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
10. **Mining: Ore through a Mine**
   - *Question:* How do extraction, hauling, processing, waste, and water management connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one site flow diagram and measured balance. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Mining: A Mine Closes**
   - *Question:* Which depletion, cost, policy, or hazard factors drove closure?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated production and closure documents. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
12. **Mining: Mine Site after Closure**
   - *Question:* What changed in water, land, work, and access?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare baseline, closure targets, and monitoring. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
13. **Mining: A Proposed Mine**
   - *Question:* What evidence would test promised output and impacts?
   - *Key Focus:* Treat feasibility and approval as separate stages. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
14. **Mining: Reserve versus Resource**
   - *Question:* Does a published mineral estimate mean recoverable production?
   - *Key Focus:* Read classification, date, and economic assumptions. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
15. **Mining: One Deposit's Decision Trail**
   - *Question:* How did exploration become extraction or rejection?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a dated permit and technical record. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Mining: Mine versus Supply Chain**
   - *Question:* Which effects belong to extraction, processing, transport, and use?
   - *Key Focus:* State commodity, place, and lifecycle unit. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
17. **Mining: Ore, Water, and Waste**
   - *Question:* Where does each stream go in one operation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use measured mass and water balances. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
18. **Mining: Recovery and Disturbance**
   - *Question:* What did a chosen mining method gain and cost?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare feasible alternatives under one deposit. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
19. **Mining: A Documented Mine Failure**
   - *Question:* Which physical and management factors were established?
   - *Key Focus:* Use regulator investigation without operational instructions. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
20. **Mining: Core Samples versus Production**
   - *Question:* Where did sampled grade fail to predict recovered output?
   - *Key Focus:* Check sampling, dilution, and processing data. Evidence base: dated mine and permit records, assays and production balances, MSHA investigations, USGS environmental studies, water monitoring, and community accounts.
