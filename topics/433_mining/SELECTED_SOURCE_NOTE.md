# Selected source and boundary note — 433 Mining

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-433-045** follows post-mining water management at the Berkeley Pit, Butte, Montana.

## Primary environmental-agency records

- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, [Butte current site status](https://www.epa.gov/butte/current-site-status), identifies the Berkeley Pit and flooded underground workings as the **Butte Mine Flooding OU3**. EPA describes monitored low-pH, high-salinity pit water, Horseshoe Bend Water Treatment Plant operations and a polishing/discharge system initiated in 2019. The page is a current-status snapshot; treatment capacity and water depth are dated agency statements, not permanent conditions.
- EPA, [2021 fifth five-year review](https://semspub.epa.gov/work/08/100010834.pdf), records the early pit-pumping and treatment pilot and reports that October 2019–December 2020 pit water level remained essentially unchanged (a **0.04-foot rise**). This narrow interval cannot prove long-term containment. The [2002 consent decree](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/buttefloodarcoCD1.pdf) gives legal remedy obligations; do not treat it as proof those obligations were fully achieved.

## Limits, access and overlap

The EPA record supports one U.S. Superfund remedy and evolving controls, not a universal mine-closure method or a health-risk calculation for residents. Water sampling, permit compliance, bird/wildlife and groundwater effects require dated monitoring records and qualified interpretation. Do not offer site access, water handling or treatment instructions; verify current restrictions with EPA/state authorities. Credit third-party imagery and assess reuse separately. **BANK-433-041** covers the general after-extraction mine-water obligation; this case isolates Berkeley Pit's agency record. **BANK-356-042** is groundwater storage/transmission geology; **BANK-432-040** is equipment energy logistics, separate from mine-water management.
