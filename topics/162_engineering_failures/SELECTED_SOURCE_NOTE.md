# Selected source note - niche 162

Checked 2026-10-24. These agency findings and incident records support bounded proposed investigations only. Each source has a date, jurisdiction and evidentiary scope; no topic has individual production approval.

## BANK-162-045 - Flight 1282 door plug

The US NTSB [Flight 1282 investigation page](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA24MA063.aspx) and [aviation investigation report AIR-25-04](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AIR2504.pdf) concern the January 5, 2024 Boeing 737-9 MAX door-plug separation. The final report is the controlling source for its findings on hardware, factory work, inspection and oversight. The archive's "paperwork void" is shorthand, not a finding to quote without the report's exact record analysis. Do not generalize this aircraft's sequence to every 737.

## BANK-162-046 - Titan

The US Coast Guard Marine Board's [August 5, 2025 report release](https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4265651/coast-guard-marine-board-of-investigation-releases-report-on-titan-submersible/) links its investigation report and summarizes multiple contributing factors and recommendations following the June 2023 implosion. Use the full report for precise hull, inspection and oversight claims. The release does not support "carbon fiber defied physics" as a single-cause explanation; the Coast Guard's conclusions concern this vessel and its operating history.

## BANK-162-047 - Champlain Towers South

NIST's [June 22, 2026 technical findings](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/06/nist-releases-technical-findings-what-caused-2021-partial-collapse) locate early failure at two pool-deck slab-to-column connections and identify original design/construction deviations, added load and degradation. These are technical investigation findings for the June 24, 2021 Florida collapse, not a generic diagnosis of all concrete towers. Follow the underlying report for detailed strength and chronology; avoid reducing the case to corrosion alone.

## BANK-162-048 - CrowdStrike

CrowdStrike's [July 24, 2024 preliminary incident review](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/falcon-content-update-preliminary-post-incident-report/) reports a problematic Rapid Response Content configuration in Channel File 291, an out-of-bounds memory read, and a July 19 deployment window. Its [August 2024 root-cause executive summary](https://www.crowdstrike.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Executive-Summary_Root-Cause-Analysis_Channel-File-291.pdf) is a first-party explanation, not an independent audit. Compare external service-impact records before attributing every outage. The archived "4-byte kernel null pointer" phrase should not be repeated without a primary technical basis.

## BANK-162-049 - Vineyard Wind blade

The US Bureau of Ocean Energy Management's [Vineyard Wind 1 project record](https://www.boem.gov/renewable-energy/state-activities/vineyard-wind-1) records the July 13, 2024 AW-38 blade failure, debris and subsequent regulatory and replacement steps. It says the manufacturer/installer root-cause analysis found a manufacturing deviation; independently verify the analysis details before stating an exact defect. BOEM's page does not establish wave-caused delamination or the archived $100 million cost.
