## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Metro Systems across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Metro Systems Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Metro Systems.
- **Tier 2: Metro Systems Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Metro Systems.
- **Tier 3: Metro Systems Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Metro Systems.
- **Tier 4: Metro Systems Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Metro Systems.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Metro Systems: A Metro Line's Purpose**
   - *Question:* Which travel demand and land-use assumptions justified one line?
   - *Key Focus:* Read original plan and later ridership. Evidence base: authority plans and timetables, observed headways and ridership, station access audits, maintenance records, safety-oversight findings, and community testimony.
2. **Metro Systems: A Metro System in Context**
   - *Question:* What does one widely cited network demonstrate?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare service, funding, and city form. Evidence base: authority plans and timetables, observed headways and ridership, station access audits, maintenance records, safety-oversight findings, and community testimony.
3. **Metro Systems: Workers and Displaced Residents**
   - *Question:* Who built the line and who lost access or land?
   - *Key Focus:* Use construction, compensation, and community records. Evidence base: authority plans and timetables, observed headways and ridership, station access audits, maintenance records, safety-oversight findings, and community testimony.
4. **Metro Systems: How a Metro Moves People**
   - *Question:* How do train frequency, dwell time, and transfer design set capacity?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one line and time period. Evidence base: authority plans and timetables, observed headways and ridership, station access audits, maintenance records, safety-oversight findings, and community testimony.
5. **Metro Systems: Reliability at High Frequency**
   - *Question:* How do signals and dwell variability propagate delays?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare actual headways and operating logs. Evidence base: authority plans and timetables, observed headways and ridership, station access audits, maintenance records, safety-oversight findings, and community testimony.
