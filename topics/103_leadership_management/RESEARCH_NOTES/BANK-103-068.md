# BANK-103-068 — open-office interaction study

Checked 2026-10-24. Selected research premise only; not an office-design or employment recommendation.

The original [Bernstein and Turban 2018 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6030579/) reports two before-and-after field studies at anonymous corporate headquarters transitioning from cubicles to open layouts. Participants wore interaction badges; the researchers also analyzed organizational email and, in the first study, instant-message records. The first study had 52 participating workers and two 15-workday measurement windows; the second followed 100 workers for two eight-week windows. The paper reports about 70% less measured face-to-face interaction in both settings, alongside increased electronic communication. The archived headline's “destroy productivity” is **not** the paper's measured primary endpoint. A confidential management review described a qualitative productivity decline at the first company, but the underlying employer data were not published.

The studies' companies were anonymized, participation was partial, redesign was not randomly assigned, and the underlying interaction data are not openly released. These findings challenge a simple more-open-means-more-interaction premise in two settings; they do not settle every layout, job type, accessibility need or productivity outcome. A script should inspect the paper's tables and measure definitions and compare later studies before using percentages. The bank keeps `verified_sources: []` because one research note is not claim-by-claim production review.
