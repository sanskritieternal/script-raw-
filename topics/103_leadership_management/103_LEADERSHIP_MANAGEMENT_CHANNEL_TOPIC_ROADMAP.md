## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Leadership & Management across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Leadership Models, 1-on-1s & Delegation Frameworks (Scratch / Beginner)** — Situational Leadership (Hersey-Blanchard), effective 1-on-1 meeting agendas (listening vs. status updates), Andy Grove's High Output Management, and task-relevant maturity delegation.
- **Tier 2: Psychological Safety, Team Dynamics & Radical Candor (Intermediate)** — Amy Edmondson's Psychological Safety research (Google's Project Aristotle), Kim Scott's Radical Candor (Caring Personally while Challenging Directly), and Tuckman's stages (Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing).
- **Tier 3: OKRs, Strategy Execution & High-Stakes Decision Making (Upper Intermediate)** — John Doerr's Objectives and Key Results (OKRs), Jeff Bezos's Type 1 vs. Type 2 reversible decisions, Porter's Five Forces, and avoiding the HIPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion) trap.
- **Tier 4: Organizational Scaling, Culture Architecture & Crisis Turnarounds (Advanced / Expert)** — Peter Drucker's 'Culture eats strategy for breakfast', Ben Horowitz's Peacetime vs. Wartime CEO doctrine, managing organizational debt, and leading systemic corporate transformations.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Leadership Models & Delegation Frameworks
1. **Psychological Safety: What Google's Project Aristotle Discovered About the World's Best Teams**
   - *Question:* Why did Google spend millions studying 180 teams only to find that team member intelligence, experience, and personalities mattered far less than psychological safety?
   - *Key Focus:* Google's multi-year Project Aristotle, Dr. Amy Edmondson's Harvard research defining psychological safety (the shared belief that team members will not be humiliated or punished for speaking up with ideas, questions, or mistakes), conversational turn-taking equality, and average social sensitivity scores.
2. **Situational Leadership: Directing, Coaching, Supporting, and Delegating**
   - *Question:* Why does micromanaging an expert cause them to quit, while hands-off delegating to an enthusiastic beginner leads to disaster? The Hersey-Blanchard model.
   - *Key Focus:* Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard's Situational Leadership matrix matching leader behavior (directive vs. supportive) to follower development levels (D1 Enthusiastic Beginner to D4 Self-Reliant Achiever), avoiding the one-size-fits-all management trap, and diagnosing competence vs. commitment.
3. **The Art of the 1-on-1 Meeting: Andy Grove's High Output Management Blueprint**
   - *Question:* Why did legendary Intel CEO Andy Grove say that spending 90 minutes in a 1-on-1 with an employee can enhance their output for 2 weeks (200 hours of leverage)?
   - *Key Focus:* The 1-on-1 as the employee's meeting (not the manager's status update), psychological listening, asking five 'Whys', uncovering subterranean organizational problems, discussing career progression and emotional energy, and maintaining an un-cancelable sacred cadence.
4. **Task-Relevant Maturity (TRM): How to Delegate Without Abdicating**
   - *Question:* How do you delegate critical multi-million-dollar tasks to team members without either breathing down their necks or completely abandoning them to drown?
   - *Key Focus:* Defining Task-Relevant Maturity (a senior VP can have low TRM for a brand-new task), matching monitoring frequency to TRM, sampling output randomly without creating distrust, clear handoff contracts, and avoiding the disaster of executive abdication.
5. **The Peter Principle: Why Companies Keep Promoting Incompetent People to Management**
   - *Question:* Why are the best software engineers and star salespeople so often promoted into terrible, miserable managers who destroy team morale?
   - *Key Focus:* Dr. Laurence J. Peter's 1969 principle ('in a hierarchy, every employee tends to rise to their level of incompetence'), the fallacy that technical individual contributor skills translate to people management, and modern dual-track career ladders (Staff/Principal Engineer tracks parallel to VP tracks).
### Tier 2: Psychological Safety & Team Dynamics
6. **Tuckman's Stages of Team Development: Forming, Storming, Norming, and Performing**
   - *Question:* Why is the painful 'Storming' phase of interpersonal conflict a non-negotiable biological necessity before any team can achieve elite performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Bruce Tuckman's 1965 group development model: Forming (polite artificial harmony), Storming (inevitable conflict over leadership, boundaries, and priorities), Norming (establishing shared rules and mutual trust), and Performing (autonomous high-speed execution); and how managers guide teams through the fire.
7. **Radical Candor in Leadership: How to Praise and Criticize with Love and Ferocity**
   - *Question:* Why is vague praise ('Great job!') useless, and how do top leaders deliver sharp, immediate negative criticism that makes employees respect and thank them?
   - *Key Focus:* Praise in public and criticize in private, delivering feedback within 24 hours (not saving it for annual reviews), Context-Observation-Impact feedback models, separating identity from performance, and encouraging teams to criticize the leader upward.
8. **Servant Leadership: Robert Greenleaf and The Leader Who Sweeps the Floor**
   - *Question:* Why did AT&T executive Robert Greenleaf flip the corporate org chart upside down, putting the CEO at the very bottom serving the frontline workers?
   - *Key Focus:* The 1970 essay 'The Servant as Leader', moving from command-and-control authoritarianism to servant stewardship, the manager's primary job as clearing roadblocks and securing resources for the team, and Simon Sinek's 'Leaders Eat Last' neurobiology of trust.
9. **Managing the Brilliant Jerk: The High-Performing Toxic Employee Dilemma**
   - *Question:* If your top engineer writes 50% of the code but bullies colleagues and terrorizes junior staff, why must you fire them immediately to save the company?
   - *Key Focus:* Reed Hastings' famous Netflix Culture memo ('Do not tolerate brilliant jerks; the cost to effective teamwork is too high'), measuring negative externalities on overall team retention, toxic culture erosion, and how firing a high-performing bully invariably increases overall team output.
10. **Meeting Bankruptcy: How Shopify, Asana, and Amazon Eliminated Meeting Bloat**
   - *Question:* How did Shopify delete 12,000 recurring meetings in a single day, and why does Amazon start every executive meeting with 30 minutes of total silence?
   - *Key Focus:* The economic cost of meetings (8 people in a 1-hour meeting = 8 person-hours of engineering time), Amazon's 6-page narrative memo read in silence before discussion, No-Meeting Wednesdays, asynchronous written updates, and calculating meeting financial cost in real-time.
### Tier 3: OKRs, Strategy & Decision Making
11. **OKRs: John Doerr, Andy Grove, and How Google Scaled from 40 to 100,000 People**
   - *Question:* How did a venture capitalist give Larry Page and Sergey Brin a simple goal-setting framework in 1999 that guided Google's meteoric multi-billion-dollar expansion?
   - *Key Focus:* John Doerr's 'Measure What Matters', Objectives (inspirational qualitative direction) matched with Key Results (aggressive, measurable, quantifiable metrics), committed vs. aspirational 'stretch' OKRs (aiming for 70% success), avoiding conflating OKRs with annual compensation, and transparent company-wide goal alignment.
12. **Type 1 vs. Type 2 Decisions: Jeff Bezos on Speed, Reversibility, and The Two-Way Door**
   - *Question:* Why do large corporations slow to a crawl because they treat every minor reversible decision with the agonizing bureaucracy of an irreversible life-or-death choice?
   - *Key Focus:* Amazon's 2015 Shareholder Letter, One-Way Doors (Type 1: irreversible, high-consequence decisions requiring deep analytical deliberation) vs. Two-Way Doors (Type 2: reversible decisions that should be made quickly with 70% information by empowered individuals), 'Disagree and Commit' culture, and decision velocity.
13. **Porter's Five Forces: The Classic Strategy Framework for Dominating Competitive Markets**
   - *Question:* Why do airlines make almost zero profit despite generating hundreds of billions, while credit card networks print cash? Michael Porter's 5 forces.
   - *Key Focus:* Harvard Professor Michael Porter's seminal 1979 framework: 1) Threat of new entrants, 2) Bargaining power of suppliers, 3) Bargaining power of buyers, 4) Threat of substitutes, 5) Industry rivalry; economic moats, and finding structurally profitable industry niches.
14. **Avoiding the HIPPO Trap: Highest Paid Person's Opinion vs. Data-Driven Experimentation**
   - *Question:* Why does a boardroom discussion where the CEO states an intuitive opinion instantly kill scientific experimentation and lead to catastrophic multi-million-dollar product flops?
   - *Key Focus:* The HIPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion) syndrome, Avinash Kaushik's analytics principles, establishing A/B testing culture (letting real consumer behavior override executive hunches), pre-mortem risk audits (Gary Klein), and creating environments where junior data scientists can comfortably prove the CEO wrong.
15. **The First 90 Days: Michael Watkins' Executive Transition Playbook**
   - *Question:* Why do 40% of newly hired leaders fail within their first 18 months, and what is the exact step-by-step roadmap for surviving your first 90 days in a new leadership role?
   - *Key Focus:* Michael Watkins' STARS model (Start-up, Turnaround, Accelerated Growth, Realignment, Sustaining Success), avoiding the action trap (listening and learning before restructuring), securing early cultural wins, building political coalitions, and negotiating expectations with your own boss.
### Tier 4: Scaling, Culture Architecture & Crisis
16. **Peter Drucker: 'Culture Eats Strategy for Breakfast' and The Power of Shared Values**
   - *Question:* How can a company with a brilliant multi-billion-dollar strategic plan collapse into ruin if its underlying organizational culture is rotten and toxic?
   - *Key Focus:* Peter Drucker's legendary management philosophy, defining culture (what people do when the CEO leaves the room), Southwest Airlines vs. legacy air carriers, Enron's paper code of ethics vs. real ruthless culture, and aligning incentives with genuine corporate behavior.
17. **Peacetime CEO vs. Wartime CEO: Ben Horowitz on Leading Through Existential Catastrophe**
   - *Question:* Why does the consensus-building, empowering leadership style that works brilliantly during economic booms fail completely when the company is running out of cash?
   - *Key Focus:* Ben Horowitz's 'The Hard Thing About Hard Things', Peacetime CEO (expanding markets, encouraging creativity, broad delegation) vs. Wartime CEO (existential survival, micro-command, decisive ruthlessness, zero tolerance for deviations), and knowing which leader your company needs right now.
18. **Organizational Debt: The Hidden Cost of Postponed Hard Conversations and Legacy Systems**
   - *Question:* Just like technical debt slows software down, what is 'Organizational Debt' and how does accumulated bureaucracy paralyze companies as they grow from 50 to 500 people?
   - *Key Focus:* Steve Blank's organizational debt concept, outdated policies created to prevent single historical mistakes that now paralyze progress, accumulated unspoken interpersonal tensions, pruning vestigial committees, and routine organizational debt jubilees.
19. **The Turnaround Blueprint: How Lou Gerstner Saved IBM from The Corporate Graveyard**
   - *Question:* How did an outsider from RJR Nabisco take over a dying, bleeding $8 billion loss IBM in 1993, reject Wall Street demands to break it up, and orchestrate the greatest corporate turnaround in history?
   - *Key Focus:* 'Who Says Elephants Can't Dance?', refusing to publish a grand vision statement in favor of relentless operational execution, shifting IBM from commodity hardware to enterprise software and IT services, dismantling internal warring fiefdoms, and tying executive compensation strictly to total company performance.
20. **The Future of Management: Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) and AI Management Agents**
   - *Question:* When algorithmic AI agents manage project timelines, assign GitHub issues, and optimize supply chains automatically, what is the evolving role of the human leader?
   - *Key Focus:* The death of middle-management spreadsheet compilation, the rise of the autonomous IC (Individual Contributor), leadership evolving from project tracking to emotional inspiration, moral stewardship, high-level vision, and building compassionate human community.
