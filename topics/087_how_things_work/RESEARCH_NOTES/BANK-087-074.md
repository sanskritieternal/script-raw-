# BANK-087-074 — Grape-sized aqueous dimers and microwave plasma

**Selected question:** Why did grape-sized aqueous dimers make plasma in a microwave experiment?

**Original study inspected (2026-10-24):** Hamza K. Khattak, Pablo Bianucci and Aaron D. Slepkov, “[Linking plasma formation in grapes to microwave resonances of aqueous dimers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410810/),” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 116(10), 4000–4005 (2019). The full paper reports thermal imaging, spectroscopy and finite-element simulations. It tested touching grape hemispheres, whole grapes and water-rich hydrogel beads. The authors attribute strong electric-field hotspots at the contact region to coupled microwave resonances of the water-rich objects; a skin bridge is not the essential cause.

**Bounded finding:** The paper supports a controlled explanation of observed plasma in particular contacting, grape-sized, water-rich configurations. It does not say every grape will spark or that a single grape cannot be heated. The archived “why you can't microwave grapes” wording wrongly turns a specific hazardous configuration into an absolute food rule.
