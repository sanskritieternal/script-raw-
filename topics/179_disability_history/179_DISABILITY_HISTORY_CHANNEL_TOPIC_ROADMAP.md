## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Disability History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Medical Model vs Social Model of Disability, Asylum Eras & Eugenics (Beginner / Scratch)** — The Medical Model (fixing the broken body) vs Social Model (society creating barriers), Victorian 'freak shows' and institutional asylums, the American eugenics movement (Buck v. Bell 1927 forced sterilizations), and Aktion T4 in Nazi Germany.
- **Tier 2: The Disability Rights Movement (The Section 504 28-day 1977 San Francisco Sit-in, ADA 1990 Capitol Crawl) (Intermediate)** — Ed Roberts and the Rolling Quads at UC Berkeley, the historic 28-day 1977 Section 504 San Francisco sit-in (Judy Heumann), the 1990 Capitol Crawl protesting for the Americans with Disabilities Act, and 'Nothing About Us Without Us'.
- **Tier 3: Assistive Technology Evolution (Braille, Wheelchairs, Cochlear Implants, Neural Prosthetics) & Deaf Culture (Upper Intermediate)** — Louis Braille's tactile alphabet, the evolution from heavy wooden bath chairs to titanium sport wheelchairs, Deaf culture linguistic identity vs cochlear implant controversies, and screen readers (JAWS).
- **Tier 4: Universal Design Architecture (Curb-Cut Effect), Neurodiversity Paradigm & Global Disability Justice (Advanced / Masterclass)** — The Curb-Cut Effect (accessible features benefiting the entire public), the Neurodiversity Paradigm (Judy Singer) reframing Autism and ADHD, and constructing fully accessible physical and digital societies.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The 1977 504 Sit-In: How 150 Disabled Activists Occupied a Federal Building for 28 Days and Won**
   - *Question:* How did 150 wheelchair users, blind activists, and people with severe disabilities stage the longest peaceful occupation of a federal building in US history without medical equipment?
   - *Key Focus:* Judy Heumann's leadership, HEW Secretary Joseph Califano refusing to sign Section 504 regulations, Black Panther Party delivering hot meals daily, deaf activists signing messages through windows when phone lines were cut, and signing the landmark civil rights rules.
2. **Medical Model vs Social Model: Is Disability in the Body or in the Architecture?**
   - *Question:* If a person in a wheelchair cannot enter a building, is the disability their paralyzed legs, or is the disability the set of stairs built at the entrance?
   - *Key Focus:* The British Union of the Physically Impaired Against Segregation (UPIAS) 1976 document, distinguishing impairment (biological condition) from disability (social and environmental barrier), and shifting from medical 'cure' to societal 'access'.
3. **The 1990 Capitol Crawl: The Disabled Activists Who Dragged Themselves Up 83 Marble Steps**
   - *Question:* Why did 60 disabled activists, including 8-year-old Jennifer Keelan-Chaffins, abandon their wheelchairs to drag their bodies up the US Capitol steps on their elbows?
   - *Key Focus:* ADAPT activists protesting stalled Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) legislation, television cameras capturing raw visceral physical exclusion, President George H.W. Bush signing the ADA in July 1990, and 'Let the shameful wall of exclusion come down'.
4. **The Eugenics Horrors: Buck v. Bell and the Forced Sterilization of 60,000 Americans**
   - *Question:* How did Supreme Court Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes declare that 'Three generations of imbeciles are enough', legally authorizing the involuntary sterilization of disabled citizens?
   - *Key Focus:* The 1927 Supreme Court ruling in Buck v. Bell, Carrie Buck's tragic institutional framing, state eugenics boards targeting disabled, impoverished, and minority women, and its chilling inspiration for Nazi Germany's hereditary health courts.
5. **Aktion T4: The Secret Nazi Program That Used Hospitals as Gas Chambers Before the Holocaust**
   - *Question:* How did Nazi doctors test carbon monoxide gas chambers on 250,000 mentally and physically disabled children and adults labeled 'life unworthy of life'?
   - *Key Focus:* Hitler's secret October 1939 decree, the Tiergartenstrasse 4 headquarters, Hadamar and Grafeneck psychiatric murder facilities, fake death certificates sent to families, Bishop von Galen's public sermons, and T4 personnel transferring directly to Treblinka and Belzec.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **The Curb-Cut Effect: How Designing for Disability Made the World Better for Everyone**
   - *Question:* Why did wheelchair activists who poured midnight cement ramps in Berkeley in the 1960s end up helping parents with strollers, delivery workers with hand trucks, and travelers with luggage?
   - *Key Focus:* Angela Glover Blackwell's Curb-Cut Effect concept, Universal Design (Ron Mace), closed captioning helping noisy bar patrons and language learners, typewriter invented for a blind countess, and electric toothbrushes.
7. **Ed Roberts and the Rolling Quads: The Iron Lung Student Who Started the Independent Living Movement**
   - *Question:* How did a post-polio quadriplegic who lived in an 800-pound iron lung force UC Berkeley to house him in Cowell Hospital and spark a global movement?
   - *Key Focus:* Ed Roberts being admitted in 1962 ('We've tried vegetables, but not people in iron lungs'), founding the 'Rolling Quads' student advocacy group, creating the Berkeley Center for Independent Living (CIL) run entirely by disabled people, and becoming California's Director of Rehabilitation.
8. **Deaf Culture and Martha's Vineyard: When an Entire Island Spoke Sign Language**
   - *Question:* Why was hereditary deafness not considered a disability on a Massachusetts island for 200 years, where hearing and deaf residents conversed fluently in sign?
   - *Key Focus:* Nora Groce's Everyone Here Spoke Sign Language, hereditary recessive deafness clustering from Kent, England, Martha's Vineyard Sign Language (MVSL), total social and economic integration with zero stigma, and the formation of American Sign Language (ASL) at Hartford.
9. **Louis Braille and the Raised Dots: How a 15-Year-Old Blind Student Unlocked the Written Word**
   - *Question:* How did a blind French teenager adapt Napoleon's secret battlefield night-writing code into a 6-dot tactile alphabet that unlocked literacy for millions?
   - *Key Focus:* Captain Charles Barbier's 12-dot phonographic night writing for trench warfare, Louis Braille's simplification to a 6-dot cell readable under a single fingertip, blind school directors burning Braille slates in fear, and international adoption.
10. **Willowbrook State School: Geraldo Rivera's 1972 Exposé That Closed America's Horrific Asylums**
   - *Question:* What did television cameras capture when an investigative reporter used a stolen key to enter a Staten Island institution housing 5,000 disabled children?
   - *Key Focus:* Geraldo Rivera's 'The Last Great Disgrace' broadcast on WABC-TV, naked children on tile floors, 1-to-50 attendant staffing ratios, deliberate hepatitis infection experiments by Dr. Saul Krugman, federal Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act (CRIPA), and national deinstitutionalization.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **Deaf President Now: The 1988 Gallaudet University Student Revolution**
   - *Question:* How did students at the world's only liberal arts university for the deaf barricade the campus gates with hijacked school buses to demand their first deaf president in 124 years?
   - *Key Focus:* Gallaudet Board of Trustees appointing a hearing president who couldn't sign, student leader Greg Hlibok, four non-negotiable demands, I. King Jordan becoming president ('Deaf people can do anything hearing people can do, except hear'), and global civil rights celebration.
12. **The Ugly Laws: When Cities Banned 'Unsightly and Deformed' People from Appearing in Public**
   - *Question:* Why did Chicago, San Francisco, and Omaha pass municipal ordinances between 1867 and 1911 imposing fines and jail time on anyone who was 'maimed, mutilated, or deformed' for walking on public streets?
   - *Key Focus:* Susan Schweik's The Ugly Laws: Disability in Public, targeting disabled beggars and Civil War amputees to sanitize commercial streetscapes, forced institutionalization, and the last known arrest under an Ugly Law in Omaha in 1974.
13. **The Neurodiversity Paradigm: How Judy Singer Re-framed Autism as a Natural Human Variation**
   - *Question:* Is Autism an illness that needs to be cured, or a neurological variation as natural and valuable to the human species as biodiversity is to an ecosystem?
   - *Key Focus:* Australian sociologist Judy Singer coining 'Neurodiversity' in 1998, moving away from pathology toward accommodation, the sensory overload reality of modern fluorescent offices, autistic self-advocacy (ASAN), and celebrating divergent cognitive strengths.
14. **Assistive Tech Evolution: From 50-Pound Steel Wheelchairs to Thought-Controlled Bionic Limbs**
   - *Question:* How did mechanical engineer Herbert Everest and Harry Jennings invent the folding tubular steel wheelchair in 1933, and how are neural implants steering robotic hands today?
   - *Key Focus:* Everest & Jennings folding wheelchair patent monopoly, Marilyn Hamilton's lightweight aluminum Quickie sport wheelchair revolution, osseointegration bionic prosthetics, brain-computer interface neural control, and accessible smartphone screen readers.
15. **FDR's Hidden Wheelchair: How a Paralyzed President Ran the World from an Unmarked Seat**
   - *Question:* Why did the White House press corps and Secret Service hide that President Franklin D. Roosevelt could not stand or walk without 10-pound steel leg braces?
   - *Key Focus:* Contracting polio at age 39 in 1921, founding the Warm Springs rehabilitation center, the unwritten gentlemen's agreement with photographers (only 2 out of 35,000 photos show his wheelchair), designing custom car hand controls, and the debate over the 1997 FDR Memorial wheelchair statue addition.
16. **The Cochlear Implant Controversy: Why a Medical Miracle Sparked a Cultural War**
   - *Question:* Why did the National Association of the Deaf in 1990 vehemently oppose surgical cochlear implants for deaf infants, branding it cultural genocide?
   - *Key Focus:* Direct electrical stimulation of the auditory nerve via cochlear electrodes, the linguistic richness of visual sign languages, fear of sign language extinction and loss of Deaf community belonging, and modern balanced bimodal-bilingual reconciliation.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **Digital Accessibility (WCAG): The Invisible Lawsuit Wave Redesigning the Internet**
   - *Question:* Why did Domino's Pizza take a blind customer all the way to the Supreme Court because he couldn't order a pizza on their website using a screen reader?
   - *Key Focus:* Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1 AA), screen reader semantic HTML markup, color contrast ratios, alt text mandates, thousands of ADA Title III federal digital accessibility lawsuits, and accessible digital civic infrastructure.
18. **Disability Justice: The 10 Principles of the Queer, BIPOC-Led Grassroots Vanguard**
   - *Question:* Why did disabled activists of color argue that the traditional legal rights framework (ADA) failed to protect the most vulnerable disabled people?
   - *Key Focus:* Sins Invalid collective and Patty Berne, the 10 Principles of Disability Justice (Intersectionality, Leadership of those most impacted, Anti-capitalist politic, Cross-disability solidarity, Collective access), and moving beyond legal compliance toward human liberation.
19. **The Economics of Accessibility: The Financial Return on Universal Design**
   - *Question:* Why do commercial businesses that invest in universal accessibility see massive increases in customer revenue and retention across the entire population?
   - *Key Focus:* The $13 trillion global disability disposable income market (The Purple Pound), retrofitting costs vs building access from day one, public transit accessibility ROI, and employee productivity gains from ergonomic accommodation.
20. **The Masterclass on Accessibility Auditing: How to Build a Truly Universal World**
   - *Question:* How does an accessibility architect inspect a building or digital system to eliminate physical, sensory, and cognitive barriers completely?
   - *Key Focus:* ADA Standards for Accessible Design checklists, slope ratios (1:12 ramp gradients), door opening clearances and actuator pressures, tactile ground indicators, acoustic dampening for auditory processing, neurodivergent sensory room design, and holistic physical-digital universal access.
