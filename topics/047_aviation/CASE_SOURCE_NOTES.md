## BANK-047-063 — Aloha Airlines Flight 243 (archive pending 11)

- The [NTSB accident record](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA88MA054.aspx) and [full report AAR-89/03](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AAR8903.pdf) identify significant disbonding and fatigue damage in a fuselage lap joint that the maintenance program failed to detect. The NTSB lists management, inspection, FAA oversight and earlier design-action deficiencies as contributing factors.
- Editorial boundary: explain how inspection and organizational safeguards failed as well as the structural failure and landing. The archive’s “convertible” phrase is spectacle, not a technical description.

## BANK-047-064 — Delta Air Lines Flight 191 (archive pending 12)

- The [NTSB investigation record](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA85AA031.aspx) and [full report AAR-86/05](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AAR8605.pdf) describe the aircraft’s encounter with a microburst on approach at Dallas/Fort Worth. The NTSB identifies the approach decision, lack of specific avoidance and escape guidance, and inadequate real-time hazard information among the causes.
- Editorial boundary: trace the hazard and investigated decisions. Any claim that this single accident “revolutionized radars” needs separate evidence on later systems and policy changes; radar was not created by the event.

## BANK-047-065 — Air France Flight 447 (archive pending 14)

- The French BEA’s [investigation page and final-report links](https://bea.aero/en/investigation-reports/notified-events/detail/accident-to-the-airbus-a330-203-registered-f-gzcp-and-operated-by-air-france-occured-on-06-01-2009-in-the-atlantic-ocean/) describe pitot-probe icing, erroneous speed indications, stall and ocean impact. The [final report](https://bea.aero/fileadmin/documents/docspa/2009/f-cp090601.en/pdf/f-cp090601.en.pdf) provides the detailed sequence and safety analysis.
- Editorial boundary: distinguish probe icing from the subsequent aircraft response and crew actions. Avoid reducing the accident to “frozen pitot tubes” or implying that one component fault fully explains the outcome.
