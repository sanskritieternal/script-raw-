## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Horror Fiction across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Horror Fiction Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Horror Fiction.
- **Tier 2: Horror Fiction Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Horror Fiction.
- **Tier 3: Horror Fiction Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Horror Fiction.
- **Tier 4: Horror Fiction Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Horror Fiction.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Horror Fiction: A Horror Motif in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated cultural or literary source shaped one frightening motif?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace particular works and avoid a universal fear story. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
2. **Horror Fiction: A Famous Fright Reexamined**
   - *Question:* How does a named scene create threat before showing it?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze image or prose, sound, timing, and audience information. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
3. **Horror Fiction: Makers of a Frightening Scene**
   - *Question:* Whose design, sound, performance, or writing contributed to it?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credits and production records. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
4. **Horror Fiction: Dread, Shock, and Aftermath**
   - *Question:* How do three distinct effects operate in one horror work?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify setup, event, and consequence in bounded passages. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
5. **Horror Fiction: Horror and Social Anxiety**
   - *Question:* Which contextual reading is supported by a specific work and period?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare text, contemporary debate, and alternative interpretations. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Horror Fiction: An Ambiguous Threat**
   - *Question:* What does the work deliberately leave unexplained?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate narrative ambiguity from missing archival evidence. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
7. **Horror Fiction: A New Horror Technique**
   - *Question:* How did one sound, makeup, camera, or game tool shape a scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Document actual production method and audience effect cautiously. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
8. **Horror Fiction: A Creator's Fear Design**
   - *Question:* Which documented choice changed how a threat appears?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts, storyboards, and credited testimony. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
9. **Horror Fiction: A Horror Release and Reaction**
   - *Question:* How did a specific release meet censorship or public response?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated rulings, reviews, and exhibition records. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
10. **Horror Fiction: Suspense from Limited Knowledge**
   - *Question:* Who knows what before a frightening reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Map character and audience information scene by scene. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Horror Fiction: A Monster's Changing Meaning**
   - *Question:* How did a particular figure's interpretation change across adaptations?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versions and situated criticism. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
12. **Horror Fiction: The Same Fear in Two Periods**
   - *Question:* How do two works stage a related threat under different norms?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare form, context, and documented reception. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
13. **Horror Fiction: An Emerging Horror Format**
   - *Question:* Which released experiment changes viewer control or proximity?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze specific work and avoid forecasting fear universally. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
14. **Horror Fiction: The Scariest Scene for Everyone?**
   - *Question:* What can reviews or audience studies actually show about reaction?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify sample, place, and selection limits. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
15. **Horror Fiction: Making a Horror Sequence**
   - *Question:* How did concept, practical effects, and edit create a finished scare?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated designs, tests, and cuts. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Horror Fiction: Defining the Threat**
   - *Question:* What was a production asked to make visible or uncertain?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, draft, and released work. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
17. **Horror Fiction: Building a Creature or Setting**
   - *Question:* How do material effects and sound sources shape a threat?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify tools and distinguish production fact from screen illusion. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
18. **Horror Fiction: A Scare Across Drafts**
   - *Question:* Which change altered timing or stakes in a scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts, rehearsals, and final cut. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
19. **Horror Fiction: The Frame Outside the Threat**
   - *Question:* How does offscreen space invite a viewer to anticipate danger?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze a bounded sequence and alternatives. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
20. **Horror Fiction: Who Designed the Fear?**
   - *Question:* How did writer, performer, designer, editor, and composer contribute?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits and production records, not single-author myth. Evidence base: identified works and versions, scripts, production and effects records, censorship documents, dated reviews, and bounded audience research.
