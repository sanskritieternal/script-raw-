## Bounded premise

The trial enrolled 20 adults in an inpatient randomized crossover experiment. Participants received an ultra-processed and an unprocessed menu for 14 days each, in random order, with ad libitum intake. Menus were matched for presented calories, sugar, fat, sodium, fiber and macronutrients. The paper reports 508 ? 106 kcal/day greater energy intake during the ultra-processed period and weight gain of about 0.9 kg versus loss of about 0.9 kg in the unprocessed period. The comparison is between provided menu packages over a short controlled interval.

## Claim and access limits

The intervention did not isolate the causal contribution of processing itself from all other food properties. The authors state that the study was not designed to identify the cause of the intake difference; eating rate and nonbeverage energy density are plausible differing features. Small sample, two-week periods and inpatient selection do not establish long-term disease risk or response in all populations. Do not generalize to every ultra-processed product, present the menu contrast as a universal nutritional rule, or offer an individual diet plan. Recheck full text and protocol before scripting, including the distinction between the original DOI and later correction DOI 10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.020.
