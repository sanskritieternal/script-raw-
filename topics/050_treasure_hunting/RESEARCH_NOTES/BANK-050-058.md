# BANK-050-058 — Black Swan wreck and sovereign immunity

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/6` calls Odyssey Marine's Black Swan operation a “$500 million gold haul that Spain took back.”

## Inspected primary court record

- [*Odyssey Marine Exploration, Inc. v. Kingdom of Spain*, U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 2011](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/201010269.pdf), consolidated appeal including No. 10-10318. The court found the recovered site was the Spanish frigate *Nuestra Señora de las Mercedes*, using historical records and coin, cannon and cargo evidence. It affirmed dismissal of Odyssey's in-rem claim under sovereign immunity and the order to release the recovered material to Spain's custody.
- The court explicitly said it **did not determine ultimate ownership of every recovered item**; sovereign immunity of the warship and its cargo controlled the U.S. action. It recorded competing private and state interests rather than a universal rule that Spain owns all shipwreck treasure.

## Claim disposition

The documented recovery included mostly **silver coins**, so “gold haul” is misleading. A high-dollar valuation in the archived title is not a court finding of market value and needs a dated appraisal and scope if used. Explain the distinction between identification, recovery, custody, sovereign immunity and ultimate ownership; do not generalize the result to every wreck or jurisdiction. A selected script should inspect the district court order, later custody/transfer record, maritime heritage rules and the relevant Spanish and Peruvian claims before making a final disposition statement.
