# Selected workplace source checks — 24 October 2026

These checks bound three organizational, occupational or rule-status questions. A published handbook describes policy; a classification defines a concept; a regulator page states current federal rule status. None establishes an individual employee’s experience, diagnosis or contract rights.

## BANK-104-050 — burnout measurement

- [WHO’s ICD-11 explanation of burn-out](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon) classifies burn-out as an **occupational phenomenon**, not a medical condition, and describes exhaustion, distance or cynicism toward work, and reduced professional efficacy. It says the term belongs in the occupational context.
- **Editorial bound:** The archived Maslach Burnout Inventory is a specific instrument with administration and licensing context; a WHO definition does not validate any particular questionnaire, score cutoff or “architecture of recovery.” A study episode must cite its instrument, sample, consent and limitations. No individual diagnosis or treatment advice.

## BANK-104-051 — GitLab asynchronous work

- [GitLab’s current public communication handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/communication/) distinguishes asynchronous and synchronous channels and says its all-remote workforce can work across locations. Its [meeting guidance](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/meetings/) describes questioning whether a meeting is needed and enabling asynchronous contribution when one is held.
- **Editorial bound:** A handbook is a dated statement of organizational practice, not an independent measure of productivity or proof that GitLab abolished meetings. Capture page version or archive date before production and compare any worker-experience claim with separately consented evidence.

## BANK-104-054 — FTC noncompete rule

- The [FTC’s current Noncompete Rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule) says the 2024 final rule **is not in effect and is not enforceable** after a 20 August 2024 district court order. It records that the FTC moved to dismiss its appeal in September 2025. The [FTC’s September 2025 statement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule) describes its decision to accede to vacatur.
- **Editorial bound:** This is federal rule status at the inspection date, not a ruling on any worker’s contract. State law and other FTC enforcement actions are separate. The archive’s “FTC ban” and “never sign” lines must not be offered as current legal advice.
