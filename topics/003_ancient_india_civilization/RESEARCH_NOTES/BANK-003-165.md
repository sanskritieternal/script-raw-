## Why this is a distinct question

The existing [Mehrgarh farming proposal](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-003-073) concerns settlement and agricultural sequence. This case asks how researchers recognized alterations in excavated human teeth, what their observations establish about living individuals, and where purpose and date remain uncertain. The preserved local queue item bundled these subjects and used a fixed “9,000-year-old” label; the two active scopes separate them without deleting its wording.

## Bounded interpretation

The 2006 report identifies artificial drilling on excavated molars and argues that smoothing around the holes indicates the teeth were used after modification. Four of its eleven teeth had decay associated with a hole. That combination makes a care-related explanation plausible for some instances; it does not prove the motive for every hole, the identity of practitioners, or a stable profession. Its bow-drill experiment demonstrates feasibility for a proposed tool type, not an archaeological identification of the exact implement used on a particular person.

The 2025 reassessment raises a separate chronological issue. It models a later start and shorter duration for a sampled Period I cemetery than older estimates for Mehrgarh. Until the 2006 MR3 individuals and the 2025 dated graves are reconciled at the context and level level, the proposal avoids a fixed “9,000-year-old dentistry” label. A future episode should show what is dated, how, and whether the drilled specimens are directly covered.
