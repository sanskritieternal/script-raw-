# BANK-067-062 — Christmas Island red-crab migration

**Selected question:** How did Christmas Island red-crab migration timing and road protection work in a named season?

**Primary source access (2026-10-24):**

- [Parks Australia, 2 December 2021](https://parksaustralia.gov.au/news/millions-of-crabs-march-on-christmas-island/), describes the 2021 wet-season migration after rainfall, the expected spawning near the last-quarter moon, and temporary barriers, signs, road closures and crab bridges. The expected 29–30 November spawning date was a forecast for that season, not a permanent calendar date.
- [Parks Australia, 5 January 2024](https://parksaustralia.gov.au/news/christmas-cheer-red-crabs-march-on/), reports another season's migration and the park's road-protection work. It describes a population estimate rising from 50 million to more than 100 million over five years. The estimate is dated and belongs to the population, not a direct count of crabs crossing one road or moving in one synchronized group.
- [Parks Australia natural-values report](https://api.parksaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-11/document/amp-document-iot-natural-values-report-final-jan-2022.pdf), section 2.6, describes the species' obligate marine larval phase and migration to coastal spawning sites. This helps define the ecological sequence a film would need to show.

**Bounded finding:** The migration is a seasonal forest-to-coast reproductive movement shaped by rainfall and lunar/tidal timing. Park staff and the island community use traffic management and crossing structures to protect migrating crabs. The archived “50 million crabs crossing Christmas Island” is a dated population-scale shorthand, not a fixed annual migration count.

**Before production:** Obtain the park's monitoring method and season-specific observation logs before quoting an exact crab count, crossing rate or roadkill reduction. Confirm filming date, location and permits; do not imply footage from different days depicts one continuous event.
