# 066 selected source inspection — 2026-10-24

These are bounded research leads for proposed wildlife episodes, not certification of every behavior, conservation status or animal-welfare claim.

## Eastern monarch migration — BANK-066-056

The [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's monarch species account](https://www.fws.gov/species/monarch-danaus-plexippus) describes an eastern migration that can reach about 3,000 miles and explains that a new fall generation makes the southward trip. Its [life-cycle account](https://www.fws.gov/story/2023-07/monarch-butterflies-emerge-closer-look-magnificent-life-cycle) distinguishes generations in the yearly cycle. A single butterfly does not complete a four-generation round trip. Specify eastern versus western population, route and measurement source.

## Tardigrades in space — BANK-066-057

The [original 2008 low-Earth-orbit study record](https://researchportal.hkr.se/en/publications/tardigrades-survive-exposure-to-space-in-low-earth-orbit-2/) identifies the exposure experiment. A later [TARDIKISS flight experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4309218/) identifies desiccated species and controlled mission conditions. Survival is conditional on species, state, duration and radiation/UV exposure; “indestructible” is not a scientific result. Do not generalize a spaceflight test to all active tardigrades in all conditions.

## Yellowstone wolves — BANK-066-061

The [National Park Service's ecosystem account](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/cyclesprocesses.htm) describes debate over behavioral trophic cascades and other influences, including bears, mountain lions, hunting and climate. A [USGS landscape-level aspen study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/are-wolves-saving-yellowstones-aspen-a-landscape-level-test-a-behaviorally-mediated) reported that its estimates did not show broad aspen recovery despite wolves. The archived “14 wolves changed rivers” claim is an unsupported compression of varied evidence. Compare measured vegetation, elk, beaver and channel data with dates and alternative drivers.

## Elephant responses to remains — BANK-066-062

An [experimental study of African elephants and skulls/ivory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1617198/) records interest in conspecific remains under controlled comparisons. A [video-based study of Asian elephant responses to dead calves](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9114935/) documents observable behaviors and discusses interpretive limits. These observations support a behavior question, not a conclusion that all elephants conduct humanlike funerals or experience grief in an identical way. Distinguish species, wild versus captive setting, sampling and welfare limits of observation.
