# Human-computer interaction source follow-up - 2026-10-24

## BANK-513-043 - Nouwens et al. cookie-consent experiment

- Primary research: Midas Nouwens, Ilaria Liccardi, Michael Veale, David Karger and Lalana Kagal, *Dark Patterns after the GDPR: Scraping Consent Pop-ups and Demonstrating their Influence*, CHI 2020, DOI 10.1145/3313831.3376321, author accepted manuscript and abstract at https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10088400/ (inspected 2026-10-24). The study scraped consent-management-platform designs on UK top websites (n=680 captured designs) and ran a field experiment with 40 participants on eight common designs. Its abstract reports that removing a first-page opt-out button increased consent by 22-23 percentage points; first-page granular controls decreased it by 8-20 points in the tested conditions.
- Access and claim limits: The institutional repository abstract and manuscript listing were inspected; exact experiment assignment, uncertainty estimates and interface screenshots require full-paper review before a script. The samples and CMP designs are UK/GDPR-era, not a representative sample of all users or all consent interfaces. An observed click/consent choice is not proof of informed preference or legal compliance. Do not imply every dark pattern has the same effect size.

## Scope and overlap

BANK-513-043 is a named controlled interface study. BANK-513-034 addresses user autonomy broadly and BANK-513-032 general usability-study inference; consumer-psychology banks cover buying behavior, not this consent-layout experiment.
