## Phoenician alphabet — BANK-041-050

The [Metropolitan Museum of Art's account of alphabet origins](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/alphabet) documents earlier Sinai alphabetic inscriptions, later Phoenician inscriptions across the Mediterranean, and Greek adoption and adaptation. Its inscription examples make this a concrete transmission case. The local pitch's claim that Phoenician letters simply replaced thousands of hieroglyphs is too linear: alphabetic and cuneiform writing coexisted, and the earlier alphabetic stage preceded Phoenician. Compare dated inscriptions and sign functions before making an origin claim.

## Rosetta Stone — BANK-041-052

The [British Museum's exhibition guide](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/Hieroglyphs_unlocking_ancient_Egypt_large_print_guide_The_British_Museum.pdf) identifies distribution of prints and casts and the collaborative decipherment history, including Champollion. The proposed episode should explain the three scripts, proper names, phonetic values, and corroborating inscriptions. Do not frame decipherment as a lone instant codebreak or imply the stone contains three languages.

## Language vitality — BANK-041-040

UNESCO's [*Language Vitality and Endangerment* framework](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/00120-EN.pdf) expressly warns that no single factor is sufficient; it includes transmission, speaker population, domains of use, responses to new domains, and learning materials. The legacy claim that one language dies every 14 days should remain a pitch rather than a verified rate. A responsible episode must define the measurement period, what counts as a language and as an extinction, and whose assessment controls.
