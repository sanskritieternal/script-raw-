## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Archery Culture across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Archery Culture Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Archery Culture.
- **Tier 2: Archery Culture Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Archery Culture.
- **Tier 3: Archery Culture Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Archery Culture.
- **Tier 4: Archery Culture Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Archery Culture.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Archery Culture: Early Evidence of an Archery Form**
   - *Question:* What dated objects or texts describe one community's practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate bow technology from current ceremonial or sporting form. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
2. **Archery Culture: A Celebrated Archery Event**
   - *Question:* What does one event reveal about participants and meaning?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare rules, craft, ritual, audience, and promotion. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
3. **Archery Culture: The Bow Makers and Teachers**
   - *Question:* Whose craft and instruction sustain participation?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit makers, stringers, range staff, and mentors. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
4. **Archery Culture: Archery Beyond Hitting a Target**
   - *Question:* How do equipment, rules, and social practice fit together?
   - *Key Focus:* Name a specific community and setting. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
5. **Archery Culture: Craft, Ritual, and Competition**
   - *Question:* How do these purposes shape different archery forms?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare practitioner accounts without treating one as definitive. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Archery Culture: An Uncertain Bow Provenance**
   - *Question:* Which evidence could establish an object's origin?
   - *Key Focus:* Check materials, maker marks, collection history, and gaps. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
7. **Archery Culture: A Bow Material Change**
   - *Question:* How did a new material affect craft and competition?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare design, maintenance, rules, and maker views. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
8. **Archery Culture: An Archer's Teaching Legacy**
   - *Question:* What did a named practitioner pass on?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented interviews and dated association records. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
9. **Archery Culture: A Range or Rule Change**
   - *Question:* How did one decision alter access to archery?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace approvals, participation, safety, and community response. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
10. **Archery Culture: An Archery Gathering**
   - *Question:* Who prepares equipment, teaches, officiates, and closes an event?
   - *Key Focus:* Document a specific current or historic form. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Archery Culture: Archery Participation Over Time**
   - *Question:* What explains a measured change?
   - *Key Focus:* Check membership, informal practice, venues, and counting. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
12. **Archery Culture: Equipment Across Generations**
   - *Question:* How did materials, techniques, and meanings change?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated bows and attributed practitioner accounts. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
13. **Archery Culture: Continuing a Craft**
   - *Question:* What would makers and archers need?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare material supply, apprenticeships, range access, and rights. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
14. **Archery Culture: An Unbroken Bow Tradition?**
   - *Question:* Which aspects are documented continuously and which revived?
   - *Key Focus:* Check archival gaps and practitioner definitions. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
15. **Archery Culture: One Bow's Working Life**
   - *Question:* How was it made, used, repaired, and kept?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented maker and owner accounts; avoid operational instruction. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.

16. **Archery Culture: A Historic Archery Description**

   - *Question:* Why do sources assign different beginnings?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate technique, named discipline, and organized event. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
19. **Archery Culture: Who Could Practise**
   - *Question:* How did cost, land, work, and gender shape access?
   - *Key Focus:* Use local membership and community records. Evidence base: dated bows and texts with provenance, community and association records, maker accounts, and consented event observation.
