# Selected source note - niche 195

Checked 2026-10-24. These sources bound proposed species and measurement questions. They do not provide wildlife handling, venom treatment or husbandry advice. All linked BANK topics remain proposed with empty verified_sources.

## BANK-195-038 - King cobra nesting

Wang et al., [*Novel Natural History Observations on the Reproductive Biology of King Cobra (Ophiophagus hannah) From Yunnan Province, China*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/inc3.70052) (Integrative Conservation, 2025), document a nest, eggs and guarding female at a named location. One field observation does not establish a universal nesting sequence, intelligence ranking or safe encounter behavior. Check the paper's species identification and current Ophiophagus taxonomy before generalizing.

## BANK-195-039 - Ball python morph welfare

[*Comparative Assessment of Computed Tomography and Magnetic Resonance Imaging of Spider Morph and Wild Type Ball Pythons for Evaluation of the Morphological Correlate of Wobble Syndrome*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36008042/) (2022) is a primary veterinary imaging comparison. It can ground a narrow question about a reported phenotype and observed anatomy; the sample does not quantify the global morph market or establish every individual animal's severity. Review full methods, ethics and welfare measures before describing causation. No breeding recommendations follow.

## BANK-195-040 - Lolong record

Guinness World Records' [largest crocodile in captivity ever](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/117291-largest-crocodile-in-captivity-ever) entry verifies the named Lolong category and 6.17-m measurement in the Philippines after 2011 capture. The record is for a measured animal in captivity, not the largest crocodile ever to have lived or a population maximum. The archive's Gustave legend has no comparable verified measurement in the checked record; claims about attacks or exact size should remain attributed and unconfirmed. Include capture and welfare context without a pursuit narrative.

## BANK-195-041 - Black mamba speed

Guinness World Records' [fastest land snake](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/70269-fastest-land-snake) entry gives a black mamba short-burst estimate of 16-19 km/h on level ground. The public entry does not expose a detailed timed-trial protocol, so this is a record-category source, not an independently reanalyzed primary measurement. Verify original observations before scripting a precise speed comparison. Speed of travel is different from strike timing, and "most feared" is not a scientific measure.

## BANK-195-042 - Leatherback dives

Houghton et al., [*The role of infrequent and extraordinary deep dives in leatherback turtles*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18689410/) (2008), analyzed satellite-relayed logs from tagged Atlantic leatherbacks and reports rare dives in the 300-1250 m range. A previous [North Atlantic telemetry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/429522a) reported a 1,230-m maximum among tagged animals while most dives were shallower. These are sample- and sensor-bounded observations, not a guarantee that every leatherback can dive to the archive's rounded 4,000-foot figure or proof of conservation trends in every ocean.
