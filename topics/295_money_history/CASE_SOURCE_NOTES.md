# Selected institutional source note — money history

Inspected 2026-10-24 for `BANK-295-043`. This is a bounded history of a British issuer's records, not a universal monetary timeline.

- [Bank of England archive catalogue, M6/4](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=M6%2F4&src=CalmView.Catalog): describes the Bank's note issue and states that gold-payment suspension began in 1797 and lasted until 1821; identifies lower denominations as a response. The catalogue is institutional description, not a full sample of notes or users' experiences.
- [Bank of England Museum, counterfeit and imitation notes](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/museum/online-collections/banknotes/counterfeit-and-imitation-notes): links the 1797 wartime gold shortage with hurried £1 and £2 note issue and forgery examples. Museum selection cannot by itself quantify overall circulation or counterfeit rates.
- [Bank of England, withdrawn banknotes register](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/banknotes/withdrawn-banknotes): supplies issue-date and design entries for historical denominations; verify the exact note series and date before image or denomination claims.

The public catalogue and museum pages were accessible; original ledgers, legislation, surviving notes and contemporary users' accounts were not inspected. Dates and causation should be checked against those records. The case concerns note issue in 295; current credit-card terms in 294 and central-bank policy in 298 remain distinct.
