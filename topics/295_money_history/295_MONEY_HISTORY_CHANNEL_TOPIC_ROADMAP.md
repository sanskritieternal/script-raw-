## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Money History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Money History Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Money History.
- **Tier 2: Money History Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Money History.
- **Tier 3: Money History Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Money History.
- **Tier 4: Money History Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Money History.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Money History: Early Evidence of Money**
   - *Question:* What is the earliest secure evidence for a named payment medium?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated objects and records without assuming one universal origin. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
2. **Money History: A Currency Object in Context**
   - *Question:* What does one famous coin or note reveal about its users?
   - *Key Focus:* Study issue authority, denomination, circulation, and surviving evidence. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
3. **Money History: Who Made and Moved Money?**
   - *Question:* Whose labor and access are missing from a monetary history?
   - *Key Focus:* Use mint, merchant, or household records for one community. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
4. **Money History: What Counted as Money?**
   - *Question:* Why did a community accept one object or record in payment?
   - *Key Focus:* Define unit, issuer, trust, and use in one period. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
5. **Money History: Value Without a Single Anchor**
   - *Question:* How did people judge value among multiple monies?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare exchange records and legal rules in one market. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Money History: An Uncertain Coinage Date**
   - *Question:* Which evidence could narrow the date or origin of a monetary object?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare hoards, inscriptions, and archaeological contexts. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
7. **Money History: A Payment Technology Changes Practice**
   - *Question:* What changed when one payment medium or record system appeared?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace adoption, limitations, and users across a dated transition. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
8. **Money History: An Issuer's Monetary Choice**
   - *Question:* Why did a named authority alter a currency's design or value?
   - *Key Focus:* Use decrees, mint records, and contemporary responses. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
9. **Money History: A Monetary Reform Reconstructed**
   - *Question:* What changed after one dated currency reform?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare legal text, circulation evidence, and prices. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
10. **Money History: One Historical Payment**
   - *Question:* How did one documented transaction actually settle?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow unit, medium, intermediaries, and record of receipt. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Money History: A Currency Gains and Loses Acceptance**
   - *Question:* What evidence shows a medium expanding or retreating in use?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated transactions across places and groups. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
12. **Money History: Two Ways to Record Payment**
   - *Question:* Which functions changed between a physical and later recorded payment?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare ownership, transfer, access, and trust in dated cases. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
13. **Money History: A Historical Claim About the Future of Money**
   - *Question:* Which past predictions about money did or did not materialize?
   - *Key Focus:* Read original forecasts against later records without predicting the next form. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
14. **Money History: Testing a Barter-to-Money Story**
   - *Question:* Does one popular origin story fit documented exchange practices?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine its source and evidence from a bounded society. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
15. **Money History: One Note's Life in the Archive**
   - *Question:* What can a surviving monetary object tell us about circulation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use provenance and issuer records, stating what cannot be inferred. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Money History: Terms Printed on a Historical Note**
   - *Question:* What could a holder legally claim under one note issue?
   - *Key Focus:* Read dated issue terms and distinguish promise from practice. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
17. **Money History: Household Payments in a Past Economy**
   - *Question:* What monetary units appear in a surviving household account?
   - *Key Focus:* Reconstruct income and bills with source limits. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
18. **Money History: A Delayed Historical Payment**
   - *Question:* What risk arose when payment and delivery occurred at different times?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one merchant or government record to trace obligations. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
19. **Money History: The Cost of Converting Money**
   - *Question:* How did mint, exchange, or transfer charges affect a transaction?
   - *Key Focus:* Use a dated fee schedule and one illustrative amount. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
20. **Money History: Historical Protection Against Bad Money**
   - *Question:* Which rule addressed counterfeiting or failed redemption?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare official rule and observed enforcement; avoid present-day advice. Evidence base: dated monetary objects, mint and issue records, legal texts, transaction ledgers, and attributed scholarship.
