## BANK-242-039 — Yellowstone's 2022 flood and northern access

- [National Park Service flood recovery and operations record](https://www.nps.gov/yell/planyourvisit/flood-recovery.htm) dates the June 2022 flood, northern road damage, evacuation and phased reopening. Its [one-year review](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/yell-flooding.htm) adds damage and recovery context.
- This supports a dated case about park access and emergency management. It does not establish today's road condition, total economic loss or whether every road decision was optimal. A production script needs the relevant incident, engineering, environmental and gateway-community records for the exact period selected. Access questions in BANK-242-032 remain about visitor concentration generally; this case tests the distinct effect of a particular flood.

## BANK-242-040 — Yosemite's tribal-partnered prescribed fire

- The [National Park Service partnership account](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/yosemite-national-park-fire-managers-partner-with-indian-tribes-for-prescribed-fire-project.htm) describes a prescribed fire project with the Southern Sierra Miwuk Nation and Tuolumne Band of Me-Wuk. [NPS Yosemite fire management](https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/nature/wildlandfire.htm) explains the park's broader mix of prescribed fire, managed wildfire and mechanical work.
- The records support a case about the project's stated cultural and ecological purposes, but NPS is one institutional narrator. Obtain both tribes' own accounts and consent for any cultural knowledge, plus project monitoring, before asserting what changed in the ecosystem or generalizing historic burning practices. BANK-242-038 is the broader fire-regime comparison; this named collaboration stays separate. Cultural-tradition niche 243 can address transmission of practice, while this question addresses a specific park management decision.
