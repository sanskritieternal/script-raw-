# Personal security source follow-up - 2026-10-24

## BANK-493-045 - BJS 2024 NCVS split sample

- Primary statistical report: U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics, Jennifer L. Truman and Susannah N. Tapp, *Victimization Estimates from the Redesigned NCVS Instrument, 2024*, NCJ 312200, published October 9, 2026, https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/victimization-estimates-redesigned-ncvs-instrument-2024 (inspected 2026-10-24). BJS reports a 2024 split-sample administration of the redesigned (NCVS-R) and legacy (NCVS-L) survey instruments. The redesigned instrument estimated 28.7 violent victimizations per 1,000 persons age 12 or older, versus 23.3 for the legacy instrument; BJS describes this as a 23% difference.
- Primary survey context: BJS, *National Crime Victimization Survey*, https://bjs.ojp.gov/data-collection/ncvs (inspected 2026-10-24). The survey covers self-reported nonfatal crime for a U.S. household sample, including incidents not reported to police.
- Limits: The two 2024 instrument estimates concern the same survey year and reveal measurement sensitivity, not an observed 23% year-over-year increase in victimization. Do not generalize U.S. survey estimates to another country, all ages or a particular individual's risk. Survey coverage, recall, response, weighting and the report's uncertainty measures matter; the publication landing page and summary were inspected, while its full PDF tables need checking for detailed subgroup claims. No individualized safety instruction or victim blame.

## Scope and overlap

BANK-493-045 is a named BJS method comparison. BANK-493-038 addresses general risk perception; BANK-121-032 covers broad survey coverage error. Other security banks should own device or cyber-attack mechanics rather than these population-estimate limits.
