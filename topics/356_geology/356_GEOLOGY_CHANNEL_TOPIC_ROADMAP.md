## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Geology across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Geology Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Geology.
- **Tier 2: Geology Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Geology.
- **Tier 3: Geology Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Geology.
- **Tier 4: Geology Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Geology.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Geology: A Rock Unit First Mapped**
   - *Question:* Which field evidence established a named geologic unit?
   - *Key Focus:* Use original map, sections, and later revision. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
2. **Geology: A Famous Geologic Landscape Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What rocks and processes actually shaped it?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare map, samples, dates, and alternative accounts. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
3. **Geology: Field and Survey Work**
   - *Question:* Who mapped, sampled, drilled, and maintained geologic records?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit local and technical contributors. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
4. **Geology: Reading a Geologic Map**
   - *Question:* What do contacts, units, and faults tell us?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one map at its stated scale. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
5. **Geology: A Subsurface Model's Uncertainty**
   - *Question:* How much can sparse outcrops and boreholes support?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare competing cross-sections and data gaps. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Geology: An Unexplained Rock Layer**
   - *Question:* Which depositional or tectonic histories fit evidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Test composition, structures, age, and context. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
7. **Geology: A New Mapping Method**
   - *Question:* What feature became detectable through remote sensing or geophysics?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare field verification and resolution. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
8. **Geology: A Geologic Interpretation Debate**
   - *Question:* How did teams resolve competing maps?
   - *Key Focus:* Read field records, samples, and published responses. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
9. **Geology: A Geologic Event Recorded**
   - *Question:* What physical evidence establishes sequence and timing?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate dated deposits from event interpretation. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
10. **Geology: Rocks Record Processes**
   - *Question:* How do mineralogy, layering, and structures support a history?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace a named outcrop. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Geology: A Geologic Model Revised**
   - *Question:* Which new dates or mapping changed it?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare maps and uncertainties across versions. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
12. **Geology: One Region Across Map Generations**
   - *Question:* What changed in unit boundaries or age assignments?
   - *Key Focus:* Use scale and method compatibility. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
13. **Geology: A Planned Geologic Survey**
   - *Question:* Which missing observation would test a model?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify location, sampling, and access. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
14. **Geology: A Map Is the Ground Itself?**
   - *Question:* Which contacts are observed versus inferred?
   - *Key Focus:* Read map legend, scale, and source data. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
15. **Geology: A Rock Sample to a Map**
   - *Question:* How did collection, analysis, interpretation, and review occur?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow location and chain of evidence. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Geology: Outcrop to Geologic History**
   - *Question:* Which observations support a proposed sequence?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish contact, date, and inferred process. Evidence base: USGS geologic maps and methods, field logs, sample provenance, geochronology, geophysical surveys, and independent mapping.

19. **Geology: Rock in Place and in Instrument**
