# Case-source note: BMW Regensburg transport fleet

Checked 2026-10-24. BMW Group’s [2024-10-29 Regensburg logistics release](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0445860EN/intelligently-connected-factory%3A-bmw-group-plant-regensburg-drives-digitalisation-and-automation-of-logistics-with-cloud-based-traffic-control-system) reports nearly 50 automated tugger trains and more than 140 Smart Transport Robots linked through its Automated Transport Services traffic system, handling approximately 10,000 part deliveries per workday. It says robots use onboard sensing to navigate around obstacles, while central ATS assigns routes and priorities; an assembly-line employee initiates a replenishment signal. BMW’s [contemporaneous photo of an employee operating the traffic system](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/photo/detail/P90575345/intelligent-vernetzte-fabrik-bmw-group-werk-regensburg%3A-mitarbeiter-bedient-cloudbasiertes-verkehrsleitsystem-10/2024?language=en) reinforces the human-operated element.

Limit: BMW is the operating company and has a promotional interest. Counts and performance claims are attributed to it, not independently audited; no measured baseline, failure rate, injury rate or net labor impact is established here. Robot obstacle navigation and centralized fleet scheduling do not demonstrate autonomous end-to-end manufacturing or lights-out plant safety. This is a historical operational snapshot, not current equipment or safety guidance.
