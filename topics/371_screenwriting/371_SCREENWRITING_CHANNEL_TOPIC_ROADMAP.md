## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Screenwriting across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Screenwriting Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Screenwriting.
- **Tier 2: Screenwriting Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Screenwriting.
- **Tier 3: Screenwriting Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Screenwriting.
- **Tier 4: Screenwriting Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Screenwriting.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Screenwriting: A Screenplay Form Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which dated production practice changed how a screenplay was written?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare surviving scripts and production demands. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
2. **Screenwriting: A Script and Its Finished Scene**
   - *Question:* What survived from a celebrated screenplay into the released sequence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated draft, shooting script, and film. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
3. **Screenwriting: The Writers Behind the Credit**
   - *Question:* Whose documented revisions shaped a screenplay?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace draft authorship and applicable credit rules. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
4. **Screenwriting: What a Scene Must Convey**
   - *Question:* How does action, dialogue, and silence deliver necessary information?
   - *Key Focus:* Annotate one bounded script scene and its screen version. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
5. **Screenwriting: Structure as a Choice**
   - *Question:* What does a particular structural pattern enable or conceal in a screenplay?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare causal beats with a viable alternative structure. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Screenwriting: A Disputed Writing Credit**
   - *Question:* Which contribution is documented and what remains uncertain?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated drafts and jurisdiction-specific credit records. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
7. **Screenwriting: Writing for a New Format**
   - *Question:* How did a format change affect one script's scene design?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versioned scripts and production constraints. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
8. **Screenwriting: A Writer's Revision Decision**
   - *Question:* Why did a writer change a documented scene or character?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts and attributed accounts; avoid invented motive. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
9. **Screenwriting: A Rewrite Under Pressure**
   - *Question:* What changed after a production decision or audience test?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated drafts and decision records. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
10. **Screenwriting: From Beat to Screen Action**
   - *Question:* How does one story beat become performable and shootable action?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow outline, scene text, and finished sequence. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Screenwriting: A Draft Idea Removed**
   - *Question:* What evidence explains why a subplot disappeared?
   - *Key Focus:* Check revision history rather than assume poor quality. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
12. **Screenwriting: A Script Across Two Eras**
   - *Question:* What differs in formatting or production function across dated examples?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare actual documents with their workflows. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
13. **Screenwriting: A New Writing Workflow**
   - *Question:* Which documented tool changes revision, collaboration, or attribution?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify production context and contractual terms. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
14. **Screenwriting: A Universal Screenplay Formula?**
   - *Question:* Does one famous structure fit diverse films and formats?
   - *Key Focus:* Test it on a bounded, varied sample and note exceptions. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
15. **Screenwriting: The Life of a Script**
   - *Question:* How did a story change from pitch through final cut?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow versioned artifacts and credited participants. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Screenwriting: Writing to a Brief**
   - *Question:* Which requirements shaped a first draft and which changed later?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, outline, revisions, and release. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
17. **Screenwriting: Drafts as Working Documents**
   - *Question:* What do markup, versioning, and scene numbers reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated copies and production annotations. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
18. **Screenwriting: A Scene's Revision Trail**
   - *Question:* Which changes sharpened stakes, action, or clarity?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare successive drafts with scene outcomes. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
19. **Screenwriting: Writing What Can Be Seen**
   - *Question:* When does dialogue explain what action could reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare scene variants and audience information. Evidence base: dated outlines and screenplay drafts, production revision pages, completed films, archive catalogs, participant accounts, and applicable guild credit rules.
20. **Screenwriting: Multiple Writers, One Screenplay**
   - *Question:* How were contributions divided and credited?
