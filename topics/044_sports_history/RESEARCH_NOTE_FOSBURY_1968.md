# Dick Fosbury at Mexico City 1968: bounded source-access note

Linked proposal: [BANK-044-069](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-044-069). Research checked 2026-10-24. This is a trail to governing-body evidence for one case; it does not mark the bank proposal as source-verified or production-ready.

## Establishable result

[World Athletics' athlete record](https://worldathletics.org/athletes/united-states/richard-douglas-fosbury-14351467) lists Dick Fosbury as the 1968 Olympic high-jump champion with a 2.24 m mark dated 20 October 1968. Its [historical account](https://worldathletics.org/heritage/news/dick-fosbury-flop) describes the back-layout technique and the attention it drew. The [IOC account](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/richard-fosbury-high-jump-revolution) likewise connects that technique to the Mexico City victory.

## Episode checks

1. Retrieve the official event result and attempt sequence before scripting the final jumps. Compare the contemporaneous result with the current athlete profile; a retrospective article alone cannot establish every attempt.
2. Inspect competition footage or photographs for the approach, clearance and landing, with rights and camera-angle limits recorded. A still image should not be treated as a complete biomechanical explanation.
3. Compare earlier back-layout experiments and the adoption timeline after 1968 before calling Fosbury the sole inventor or saying every elite jumper changed immediately. The question is about a documented turning point and diffusion, not a single moment of invention.
4. Distinguish his 2.24 m Olympic record from a world record. Use the governing body's dated record lists for any comparison across eras.
