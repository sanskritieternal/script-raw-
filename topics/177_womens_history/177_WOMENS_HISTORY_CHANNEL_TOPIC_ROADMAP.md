## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Women's History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Waves of Feminism (Suffrage, Workplace/Reproductive, Intersectional, Digital), Legal Coverture (Beginner / Scratch)** — The four waves of feminism, the doctrine of coverture (civil death of women upon marriage), the 1848 Seneca Falls Convention, and primary archival records of women's historical lives.
- **Tier 2: Forgotten Women of Science and Industry (Rosalind Franklin, Ada Lovelace, Radium Girls) (Intermediate)** — Matilda effect in scientific credit erasure (Rosalind Franklin's Photo 51), Ada Lovelace's first computer algorithm, the Radium Girls court battle establishing occupational disease law, and wartime industrial mobilization (Rosie the Riveter).
- **Tier 3: Matriarchies, Wartime Rosie the Riveter Industrial Mobilization & Domestic Labor Economics (Upper Intermediate)** — Anthropological matriarchies and matrilineal societies (Haudenosaunee clan mothers), the economic invisibility of unpaid domestic care work, the Lowell Mill Girls' early labor strikes, and intersectional Black suffrage (Sojourner Truth, Ida B. Wells).
- **Tier 4: Global Feminist Movements, Post-Colonial Feminisms & Institutional Power Parity (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Global non-Western feminist movements (the Gulabi Gang, Green Belt Movement), reproductive rights legal battles worldwide, corporate glass ceilings and gender wage gap economics, and archiving recovered subaltern histories.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Radium Girls: How Poisoned Dial Painters Fought Corporate Giants and Created Modern Labor Law**
   - *Question:* How did teenage factory girls who painted glowing watch dials with radioactive paint fight the US Radium Corporation from their deathbeds and win?
   - *Key Focus:* The 'lip, dip, paint' technique swallowing radium, radium jaw necrosis, corporate medical coverups, Grace Fryer's courageous lawsuit, the establishment of OSHA workplace safety standards, and occupational disease law.
2. **The Doctrine of Coverture: When Marriage Was Legal 'Civil Death' for Women**
   - *Question:* Why did English and American law decree that upon marriage, a woman's legal identity ceased to exist, transferring all property and wages to her husband?
   - *Key Focus:* William Blackstone's Commentaries on the Laws of England ('husband and wife are one person in law'), inability to sign contracts, own property, or keep wages, and the 19th-century Married Women's Property Acts.
3. **The Four Waves of Feminism: From the Ballot Box to Digital Intersectional Activism**
   - *Question:* How did the feminist struggle evolve from fighting for basic voting rights in 1900 to demanding bodily autonomy, economic equity, and intersectional justice?
   - *Key Focus:* 1st Wave (suffrage, legal rights), 2nd Wave (reproduction, workplace, The Feminine Mystique), 3rd Wave (riot grrrl, queer theory, Kimberly Crenshaw's intersectionality), and 4th Wave (#MeToo, digital solidarity).
4. **The Matilda Effect: How Men Stole the Nobel Prizes of Female Scientists**
   - *Question:* Why did James Watson and Francis Crick get the Nobel Prize for discovering the double helix while Rosalind Franklin's Photo 51 was taken without her consent?
   - *Key Focus:* Matilda Joslyn Gage's credit erasure theory, Jocelyn Bell Burnell discovering pulsars, Lise Meitner calculating nuclear fission, Chien-Shiung Wu disproving parity, and the systematic erasure of women from the history of science.
5. **The Lowell Mill Girls: The Teenage Farm Daughters Who Led America's First Factory Strikes**
   - *Question:* How did young New England farm women living in strict factory boardinghouses organize the 1834 and 1836 'turnouts' that terrified mill barons?
   - *Key Focus:* Francis Cabot Lowell's industrial experiment, 14-hour workday deafening textile looms, wage cuts sparking spontaneous walkouts, the Lowell Offering literary magazine, and petitions for the 10-hour workday.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **Sojourner Truth and Ida B. Wells: The Unflinching Vanguard of Black Women's Suffrage**
   - *Question:* Why did white suffrage leaders ask Black women to march in the back of the 1913 Washington Parade, and how did Ida B. Wells refuse and march up front?
   - *Key Focus:* Sojourner Truth's 1851 'Ain't I a Woman?' speech, Ida B. Wells investigating Southern lynchings with investigative journalism, the Alpha Suffrage Club, and the intersectional fight against racism and sexism simultaneously.
7. **Haudenosaunee Clan Mothers: The Indigenous Democracy That Inspired American Feminism**
   - *Question:* How did the matrilineal democracy of the Iroquois Confederacy give Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Matilda Gage the vision for women's political equality?
   - *Key Focus:* Haudenosaunee Clan Mothers holding sole authority to appoint and impeach male chiefs, women retaining property, lineage, and physical bodily safety, and early suffragists being shocked by European patriarchal inferiority.
8. **Rosie the Riveter: The World War II Factory Mobilization and the Post-War Betrayal**
   - *Question:* How did six million women build B-17 bombers and battleships in round-the-clock shipyards, only to be fired and told to go back to the kitchen in 1945?
   - *Key Focus:* J. Howard Miller's 'We Can Do It!' Westinghouse poster reality, breaking racial and gender hiring bans in defense plants, the Kaiser Shipyards federally subsidized 24-hour daycare, and the post-war psychological friction.
9. **The Night Witches: The Soviet Female Bomber Pilots Who Terrified the Nazi Military**
   - *Question:* How did the 588th Night Bomber Regiment fly 30,000 nocturnal combat missions in wood-and-canvas biplanes with no parachutes, radios, or radar?
   - *Key Focus:* Colonel Marina Raskova, the Polikarpov Po-2 biplane gliding with cut engines in pitch darkness (the 'whooshing broom' sound that terrified German troops), female mechanics arming bombs in -40°C blizzard, and 23 Heroes of the Soviet Union.
10. **The Pill: The Biochemical Breakthrough That Unlocked Female Autonomy**
   - *Question:* How did activist Margaret Sanger, philanthropist Katharine McCormick, and biologist Gregory Pincus secretly finance and create oral contraception?
   - *Key Focus:* Enovid FDA approval in 1960, Puerto Rico clinical trial ethical controversies, decoupling sexual intimacy from inevitable pregnancy, the dramatic surge in female university graduation and legal/medical careers.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **The Wages for Housework Campaign: The Unpaid Bill of Domestic Labor**
   - *Question:* Why does classical capitalism treat cooking, cleaning, and childrearing as zero-cost 'acts of love' while relying on it to reproduce the daily workforce?
   - *Key Focus:* Silvia Federici's Social Reproduction Theory, the 1972 International Wages for Housework Campaign, calculating global unpaid care work at $11 trillion annually, and modern debates over universal parental stipends.
12. **The Seneca Falls Convention: How 300 People Launched the Modern Fight for Equality**
   - *Question:* How did Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott draft the Declaration of Sentiments in 1848 by deliberately rewording the US Declaration of Independence?
   - *Key Focus:* 'All men and women are created equal', the controversial demand for the elective franchise (voting), Frederick Douglass' pivotal speech in support, public ridicule in contemporary newspapers, and setting a 72-year legislative march.
13. **The Suffragette Militancy: Hunger Strikes, Cat and Mouse Acts, and Martyrdom**
   - *Question:* Why did Emmeline Pankhurst's Women's Social and Political Union abandon peaceful protest to smash windows, burn mailboxes, and endure forced feeding?
   - *Key Focus:* 'Deeds, not words' militancy, the brutal reality of nasal tube force-feeding in Holloway Prison, the 1913 Cat and Mouse Act (Prisoners' Temporary Discharge for Ill-Health), Emily Wilding Davison's death at the Epsom Derby, and ethical debate over radical flank effects.
14. **Wangari Maathai and the Green Belt Movement: Planting Millions of Trees as Democratic Resistance**
   - *Question:* How did a Kenyan biologist mobilize rural women to plant 50 million trees, defeating deforestation and Daniel arap Moi's authoritarian regime?
   - *Key Focus:* Ecofeminism in practice, soil erosion mitigation, rural women's firewood and clean water access, enduring state police beatings in Uhuru Park, and becoming the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize (2004).
15. **The Real 'Hidden Figures': The Human Computers Who Guided NASA to the Moon**
   - *Question:* How did Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, and Mary Jackson calculate orbital trajectories on paper for John Glenn during the Jim Crow segregation era?
   - *Key Focus:* The West Area Computing unit at Langley, solving trajectory equations by hand with Marchant mechanical calculators, calculating Apollo 11 trans-lunar injection, integrating IBM 7090 mainframes, and Presidential Medals of Freedom.
16. **The Gulabi Gang: The Pink Sari Vigilantes Fighting Violence in Rural India**
   - *Question:* How did Sampat Pal Devi unite 400,000 impoverished village women armed with bamboo lathi sticks to confront corrupt police and abusive husbands in Uttar Pradesh?
   - *Key Focus:* Pink sari uniform symbolism, intervening in domestic violence and dowry deaths, surrounding corrupt police stations until charges are filed, organizing self-defense classes, and challenging entrenched caste and patriarchal hierarchy.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **The Glass Ceiling and the Motherhood Penalty: The Unspoken Economics of Career Stagnation**
   - *Question:* Why does the gender wage gap remain nearly flat in early twenties careers, only to blow wide open the moment a woman has her first child?
   - *Key Focus:* Claudia Goldin's 2023 Nobel Memorial Prize research, 'greedy work' hours penalizing schedule flexibility, paternity leave disparities, the fatherhood bonus vs motherhood penalty, and structural workplace design reforms.
18. **Recovering Lost Her-Stories: Decolonizing and Archiving Subaltern Women's Lives**
   - *Question:* How do contemporary feminist historians reconstruct the lives of enslaved women, indigenous midwives, and factory workers whose names were deliberately omitted from colonial archives?
   - *Key Focus:* Saidiya Hartman's 'Critical Fabulation' methodology, reading archives against the grain, oral storytelling lineages, textiles and quilt codes as historical texts, and digital open-access feminist archival repositories.
19. **The Business of Suffrage: The Funding Networks, Merchandising, and Wealth Behind the Movement**
   - *Question:* Who funded the million-dollar printing presses, nationwide train tours, and purple-white-and-green merchandise that sustained the suffrage fight for decades?
   - *Key Focus:* Gilded Age heiress Alva Belmont's multi-million-dollar funding, the Suffrage Shop retail stores, pins, sashes, and postcards merchandising, state-by-state campaign travel budgeting, and wealthy female philanthropic cartels.
20. **The Masterclass on Feminist Historiography: How to Research and Write Restorative Women's Biographies**
   - *Question:* How does an elite biographer rescue an extraordinary woman from historical obscurity and write a definitive, academically rigorous biography?
   - *Key Focus:* Navigating male-centered indexing in national archives, cross-referencing maiden names through legal estate deeds, contextualizing patriarchal societal constraints without minimizing agency, and crafting compelling narrative nonfiction.
