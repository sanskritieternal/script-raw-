# Selected source and boundary note — 392 Stand-Up & Comedy History

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-392-044**: Phyllis Diller's gag file as a record of repertoire organization.

## Institutional object evidence

- Smithsonian National Museum of American History, [*The Gag File*](https://americanhistory.si.edu/documentsgallery/exhibitions/diller/3.html), describes Diller's 48-drawer cabinet with more than 50,000 typewritten joke cards organized by subject. It names writer Mary MacBride as one contributor and says attributed contributions appear on cards. This is a museum account of a preserved work object, not a complete transcript of any one performance.
- The Smithsonian's [2017 digitization account](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/help-us-transcribe-phyllis-dillers-jokes-and-enjoy-some-laughs-along-way) reports 52,569 cards and a transcription project. The exact inventory count and access state are institution-specific and dated; check current item records before quoting a card or claiming all cards are accessible.

## Limits and overlaps

A card can document stored material and, when marked, contributor credit; it cannot independently establish joke authorship, a set list, delivery, audience reaction, or when and where a bit was performed. Pair any performance claim with dated audio, video, program or venue evidence and permissions. Quote only brief extracts under applicable rights. This is stand-up performance history and archival method, distinct from **BANK-378-032**'s fictional comedy reveal and **BANK-080-030**'s film timing analysis. It adds a named case to **BANK-392-034** and **BANK-392-039** without claiming a first or inventor. New proposal remains unapproved with empty verified_sources; no IDEA readiness is asserted.
