# Selected source and boundary note — 393 Interior Design

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-393-044**: the built-in alcove sofa at the Eames House.

## Primary steward's account

- The [Eames Foundation's Alcove Sofa account](https://eamesfoundation.org/work/alcove-sofa/) states that the main living room has a 17-foot ceiling while the alcove below the bedroom is eight feet high, and that a standard sofa would not fit the alcove. It describes Charles and Ray Eames's built-in seating, kitchen pass-through, and magazine rack added in the first five years. The Foundation is steward of the site and the account supports a bounded spatial-design case. It does not provide dimensional drawings, user testing or evidence that the solution fits other homes.
- The Foundation's [conservation management page](https://eamesfoundation.org/eames-house/conservation-management/) points to a conservation plan prepared with the Getty Conservation Institute. Historic fabric and interior access are managed; any filming, image reuse or intervention would require current site and rights approval.

## Limits and overlaps

The documented room is a historical, site-specific interior, not a current residential accessibility or building-code recommendation. Do not infer visitor comfort, universal ergonomic benefit or cost from the Foundation narrative. **BANK-393-036** addresses general small-room furniture and circulation; **BANK-393-034** addresses replaceable specifications. The new case examines how this particular built-in and later alteration answered the Eameses' own use of the alcove. Architecture and heritage-restoration banks may treat the building fabric or conservation process; this bank stays with room-use and furnishing evidence.
