## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Interior Design across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Interior Design Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Interior Design.
- **Tier 2: Interior Design Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Interior Design.
- **Tier 3: Interior Design Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Interior Design.
- **Tier 4: Interior Design Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Interior Design.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Interior Design: A Room Plan in Its Period**
   - *Question:* Which use, material, or household constraint shaped a dated interior?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare plans, photographs, and occupant records. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
2. **Interior Design: A Celebrated Interior Rechecked**
   - *Question:* Which spatial choices actually support daily use in a named room?
   - *Key Focus:* Use measured plan and occupant evidence, not photographs alone. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
3. **Interior Design: Who Made the Room?**
   - *Question:* Whose installation, cleaning, repair, and household work does a design account omit?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace labor records and user accounts. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
4. **Interior Design: Reading a Room Plan**
   - *Question:* How do circulation, light, storage, and furniture fit a measured space?
   - *Key Focus:* Show dimensions and intended users. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
5. **Interior Design: Tradeoffs in an Interior**
   - *Question:* How do acoustics, air, light, privacy, and access compete?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare options against explicit requirements and measured conditions. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Interior Design: An Unverified Design Claim**
   - *Question:* Does a marketed layout solve the problem it promises?
   - *Key Focus:* Define outcome and seek use evidence or mark gap. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
7. **Interior Design: A New Interior Product**
   - *Question:* Which task does a device or material change in practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented performance, cost, and maintenance. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
8. **Interior Design: A Designer's Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a documented plan change after occupant feedback?
   - *Key Focus:* Use versioned drawings and attributed interviews. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
9. **Interior Design: A Home Renovation**
   - *Question:* What changed for residents after a specific remodel?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare before and after access, use, cost, and disruption. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
10. **Interior Design: From Brief to Installed Room**
   - *Question:* Which decisions connect measurements, layout, procurement, and use?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated plans, invoices, and postoccupancy evidence. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Interior Design: A Design Trend Reconsidered**
   - *Question:* What practical costs or user needs changed a once-popular layout?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated examples and occupant reports. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
12. **Interior Design: One Room across Renovations**
   - *Question:* Which functions improved or worsened between two plans?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dimensions, use, and household composition. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
13. **Interior Design: A Proposed Interior Upgrade**
   - *Question:* What budget, code, access, and maintenance questions remain?
   - *Key Focus:* Treat concept images as conditional plans. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
14. **Interior Design: A Room Looks Bigger?**
   - *Question:* What do photographs hide about clearances and actual capacity?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare lens view with measured floor plan. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
15. **Interior Design: A Room's Design Trail**
   - *Question:* How did brief, plans, installation, and feedback reshape it?
   - *Key Focus:* Use versions and credit every contributor. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Interior Design: Living with the Layout**
   - *Question:* Which daily tasks became easier or harder after a redesign?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented occupant accounts and a defined follow-up period. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
17. **Interior Design: Approving an Interior Change**
   - *Question:* Which landlord, building, or permit decision controls a proposed alteration?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace actual authority and jurisdiction. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
18. **Interior Design: Why a Room Looks This Way**
   - *Question:* How do budget, resale expectations, and household norms shape the plan?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, quotes, and resident priorities. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
19. **Interior Design: Who Can Use the Room?**
   - *Question:* Which route, reach, sensory, or cost barriers exclude users?
   - *Key Focus:* Test with named user needs and applicable rules. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
20. **Interior Design: Different Households, Same Floor Plan**
   - *Question:* How do varied household routines change a layout's usefulness?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare situated use without universalizing. Evidence base: dimensioned plans, dated briefs and revisions, product specifications, applicable access guidance, itemized costs, and consented occupant evidence.
