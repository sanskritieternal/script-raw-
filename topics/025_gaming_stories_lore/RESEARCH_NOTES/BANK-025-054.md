# BANK-025-054 — Corrupted Blood and epidemiology claims

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/8` title says World of Warcraft's Corrupted Blood incident “became real CDC pandemic data.” This note checks that claim against the early published research record.

## Inspected primary publications

- Ran D. Balicer, [“Modeling infectious diseases dissemination through online role-playing games,” *Epidemiology* 18 (2007), 260–261](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17301707/), DOI 10.1097/01.ede.0000254692.80550.60. The abstract discusses an **unplanned virtual outbreak** and the possible value of online games for infectious-disease modelling. It is a proposal and discussion, not a CDC dataset or proof that the outbreak predicts a real epidemic.
- Eric T. Lofgren and Nina H. Fefferman, [“The untapped potential of virtual game worlds to shed light on real world epidemics,” *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* 7 (2007), 625–629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17714675/). The abstract uses the accidental game event to argue that virtual worlds could help address limits of modelling human behavior. It does not establish that CDC used Corrupted Blood as pandemic data.

## Claim disposition

These papers substantiate scholarly interest in the incident, not the archived CDC-data headline. No primary CDC record or released Blizzard dataset was found in this bounded search. Do not repeat either claim without a dated direct source. A selected episode needs the relevant game version, a documented chronology and mechanics from Blizzard or archived gameplay, the full papers and any actual data-access history. Distinguish player observations from logged population data and model validation.
