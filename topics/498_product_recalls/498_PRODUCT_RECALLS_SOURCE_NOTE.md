# Product recalls source follow-up - 2026-10-24

## BANK-498-043 - Philips Respironics 2021 breathing-device recall

- Primary regulator overview: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, *Recalled Philips Ventilators, BiPAP Machines, and CPAP Machines*, April 2024 update, https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/respiratory-devices/recalled-philips-ventilators-bipap-machines-and-cpap-machines (inspected 2026-10-24). FDA describes the June 2021 foam-related recall, affected device families and the April 2024 federal consent decree. The decree requires a Recall Remediation Plan with new, reworked or remediated devices or, for certain devices, a partial-refund option.
- Primary regulator chronology: FDA, *FDA Activities Related to Recalled Philips Ventilators, BiPAP Machines, and CPAP Machines*, https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/recalled-philips-ventilators-bipap-machines-and-cpap-machines/fda-activities-related-recalled-philips-ventilators-bipap-machines-and-cpap-machines (inspected 2026-10-24). It records a March 2022 notification order and the agency's April 2023 caution that a count of shipped replacements and repair kits did not equal the number of completed consumer remedies.
- Limits: This is a U.S. FDA oversight case involving specific affected medical devices and a dated legal remedy. A recall listing or shipment count does not prove the exact household unit is included, repaired, safe for a particular patient, or that every user has received relief. Recall status and remediation progress may change. Do not give advice on stopping or changing respiratory therapy; clinical decisions belong with a qualified provider and current FDA notice. Distinguish this case from toy and EV battery recalls.

## Scope and overlap

BANK-498-043 follows FDA notification and remediation evidence. BANK-421-047 owns the GM Bolt battery filing; BANK-497-036 owns warranty versus statutory rights. No automatic equivalence between a medical-device recall and a consumer warranty remedy.
