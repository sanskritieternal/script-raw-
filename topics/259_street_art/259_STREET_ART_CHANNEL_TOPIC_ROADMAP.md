## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Street Art across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Street Art Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Street Art.
- **Tier 2: Street Art Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Street Art.
- **Tier 3: Street Art Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Street Art.
- **Tier 4: Street Art Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Street Art.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Street Art: A City's Early Street-Art Record**
   - *Question:* What dated images or accounts show a local scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish earliest documentation from first work. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
2. **Street Art: A Viral Mural Revisited**
   - *Question:* What does a famous wall mean in its neighborhood?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare artist intent, resident views, ownership, and tourism. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
3. **Street Art: Work Around the Wall**
   - *Question:* Who prepares, permits, photographs, cleans, or removes a piece?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace labor and institutional roles. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
4. **Street Art: Reading Street Art in Place**
   - *Question:* How do image, surface, location, and legality shape meaning?
   - *Key Focus:* Name artist or anonymous status, site, date, and context. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
5. **Street Art: Public Art and Property**
   - *Question:* Who controls an image on a privately owned public-facing wall?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine permission, rights, maintenance, and jurisdiction. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Street Art: Who Made an Unsigned Work**
   - *Question:* Which attribution evidence is credible?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare artist statements, dated images, technique, and uncertainty. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
7. **Street Art: A Street Work Goes Online**
   - *Question:* How did mapping or social media alter its audience?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credit, location exposure, copying, and local impact. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
8. **Street Art: An Artist's Public Decision**
   - *Question:* Why did a named artist choose one site or collaboration?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented interviews and dated proposals. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
9. **Street Art: A Wall After Redevelopment**
   - *Question:* How did one project change the art and surrounding community?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace permits, removal, relocation, and resident response. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
10. **Street Art: From Proposal to Wall**
   - *Question:* How does a permitted public artwork get made?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace artist, owner, community, materials, access, and maintenance. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Street Art: A District's Changing Wall Count**
   - *Question:* Does a claimed boom reflect new work or better documentation?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated surveys, turnover, and definitions. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
12. **Street Art: One Wall Across Years**
   - *Question:* What happened to the image and its context?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated photographs, repair records, and ownership changes. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
13. **Street Art: What Happens to a Temporary Work**
   - *Question:* Which choices do artist and community favor?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare repainting, documentation, removal, and consent. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
14. **Street Art: Always Unauthorized?**
   - *Question:* How do permissions and commissioning vary?
   - *Key Focus:* Check a named work's agreement and jurisdiction. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
15. **Street Art: One Mural's Life**
   - *Question:* How was it created, received, altered, and removed?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a dated record with artist and neighbor voices. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Street Art: An Image for a Public Wall**
   - *Question:* Which site and audience constraints shape the commission?
   - *Key Focus:* Document artist aims, permissions, surface, access, and budget. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
17. **Street Art: Paint on an Outdoor Surface**
   - *Question:* How do wall and pigment choices affect longevity?
   - *Key Focus:* Use artist and conservator evidence, not application instructions. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
18. **Street Art: Making a Permitted Mural**
   - *Question:* Which stages involve consultation and revision?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace proposal, agreement, preparation, execution, and care. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
19. **Street Art: Image at Street Scale**
   - *Question:* How does viewing distance or movement shape composition?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare sketches, site photographs, and artist explanation. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
20. **Street Art: Who Owns the Idea**
   - *Question:* How do artist, assistants, residents, and sponsor influence the work?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace credit and decision rights. Evidence base: dated site photographs and proposals, artist and property permissions, municipal records, conservation reports, and consented resident accounts.
