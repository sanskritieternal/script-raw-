# Selected primary-source note: global economy

Checked 2026-10-24. Proposed cases BANK-311-042 and BANK-311-043.

The IMF World Economic Outlook April and October 2022 database archives preserve dated forecast vintages: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO/weo-database/2022/April/ and https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO/weo-database/2022/October . The October 2022 report gives then-current global growth and inflation projections and lists inflation, financial tightening, the war in Ukraine and COVID-19 as context: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WEO/Issues/2022/10/11/world-economic-outlook-october-2022 . Compare the same forecast year and variable in each release. Revisions are a record of changing information and assumptions, not proof that any single event caused the difference or that either vintage matched eventual outcomes. Cite vintage, units and aggregate method.

The International Labour Organization's Global Wage Report 2022-23 analyzes average wages, employment composition and total real wage bills over 2019-22: https://webapps.ilo.org/static/english/reports/flagship/global_wage_report_2022-2023/3.%20Wage%20trends%20in%20the%20context%20of%20the%20COVID-19%20crisis%20and%20rising%20price%20inflation.html ; executive summary https://webapps.ilo.org/static/english/reports/flagship/global_wage_report_2022-2023/executive%20summary.html . The ILO explains that loss of low-paid jobs can raise the average among remaining wage employees even while total earnings fall. Report years, country/sample coverage, wage versus wage-bill definition, inflation adjustment and possible revisions. Do not infer every worker's trajectory or current wage conditions from this 2022 publication.
