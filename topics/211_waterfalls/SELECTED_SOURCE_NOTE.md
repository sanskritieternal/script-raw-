# Selected primary-source checks - Waterfalls
Checked 2026-10-24. Selected questions remain proposed; no access, safety or production readiness is implied.

- BANK-211-037: Niagara Parks winter-flow explanation, https://www.niagaraparks.com/things-to-do/frozen-falls/ . Spray and river ice can make a frozen-looking surface while water flows beneath and over the falls. The published diversion and flow figures are agency descriptions of a management regime, not timeless constants or evidence of a fully stopped waterfall.
- BANK-211-038: USGS 1996 original Havasu Creek flood investigation, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/wri964059 . Historical photos, flow records and field evidence document flood-driven changes to travertine pools, falls and riparian vegetation. This site and time period do not predict current shapes or access. The Havasupai Tribe's official page describes its homeland and access context, https://havasupaireservations.com/ ; access details can change and must be checked with the Tribe before any visitor-facing material.
- BANK-211-021: NOAA explains the density-driven Denmark Strait cataract, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/largest-waterfall.html . It is an undersea current, not a free-falling open-air river.
- BANK-153-019: NSF polar program account describes iron-rich brine at Antarctic Blood Falls, https://www.nsf.gov/geo/opp/documents/NSF%20Science%20on%20the%20Ice_fifth_edition.pdf . That named polar case is already active elsewhere; the release trigger remains uncertain.
