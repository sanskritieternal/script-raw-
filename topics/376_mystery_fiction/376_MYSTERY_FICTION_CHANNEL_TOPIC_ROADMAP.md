## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Mystery Fiction across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Mystery Fiction Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Mystery Fiction.
- **Tier 2: Mystery Fiction Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Mystery Fiction.
- **Tier 3: Mystery Fiction Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Mystery Fiction.
- **Tier 4: Mystery Fiction Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Mystery Fiction.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Mystery Fiction: A Detective Form Develops**
   - *Question:* Which dated publication changed how clues and inquiry were narrated?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare original texts and publishing context without a single-origin claim. Evidence base: identified editions, dated drafts and outlines, clue inventories, publication and adaptation records, relevant historical sources, and situated reader responses.
2. **Mystery Fiction: A Famous Reveal Rechecked**
   - *Question:* Were the clues for a celebrated solution actually available to readers?
   - *Key Focus:* List each clue by page and time of disclosure. Evidence base: identified editions, dated drafts and outlines, clue inventories, publication and adaptation records, relevant historical sources, and situated reader responses.
3. **Mystery Fiction: Investigators Beyond the Canon**
   - *Question:* Which overlooked narrators or writers changed mystery fiction?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated editions, archives, and reception. Evidence base: identified editions, dated drafts and outlines, clue inventories, publication and adaptation records, relevant historical sources, and situated reader responses.
4. **Mystery Fiction: Question, Clue, Solution**
   - *Question:* How does a mystery make its central question answerable?
   - *Key Focus:* Map evidence, inference, and final explanation in one short work. Evidence base: identified editions, dated drafts and outlines, clue inventories, publication and adaptation records, relevant historical sources, and situated reader responses.
5. **Mystery Fiction: Unreliable Evidence in a Mystery**
