# Selected source and boundary note — 404 Knitting

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-404-044** and **BANK-404-045** are separate named object and print-instruction cases.

## Primary institutional collection account

The Victoria and Albert Museum, [*The history of hand-knitting*](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-history-of-hand-knitting), identifies its Italian jacket **473-1893** as dated 1600–1620. It describes plain silk and partly silver-wrapped yarn, flat-knitted back and two front pieces joined together, silk-lined sleeves and silver-thread buttonholes. The article says Italian panel import and client assembly have been suggested, not proven. The same museum account identifies **T.307&A-1979**, *The National Society's Instructions on Needlework and Knitting* (1838), as a charity-school publication and cautions that its early written directions were difficult to follow without a teacher. These two physical collection items support distinct construction and teaching questions. The accessible museum article summarizes them; a complete technical examination of the jacket and full page-level analysis of the book remain follow-up work.

## Limits, attribution and overlap

Do not infer the jacket's individual maker, wearer, exact panel trade route or original fit from this summary. Do not present the 1838 book as a stand-alone self-teaching success or describe all nineteenth-century knitters from one school text. V&A object images are credited to the museum; evaluate reproduction permission separately. The jacket case complements **BANK-404-041**'s generic seaming choice while keeping historical material evidence distinct from **BANK-403-039**'s stretch-fabric seam failure and textile/weaving techniques. The schoolbook case concerns knitting instruction within a book that also covers needlework; it does not replace the sewing bank's construction questions. Both new topics stay proposed with empty verified_sources; no IDEA readiness is asserted.
