## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Waste Management across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Waste Management Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Waste Management.
- **Tier 2: Waste Management Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Waste Management.
- **Tier 3: Waste Management Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Waste Management.
- **Tier 4: Waste Management Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Waste Management.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Waste Management: How Waste Management Developed**
   - *Question:* How did a municipality change its waste collection and disposal system?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one municipality's waste system from earlier disposal practices to a dated reform; use service and policy records rather than tidy progress narratives. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: weighed inputs and outputs, contracts, destination records and waste-worker accounts. Starting framework: US EPA materials, waste and recycling data (https://www.epa.gov/facts-and-figures-about-materials-waste-and-recycling). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: weighed inputs and outputs, contracts, destination records and waste-worker accounts. Starting framework: US EPA materials, waste and recycling data (https://www.epa.gov/facts-and-figures-about-materials-waste-and-recycling).
