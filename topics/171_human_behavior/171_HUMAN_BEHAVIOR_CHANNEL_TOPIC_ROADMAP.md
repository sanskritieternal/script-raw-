## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Human Behavior across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Cognitive Biases, Heuristics & System 1 vs System 2 Thinking (Beginner / Scratch)** — Daniel Kahneman's dual-system framework, availability heuristic, anchoring bias, confirmation bias, loss aversion, and fundamental attribution error in everyday judgment.
- **Tier 2: Evolutionary Psychology, Social Status Games & Mating Signals (Intermediate)** — Costly signaling theory, status hierarchies and dominance vs prestige, Dunbar's number (150 social limit), in-group/out-group tribal cognition, and evolutionary mismatched environments.
- **Tier 3: Mob Psychology, Herd Contagion & De-individuation Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Gustave Le Bon's crowd psychology, informational vs normative social influence, bystander effect diffusion of responsibility, panic stampede fluid dynamics, and moral outrage cascades.
- **Tier 4: Behavioral Economics Nudges, Choice Architecture & Neurobiology of Willpower (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Thaler and Sunstein's nudge theory, default opt-in choice architecture, dopamine reward prediction error loops, ego depletion controversies, and designing behavioral interventions for public health.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **Thinking, Fast and Slow: How Daniel Kahneman's Two Systems Dictate Every Human Choice**
   - *Question:* Why does your brain rely on an automatic, unconscious mental autopilot for 95% of daily decisions, and how does it trick you into costly blunders?
   - *Key Focus:* System 1 (fast, instinctive, emotional) vs System 2 (slow, deliberative, logical), cognitive ease, the Linda problem, prospect theory, and Kahneman's 2002 Nobel Prize.
2. **Loss Aversion: Why Losing $100 Hurts Twice as Much as Winning $100 Feels Good**
   - *Question:* Why will people take reckless financial risks to avoid a guaranteed loss, but play it ultra-safe when facing guaranteed gains?
   - *Key Focus:* The S-shaped value curve of prospect theory, the endowment effect (overvaluing things simply because you own them), sunk cost fallacies, and marketing trial periods.
3. **Anchoring Bias: How a Random Number Dictates What You Are Willing to Pay**
   - *Question:* How do luxury jewelers, real estate agents, and restaurant menus use high decoy numbers to manipulate your perception of value?
   - *Key Focus:* Kahneman and Tversky's wheel-of-fortune experiment, the first-number advantage in salary negotiations, 'was $199, now $99' retail tricks, and cognitive adjustment failure.
4. **Confirmation Bias: Why Facts Don't Change Our Minds**
   - *Question:* Why do two opposing people look at the exact same scientific study and both walk away feeling even more convinced they were right all along?
   - *Key Focus:* Motivated reasoning, identity-protective cognition (Dan Kahan), selective exposure in search algorithms, backfire effect nuances, and active epistemic humility techniques.
5. **Dunbar's Number: Why Your Brain Can Only Handle 150 Real Relationships**
   - *Question:* How did Robin Dunbar predict that humans can only maintain 150 stable friendships based on the size of our primate neocortex?
   - *Key Focus:* Neocortex ratio scaling in primates, hunter-gatherer village sizes, Roman military century units (maniple), Gore-Tex factory size caps (150 workers), and social media superficiality.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **Costly Signaling Theory: Why Peacocks Grow Giant Tails and Humans Buy Rolexes**
   - *Question:* Why do biological organisms waste immense energy on bright feathers, heavy antlers, and diamond watches that provide zero survival utility?
   - *Key Focus:* Amotz Zahavi's handicap principle, unforgeable honesty of expensive signals, conspicuous consumption (Thorstein Veblen), philanthropic donations as status displays, and virtue signaling.
7. **The Bystander Effect: Why a Crowd of 50 People is Less Likely to Help Than a Single Stranger**
   - *Question:* What happens in a crowded subway station when someone collapses, and why does everyone freeze waiting for someone else to act?
   - *Key Focus:* Diffusion of responsibility, pluralistic ignorance (looking at others' calm faces to gauge danger), the Kitty Genovese myth vs reality, and how to break the bystander spell by singling out individuals.
8. **The Dunning-Kruger Effect: Why the Least Competent People Are the Most Confident**
   - *Question:* Why does a complete beginner on day two feel like an expert, while real PhDs suffer from crushing imposter syndrome?
   - *Key Focus:* David Dunning and Justin Kruger's 1999 paper, lack of metacognitive awareness preventing people from recognizing their own incompetence, Mount Stupid vs Valley of Despair, and intellectual modesty.
9. **Mob Mentality: The Terrifying Psychology of De-individuation in Crowds**
   - *Question:* How do peaceful, law-abiding citizens turn into violent rioters when absorbed into a screaming, anonymous stadium crowd?
   - *Key Focus:* Gustave Le Bon's The Crowd (1895), loss of individual self-awareness, diffusion of moral agency under anonymity, emergent norm theory, soccer hooliganism, and online digital lynch mobs.
10. **Nudge Theory: How Changing a Default Option Can Save Billions in Healthcare and Retirement**
   - *Question:* Why does switching an organ donation form from 'check box to opt in' to 'check box to opt out' increase donor rates from 15% to 99%?
   - *Key Focus:* Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein's libertarian paternalism, automatic enrollment 401(k) savings plans, plate size psychology in buffets, fly stickers in Schiphol urinals, and ethical boundaries of nudging.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **The Fundamental Attribution Error: Why We Blame Others' Character but Excuse Our Own Circumstances**
   - *Question:* When someone cuts you off in traffic, why do you assume they are an aggressive jerk, but when you do it, you excuse yourself because you were late for an interview?
   - *Key Focus:* Lee Ross' 1977 formulation, actor-observer asymmetry, dispositional vs situational attributions, cultural differences (Western individualist vs East Asian collectivist attribution), and building empathy.
12. **Dopamine and Variable Reward Schedules: Why Slot Machines and TikTok Keep You Hooked**
   - *Question:* How does unpredictable intermittent reward release more dopamine than guaranteed rewards, turning scrolling feeds into addiction machines?
   - *Key Focus:* B.F. Skinner's operant conditioning chambers (variable ratio reinforcement), Wolfram Schultz's dopamine reward prediction error, pull-to-refresh swipe mechanics, and casino sensory architecture.
13. **Moral Outrage Contagion: Why Social Algorithms Run on Anger**
   - *Question:* Why do tweets and video clips containing moral-emotional words spread 20% faster for every additional emotional adjective included?
   - *Key Focus:* Molly Crockett's research on digital moral outrage, low cost of online signaling vs physical protest risk, virtue reinforcement from in-group peers, algorithmic engagement amplification, and civic exhaustion.
14. **The Hedonic Treadmill: Why Winning the Lottery Doesn't Make You Permanently Happy**
   - *Question:* Why do lottery winners and accident survivors returning to identical baseline happiness levels just one year after their life-altering events?
   - *Key Focus:* Brickman and Campbell's hedonic adaptation hypothesis, set-point theory of happiness, the evolutionary utility of never being permanently satisfied, and intentional activities that actually boost lasting well-being.
15. **The Decoy Effect: How a Fake Option Manipulates You into Buying the Most Expensive Drink**
   - *Question:* Why does adding a 'print-only' option for $125 persuade 84% of readers to buy the 'print + digital' bundle for the exact same price?
   - *Key Focus:* Dan Ariely's famous The Economist subscription experiment, Asymmetric Dominance Principle, altering choice sets to make a target option look like a no-brainer steal, and movie theater popcorn pricing.
16. **Halo and Horn Effects: How One Single Trait Distorts Everything People Think About You**
   - *Question:* Why do handsome courtroom defendants receive lighter prison sentences, and why do taller CEOs earn significantly higher corporate compensation?
   - *Key Focus:* Edward Thorndike's 1920 discovery, cognitive spillover from one positive attribute (physical attractiveness) to unrelated virtues (intelligence, kindness, competence), horn effect demonization, and blind auditions.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **Tribalism and the Minimal Group Paradigm: Why We Form Gangs Over Tossed Coins**
   - *Question:* How did Henri Tajfel prove that sorting random strangers into 'Group X' and 'Group W' based on a coin flip makes them instantly discriminate against outsiders?
   - *Key Focus:* Social Identity Theory, ingroup favoritism without pre-existing animosity, maximizing the relative advantage of one's own group even at the expense of absolute gains, and de-polarization strategies.
18. **The Ultimatum Game: Why Humans Choose to Punish Unfairness Even at a Personal Cost**
   - *Question:* If someone offers you $1 out of $100 and you either accept it or you both get zero, why do 80% of humans reject the free dollar out of pure spite?
   - *Key Focus:* Homo economicus rationality breakdown, altruistic punishment as an evolutionary enforcement mechanism for community cooperation, cross-cultural Machiguenga vs Western responses, and neural insula activation.
19. **The Business of Behavioral Manipulation: How Casino Floor Design Traps Gamblers**
   - *Question:* Why are there no clocks, no windows, curved labyrinth carpets, and free alcohol on the gaming floors of Las Vegas and Macau?
   - *Key Focus:* Natasha Dow Schüll's Addiction by Design, the 'machine zone' trance, placing high-hold slots at high-traffic maze corners, sensory overload audio-visual reinforcement, and player tracking telemetry analytics.
20. **The Masterclass on Behavioral Science: Designing Interventions That Actually Change Habits**
   - *Question:* How do top behavioral scientists systematically engineer lasting lifestyle and policy transformations that defeat human willpower failure?
   - *Key Focus:* Susan Michie's COM-B behavioral system (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation -> Behavior), implementation intentions ('if-then' planning), habit stacking, environmental friction reduction, and randomized controlled trial (RCT) behavioral audits.
