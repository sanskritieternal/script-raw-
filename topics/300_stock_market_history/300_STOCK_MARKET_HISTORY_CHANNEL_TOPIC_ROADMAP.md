## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Stock Market History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Stock Market History Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Stock Market History.
- **Tier 2: Stock Market History Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Stock Market History.
- **Tier 3: Stock Market History Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Stock Market History.
- **Tier 4: Stock Market History Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Stock Market History.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Stock Market History: How One Exchange Took Shape**
   - *Question:* Which trading need and membership rules shaped an early exchange?
   - *Key Focus:* Use charters, price lists, and trading records. Evidence base: dated exchange rules, prices, issuer filings, legal records, archival news, and scholarly analyses.
2. **Stock Market History: A Famous Market Episode Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What does one celebrated boom or crash actually show?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare contemporary prices, participation, and later retellings. Evidence base: dated exchange rules, prices, issuer filings, legal records, archival news, and scholarly analyses.
3. **Stock Market History: Who Could Trade?**
   - *Question:* Whose exclusion or labor is absent from exchange history?
   - *Key Focus:* Use membership, brokerage, and worker records. Evidence base: dated exchange rules, prices, issuer filings, legal records, archival news, and scholarly analyses.
4. **Stock Market History: A Historical Share Trade**
   - *Question:* How did a buyer and seller transfer a share in one period?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace listing, broker, price, settlement, and ownership record. Evidence base: dated exchange rules, prices, issuer filings, legal records, archival news, and scholarly analyses.

   - *Question:* How did market design affect the ability to trade a share?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare order handling and spreads in a dated setting. Evidence base: dated exchange rules, prices, issuer filings, legal records, archival news, and scholarly analyses.
