# Symbol case-source notes — 24 October 2026

These are bounded checks for three named symbol questions. They do not verify the other 54 active proposals or the archived queue's popularity claims. Original pitch wording and statuses remain preserved.

## BANK-060-053 — Eye above the Great Seal pyramid (archive pending 1 and trending 21)

- The US Department of State’s [Great Seal history](https://diplomacy.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Great-Seal-PDF.pdf) describes the committee designs leading to the 1782 seal, including the Eye of Providence and 13-step pyramid. The Department’s [official identification guidance](https://fam.state.gov/fam/05fah07/05fah070120.html) identifies those elements as part of the Seal. A [State Department publication](https://1997-2001.state.gov/publications/great_seal.pdf) connects the reverse design to the one-dollar bill.
- Editorial boundary: discuss documented US design choices and later placement on currency. The archived claim that an Egyptian sun-god’s eye “sits on the bill” is not established by these records and should not be presented as origin or intent.

## BANK-060-058 — Caduceus and Staff of Asclepius (archive pending 7 and trending 23)

- The US Army Medical Department Center of History and Heritage [explains its caduceus insignia](https://achh.army.mil/history/tools-faq/): use on enlisted uniforms began in 1851 and Medical Corps officers adopted it in 1902; the institution gives its non-combatant interpretation. The Army’s [2014 insignia explanation](https://www.army.mil/article/138785/the_new_army_medical_department_amedd_regimental_distinctive_insignia) also identifies the one-serpent Staff of Aesculapius as a medical emblem.
- Editorial boundary: distinguish the two depicted staffs and documented institutional adoption. “Every hospital” and “every ambulance” are unsupported universal claims. Calling the Army’s chosen emblem simply a mistake would erase the Army’s recorded rationale.

## BANK-060-070 — Emoji standardization (archive pending 20 and trending 27)

- Unicode’s [emoji proposal guidelines](https://www.unicode.org/emoji/proposals.html) specify who can propose a character, evidence requirements and selection factors. [Unicode Technical Standard #51](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr51/index.html) defines encoded emoji characters and sequences; Unicode’s [FAQ](https://unicode.org/faq/emoji_dingbats.html) distinguishes character encoding from vendor-specific display.
- Editorial boundary: encoding lets devices exchange characters; it does not make their visual rendering or everyday meaning universal. Claims that emoji are replacing language need linguistic evidence beyond the standard’s existence.
