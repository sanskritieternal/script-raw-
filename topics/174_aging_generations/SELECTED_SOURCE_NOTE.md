## BANK-174-044 - Older-adult mobility and housing

The US Census Bureau's [*Domestic Migration of Older Americans: 2015-2019*](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2022/demo/p23-218.pdf) uses survey-based mobility measures for adults aged 65 and over and reports that some older adults move, including short-distance moves. Freddie Mac's [*Aging Boomers and the Impact on the Housing Market Over the Next Decade*](https://www.freddiemac.com/research/insight/baby-boomers-impact) (2024) is a housing-market research forecast, not a measured causal effect of any one generation on starter-home scarcity. Distinguish historic move rates, ownership, household size, local supply and model assumptions. Neither source establishes that older owners are refusing to move out of selfishness or that their choices alone froze the market.

## BANK-174-045 - New Ground

The resident organization Older Women's Co-Housing describes [New Ground's structure and governance](https://www.owch.org.uk/structure-of-owch): 25 self-contained flats, shared facilities, owner and social-rental tenures, resident meetings and mutual support. The group's stated boundary is to look out for rather than provide formal care to one another. This is primary organizational evidence for a specific London project, not an independent outcome evaluation of loneliness, care needs or affordability. Confirm current membership rules and seek resident consent and voices before portraying private lives.

## BANK-174-046 - Total-body MRI screening

The American College of Radiology's [April 17, 2023 statement on screening total-body MRI](https://www.acr.org/News-and-Publications/Media-Center/2023/ACR-Statement-on-Screening-Total-Body-MRI) says there is insufficient evidence to recommend total-body screening for patients without symptoms, risk factors or relevant family history, and highlights incidental findings and downstream testing. This is a dated US professional-society position, not a patient-specific diagnosis and not evidence about every indicated MRI exam. The archive's $50,000 price and promise of presymptomatic detection are product-marketing claims requiring model, date, protocol and outcome verification.
