## Category: Forest change

<a id="catalog-115-r06"></a>
### Disturbance and recovery

Subcategory `CATALOG-115-R06` · intermediate.

<a id="bank-115-034"></a>
#### BANK-115-034 — Why can two forests recover differently after the same fire?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare fire severity, seed sources, soils, weather and prior management; distinguish regrowth from recovery of the original community.

<a id="bank-115-035"></a>
#### BANK-115-035 — What changes below ground when a forest is cleared?

**Individual coverage scope:** Trace roots, soil structure, erosion, moisture and microbial communities; separate short-term observations from long-term carbon estimates.

<a id="bank-115-036"></a>
#### BANK-115-036 — When does an introduced plant become an invasive forest species?

**Individual coverage scope:** Examine spread, establishment and ecological effects in one region; distinguish foreign origin from demonstrated harm.

<a id="bank-115-037"></a>
#### BANK-115-037 — How can wildlife corridors help some forest species but not others?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare movement ranges, edge exposure, bottlenecks and habitat quality; avoid treating connectivity as a universal solution.

<a id="bank-115-047"></a>
#### BANK-115-047 — How does measured Amazon evapotranspiration contribute to downwind rainfall?

**Individual coverage scope:** Choose a basin, season and atmospheric transport study; distinguish plot-level water fluxes from a universal per-tree daily rate or an exact river-volume comparison.

<a id="bank-115-048"></a>
#### BANK-115-048 — When do peat fires survive winter and re-emerge in spring?

**Individual coverage scope:** Choose a documented boreal region and year, combine fire and peat observations, and distinguish observed overwintering combustion from every spring ignition.
