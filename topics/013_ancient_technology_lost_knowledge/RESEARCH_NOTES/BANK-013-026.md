# BANK-013-026 — Roman concrete and a hot-mixing hypothesis

Status: bounded primary-study review for an existing proposal, not a verified script. Inspected 2026-10-24.

## Primary study inspected

- [Seymour et al., “Hot mixing: Mechanistic insights into the durability of ancient Roman concrete,” *Science Advances* 9, eadd1602 (2023)](https://media.screeningeagle.com/asset/Downloads/sciadv.add1602.pdf). The authors chemically examined lime clasts in mortar from the Roman site of Privernum and argued that their features support quicklime hot mixing. They then made modern Roman-inspired mixtures and observed filling of induced cracks. These are distinct observations: ancient material characterization, a historical-process inference, and a modern-material experiment.

## Editorial boundary

The archived pitch says Roman mortar “outlasted modern cement” and self-healed. The selected ancient samples and modern analog tests do not establish a universal lifespan comparison with every modern concrete, nor directly observe each ancient crack healing over centuries. The paper itself treats the lime-clast mechanism as a proposed explanation. A future episode should specify the site, sample, mortar formulation, exposure conditions and comparator; inspect subsequent independent studies and competing durability mechanisms; and distinguish concrete from its mortar binder. No blanket superiority or secret-recipe claim is approved here.
