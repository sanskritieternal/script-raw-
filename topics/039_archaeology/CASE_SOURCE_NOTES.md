# Archaeology case-source notes — 24 October 2026

These are bounded checks of three case routes. They support the questions and editorial limits below, not the full 61-proposal bank or the archived queue's popularity claims. The original archive records and status fields remain unchanged.

## BANK-039-073 — White Sands dating challenge (archive trending 26)

- The [2021 research record hosted by the National Park Service](https://irmadev.nps.gov/DataStore/Reference/Profile/2287495) describes in situ human footprints bracketed by radiocarbon-dated seed layers at about 23,000–21,000 years ago.
- The [2023 USGS-hosted research record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70249447) reports independent ages from terrestrial pollen in the same stratigraphic horizons and optically stimulated luminescence from footprint-bearing sediment. The [USGS synthesis](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20253046/full) explains why terrestrial pollen addresses the aquatic-seed hard-water concern.
- Editorial boundary: tell the dating-method dispute and its tests. The dates do not by themselves identify the people, a migration route, or the age of every footprint at the park. The archived headline’s reference to teenagers and sloths must not be turned into a single demonstrated encounter.

## BANK-039-070 — Upano Valley lidar (archive trending 22)

- Rostain and coauthors’ [2024 primary paper, *Science*, DOI 10.1126/science.adi6317](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi6317) reports lidar and fieldwork mapping platforms, plazas, streets, drainage and terraces in Ecuador’s Upano Valley. The [research team's CNRS account](https://news.cnrs.fr/articles/ancient-cities-in-the-amazon) explains the discovery context.
- Editorial boundary: characterize the mapped settlement network and its ground evidence. Avoid the archived pitch’s unqualified population of 30,000; lidar records surface form, while population estimates require additional assumptions.

## BANK-039-069 — Khufu North Face Corridor (archive trending 21)

- The ScanPyramids team’s [2023 *Nature Communications* paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-36351-0) reports the corridor-shaped structure detected with cosmic-ray muons and estimates its shape and position. A [2023 nondestructive testing paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0963869523000245) reports ground-penetrating radar and ultrasound checks at the chevron.
- Editorial boundary: separate detection, geometric characterization and visual access. The underlying studies do not establish the corridor’s purpose. The archived title’s “2024” framing and camera-detail claims need a separate contemporaneous source check before scripting.
