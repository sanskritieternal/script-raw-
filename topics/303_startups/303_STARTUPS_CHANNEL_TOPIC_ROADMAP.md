## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Startups across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Startups Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Startups.
- **Tier 2: Startups Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Startups.
- **Tier 3: Startups Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Startups.
- **Tier 4: Startups Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Startups.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Startups: How One Startup Was Founded**
   - *Question:* What customer problem and resources shaped its first version?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated pitches, product records, and team accounts. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
2. **Startups: A Startup Success Rechecked**
   - *Question:* Which metrics support its reputation?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate users, revenue, margin, cash flow, and valuation. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
3. **Startups: The Team Behind a Founder**
   - *Question:* Whose engineering, support, sales, and operations made growth possible?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace credits, equity, and labor. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
4. **Startups: Testing a Startup Hypothesis**
   - *Question:* What evidence shows a product solves a defined problem?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare interviews, experiments, usage, and paying customers. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
5. **Startups: Growth Versus Unit Economics**
   - *Question:* When can fast adoption still destroy value?
   - *Key Focus:* Model contribution margin, retention, acquisition, and burn. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Startups: Why Growth Stalled**
   - *Question:* Which data distinguish weak demand from execution failure?
   - *Key Focus:* Check cohorts, pricing, competitors, and product changes. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
7. **Startups: A Claimed Technical Edge**
   - *Question:* Does a named feature create measurable advantage?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare baseline, performance, reliability, cost, and user outcomes. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
8. **Startups: A Founder Decision Under Pressure**
   - *Question:* What alternatives were available at a turning point?
   - *Key Focus:* Use board records, team accounts, funding, and outcome. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
9. **Startups: A Funding Round or Layoff**
   - *Question:* How did one event change the firm's plan?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace cash runway, staffing, product, and investor terms. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
10. **Startups: From Prototype to Repeat Use**
   - *Question:* What systems support a startup's product?
   - *Key Focus:* Map build, onboarding, payments, service, and support. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Startups: A Startup's Growth Curve**
   - *Question:* Which metric actually rose or fell?
   - *Key Focus:* Define cohort, period, channel, and denominator. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
12. **Startups: Product Before and After Funding**
   - *Question:* What changed in service, cost, and customers?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated versions and financial records. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
13. **Startups: Paths to Sustainable Operation**
   - *Question:* Which assumptions determine runway and scale?
   - *Key Focus:* State burn, revenue, retention, capital, and risk scenarios. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
14. **Startups: Growth at Any Cost?**
   - *Question:* Did rapid spending improve durable customer value?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare cohort returns and full costs. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
15. **Startups: One Startup Experiment**
   - *Question:* What was proposed, measured, learned, and changed?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated experiment records; separate post-hoc narrative. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Startups: The Startup's Dependencies**

18. **Startups: One Customer Cohort**

   - *Key Focus:* Define cohort, churn, gross margin, payback, and assumptions. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
19. **Startups: Does the Product Help**
   - *Question:* What measurable outcome improved for users?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare baseline, harms, access, and alternatives. Evidence base: dated product versions and experiments, customer and financial cohorts, financing terms, incidents, and consented team and user accounts.
20. **Startups: The Market Actually Served**
   - *Question:* Which customers and substitutes constrain growth?
