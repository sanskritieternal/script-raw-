# Selected hospital cases - 2026-10-24

These notes bound proposed editorial questions. They do not provide clinical instruction, authorize filming patients, or approve an IDEA handoff.

## BANK-181-045 - da Vinci 5 regulatory scope

- FDA [510(k) summary K232610](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232610.pdf), prepared 12 March 2024, identifies the da Vinci Surgical System Model IS5000/Gen5 as a computer-controlled endoscopic instrument system and lists predicate devices and model-specific indications. The document describes human-controlled surgical tools, not an autonomous surgeon.
- **Claim limit:** Clearance and substantial equivalence do not demonstrate superiority, universal patient benefit, an AI-directed operating room or a particular hospital's outcome. Check current labeling, recall status, procedure, comparator and clinical evidence before a treatment-performance claim.
- **Overlap:** BANK-181-015 owns general sterile-theatre/team operations. This case is the named 2024 device and regulatory boundary.

## BANK-181-046 - ACGME resident work hours

- The ACGME [resident-hour FAQ](https://www.acgme.org/about/acgme-frequently-asked-questions/) describes a maximum of 80 clinical and educational work hours per week averaged over four weeks, with limits on continuous duty and time off. Its [current requirements page](https://www.acgme.org/programs-and-institutions/programs/common-program-requirements/) links the effective version and specialty-specific context.
- **Claim limit:** This accreditation maximum is not an instruction to schedule 80 hours, a record that all residents work that much, a measure of sleep, or proof of a patient-safety effect. Check actual reported/remote work, specialty, institution, date and outcome study before causal claims.
- **Overlap:** BANK-181-031 covers care handoffs; this case is the time-limit and measurement question.

## BANK-181-029 - hospital-at-home policy scope

- CMS's [Acute Hospital Care at Home program page](https://qualitynet.cms.gov/acute-hospital-care-at-home) describes the U.S. waiver initiative and its current statutory extension. It sets an administrative/program scope, not patient-level equivalence to inpatient care.
- **Claim limit:** Avoid saying that sensors and daily visits are appropriate for every acute condition. A script needs named eligibility rules, staffing, escalation capability, clinical comparison and date-specific approval. Protect patient privacy and obtain consent for any depicted care.
