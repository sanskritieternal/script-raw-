## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Community Stories across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Defining Community, Place-Based Belonging & Third Places (Beginner / Scratch)** — Gemeinschaft (intimate community) vs Gesellschaft (impersonal society), identifying natural community anchors (libraries, community gardens, local diners), oral history gathering basics, and neighborhood mapping.
- **Tier 2: Mutual Aid Networks, Community Land Trusts & Cooperative Enterprise Models (Intermediate)** — Mutual aid logistics vs top-down charity models, Community Land Trusts (CLT) taking land off the speculative market to guarantee permanent affordability, worker cooperatives (Mondragon model), and community-led disaster recovery.
- **Tier 3: Intentional Communities, Communes & Post-Industrial Resilience Initiatives (Upper Intermediate)** — Intentional community governance architectures (consensus decision making, sociocracy), eco-village off-grid resource sharing, post-industrial rust-belt revitalization collectives (Detroit urban farming), and managing communal conflict.
- **Tier 4: Digital Decentralized Autonomous Communities (DAOs), Indigenous Land Reclamation & Intergenerational Healing (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Decentralized digital communities bridging physical spaces, Indigenous Land Back sovereignty governance, participatory municipal budgeting (Porto Alegre model), and intergenerational communal memory preservation.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Freetown of Christiania: 50 Years of an Anarchist Commune in the Heart of Copenhagen**
   - *Question:* How did squatters who took over abandoned military barracks in Denmark in 1971 build a self-governing community of 1,000 residents that survived for half a century?
   - *Key Focus:* Consensus democracy meetings, banning cars and private property sales, homemade architectural recycling, the Pusher Street hash market conflict, and collective community stock buyouts.
2. **Third Places: Why Every Thriving Neighborhood Needs Somewhere That Isn't Home or Work**
   - *Question:* What is the magical ingredient that makes local barbershops, diners, and public libraries the bedrock of civil society?
   - *Key Focus:* Ray Oldenburg's eight characteristics of a third place (neutral ground, leveler, conversation is main activity, regular characters, low profile), and how their loss causes social atomization.
3. **Community Land Trusts: How One Neighborhood Stopped Gentrification Forever**
   - *Question:* How did the Dudley Street Neighborhood Initiative in Boston win the power of eminent domain to take land off the speculative market permanently?
   - *Key Focus:* Separating land ownership from building ownership, 99-year ground leases, tripartite board governance (residents, businesses, public), and keeping homes affordable for generations regardless of market booms.
4. **Mutual Aid vs Charity: 'Solidarity, Not Charity' in Practice**
   - *Question:* Why did the Black Panther Party's Free Breakfast Program terrify FBI Director J. Edgar Hoover more than their armed patrols?
   - *Key Focus:* Mutual aid's horizontal reciprocity vs charity's hierarchical moral gatekeeping, Community Survival Programs feeding 20,000 children daily, and modern community fridges and mutual aid disaster relief.
5. **Mondragon Corporation: The World's Largest Worker-Owned Cooperative Empire**
   - *Question:* How did a Catholic priest and five young engineers in the Basque mountains build a multi-billion-dollar federation of 80,000 worker-owners with zero external shareholders?
   - *Key Focus:* Father José María Arizmendiarrieta, one-worker-one-vote governance, CEO salary capped at 6x lowest paid worker, internal cooperative banking (Caja Laboral), and weathering economic recessions without mass layoffs.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **Detroit's Urban Agriculture Revolution: Reclaiming Vacant Lots into Community Food Forests**
   - *Question:* How did residents in the hollowed-out Motor City turn 20 square miles of abandoned urban blight into a vibrant network of over 1,400 urban farms?
   - *Key Focus:* The D-Town Farm and Malik Yakini, food desert mitigation, soil lead testing and phytoremediation with sunflowers, rain catchment irrigation, and building African-American community food sovereignty.
7. **The Tool Library Movement: Why Does Every Single House Need Its Own Lawn Mower?**
   - *Question:* If the average power drill is used for only 13 minutes in its entire lifespan, why don't neighborhoods share their tools?
   - *Key Focus:* The Berkeley Tool Lending Library, tool checkout logistics and maintenance, volunteer repair cafes, community woodworking spaces, and reducing household consumption through shared commons.
8. **The Gander Miracle: When 38 Plane Loads of Stranded 9/11 Strangers Were Welcomed as Family**
   - *Question:* How did a tiny Newfoundland town of 10,000 people take in 6,700 stranded international airline passengers on September 11, housing and feeding them for five days?
   - *Key Focus:* Operation Yellow Ribbon, town hockey rinks and schools converted into shelters, pharmacy volunteer prescription triage, refusing payment, and the Broadway musical Come From Away.
9. **Eco-Villages and Off-Grid Communes: Surviving the Reality of Shared Living**
   - *Question:* Why do 90% of intentional communities and communes collapse within five years, and what makes the survivors last for decades?
   - *Key Focus:* Twin Oaks community in Virginia (income-sharing labor credits), Findhorn ecovillage in Scotland, overcoming interpersonal drama and burnout, sociocracy consensus governance, and financial sustainability models.
10. **Participatory Budgeting: Letting Real Citizens Decide How Tax Dollars Are Spent**
   - *Question:* What happens when a city government hands millions of dollars directly over to neighborhood assemblies to vote on local projects?
   - *Key Focus:* Porto Alegre, Brazil's 1989 democratic invention, moving from bureaucratic backrooms to public community hall votes, funding water access and schools in favelas, and global adoption from New York to Paris.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **The Public Library Under Siege: The Last Free Civic Space in America**
   - *Question:* Why are public libraries the only places left in modern cities where you can sit for six hours in the warmth without spending a dime?
   - *Key Focus:* Eric Klinenberg's Palaces for the People, librarians as social workers and overdose first-responders, book challenge controversies, closing digital divides, and defending social infrastructure against budget cuts.
12. **The Amish Barn Raising: The Physics and Sociology of Building a Barn in One Single Day**
   - *Question:* How can 200 volunteer men, women, and teenagers assemble, raise, and shingle a massive timber-frame dairy barn from sunrise to sunset?
   - *Key Focus:* The Amish 'Frolic' mutual aid tradition, timber framing joinery pre-fabrication, division of specialized labor without central management or money, communal feast preparation, and religious mutual accountability.
13. **Community-Owned Broadband: How Chattanooga Built America's Fastest Internet**
   - *Question:* How did a municipal electric power board in Tennessee defeat telecom corporate lobbyists to build a 10-gigabit fiber network that transformed their city into a tech hub?
   - *Key Focus:* Chattanooga EPB municipal fiber rollout, smart grid electrical outage self-healing, providing free high-speed internet to low-income public school students, and state-level telecom monopoly lobbying blocks.
14. **Indigenous Land Back: The Redefinition of Conservation and Sovereignty**
   - *Question:* What happens when national parks and ancestral forests are returned to tribal management, and how does traditional ecological knowledge outperform federal agencies?
   - *Key Focus:* The Land Back movement, cultural controlled burns preventing megafires (Yurok and Karuk tribes), dam removals on the Klamath River restoring salmon runs, and co-management conservation agreements.
15. **The Community Fridge Movement: Direct Food Rescue in the Heart of the Pandemic**
   - *Question:* How did brightly painted refrigerators plugged into sidewalk extension cords feed thousands of families during lockdown with 'Take what you need, leave what you can'?
   - *Key Focus:* Direct food waste diversion from grocery stores, volunteer cleaning and temperature check rotations, city health department regulatory challenges, and overcoming social stigma associated with soup kitchens.
16. **The Village That Bought Its Own Pub: Saving the British Village Social Anchor**
   - *Question:* When commercial pub chains shutter rural British village taverns, how do hundreds of local residents chip in £500 each to purchase the freehold?
   - *Key Focus:* Plunkett Foundation community business models, co-locating post offices and grocery shops inside community pubs, volunteer staffing, and preserving rural social cohesion against loneliness.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **Disaster Collectives: How Neighbors Save Neighbors While FEMA is Still en Route**
   - *Question:* Why do ad-hoc volunteer networks with bass boats like the 'Cajun Navy' consistently rescue thousands of flood victims days before government aid arrives?
   - *Key Focus:* The Cajun Navy and Mutual Aid Disaster Relief (MADR), decentralized Zello walkie-talkie radio coordination, spontaneous local supply depots, avoiding bureaucratic bottlenecks, and Rebecca Solnit's A Paradise Built in Hell.
18. **Digital DAOs and Network States: Can Decentralized Online Communities Build Real-World Cities?**
   - *Question:* Can internet-native communities organized around crypto treasuries crowdfund real physical land to build autonomous, charter-governed micro-cities?
   - *Key Focus:* Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs), Balaji Srinivasan's The Network State thesis, Cabin.city and CityDAO experiments, legal recognition hurdles, and reconciling digital governance with physical municipal reality.
19. **The Economics of Community Development: How CDFIs Invest Where Commercial Banks Refuse**
   - *Question:* How do Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs) lend hundreds of millions to minority-owned businesses and affordable housing with near-zero default rates?
   - *Key Focus:* Underwriting based on community character and character-based lending vs credit scores, leveraging federal CDFI Fund grants with private bank CRA commitments, and measuring social return on investment (SROI).
20. **The Masterclass on Community Organizing: The Saul Alinsky Rules for Radicals Blueprint**
   - *Question:* How do grassroots organizers unite fragmented neighborhood residents to outmaneuver entrenched political machines and corporate slumlords?
   - *Key Focus:* Saul Alinsky's community organizing principles (Rules for Radicals), conducting one-on-one relational interviews, building power through coalitions, identifying winnable cutting-edge campaign issues, nonviolent creative direct action, and sustaining long-term community power.
