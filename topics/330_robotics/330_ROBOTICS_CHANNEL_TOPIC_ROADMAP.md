## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Robotics across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Robotics Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Robotics.
- **Tier 2: Robotics Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Robotics.
- **Tier 3: Robotics Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Robotics.
- **Tier 4: Robotics Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Robotics.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Robotics: A Robot Enters a Workplace**
   - *Question:* Which task and constraint drove one early robot deployment?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated design, safety, and production records. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
2. **Robotics: A Famous Robot Demonstration Rechecked**
   - *Question:* Which tasks did it complete outside a staged demonstration?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare trials, conditions, and operator assistance. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
3. **Robotics: Technicians Behind Automation**
   - *Question:* Who installs, calibrates, repairs, and supervises the system?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented worker accounts and maintenance records. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
4. **Robotics: Sense, Plan, Act**
   - *Question:* How do sensor readings become a robot movement?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one bounded task through control and actuation. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
5. **Robotics: Precision Versus Robustness**
   - *Question:* Can a system handle changed objects and environments?
   - *Key Focus:* Measure repeatability across defined conditions. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Robotics: An Unexpected Robot Stop**
   - *Question:* Which sensor, software, power, or human condition explains an incident?
   - *Key Focus:* Use logs and public reports without unsafe troubleshooting steps. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
7. **Robotics: A New Gripper or Sensor**
   - *Question:* Did it improve a defined task and at what cost?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare matched objects, speed, errors, and safety. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
8. **Robotics: A Robot Design Decision**
   - *Question:* Who set the task and safety requirements?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace decisions and affected workers. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
9. **Robotics: A Robot Deployment Timeline**
   - *Question:* What happened from pilot to routine use?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare acceptance tests with operational outcomes. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
10. **Robotics: A Robot Cell in Operation**
   - *Question:* How do controller, sensors, actuators, guards, and operator interact?
   - *Key Focus:* Map one application and its safe boundaries. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Robotics: Why a Robot Was Retired**
   - *Question:* Which performance, maintenance, or task change drove retirement?
   - *Key Focus:* Use operating logs and alternatives. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
12. **Robotics: One Task Across Robot Generations**
   - *Question:* How did throughput, quality, safety, and labor change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare matched tasks and environments. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
13. **Robotics: A Proposed Robot Capability**
   - *Question:* What test would demonstrate field reliability?
   - *Key Focus:* Define task, variation, intervention, and failure threshold. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
14. **Robotics: Autonomous Means No Humans?**
   - *Question:* Which setup, monitoring, and exception work remains?
   - *Key Focus:* Observe a complete workflow. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
15. **Robotics: A Shift with a Robot**
   - *Question:* How does one team use and recover the system day to day?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented observation and logs. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Robotics: What the Robot Controls**
   - *Question:* Where do machine, fixtures, software, operator, and facility responsibilities end?
   - *Key Focus:* Draw one deployed system boundary. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
17. **Robotics: A Pick-and-Place Cycle**
   - *Question:* What inputs and outputs shape a single safe cycle?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace sensors, decisions, movement, and stop conditions. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
18. **Robotics: Speed and Contact Safety**
   - *Question:* How do performance goals interact with protection measures?
   - *Key Focus:* Use applicable standards and tested configuration. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
19. **Robotics: A Failed Grasp**
   - *Question:* What conditions cause an object to be dropped or damaged?
   - *Key Focus:* Review tests and mitigation, not hazardous operating steps. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
20. **Robotics: Lab Task Versus Working Floor**
   - *Question:* Which changes in lighting, objects, wear, and people alter results?
   - *Key Focus:* State trial conditions and sample size. Evidence base: NIST robot test methods, applicable standards, dated design and service records, controlled trials, and consented worker accounts.
