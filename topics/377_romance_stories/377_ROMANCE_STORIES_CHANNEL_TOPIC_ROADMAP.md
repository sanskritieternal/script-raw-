## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Romance Stories: A Love-Story Convention Changes**
   - *Question:* Which dated publishing or performance context shaped a romance form?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare works and audience context without one origin story. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
2. **Romance Stories: A Celebrated Relationship Arc**
   - *Question:* What choices make a named couple's change believable in the text?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace decisions, dialogue, obstacles, and revisions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
3. **Romance Stories: Readers and Writers Missing from the Canon**
   - *Question:* Whose romance traditions were left out of a standard account?
   - *Key Focus:* Use attributed works and publication records. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
4. **Romance Stories: The Central Relationship**
   - *Question:* How does a romance keep the relationship central alongside other plots?
   - *Key Focus:* Map scenes and consequences in one work. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
5. **Romance Stories: Love, Power, and Choice**
   - *Question:* How do asymmetries of status or resources shape consent and agency?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace character options without diagnosing people. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Romance Stories: An Ambiguous Ending**
   - *Question:* Does a story establish a continuing relationship or leave it open?
   - *Key Focus:* Read ending, earlier promises, and alternate editions. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
7. **Romance Stories: Romance in a New Medium**
   - *Question:* How does serial or interactive delivery alter relationship pacing?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare released formats and audience choices. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
8. **Romance Stories: A Writer's Relationship Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a creator change a character's choice?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts and attributed accounts, not speculation. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
9. **Romance Stories: A Romance Publication Milestone**
   - *Question:* Which dated release changed access to a particular love story?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace editions, translation, adaptation, and reception. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
10. **Romance Stories: A Relationship Changes**
   - *Question:* Which interaction changes what two characters know or want?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace reciprocal action and consequence scene by scene. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Romance Stories: A Romance Trope Reassessed**
   - *Question:* Why did readers' interpretation of one trope change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated works and situated criticism. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
12. **Romance Stories: Two Love Stories, Different Norms**
   - *Question:* How do two period-specific works frame marriage, work, or autonomy?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare actual choices within historical context. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
13. **Romance Stories: A New Romance Format**
   - *Question:* What reader agency does an emerging format actually allow?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze a released work rather than predict the genre. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
14. **Romance Stories: One Required Romance Ending?**
   - *Question:* Which subgenres or adjacent love stories use different resolution rules?
   - *Key Focus:* Define the publishing category and compare examples. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
15. **Romance Stories: Making a Relationship Arc**
   - *Question:* How did draft and editorial changes reshape a couple?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow versioned scenes and creator accounts. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Romance Stories: What Relationship Is Promised?**
   - *Question:* What emotional trajectory did a specific brief or pitch propose?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, revisions, and finished story. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
17. **Romance Stories: Character Notes and Scene Maps**
   - *Question:* How do planning artifacts track two perspectives?
   - *Key Focus:* Check dates, authorship, and final text. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
18. **Romance Stories: Revising a Turning Point**
   - *Question:* Which change made a choice clearer or more consequential?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts and subsequent scenes. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
19. **Romance Stories: A Conversation with Subtext**
   - *Question:* What do gesture, silence, and dialogue reveal about a character's choice?
   - *Key Focus:* Close-read a bounded scene and alternatives. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
20. **Romance Stories: An Adapted Love Story**
   - *Question:* How did author, editor, performers, and director shape its central relationship?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits and documented version history. Evidence base: identified texts and editions, creator drafts, publisher framing, period context, adaptation records, and situated reader criticism.
