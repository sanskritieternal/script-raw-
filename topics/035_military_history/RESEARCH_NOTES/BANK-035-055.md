# BANK-035-055 — first day of the Somme

Research check: 2026-10-24. Editorial proposal only; not a certified episode script.

## Checked premise

The archived title says machine-gun fire on one day “slaughtered 57,000 British soldiers.” The National Army Museum reports about **57,000 British casualties** on 1 July 1916, of whom roughly **20,000 were fatal**. Casualties include people killed and wounded, so 57,000 is not a death count. The museum also describes artillery and rifle fire as well as machine-gun fire. A one-weapon explanation hides planning, bombardment, terrain, defensive positions, communication and local variation. The broader claim that the day “broke Victorian optimism forever” is an interpretation requiring its own evidence.

## Source trail

- National Army Museum, “Battle of the Somme,” public collection and interpretive account: https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-somme . Institutional source for first-day British casualty scale, campaign dates, and first-hand material in the collection.
- National Army Museum, “The Battle of the Somme: 100 Years On”: https://ww1.nam.ac.uk/focus/volunteers-conscripts/somme-100-years/ . Institutional statement that nearly 60,000 casualties included about 20,000 fatal casualties.
- National Army Museum collection record, British artillery bombardment at Beaumont Hamel, 1 July 1916: https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1952-01-33-55-280 . Contemporary object context in an institutional collection; this does not itself establish the casualty total.

## Editorial boundary

The episode should label the population, date, geography and casualty definition each time a number is used. Compare British, French and German records only with compatible definitions. Use unit-level histories and contemporary records to test local experiences; do not present one uniform march into a single machine-gun field.
