## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Family History across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Vital Statistics Registries, Census Records & Family Tree Mapping (Beginner / Scratch)** — Civil registration of births, marriages, and deaths (BMD), decennial census records analysis, church baptism and burial registries, pedigree chart construction, and overcoming common surname pitfalls.
- **Tier 2: Autosomal DNA Matching, Centimorgan Triangulation & Genetic Genealogy (Intermediate)** — Autosomal DNA testing mechanics (AncestryDNA, 23andMe), Centimorgan (cM) shared segment analysis, the Shared cM Project probability tables, Y-DNA paternal haplogroups, and mitochondrial mtDNA maternal lineages.
- **Tier 3: Paleography (Deciphering Old Scripts), Land Deeds & Immigrant Ship Manifests (Upper Intermediate)** — Deciphering Secretary hand and Kurrentschrift historic scripts, Ellis Island and Castle Garden ship passenger manifests, probate wills and estate land deeds, and naturalization petitions.
- **Tier 4: Breaking 200-Year Brick Walls, Resolving Non-Paternity Events (NPE) & Preserving Oral Family Archives (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Investigative genetic genealogy solving 19th-century brick walls and adoption mysteries, managing sensitive Non-Paternity Events (NPE) and family secrets, archival digital digitization standards, and writing enduring family histories.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Golden State Killer: How Investigative Genetic Genealogy Solved a 40-Year Cold Case**
   - *Question:* How did genealogist Barbara Rae-Venter upload crime scene DNA to open-source genealogy databases to track down a serial killer using distant third cousins?
   - *Key Focus:* GEDmatch public database search, building multi-branch descendancy trees back to 19th-century great-great-grandparents, centimorgan segment triangulation, eliminating branches, and the arrest of Joseph DeAngelo.
2. **The Census Detective: How to Extract Secrets from 100-Year-Old Hand-Written Sheets**
   - *Question:* Why does the US keep federal census records locked in a vault for exactly 72 years, and what can a single 1930 line reveal about your ancestors?
   - *Key Focus:* The 72-Year Privacy Rule, analyzing occupation columns, citizenship status, home ownership vs mortgages, radio ownership, literacy, and spotting enumerator spelling errors.
3. **Autosomal DNA vs Y-DNA vs mtDNA: Which Genetic Test Actually Solves Your Mystery?**
   - *Question:* Why is autosomal DNA useless beyond 5 to 7 generations, while Y-DNA can trace your surname back 500 years to medieval clan chiefs?
   - *Key Focus:* Autosomal chromosome recombination and halving every generation, Y-chromosome passing virtually unchanged father-to-son, mitochondrial DNA maternal lineage tracking, and selecting the right testing platform.
4. **The Shared cM Project: Decoding the Math of Genetic Cousins**
   - *Question:* When Ancestry says you share 215 centimorgans with a total stranger, are they a second cousin, a first cousin twice removed, or a half-aunt?
   - *Key Focus:* Blaine Bettinger's Shared cM Project, probability relationship histograms, endogamy distortions (Jewish, Amish, and island populations inflating shared DNA), and mapping segment overlaps.
5. **The Ellis Island Myth: The Names Were Never Changed at the Docks**
   - *Question:* Did immigration inspectors really butcher foreign surnames at Ellis Island, or did your ancestors choose to change their own names years later?
   - *Key Focus:* Inspectors checking passengers against shipping line manifests created in Europe, lack of power to change legal documents at the port, court naturalization name petitions, and cultural assimilation pressure.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **Breaking the Brick Wall: Strategies for Finding the Father of an 1850s Ancestor**
   - *Question:* What do you do when the trail goes completely cold and an ancestor seems to have dropped out of thin air with no birth certificate?
   - *Key Focus:* Elizabeth Shown Mills' FAN Club methodology (Friends, Associates, Neighbors), collateral research, county courthouse probate court dockets, tax lists tracking missing years, and land platting.
7. **Paleography: How to Decipher 17th-Century Handwriting That Looks Like Hieroglyphs**
   - *Question:* How do researchers read cursive wills and parish records written in Secretary Hand, Old English script, or German Kurrent?
   - *Key Focus:* Historical ligature forms, long 's' (looks like an f), thorn symbol (Y = th, origin of 'Ye Olde'), archaic contractions, reading letter-by-letter with a tracing alphabet, and digital contrast enhancement.
8. **The Non-Paternity Event (NPE): Navigating DNA Discoveries That Shatter Family Trees**
   - *Question:* What happens when a holiday DNA test reveals that the father who raised you—or your grandfather—is not your biological relative?
   - *Key Focus:* Prevalence of NPEs in genetic genealogy (1-3% per generation), late-discovery adoptions, donor conceived individuals, emotional shock and ethical etiquette in contacting biological kin, and genetic counseling.
9. **The WPA Slave Narratives and Freedmen's Bureau: Overcoming the 1870 African American Brick Wall**
   - *Question:* How do Black genealogists trace enslaved ancestors back before the 1870 census, when enslaved people were listed only as anonymous tick marks on slave schedules?
   - *Key Focus:* Freedmen's Bureau labor contracts and bank records, plantation account ledgers, wills and probate estate sales listing names and ages, slave manifests, and combining DNA clusters with local white family papers.
10. **Church Parish Registers: Tracking Ancestors Back to the 1500s in European Villages**
   - *Question:* Before governments kept birth certificates, how did village parish priests in England, Germany, and Italy keep records that survived for 500 years?
   - *Key Focus:* Council of Trent (1563) mandating Catholic parish registers, English Parish Registers (1538 Henry VIII mandate), Latin terminology decoders, churchwardens' accounts, and microfilms in salt mine vaults.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **Land Deeds and Metes & Bounds: Mapping Ancestral Farms from 'White Oak to a Pile of Stones'**
   - *Question:* How can an 18th-century property description measured in surveying chains and boundary trees pinpoint your ancestor's exact log cabin on modern Google Earth?
   - *Key Focus:* Metes and bounds surveying vs the Public Land Survey System (township/range/section grid), deed mapper software, following property chains of title back to original crown grants, and historical neighbors.
12. **Military Service Records: Pension Files That Contain Intimate Family Letters and Bible Pages**
   - *Question:* Why are Revolutionary War and Civil War widow pension applications the richest goldmines in genealogical research?
   - *Key Focus:* National Archives (NARA) Record Group 15, physical family Bible birth pages torn out and submitted as proof of marriage, sworn eyewitness neighbor affidavits, casualty surgeon notes, and bounty land warrants.
13. **Oral History Interviews: How to Record Your Grandparents Before Their Stories Are Lost Forever**
   - *Question:* What questions should you ask an aging elder to unlock vivid sensory memories rather than polite generic answers?
   - *Key Focus:* StoryCorps interview techniques, open-ended sensory prompts ('what did your childhood kitchen smell like?'), using old photograph albums as memory triggers, high-resolution audio recording standards, and archiving transcripts.
14. **The Orphan Train Movement: Finding the Lost Roots of 250,000 Relocated Children**
   - *Question:* How did a massive 19th-century social experiment take street children from New York tenements and place them on trains to farming families across the American West?
   - *Key Focus:* Charles Loring Brace and the Children's Aid Society (1854-1929), train station inspection line lineups, Catholic New York Foundling Hospital placement books, changed names and lost siblings, and genetic reconnection.
15. **Coroner Inquests and Asylums: Uncovering the Dark and Tragic Chapters in Your Tree**
   - *Question:* How do genealogists handle finding ancestral murders, suicides, workhouse pauper graves, and psychiatric institutionalizations?
   - *Key Focus:* Coroner jury inquest transcripts, historical asylum patient case books, almshouse intake registers, sensational historical newspaper archives (Chronicling America), and ethical family narrative framing.
16. **Endogamy and Pedigree Collapse: Why We All Have Fewer Ancestors Than Math Suggests**
   - *Question:* If you double your ancestors every generation back 40 generations, you get 1 trillion ancestors—more people than ever lived. Where does the math break?
   - *Key Focus:* Pedigree collapse (ahnenschwund), royal inbreeding (Charles II of Spain), the Most Recent Common Ancestor (MRCA), why anyone of European descent is related to Charlemagne, and genetic implications of cousin marriage.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **Preserving Family Photos and Heirlooms: Archival Acid-Free Boxes and 1,200 DPI Scans**
   - *Question:* How do you preserve 150-year-old tintypes, fragile glass plate negatives, and crumbling paper scrapbooks for another two centuries?
   - *Key Focus:* Daguerreotypes, ambrotypes, and albumen prints identification; avoiding sticky magnetic photo albums; archival acid-free lignin-free storage sleeves; flatbed 1,200 DPI TIFF master scanning; and offsite redundant cloud preservation.
18. **AI in Genealogy: Transcribing Cursive, Enhancing Blurry Faces, and Clustering DNA**
   - *Question:* How are neural networks reading centuries of uncataloged handwritten cursive records that no human eye had time to index?
   - *Key Focus:* Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) on FamilySearch archives, AI facial clustering matching unknown tintype portraits, automated tree hint validation algorithms, and synthetic video animation of ancestor photos (Deep Nostalgia).
19. **The Multi-Billion Dollar Family History Industry: The Economics of Ancestry and 23andMe**
   - *Question:* Why did private equity firms buy Ancestry.com for $4.7 billion, and how do consumer DNA databases monetize genetic data?
   - *Key Focus:* Subscription recurring revenue models, DNA testing microarray chip manufacturing costs, pharmaceutical research partnerships (GSK using 23andMe genetic databases), and privacy regulatory scrutiny.
20. **The Masterclass on Writing a Family History Book: From Dry Pedigree to Compelling Narrative**
   - *Question:* How do you take names and dates on a chart and turn them into an unputdownable narrative non-fiction book that your family will treasure for generations?
   - *Key Focus:* Weaving social history context around individual lives, handling historical trauma and secrets ethically, source citation standards (Board for Certification of Genealogists), photographic layout, and self-publishing archival hardcover editions.
