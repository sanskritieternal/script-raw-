# Hatha yoga versus stretching-strengthening: bounded trial note

Linked proposal: [BANK-077-051](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-077-051). Research checked 2026-10-24. This is a study-method source trail, not personal exercise or medical advice.

The original [randomized trial report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5864160/) compared an **8-week hatha-yoga programme** with stretching-strengthening exercises among 118 sedentary, generally healthy, community-dwelling adults, mean age about 62. Classes were scheduled three times weekly for one hour. The investigators measured functional tests including chair stands, arm curls, flexibility, mobility and balance. The [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26297940/) gives the indexed trial design and outcome summary.

For an episode, retain the actual comparison, ages, eligibility and follow-up period. Distinguish arm-curl and chair-stand performance from direct measurement of muscle size or maximal strength. Compare between-group results and uncertainty rather than reporting improvement in both groups as proof that one programme is superior. Check adherence, adverse events and the specific taught yoga sequence before extrapolating to an online class or a younger population.

The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health's [yoga evidence and safety overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety?nav=tw) notes variation among programmes and study methods. The selected trial does not show that yoga replaces resistance training for every outcome, treats a condition, or is suitable for every participant. Those remain separate evidence and professional-scope questions. The BANK record stays proposed and source-unverified beyond this bounded note.
