## *Brown* in 1954 — BANK-144-045

- The [published U.S. Reports opinion, *Brown v. Board of Education*, 347 U.S. 483](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep347/usrep347483/usrep347483.pdf), is the primary record of the Court's 17 May 1954 reasoning about segregation in public education. The [Library of Congress record of Chief Justice Warren's reading copy](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/declarations-promise/about-this-exhibition/created-equal/brown-v-board-of-education/) identifies a contemporaneous opinion artifact.
- The opinion discusses education, equality, history and psychological effects, citing social-science work. It does not say one doll test alone persuaded every justice. The 1954 holding must also be distinguished from subsequent remedy and implementation decisions and from the actual pace of school desegregation.

## The 1944 GI Bill — BANK-144-046

- The [National Archives enrolled Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944 and transcript](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/servicemens-readjustment-act) provide the enacted benefit terms. A [Veterans Administration historical education-and-training summary](https://department.va.gov/history/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2026/04/NVAHCA.2023.09.Box1_.History.of_.GI_.Bill_.Comparisons.and_.Summaries.pdf) reports about 7.8 million World War II veterans participated in education **or training** by the original program's end; about 2.2 million attended higher-learning institutions, with other participants in vocational, on-the-job and farm training.
- Enrollment is not identical to eligibility or completion. The archive's “8 million college students created the middle class” framing merges different categories and makes a causal claim not proven by this count. The National Archives notes unequal access for Black veterans, including barriers in housing; education access and local institutional discrimination require their own records and denominators.
