# Research note: insect experiments and permit history

Checked 2026-10-24. These are bounded source-access notes for proposed BANK questions, not a verified_sources entry or IDEA readiness.

## BANK-193-037 — Oxitec OX5034 permit history

- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, [March 7, 2022 experimental-use permit amendment](https://www.epa.gov/pesticides/following-review-available-data-and-public-comments-epa-expands-and-extends-testing), describes OX5034 Aedes aegypti field-testing scope in Florida, extension to April 30, 2024, and proposed efficacy-data collection. Its 2020 original permit is described there.
- U.S. EPA, [later fact check on authorization](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/fact-check-release-genetically-engineered-mosquitoes-not-authorized-epa), accessed 2026-10-24, states that the permit expired in 2024, no U.S. releases are currently authorized, and EPA is unaware of releases after expiration.

**Claim limit:** The 2022 decision was permission for specified experiments, not proof of reduced dengue transmission or broad public-health benefit. OX5034 self-limiting progeny is not the CRISPR self-copying gene-drive mechanism in BANK-155-024 and BANK-193-027. The authorization is historical; do not imply an active U.S. release program. No breeding, release or genetic engineering instructions.

## BANK-193-038 — dung beetle orientation experiment

- Dacke, Baird, Byrne, Scholtz and Warrant, [2013 original Current Biology paper (university-hosted PDF)](https://web.mnstate.edu/wisenden/AnBeh13%20Paper%205%20Dacke%20et%20al%202013.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2012.12.034. The authors compare paths of the nocturnal ball-rolling species Scarabaeus satyrus under clear and overcast moonless sky and under planetarium sky conditions, including a Milky Way display.
- [Publisher's paper page](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(12)01507-2) is a traceable original-publication address but returned HTTP 403 on this access; the university-hosted paper was used for content.

**Claim limit:** This is a controlled orientation result for a studied species and conditions, not evidence that every dung beetle reads individual stars, navigates long distances, or relies on a human-like map. Distinguish path straightness in an arena from landscape-scale travel. BANK-193-035 is about dung processing, a different question.

## Wider ecological limits

An insect-trap trend from one landscape cannot be generalized to every insect taxon or region. No home rearing, stinging-insect handling or disease-control directions follow from these cases.
