# Home-lifestyle case-source notes — 24 October 2026

These are bounded checks for three household claims. They do not verify the other 54 active proposals or the archive's trend and savings claims. Original pitch wording and status values remain preserved.

## BANK-064-051 — Indoor air quality (archive pending 4)

- The US EPA’s [indoor pollutants and sources guide](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/indoor-pollutants-and-sources) identifies indoor activities and materials that can release gases or particles. Its [home improvement guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/what-can-i-do-improve-indoor-air-quality-my-home) distinguishes source control, ventilation and supplemental filtration.
- Editorial boundary: specify the pollutant, source, room and measurement before discussing a remedy. “Invisible chemical smog inside every sealed home” is a dramatic framing, not a measured condition or medical diagnosis.

## BANK-064-068 — Suspected indoor mold (archive trending 26)

- The EPA’s [home mold and moisture guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) emphasizes correcting water problems and assessing the extent of growth. The CDC’s [mold health overview](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/) describes respiratory and allergic effects that may occur, while [CDC’s Stachybotrys page](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/data-research/facts-stats/index.html) cautions against linking a particular species to a person’s symptoms through a single test.
- Editorial boundary: this is a household documentation and source-control topic, not diagnosis, treatment or a claim that “black mold” causes brain injury. A clinician handles individual health concerns; repair responsibilities and remediation rules require location-specific checks.

## BANK-064-069 — Low-flow showerhead claim (archive trending 27)

- EPA WaterSense’s [showerhead criteria](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/showerheads) set a maximum labeled flow of 2.0 gallons per minute and include spray-performance tests. EPA’s [water-savings calculation guide](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100TFZF.txt) shows that savings depend on the old and new flow rates, shower duration and household use.
- Editorial boundary: compare a measured baseline, local water and energy rates, and a specified replacement product. The archive’s $50 price and half-bill promise are not supported as universal outcomes. Flow and perceived spray quality are different measurements.
