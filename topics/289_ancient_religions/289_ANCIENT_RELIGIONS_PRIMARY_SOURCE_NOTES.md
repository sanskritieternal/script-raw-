## BANK-289-044: Gudea Eninnu cone

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Votive cone with cuneiform inscription of Gudea](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/324015), object 45.90, inspected 2026-10-24. The museum dates the clay cone circa 2090 BCE, assigns it to Girsu, and summarizes an inscription commemorating Gudea's building of the Eninnu temple for Ningirsu.
- Limits: the catalog paraphrase is not a full transliteration or independently checked translation. A ruler's dedication does not give a census of workers, a complete ritual sequence, or the beliefs of everyone at Girsu. Keep temple/project attribution tied to this object and cite the museum's approximate date; check a cuneiform edition before quoting a line.

## BANK-289-045: Userhat votive stela

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Votive stela of Userhat](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544776), object 05.4.2, inspected 2026-10-24. The museum dates it circa 1328-1300 BCE and records its 1905 excavation at the Temple of Mentuhotep II at Deir el-Bahri. It identifies Userhat, his wife Nefertari and Userhat's mortuary-cult priesthood, while describing the divine addressee as probably Amun.
- Limits: 'probably Amun' remains a qualified curatorial identification. The stela's findspot and inscription do not by themselves reconstruct a whole household's daily ritual or all New Kingdom religion. Do not assume Nefertari's personal views from her image or erase her named presence. Verify translation against an Egyptological edition before quoting wording. This named object complements, but does not duplicate, the bank's general votive-inscription method.
