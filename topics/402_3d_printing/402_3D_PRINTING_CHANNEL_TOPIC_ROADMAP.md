## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of 3D Printing across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: 3D Printing Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in 3D Printing.
- **Tier 2: 3D Printing Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of 3D Printing.
- **Tier 3: 3D Printing Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in 3D Printing.
- **Tier 4: 3D Printing Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for 3D Printing.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of 3D Printing: An Additive Process Emerges**
   - *Question:* Which dated patent or machine changed a specific printing process?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish prototype, commercialization, and later variants. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
2. **3D Printing: A Printed Part under Test**
   - *Question:* What does a named printed object's documented test prove?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify material, orientation, machine, process, and load. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
3. **3D Printing: Work behind a Printed Object**
   - *Question:* Who modeled, prepared, operated, postprocessed, and inspected it?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits and process logs. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
4. **3D Printing: From Model to Part**
   - *Question:* How do geometry, slicing, build, postprocessing, and inspection connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one documented part and record versions. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
5. **3D Printing: Anisotropy in a Print**
   - *Question:* How can build orientation change measured properties?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare controlled specimens and test methods. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **3D Printing: Why Did a Print Fail?**
   - *Question:* Which data distinguish feedstock, geometry, process, and handling causes?
   - *Key Focus:* Review machine log and material test; avoid diagnosis from a photo. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
7. **3D Printing: A New Printing Method**
   - *Question:* Which measurable limit did a particular process improve?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare accuracy, throughput, material, and repeatability. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
8. **3D Printing: A Designer Revises a Part**
   - *Question:* What test result led to a documented redesign?
   - *Key Focus:* Use model versions and inspection reports. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
9. **3D Printing: A Standard Changes Qualification**
   - *Question:* How did a dated test or standard alter a specified application?
   - *Key Focus:* Check edition, jurisdiction, and actual adoption. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
10. **3D Printing: Building Layer by Layer**
   - *Question:* Which process variables affect one part's geometry and properties?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe principle and measured parameters, not unsafe machine operation. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **3D Printing: A Printed Prototype Replaced**
   - *Question:* Why was a print abandoned for another process?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented cost, quality, and function. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
12. **3D Printing: A Part across Machine Versions**
   - *Question:* Which properties changed after hardware or software update?
   - *Key Focus:* Use same material and test where possible. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
13. **3D Printing: A Proposed Printed Application**
   - *Question:* What qualification evidence is missing for intended use?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify tests and regulatory path without prediction. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
14. **3D Printing: Can Print Anything?**
   - *Question:* Which size, material, finish, and strength limits matter for a named use?
   - *Key Focus:* Use actual machine specification and test. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
15. **3D Printing: One Part's Digital Thread**
   - *Question:* How did CAD revision, print log, and test produce the final object?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow provenance and accountable approvals. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **3D Printing: What the Part Must Do**
   - *Question:* Which fit, load, surface, and service-life requirements were specified?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief with measured output. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
17. **3D Printing: Feedstock and Machine Match**
   - *Question:* What do material batch and machine condition contribute?
   - *Key Focus:* Use certificates, logs, and process notes. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
18. **3D Printing: A Test Print's Lessons**
   - *Question:* Which measured deviation changed the model or settings?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare build, inspection, and revision record. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
19. **3D Printing: Form Enabled by Printing**
   - *Question:* Which geometry could be made more simply or differently?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare with alternative fabrication and function. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
20. **3D Printing: A Printed Part's Makers**
   - *Question:* Who designed, prepared, printed, finished, and qualified it?
   - *Key Focus:* Use revision history and credits. Evidence base: CAD and slicer versions, machine logs, feedstock batches, material characterization, part inspection and test records, NIST measurement research, and NIOSH emissions guidance.
