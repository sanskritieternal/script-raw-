# Selected retro-gaming case-source notes

Inspected 2026-10-24. These notes bound two proposed artifact cases. They do not verify the full archived console history or give instructions to modify electrical equipment. Proposed topics retain empty `verified_sources` until production review.

## Alamogordo cartridges — BANK-132-044

- The [Smithsonian National Museum of American History object record](https://americanhistory.si.edu/ne/collections/object/nmah_1519322) catalogs an *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial* Atari 2600 cartridge excavated at Alamogordo on 26 April 2014 and records its City of Alamogordo certification. [The Henry Ford artifact record](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/403653) catalogs a dated excavation image in the Atari Video Game Burial Collection; its [collection account](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/articles/detail/articles/2015/07/01/atari) describes recovered cartridges and associated material.
- These museum accession records establish the recovered artifacts and provenance of specific objects, not a precise count of every item buried or the archive's assertion that *E.T.* alone caused the 1983 industry crash. Museum narrative estimates should be distinguished from excavated sample counts and Atari business records.

## CPS-2 battery-key failure — BANK-132-045

- In a [first-person 2016 MAME/MESS project report](https://mamedev.emulab.it/fsanches/2016/03/07/recovering-a-dead-cps2-board-street-fighter-zero-2-brazil/), Felipe Sanches documents examination of a Brazilian *Street Fighter Zero 2* CPS-2 board. He reports that a failed battery lost battery-backed decryption-key data, preventing encrypted program code from running, and describes partial restoration using related decrypted code. Video remained partly corrupted, so the account is not a record of complete repair.
- The [MAME project CPS-2 source](https://github.com/mamedev/mame/blob/master/src/mame/capcom/cps2.cpp) is a traceable technical project record for the platform, but versions evolve. The phrase “suicide battery” is hobby shorthand. It should not be presented as evidence that every failed board is beyond repair, that Capcom intended permanent loss, or as a maintenance procedure.
