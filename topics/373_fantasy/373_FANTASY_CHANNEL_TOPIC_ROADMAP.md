## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Fantasy across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Fantasy Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Fantasy.
- **Tier 2: Fantasy Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Fantasy.
- **Tier 3: Fantasy Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Fantasy.
- **Tier 4: Fantasy Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Fantasy.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Fantasy: A Fantasy Tradition in Context**
   - *Question:* Which earlier narrative practice shaped one named fantasy work?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated works and documented influence; avoid a single origin story. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
2. **Fantasy: A Fantasy World Revisited**
   - *Question:* What choices make a named work's impossible world legible?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare text, maps if authored, and contemporary reception. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
3. **Fantasy: Sources of an Invented World**
   - *Question:* Whose language, lore, or craft influenced a fantasy setting?
   - *Key Focus:* Use attributed creator records and source traditions. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
4. **Fantasy: How Fantasy Rules Work**
   - *Question:* What can and cannot happen under one story's stated rules?
   - *Key Focus:* Read examples and exceptions before summarizing a system. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
5. **Fantasy: Power and Consequence in Fantasy**
   - *Question:* How does an invented power alter social institutions in a novel?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace rule, access, cost, and resulting choices. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Fantasy: An Ambiguous World Rule**
   - *Question:* Does a seeming contradiction have an explanation in the text?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare editions and authorized paratext; leave gaps open. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
7. **Fantasy: in an Interactive Medium**
   - *Question:* Which story possibilities change when a reader can choose actions?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare fixed text and an interactive adaptation. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
8. **Fantasy: A Fantasy Maker's Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a creator change a creature, map, or rule?
   - *Key Focus:* Use drafts and attributed accounts rather than inferred intent. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
9. **Fantasy: A Fantasy Work Finds New Readers**
   - *Question:* What dated publication or adaptation changed its circulation?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace editions, access, and situated reception. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
10. **Fantasy: A Magical Rule Causes a Plot Turn**
   - *Question:* Which prior rule makes a later action plausible?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a cited chain from setup to consequence. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Fantasy: A Fantasy Trope Reassessed**
   - *Question:* How did criticism of a particular trope change over time?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated works and reviews across audiences. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
12. **Fantasy: Two Eras of Fantasy Worldbuilding**
   - *Question:* What changes in whose viewpoint and whose history a world includes?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare bounded texts and publication contexts. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
13. **Fantasy: A New Fantasy Format**
   - *Question:* Which documented experiment changes reader agency or world continuity?
   - *Key Focus:* Use released work and production constraints, not forecasts. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
14. **Fantasy: One Fantasy Formula?**
   - *Question:* Do counterexamples undermine a claimed universal quest pattern?
   - *Key Focus:* Define pattern and test diverse works. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
15. **Fantasy: The Making of a Fantasy World**
   - *Question:* How did a setting evolve from notes to publication?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated drafts, illustrations, and editorial decisions. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Fantasy: Inventing a Bounded World**
   - *Question:* What story question did the creator's brief require the world to support?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare stated brief, rules, and finished narrative. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
17. **Fantasy: Maps, Glossaries, and Drafts**
   - *Question:* What do worldbuilding artifacts explain and what do they omit?
   - *Key Focus:* Check version and authorship against the text. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
18. **Fantasy: Revising a Fantasy Rule**
   - *Question:* Which draft change solved or created a plot problem?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare documented versions and resulting scenes. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
19. **Fantasy: Revealing an Impossible Place**
   - *Question:* When does description withhold or supply a world rule?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze a bounded passage and reader knowledge. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
20. **Fantasy: A Shared Fantasy World**
   - *Question:* Who designed text, art, lore, and adaptation rules?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits, agreements, and version history. Evidence base: identified editions, author drafts, source traditions, production notes, archive records, attributed criticism, and situated reader accounts.
