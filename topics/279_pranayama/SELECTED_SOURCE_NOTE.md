# Selected primary-study note: pranayama

Checked 2026-10-24. Proposed cases BANK-279-127 and BANK-279-128.

Gandharva et al., *Complementary Medicine Research* 2025, randomized crossover study, involved 45 adults in five separate 15-minute sessions: right-nostril, left-nostril, alternate-nostril, breath awareness and quiet sitting. It recorded baseline nostril patency and pre/post mood and attention tasks: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39908000/ ; publisher abstract https://karger.com/cmr/article-abstract/32/3/205/920370/A-Randomized-Controlled-Crossover-Trial-to?redirectedFrom=fulltext . Abstract access supports design and stated short-term outcomes; full methods/supplement should be inspected before a numerical effect claim. A mood or cancellation-task change is not treatment efficacy, durable attention improvement, or proof of traditional nadi theory. Do not supply breathing instructions from the protocol.

Yuce and Tasci, *Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice* 2020, single-blind randomized asthma study, compared one month of daily pranayama with relaxation, both adjunctive to standard treatment. The abstract reports higher Asthma Control Test and quality-of-life scores in the pranayama group but no significant between-group difference in pulmonary-function tests or peak expiratory flow: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32056817/ ; registered record https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04110951 . This is one study, not proof of cure, improved lung physiology, medication substitution or advice for people with asthma. Confirm sample, specific practice and analysis from full text before quantitative scripting; publisher full text may be restricted.

BANK-279-034/058/079/081/092 already frame generic patency, comparator, stress/asthma questions. These two proposals identify particular studies and separate short-term physiology/behavior from clinical outcomes.
