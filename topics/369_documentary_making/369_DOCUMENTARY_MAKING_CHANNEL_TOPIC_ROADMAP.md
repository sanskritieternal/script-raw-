## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Documentary Making across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Documentary Making Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Documentary Making.
- **Tier 2: Documentary Making Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Documentary Making.
- **Tier 3: Documentary Making Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Documentary Making.
- **Tier 4: Documentary Making Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Documentary Making.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Documentary Making: A Documentary Practice Emerges**
   - *Question:* Which specific production practice changed nonfiction filmmaking?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated films, production notes, and contemporary debate. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
2. **Documentary Making: A Documentary Claim Examined**
   - *Question:* How does a named film support its central factual claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace footage, documents, interviews, and omitted counterevidence. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
3. **Documentary Making: Subjects Behind the Camera**
   - *Question:* How did participants influence or contest the finished account?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consent records, production testimony, and participant responses. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
4. **Documentary Making: What Makes Footage Evidence?**
   - *Question:* What can one recorded scene establish and what lies outside its frame?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify date, location, recorder, edit, and corroboration. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
5. **Documentary Making: Truth in the Edit**
   - *Question:* How do selection, ordering, and narration change a factual inference?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare raw or earlier material with the release when available. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Documentary Making: A Documentary's Unverified Claim**
   - *Question:* Which evidence is missing for a key assertion?
   - *Key Focus:* Name the claim, search primary records, and mark uncertainty. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
7. **Documentary Making: A New Recording Method**
   - *Question:* How did a tool alter access, intimacy, or verification in a production?
   - *Key Focus:* Document equipment and subject conditions, not just image quality. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
8. **Documentary Making: A Director and Their Subjects**
   - *Question:* Whose choices shaped a sensitive scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Check filmmaker and participant accounts against footage and records. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
9. **Documentary Making: A Documentary Changes Public Debate**
   - *Question:* What documented response followed one film's release?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate temporal sequence from causal impact. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
10. **Documentary Making: Building a Verified Sequence**
   - *Question:* How are actuality footage, archive, narration, and graphics sourced?
   - *Key Focus:* Make a shot-level evidence and rights map. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Documentary Making: A Claim Under Challenge**
   - *Question:* How did new evidence change a documentary's account?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare original claim, correction, and subsequent response. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
12. **Documentary Making: Revisiting a Filmed Subject**
   - *Question:* What changed after the original filming, and who can verify it?
   - *Key Focus:* Date all follow-up evidence and seek subject response. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
13. **Documentary Making: Documentary Distribution Choices**
   - *Question:* Which current access route changes who can see or challenge a film?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify market, platform terms, and reachable audience. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
14. **Documentary Making: The Camera Never Lies?**
   - *Question:* Which filmed details are real yet misleading without context?
   - *Key Focus:* Show framing, timing, selection, and corroboration. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
15. **Documentary Making: An Investigation on Film**
   - *Question:* How did a production move from question to defensible public claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace leads, verification, dissent, and revisions. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Documentary Making: The Documentary's Promise**
   - *Question:* What question and duty to participants did the initial brief establish?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare proposal, consent language, and finished film. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
17. **Documentary Making: Camera, Recorder, and Archive**
   - *Question:* Which capture and archive choices affect evidence quality?
   - *Key Focus:* Track metadata, continuity, access, and source provenance. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
18. **Documentary Making: From Research to Release**
   - *Question:* What checks occur between interview, transcription, edit, and publication?
   - *Key Focus:* Map each step to its accountable record. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
19. **Documentary Making: Framing a Real Person**
   - *Question:* What interpretation does a portrait shot invite?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare framing with participant context and explicit factual claims. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
20. **Documentary Making: Whose Documentary Is It?**
   - *Question:* What did participants, researchers, editors, and translators contribute?
   - *Key Focus:* Check agreements, credits, and conflicting testimony. Evidence base: original footage and metadata, transcripts, releases, primary records, archive provenance, production logs, participant responses, and documented corrections.
