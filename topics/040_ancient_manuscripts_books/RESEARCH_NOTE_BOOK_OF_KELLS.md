# Book of Kells: bounded source-access note

Linked proposal: [BANK-040-050](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-040-050). Research checked 2026-10-24. This is a source trail for case selection, not an episode claim ledger or a verified-source designation in the catalog.

## Object and question

Trinity College Dublin identifies the Book of Kells as **TCD MS 58**, a vellum Gospel book with 340 surviving folios. Its [collection description](https://www.tcd.ie/library/research-collections/book-of-kells.php) describes four scribal hands, decoration by several artists, corrections in the text, and later property transactions written on blank spaces of specific folios. Those traces support a focused question about production and later use of this particular book.

## Checks for a researched episode

1. Inspect folio 34r (Chi Rho page), folios 218v–219r (a copied passage and its correction), and folios 5v–7v and 27r (later property entries) in the [holding library's description](https://www.tcd.ie/library/research-collections/book-of-kells.php) and its linked digital images. Record the exact folio and image citation for each scene.
2. Compare the holding library's description of [pigment research](https://www.tcd.ie/library/research-collections/book-of-kells.php) with its [conservation project account](https://www.tcd.ie/History_of_Art/research/gospel-books.php) before naming a pigment or demonstrating a workshop process. In particular, the library says lapis lazuli was probably **not** used in its blue paint.
3. Treat place of production as contested. The holding library lists Iona as the prevailing attribution while reporting other proposed origins; do not turn an attribution into a proven workshop address.
4. Distinguish manuscript observation from interpretation: four scribal hands are an expert attribution, the property entries are later use, and the claim that the book was intended for ceremony is an inference from decoration and textual errors.

## Limits

These collection pages establish accessible object facts and research leads. The episode still needs direct inspection of the cited folios, edition and image rights checks, and a scholarly comparison of script attribution and provenance before production.
