# Selected primary-source checks (2026-10-24)

## BANK-217-038: asperitas

- WMO International Cloud Atlas, official asperitas entry: https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/clouds-supplementary-features-asperitas.html
- WMO account of the 2017 atlas additions: https://public.wmo.int/news/media-centre/international-cloud-atlas-translated-official-un-languages
- Asperitas is an atlas *supplementary feature*, commonly seen with stratocumulus and altocumulus, not a new cloud genus. The formal visual description does not alone prove a unique process or forecast a severe storm. Separate classification date from the date of any photographed sky.

## BANK-217-039: ship tracks

- Yuan et al., original research, *Science Advances* (2022), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.abn7988: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abn7988
- NASA account of its MODIS study, 18 October 2022: https://www.nasa.gov/missions/aqua/nasa-study-finds-evidence-that-fuel-regulation-reduced-air-pollution-from-shipping/
- NASA reports a 2003-2020 daytime satellite record and fewer visible ship tracks after the 2020 global marine fuel sulfur rule; pandemic shipping changes were considered. Direct DOI access returned HTTP 403 in this session, so details beyond the accessible agency account require full-paper review before production. Visible ship-track counts are not the complete cloud, aerosol, or climate response; causal attribution and selection bias require method review.
