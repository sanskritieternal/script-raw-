### Sensors, signals and growth direction — foundation

Subcategory `CATALOG-204-R01` · foundation.

<a id="bank-204-011"></a>
#### BANK-204-011 — Sensory Overload: How Plants Possess Over 15 Sensory Channels Without Sense Organs

**Video question:** How do plants sense humidity gradients, soil chemical toxins, electromagnetic fields, and mechanical vibrations across their entire bodies?

**Individual coverage scope:** Mechanosensitive ion channels (MSCs), hygroscopic sensing, heavy metal root avoidance, blue and red photoreceptor ratios detecting competitor shade, and chemical volatile receptors.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-013"></a>
#### BANK-204-013 — Photoreception Networks: Phytochromes Measuring Day Length to the Minute

**Video question:** How do plants know the exact day of spring to burst into bloom without ever looking at a calendar?

**Individual coverage scope:** Phytochrome red/far-red (Pr to Pfr) reversible conformational switching, night-break experiments, CONSTANS and Flowering Locus T (FT) 'Florigen' mobile protein messengers, and circadian gene loops.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-014"></a>
#### BANK-204-014 — Gravitropism and Statoliths: How Dense Starch Grains Roll to Tell Up from Down

**Video question:** What happens inside a root cap cell when a potted plant is tipped on its side, and how does it redirect roots downward within minutes?

**Individual coverage scope:** Heavy amyloplast starch statoliths settling along the lower endoplasmic reticulum membrane, triggering asymmetric PIN protein auxin efflux carriers, and contrasting shoot vs root auxin responses.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

### Sensors, signals and growth direction — intermediate

Subcategory `CATALOG-204-R02` · intermediate.

<a id="bank-204-015"></a>
#### BANK-204-015 — Electrical Signaling Along Phloem Cables: Action Potentials Matching Animal Nerves

**Video question:** How do plants transmit rapid electrical action potentials through vascular sieve tubes at speeds of centimeters per second?

**Individual coverage scope:** Plasma membrane depolarization via chloride efflux and potassium influx, slow wave potentials (variation potentials), electrical transmission through plasmodesmata, and wound response coordination.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-017"></a>
#### BANK-204-017 — Calcium Waves and Wound Signals: How a Bug Bite Warns the Entire Plant in Seconds

**Video question:** How do fluorescent GFP biosensors reveal glowing waves of calcium ions rushing through a plant's vascular veins after a caterpillar bites one leaf?

**Individual coverage scope:** Glutamate receptor-like (GLR) channels releasing cytosolic calcium spikes, propagating systemic systemic acquired resistance (SAR), and triggering distal leaves to synthesize defensive tannins.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-020"></a>
#### BANK-204-020 — Intelligent Foraging: How Roots Navigate Toward Nutrients While Avoiding Obstacles

**Video question:** How do underground root tips assess cost-benefit trade-offs, investing energy into dense root branching only when encountering high-grade compost?

**Individual coverage scope:** Economic foraging theory applied to root systems, detecting nutrient gradient patches (nitrate and phosphate hotspots), altering growth angles before physical collisions, and root competition.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-022"></a>
#### BANK-204-022 — Parasitic Stalking: How the Dodder Vine (Cuscuta) Sniffs Out Tomato Hosts

**Video question:** How does a leafless, rootless parasitic vine slither across soil toward the scent of a tomato seedling like a hunting predator?

**Individual coverage scope:** Volatile chemical chemoreception (sniffing beta-phellandrene and beta-myrcene), rejecting poor host wheat plants, coiling around nutrient-rich tomato stems, and driving haustoria into phloem veins.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-023"></a>
#### BANK-204-023 — Game Theory in the Canopy: Branch Growth Adjusting Angles to Avoid Shading Neighbors

**Video question:** Why do forest trees adjust their branch growth to leave mysterious gaps called 'crown shyness' where canopies never touch?

**Individual coverage scope:** Crown shyness mutual boundary avoidance, Far-red light reflection from leaf edges warning of impending shade, minimizing wind collision branch breakage, and evolutionary game theory equilibria.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

### Experiments on retained responses and interactions — intermediate

Subcategory `CATALOG-204-R03` · intermediate.

<a id="bank-204-016"></a>
#### BANK-204-016 — The Mimosa Pudica Habituation: Monica Gagliano's Drop Tests Demonstrating Memory

**Video question:** How did repeated mechanical dropping of sensitive Mimosa plants prove they can learn that a stimulus is harmless and retain the memory for 40 days?

**Individual coverage scope:** Non-associative learning (habituation), ruling out motor fatigue by shaking the plant after drops (causing immediate closure), persistence of memory in darkness, and challenging the brain-only paradigm.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-018"></a>
#### BANK-204-018 — Acoustic Perception: How Roots Sense the Vibration of Water and Chewing Caterpillars

**Video question:** Can plants actually hear? How do pea seedling roots grow toward acoustic recordings of running water inside sealed plastic tubes?

**Individual coverage scope:** Acoustic mechanoreception, Arabidopsis plants priming chemical toxins when exposed to audio recordings of caterpillars chewing leaves (but ignoring wind sounds), and phonotropism.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-019"></a>
#### BANK-204-019 — Epigenetic Memory: How Plants 'Remember' Winter Cold and Heatwaves for Future Seasons

**Video question:** How does a winter wheat seed 'remember' that it was exposed to 40 days of sub-zero cold months after the blizzard ends?

**Individual coverage scope:** Vernalization genetics, histone methylation and chromatin remodeling turning off the flowering repressor FLC (Flowering Locus C), passing stress memories to offspring via small RNAs.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-021"></a>
#### BANK-204-021 — Kin Recognition in Plants: Sharing Root Zones with Siblings While Strangling Strangers

**Video question:** How does a plant tell whether an adjacent root in the dark soil belongs to its own seed sibling or a genetic competitor?

**Individual coverage scope:** Root exudate chemical signatures, Cakile edentula (sea rocket) growing polite restrained roots beside siblings vs aggressive competitive proliferation beside strangers, and altruism.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.

<a id="bank-204-024"></a>
#### BANK-204-024 — Maternal Care in Plants: How Mother Trees Preferentially Route Carbon to Saplings

**Video question:** How do old-growth Douglas firs use fungal networks to recognize their own genetic offspring and feed them life-saving sugars in deep forest shade?

**Individual coverage scope:** Suzanne Simard's isotope labeling experiments (tracing Carbon-13 and Carbon-14 between trees), preferential kin carbon allocation, reducing root competition near offspring, and intergenerational forest legacy.

Source: preserved local roadmap proposal. Its original framing is not a verified finding.
