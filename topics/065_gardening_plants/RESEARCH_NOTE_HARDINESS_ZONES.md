# 2023 USDA hardiness map: bounded use note

Linked proposal: [BANK-065-054](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-065-054). Research checked 2026-10-24. This note addresses the United States map and is not a planting prescription for every country or season.

The [USDA Agricultural Research Service 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map](https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/) defines zones by **average annual extreme minimum winter temperature**, shown in 10°F zones and 5°F half-zones. The agency's [map-creation page](https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/pages/map-creation) explains the interpolation from weather stations and adjustments for elevation and topography. The [national map PDF](https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/system/files/National_Map_HZ_36x24_300.pdf) labels its underlying climate period as 1991–2020.

For an episode, pick one US ZIP code and a named perennial cultivar, record the zone from the current map, and check the cultivar's stated hardiness range. Then inspect heat exposure, drainage, wind, snow cover, site microclimate and actual local frost dates separately. A zone does not specify vegetable sowing dates or guarantee that a particular plant survives an unusually cold year. A non-US garden needs its own regional climate source and local growing guidance.

The source establishes what the map measures, not the fate of any individual plant. [BANK-065-025](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-065-025) remains the question for a local vegetable calendar. The bank record remains proposed and source-unverified beyond this bounded source access.
