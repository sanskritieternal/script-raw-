# Container gravel-layer claim: bounded extension note

Linked proposal: [BANK-065-064](IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-065-064). Research checked 2026-10-24. This note concerns a typical potted-plant setup; it is not a diagnosis of a particular plant.

The University of Illinois Extension's [container drainage guidance](https://extension.illinois.edu/container-gardens/container-drainage-options) says a gravel layer under potting mix does **not** improve drainage inside an individual pot and emphasizes drainage holes. Its [houseplant watering guidance](https://extension.illinois.edu/houseplants/watering) lists plant type, light, pot size, media and season among factors that change watering frequency. The [University of Minnesota Extension drainage explanation](https://extension.umn.edu/natural-resources/conservation/agricultural-soil-and-water/how-agricultural-drainage-works) explains why a drainage outlet lets free water leave while water remains held by the substrate.

For a demonstration, use otherwise identical pots with the same mix, plant and bottom holes, varying only the gravel layer; measure root-zone depth, drainage and moisture over time. Keep an outer decorative cachepot distinct from a layer *inside* the growing pot. A gravel layer is not proof that a plant was harmed, and standing water, poor media, plant species and watering schedule need separate inspection.

These institutional guides support case selection and a test design. They do not establish that every plant in every pot will develop root rot, or that one watering interval fits all seasons. The catalog proposal remains unverified and not production-ready.
