## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Food Systems across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Food Systems Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Food Systems.
- **Tier 2: Food Systems Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Food Systems.
- **Tier 3: Food Systems Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Food Systems.
- **Tier 4: Food Systems Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Food Systems.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Food Systems: How Food Systems Developed**
   - *Question:* How did production and distribution changes shape a regional food system?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one regional food system through changes in production, processing and distribution; define the foods, geography and historical period. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Food Systems: A Documented Food Systems Case**
   - *Question:* Who benefited from a food-system intervention within its stated boundary?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one food-system intervention through farmer, worker and consumer outcomes; compare claimed benefits with its chosen system boundary. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
3. **Food Systems: People behind Food Systems**
   - *Question:* Whose labor or knowledge is missing from a food supply-chain account?
   - *Key Focus:* Document overlooked labor or knowledge in one food supply chain; use consented accounts and distinguish paid work from household or informal labor. Evidence base: Use attributable contribution records and consented participant accounts. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
4. **Food Systems: Understanding Food Systems**
   - *Question:* How do food flows, prices and nutritional access connect for one product?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain production, processing, distribution and consumption through one food product; distinguish physical flows, prices and nutritional access. Evidence base: Use primary definitions and a documented end-to-end example. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
5. **Food Systems: Tradeoffs in Food Systems**
   - *Question:* Which system-boundary choices change a food-system comparison?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare food-system analyses using consistent functional units, lifecycle boundaries, allocation and assumptions about waste or substitution. Evidence base: Compare original methods, assumptions, measures and uncertainty. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

6. **Food Systems: An Open Food Systems Question**

Evidence base: Compare competing explanations and specify missing discriminating evidence. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Evidence base: Compare validation, deployment and follow-up against a stated baseline. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Case records: material and price flows, production and distribution records, worker accounts and household access measures. Starting framework: FAO agrifood systems resources (https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
