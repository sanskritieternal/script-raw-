## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Cognitive Science across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Cognitive Science Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Cognitive Science.
- **Tier 2: Cognitive Science Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Cognitive Science.
- **Tier 3: Cognitive Science Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Cognitive Science.
- **Tier 4: Cognitive Science Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Cognitive Science.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Cognitive Science: A Cognitive Idea Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which experiment introduced a testable model of memory or attention?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated protocols and later replication. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
2. **Cognitive Science: A Classic Cognitive Task Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What did participants actually do and what does the score mean?
   - *Key Focus:* Read task design, sample, and limits. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
3. **Cognitive Science: Participants Behind a Finding**
   - *Question:* Who took part and whose experience was excluded?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine recruitment, compensation, and access. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
4. **Cognitive Science: Attention in a Defined Task**
   - *Question:* How do stimulus, goal, response, and measurement connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one experiment without claiming a single brain location. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
5. **Cognitive Science: Task Score Versus Cognitive Ability**
   - *Question:* Does one test measure the construct it names?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare reliability, convergent evidence, and everyday function. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Cognitive Science: A Cognitive Effect That Failed to Replicate**
   - *Question:* Which protocol or population differences matter?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare registered methods and outcomes. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
7. **Cognitive Science: A New Cognitive Measurement**
   - *Question:* Did a digital task or sensor improve inference?
   - *Key Focus:* Test validity, accessibility, and error. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
8. **Cognitive Science: A Cognitive Theory Debate**
   - *Question:* How did researchers test rival accounts?
   - *Key Focus:* Read original studies, critiques, and replications. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
9. **Cognitive Science: A Replication Changes Confidence**
   - *Question:* What did a multi-site test establish or limit?
   - *Key Focus:* Inspect common protocol, sample, and effect estimates. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
10. **Cognitive Science: One Decision in Context**
   - *Question:* What information and memory does a participant use?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace task design and observed behavior. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Cognitive Science: A Cognitive Claim Reassessed**
   - *Question:* Which new data changed confidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare effect sizes and bias over time. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
12. **Cognitive Science: One Task Across Methods**
   - *Question:* How did controlled and naturalistic measures differ?
   - *Key Focus:* Use matched construct and population. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
13. **Cognitive Science: A Proposed Cognitive Model**
   - *Question:* What observation would make it useful beyond a lab task?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify ecological and construct validity tests. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
14. **Cognitive Science: A Brain Task Reveals Intelligence?**
   - *Question:* What does the task measure and what does it not?
   - *Key Focus:* Avoid reducing a person to one score. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
15. **Cognitive Science: An Experiment from Design to Replication**
   - *Question:* How did recruitment, protocol, analysis, and review unfold?
   - *Key Focus:* Use deidentified records and consent. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Cognitive Science: Reaction Time to Mental Process**
   - *Question:* Which assumptions link a delay to a cognitive stage?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare alternative models and controls. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
17. **Cognitive Science: Measuring Memory or Attention**
   - *Question:* Which task, score, and reliability evidence are used?
   - *Key Focus:* State population, language, and setting. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
18. **Cognitive Science: Momentary Task and Lifelong Ability**
   - *Question:* What can a brief result say about stable performance?
   - *Key Focus:* Use repeat measures and context. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
19. **Cognitive Science: Cognition in Daily Life**
   - *Question:* Does a controlled effect appear in ordinary environments?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare ecological validity and confounders. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
20. **Cognitive Science: Different Ways to Solve a Task**
   - *Question:* How do strategy and prior experience change scores?
   - *Key Focus:* Report within-person and group variation. Evidence base: original experimental protocols, registered replications, construct-validation studies, deidentified participant data, and naturalistic observations.
