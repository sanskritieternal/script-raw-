# Selected primary-source research: internet history cases

Checked 2026-10-24. These three source checks bound the linked proposals only. They do not verify the other archived numbers, anecdotes or sweeping cause claims.

## CERN’s 30 April 1993 web-software release — BANK-129-044

- Primary institution record: CERN, [Licensing the Web](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/licensing-web/). CERN identifies the line-mode client, basic server and common code library in its public-domain statement of 30 April 1993.
- Limit: The statement concerns specified CERN web software. It does not establish that CERN relinquished every later web component, that HTTP/HTML/URLs were all patented and waived, or that this one act alone caused global adoption. Distinguish software licensing from standards development and infrastructure adoption.

## United States v. Microsoft browser litigation — BANK-129-045

- Primary court record: U.S. Department of Justice’s copy of the [district court’s 1999 findings of fact](https://www.justice.gov/atr/us-v-microsoft-courts-findings-fact), [2001 Court of Appeals opinion](https://www.justice.gov/atr/case-document/file/504276/dl), and [case docket](https://www.justice.gov/atr/case/us-v-microsoft-corporation-browser-and-middleware).
- The findings discuss Windows OEM restrictions and browser distribution, including Internet Explorer and Navigator usage estimates for the specified 1990s periods. The appellate decision must be read separately for which liability findings survived, which were reversed, and which remedy issues were remanded.
- Limit: The original pitch’s “crush” and “biggest trial” language is not a legal holding. The record concerns particular markets, conduct and dates. Avoid saying Microsoft was convicted of every alleged browser offense or that software bundling alone explains Netscape’s decline.

## ICANN’s 2018 DNSSEC root-key rollover — BANK-129-046

- Primary operator record: ICANN, [2018 root-zone KSK rollover overview](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/ksk-rollover-2018-en) and [March 2019 review](https://www.icann.org/en/system/files/files/review-2018-dnssec-ksk-rollover-04mar19-en.pdf).
- ICANN reports that the new key signed the root-zone DNSKEY set on 11 October 2018. Its review reports limited visible disruption and also notes unprepared validating resolvers; resolver operators needed the updated trust anchor.
- Limit: The key protects DNSSEC validation of the DNS root. It is neither an on/off key for all internet traffic nor a way for a small group to reboot the global network. Impacts differ by resolver configuration. Keep the 2018 event separate from later rollovers or current root-zone procedures.
