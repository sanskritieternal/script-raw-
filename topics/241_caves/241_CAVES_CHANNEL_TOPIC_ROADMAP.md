## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Caves across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Caves Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Caves.
- **Tier 2: Caves Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Caves.
- **Tier 3: Caves Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Caves.
- **Tier 4: Caves Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Caves.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Caves: How a Cave Became Known**
   - *Question:* What evidence documents discovery, use, and later mapping?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish long-standing local knowledge from named exploration records. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
2. **Caves: A Celebrated Cave Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What does one famous cave illustrate and what is exceptional?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare geology, access, conservation, and source dates. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
3. **Caves: People Missing from Cave History**
   - *Question:* Whose knowledge or labor shaped exploration and protection?
   - *Key Focus:* Use attributed community, guide, and survey records. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
4. **Caves: What Makes This a Cave**
   - *Question:* How did a named void form in its host rock?
   - *Key Focus:* Define rock, water or lava process, scale, and evidence. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
5. **Caves: Reading Cave Development**
   - *Question:* Which features distinguish competing formation sequences?
   - *Key Focus:* Use mapped passages, sediments, mineral dates, and uncertainty. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Caves: An Unmapped Passage?**
   - *Question:* What remains unknown in a documented cave survey?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate survey gaps from unsupported hidden-chamber claims. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
7. **Caves: A Cave Survey Method Tested**
   - *Question:* What did one mapping technique reveal or miss?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare survey accuracy, access limits, and conservation effects. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
8. **Caves: A Decision About Cave Access**
   - *Question:* Who decided whether a cave would open to visitors?
   - *Key Focus:* Use management records and affected community views. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
9. **Caves: A Closure That Changed a Cave**
   - *Question:* What documented event altered access or conservation?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dates, evidence, rule changes, and outcomes. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
10. **Caves: Water, Rock, and Passage**
   - *Question:* How do processes create and alter one cave system?
   - *Key Focus:* Use local geology, hydrology, mapped features, and timescale. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Caves: Changing Cave Visitation**
   - *Question:* What explains a documented rise or decline?
   - *Key Focus:* Check counting method, access changes, season, and conservation limits. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
12. **Caves: A Cave Across Surveys**
   - *Question:* What changed between two dated maps?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate new discovery from changed measurement or damaged features. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
13. **Caves: Possible Cave Changes**
   - *Question:* Which physical or management conditions could alter this cave?
   - *Key Focus:* State water, climate, visitation, and conservation assumptions. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
14. **Caves: A Cave Legend Checked**
   - *Question:* Which parts of a local story have documentary support?
   - *Key Focus:* Respect oral tradition while distinguishing genre and evidence. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
15. **Caves: A Cave Survey Day**
   - *Question:* How did a documented survey build a map?
   - *Key Focus:* Use field notes and consent; omit sensitive location details. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Caves: Where a Cave Map Misleads**
   - *Question:* Which vertical, flooded, or unstable features need other evidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare survey grade, date, and managed visitor route. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
17. **Caves: The Managed Cave Route**
   - *Question:* Why does a site offer one route and restrict another?
   - *Key Focus:* Use conservation, safety, accessibility, and authority records. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
18. **Caves: A Cave's Seasonal Water**
   - *Question:* How do rainfall and groundwater change conditions?
   - *Key Focus:* Use monitoring series and current site notices. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
19. **Caves: Communities and a Cave Site**
   - *Question:* How do custodians and nearby residents describe access?
   - *Key Focus:* Seek varied, consented views and land-rights context. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
20. **Caves: The Catchment Above the Cave**
   - *Question:* How does surface land use affect underground water?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace drainage, pollutants, and cave measurements. Evidence base: geological and hydrological studies, dated surveys, site management plans, monitoring, and attributed community accounts.
