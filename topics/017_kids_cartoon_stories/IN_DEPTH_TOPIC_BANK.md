## Category: Characters, problems and social life

<a id="catalog-017-d01"></a>
### Character goals and feelings

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D01` · foundation.

<a id="bank-017-013"></a>
#### BANK-017-013 — How can a shy cloud learn to ask for help through visible action?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give it a specific task, a communication attempt and a shared solution rather than an adult lecture disguised as dialogue.

<a id="bank-017-014"></a>
#### BANK-017-014 — How can a tiger cub apologize without receiving an instant reward?

**Individual coverage scope:** Show recognition, repair and changed behavior; let the other character retain feelings and choices.

<a id="bank-017-015"></a>
#### BANK-017-015 — What makes a toy-sharing conflict understandable to a young audience?

**Individual coverage scope:** Clarify ownership, limited resources and a negotiated outcome without making every disagreement selfishness.

<a id="bank-017-016"></a>
#### BANK-017-016 — How can a robot express two feelings at the same time?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use simple situation cues and choices without implying one emotion must be suppressed for the story to end.

<a id="catalog-017-d02"></a>
### Everyday problem solving

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D02` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-017"></a>
#### BANK-017-017 — How can two animal friends solve a broken-bridge problem by combining different skills?

**Individual coverage scope:** Make each contribution necessary and observable; avoid solving the conflict through an unexplained magical arrival.

<a id="bank-017-018"></a>
#### BANK-017-018 — What can a seed's journey show about patience without promising all seeds grow?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use a fictional character while distinguishing real plant needs and uncertainty from the story's personification.

<a id="bank-017-019"></a>
#### BANK-017-019 — How can a moon rover solve a navigation puzzle at a child's level?

**Individual coverage scope:** Establish landmarks, a limited choice set and a testable plan rather than overwhelming the scene with technical terms.

<a id="bank-017-020"></a>
#### BANK-017-020 — How can a river-cleanup story avoid blaming one villain for every environmental problem?

**Individual coverage scope:** Show specific sources, collective actions and limits of a small project without inventing guaranteed ecological recovery.

<a id="catalog-017-d03"></a>
### Social learning through story

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D03` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-021"></a>
#### BANK-017-021 — How can a character say no to a game while remaining part of the group?

**Individual coverage scope:** Model boundaries, alternatives and respect without framing participation as the price of friendship.

<a id="bank-017-022"></a>
#### BANK-017-022 — How can a misunderstanding be resolved through checking rather than guessing intentions?

**Individual coverage scope:** Let characters ask, clarify and revise an assumption through dialogue and action.

<a id="bank-017-023"></a>
#### BANK-017-023 — What makes a mistake safe enough for a character to admit?

**Individual coverage scope:** Show a proportionate response and practical repair rather than humiliation or instant absolution.

<a id="bank-017-024"></a>
#### BANK-017-024 — How can a new character join an established group without becoming identical to it?

**Individual coverage scope:** Preserve different interests and strengths while building shared activity and mutual respect.

## Category: Structure, visuals and language

<a id="catalog-017-d04"></a>
### Age-appropriate structure

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D04` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-025"></a>
#### BANK-017-025 — How does the intended age range change the number of plot steps?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare vocabulary, causal links, duration and repetition using audience testing rather than assuming all children need the same structure.

<a id="bank-017-026"></a>
#### BANK-017-026 — When does repetition reinforce comprehension instead of merely extending runtime?

**Individual coverage scope:** Repeat a meaningful pattern with a changed detail or choice and check what children can predict.

<a id="bank-017-027"></a>
#### BANK-017-027 — How can suspense remain gentle without making the story inert?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use manageable uncertainty, reassurance and visible options rather than threats beyond the intended audience's understanding.

<a id="bank-017-028"></a>
#### BANK-017-028 — What helps a bedtime episode settle toward its ending?

**Individual coverage scope:** Reduce unresolved demands, pacing and sound intensity while avoiding medical promises about sleep.

<a id="catalog-017-d05"></a>
### Visual clarity and movement

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D05` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-029"></a>
#### BANK-017-029 — How can silhouette distinguish characters when the screen is small?

**Individual coverage scope:** Test shape, scale, costume and motion at actual viewing size rather than relying on detailed facial decoration.

<a id="bank-017-030"></a>
#### BANK-017-030 — What makes an animated action legible before a character explains it?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use anticipation, staging, clear contact and consequences rather than replacing visual logic with narration.

<a id="bank-017-031"></a>
#### BANK-017-031 — How should backgrounds support attention without overwhelming the main action?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare contrast, motion and clutter; preserve information needed to understand the scene.

<a id="bank-017-032"></a>
#### BANK-017-032 — How can a series maintain believable prop sizes across episodes?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use scale references, character turnarounds and continuity checks rather than independent attractive designs.

<a id="catalog-017-d06"></a>
### Language and accessibility

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D06` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-033"></a>
#### BANK-017-033 — How can a story introduce an unfamiliar word without stopping for a definition?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use action, repetition and context; check comprehension with the intended audience.

<a id="bank-017-034"></a>
#### BANK-017-034 — What makes captions useful for a children's animation?

**Individual coverage scope:** Check reading speed, speaker identification, vocabulary and important sounds without assuming captions suit every age equally.

<a id="bank-017-035"></a>
#### BANK-017-035 — How can dialogue remain understandable across different accents?

**Individual coverage scope:** Prioritize clarity, context and respectful performance rather than treating one accent as the only correct voice.

<a id="bank-017-036"></a>
#### BANK-017-036 — How should a bilingual story handle code-switching meaningfully?

**Individual coverage scope:** Anchor language changes to characters and situations while preserving enough context for the intended viewers.

## Category: Learning claims and series stewardship

<a id="catalog-017-d07"></a>
### Educational claims and representation

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D07` · advanced.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-037"></a>
#### BANK-017-037 — When does an entertaining educational label overstate what a story teaches?

**Individual coverage scope:** Define the learning goal and evaluate actual comprehension or transfer rather than viewing time alone.

<a id="bank-017-038"></a>
#### BANK-017-038 — How can a story represent a disabled child without making disability its only conflict?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give agency, relationships and ordinary goals; use relevant community input for specific access needs.

<a id="bank-017-039"></a>
#### BANK-017-039 — What should be reviewed before using cultural dress or rituals in animal-character stories?

**Individual coverage scope:** Check attribution, context and stereotyped simplification rather than treating a culture as decorative costume.

<a id="bank-017-040"></a>
#### BANK-017-040 — How can a cartoon distinguish fantasy behavior from real-world actions children may imitate?

**Individual coverage scope:** Review foreseeable imitation, framing and age appropriateness without turning the story into a warning checklist.

<a id="catalog-017-d08"></a>
### Series planning and review

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-D08` · advanced.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-017-D01](#catalog-017-d01).

<a id="bank-017-041"></a>
#### BANK-017-041 — How can recurring characters grow without contradicting earlier lessons?

**Individual coverage scope:** Track prior choices and relationships; let new situations complicate understanding rather than erase development.

<a id="bank-017-042"></a>
#### BANK-017-042 — What makes an episode's lesson emerge from consequence rather than a final speech?

**Individual coverage scope:** Tie the resolution to an observable decision and preserve emotional nuance.

<a id="bank-017-043"></a>
#### BANK-017-043 — How can a production review identify accidental scary details?

**Individual coverage scope:** Check images, sounds, wording and unresolved implications with the intended age group and appropriate adult input.

<a id="bank-017-044"></a>
#### BANK-017-044 — How can a channel avoid making every child-facing story a moral correction?

**Individual coverage scope:** Include curiosity, wonder, play, cooperation and open-ended discovery as valid story goals.

## Category: Children's story systems

<a id="catalog-017-r01"></a>
### Recurring characters and accessible episodes

Subcategory `CATALOG-017-R01` · intermediate.

<a id="bank-017-045"></a>
#### BANK-017-045 — How can a cartoon resolve two friends' incompatible wishes without making either child the villain?

**Individual coverage scope:** Build a concrete conflict, each viewpoint, attempted solutions and a negotiated ending suited to a defined age group.

<a id="bank-017-046"></a>
#### BANK-017-046 — What makes a recurring cartoon location easy to understand for a child watching out of order?

**Individual coverage scope:** Define stable landmarks, simple relationships, visual cues and self-contained stakes.

<a id="bank-017-047"></a>
#### BANK-017-047 — How can an episode let young viewers predict an outcome without relying on rapid visual stimulation?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use repetition with variation, clear timing, visual landmarks and a calm payoff; do not claim measured learning without evaluation.
