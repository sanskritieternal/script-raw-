# BANK-059-067 — Loch Ness environmental DNA

**Selected question:** What did the Loch Ness environmental-DNA survey detect and leave unresolved?

**Sources inspected (2026-10-24):**

- University of Otago, “[First eDNA Study Of Loch Ness Points To Something Fishy](https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy)” (5 September 2019), reporting lead investigator Neil Gemmell's account of the project. It describes 250 water samples, DNA sequencing/database comparisons, eel DNA at nearly every sampled location, and no detected signal supporting the selected plesiosaur, catfish, sturgeon or shark suggestions. The investigator explicitly says the data do not reveal eel size.
- [Loch Ness Project fieldwork account](https://www.lochnessproject.org/FIELDWORKGROUNDTRUTH/eDNA%20LOCH%20NESS/eDNA%20LOCHNESS_index.html), a local project participant's account of the survey and announcement. It explains that a rare/transient animal might be missed and that the eel DNA cannot confirm or refute unusual eel size because known eels would yield the same species signal.

**Bounded finding:** The sampled environmental DNA supports eel presence. It neither demonstrates a giant eel nor proves that every reported sighting has an eel explanation. A failure to detect other taxa in these samples is conditional on sampling time, location, assay and reference-database coverage; it is not a universal absence proof.

**Access and next checks:** These are project/institutional statements, not an inspected full peer-reviewed survey paper or raw read-level dataset. Before a script quotes exact species coverage or detection probabilities, obtain the methods, sample map, sequence data and relevant controls. Distinguish the 2018 sampling from the 2019 public announcement. The other creature-origin, fossil-memory and textual premises in this bank remain unverified, and no IDEA or production status changes follow from this note.
