# BANK-009-142 — Milgram's 1963 Yale obedience condition

Status: bounded primary-paper review for a topic proposal, not a verified script. Inspected 2026-10-24.

## Primary source inspected

- [Stanley Milgram, “Behavioral Study of Obedience,” *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology* 67 (1963), 371–378](https://www.psy.miami.edu/_assets/pdf/rpo-articles/milgram-1963.pdf). The published paper describes 40 male volunteers recruited around New Haven for the reported condition. Its learner was a confederate and the shocks to that learner were simulated. The generator displayed labels up to 450 volts; 26 of the 40 participants continued to the maximum setting in this condition.

## Editorial boundary

The archived queue asks whether “normal people” would shock a stranger “to death.” This experiment did not administer a fatal shock and does not estimate how all people would behave. A future episode should distinguish the participant's belief and observed switch use from real harm, explain the deception and sample, identify the exact condition when quoting a proportion, and compare later variants and methodological critiques before drawing a general conclusion. The archive and later-study review remain open. Do not use this note as production approval.
