# BANK-037-058 — Mont-Saint-Michel, access and maritime restoration

## Decision and question

Two archived pitches describe Mont-Saint-Michel as a gravity-defying abbey fortress and claim highway removal restored its “true tidal isolation.” The bounded case asks how an abbey and fortified village occupy the tidal rock, and how the recent access and sediment works changed the surrounding bay. `BANK-037-058` remains an unverified production proposal.

## Inspected institutional and operator records

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, “Mont-Saint-Michel and its Bay”](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/80/), inspected 2026-10-24. The site record describes the abbey and fortified village, the 1879 access causeway's role in silting, completion of major French state work in 2015, replacement with a footbridge and shuttle, and the Couesnon dam's sediment-flushing role.
- [European Commission regional-policy project record](https://regions-and-cities.ec.europa.eu/policy/communication/campaigns/places-europe/6a-mont-saint-michel-france_en), inspected 2026-10-24. It describes removal of the old causeway, a new dam, elevated footbridge, parking move and shuttle access during the 2000–2015 programme. Its budget figures are project accounting, not proof of hydrological outcome.
- [French public-site Couesnon dam account](https://montsaintmichel.gouv.fr/sous-partie-amenagements-et-grands-travaux/le-barrage-ouvrage-essentiel), inspected 2026-10-24. It reports 2009 commissioning and controlled water releases with bay monitoring; engineering figures are operator claims and would require hydrological reports for quantitative outcome claims.

## Claim boundaries and overlap

- “Restored maritime character” is the source language. A permanent isolated island or a completely natural tide system does not follow: the present landscape includes a footbridge, shuttles, hydraulic works and ongoing sediment management. Visitor access remains an operating system.
- UNESCO describes a sanctuary, abbey and fortified village, not a single medieval “fortress” designed in one stage. Its Gothic buildings and later restoration have separate chronologies. `BANK-037-013` covers a changing site generally; `BANK-037-035` covers water damage to forts; this case joins one place's built heritage, tidal setting and modern access intervention.
- A script giving exact sediment reduction, tidal exposure frequency, visitor effects or economic benefit needs dated before/after monitoring and resident/operator perspectives. Current access rules must be checked again at production time.
