# Selected source note - niche 156

Checked 2026-10-24. These are bounded leads for proposed questions, not individual approvals or a claim that all chemistry premises have been verified.

## BANK-156-046 - Household PFAS filters

The US EPA's [guide to identifying drinking-water filters certified to reduce PFAS](https://www.epa.gov/water-research/identifying-drinking-water-filters-certified-reduce-pfas) identifies granular activated carbon and reverse osmosis and directs consumers to contaminant-specific certification under NSF/ANSI 53 or 58. Its [April 2024 filter fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-04/water-filter-fact-sheet.pdf) warns that certification at that date did not necessarily demonstrate reduction to the EPA's newer drinking-water limits and stresses replacement on schedule. These are regulator statements about standards and use, not measurements of a chosen home filter. Check the current standard, specific product listing, analyte, local water test and maintenance date before making a performance claim. Neither a generic carbon pitcher nor a certified claim proves removal of every PFAS.

## BANK-156-047 - Plastic teabag particles

Hernandez et al., [*Plastic Teabags Release Billions of Microparticles and Nanoparticles into Tea*](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.9b02540), Environmental Science & Technology (2019), is a primary steeping experiment on selected plastic teabags. Its particle-count results concern tested materials and methods. The archived phrase "billion-particle plasticizer breakdown" conflates particles with dissolved additives; it is not a demonstrated human exposure or harm estimate. Read the full methods, blanks and identification protocol before using any numerical count.

## BANK-156-050 - Candle emissions

The primary chamber study [*Measurement and evaluation of gaseous and particulate emissions from burning scented and unscented candles*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33964641/) (2021) measured combustion gases, organic compounds and particulate emissions from candles made for comparative tests. Chamber results depend on formulation, wick, burn cycle and ventilation. The abstract does not establish a universal paraffin-versus-soy ranking, health outcome, or value of a $100 candle. Obtain the full methods and conflict-of-interest details before comparing named products.

## Semantic and claim boundaries

The original cookware/PTFE proposal BANK-156-010 already holds the air-fryer coating debate; a named model would need independent temperature and exposure evidence. BANK-091-058 in cooking covers sourdough yeast, bacteria, flour and time, so the archive pitch routes there. BANK-156-048 is about a specified cast-iron oil film rather than general rust or materials selection; BANK-156-049 is a controlled detergent product comparison rather than a general soap explanation. The 157 materials bank covers physical structure and properties, while this bank tests everyday chemical processes and product claims. Avoid converting detected chemicals or particles into health diagnoses without dose and exposure evidence.
