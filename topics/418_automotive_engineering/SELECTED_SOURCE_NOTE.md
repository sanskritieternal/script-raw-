# Selected source and boundary note — 418 Automotive Engineering

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-418-045**: Toyota Prius/Prius V U.S. safety recall 20V-369 (2020), framed as a hybrid-control failsafe design case.

## Primary records

- [Toyota's Part 573 Safety Recall Report filed with NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2020/RCLRPT-20V369-6392.PDF) describes a hybrid system that could shut down instead of entering reduced-power failsafe mode after specified faults, with potential loss of motive power at speed; it says steering and braking would remain operational. This is the manufacturer's regulatory account, not an independent reconstruction of every reported incident.
- [Toyota's June 24, 2020 remedy FAQ, hosted by NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2020/RCMN-20V369-9831.pdf) scopes certain 2013–2015 Prius and 2014–2017 Prius V vehicles and states that dealers would update hybrid-control software; specified inverter failures might receive repair or replacement. Use recall number, VIN and date when discussing an individual vehicle, because campaigns and completion status change. No repair procedure or owner safety advice is proposed here.

## Limits and overlap

The documents establish Toyota's filed defect and remedy scope in the United States; they do not establish that every Prius, hybrid or inverter shares the fault, prove frequency under every driving condition, or substitute for current VIN-level recall status. Do not give diagnostic or repair instructions; refer affected owners to official current recall information. **BANK-418-034** treats generic bench-versus-road failure, while this case follows a defined software failsafe/recall path. **BANK-022-031** covers why recall matters to an owner, **BANK-498-030** checking whether a product is included, and **BANK-087-054** generic failsafe principles. This bank stays with vehicle-system engineering. New topic remains proposed with empty verified_sources; no IDEA readiness is asserted.
