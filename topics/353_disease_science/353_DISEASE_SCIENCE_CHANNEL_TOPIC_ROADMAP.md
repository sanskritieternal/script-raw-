## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Disease Science across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Disease Science Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Disease Science.
- **Tier 2: Disease Science Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Disease Science.
- **Tier 3: Disease Science Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Disease Science.
- **Tier 4: Disease Science Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Disease Science.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Disease Science: A Disease Concept Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which observation separated a named condition from similar illness?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated clinical, laboratory, and classification records. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
2. **Disease Science: A Famous Disease Study Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What population and outcome did it actually examine?
   - *Key Focus:* Read design, controls, uncertainty, and later replication. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
3. **Disease Science: Patients and Public Health Workers**
   - *Question:* Whose care, reporting, and sample work made evidence possible?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented accounts and credit teams. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
4. **Disease Science: Disease Mechanism and Diagnosis**
   - *Question:* How do symptoms, pathology, and testing relate for one condition?
   - *Key Focus:* Use current authoritative definitions without individual advice. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
5. **Disease Science: Cause Versus Association**
   - *Question:* Which evidence supports a mechanism rather than a risk marker?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare longitudinal, experimental, and clinical data. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Disease Science: A Cluster with Unclear Cause**
   - *Question:* What is established about cases, exposure, and alternative explanations?
   - *Key Focus:* Use official investigation and avoid speculation about patients. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
7. **Disease Science: A New Diagnostic Test**
   - *Question:* Did it improve a defined clinical decision?
   - *Key Focus:* Check sensitivity, specificity, population, and harms. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
8. **Disease Science: A Patient's Role in Research**
   - *Question:* How did affected people shape priorities or study design?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented testimony and documented changes. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
9. **Disease Science: A Disease Classification Change**
   - *Question:* Which evidence changed a case definition?
   - *Key Focus:* Date the revision and compare before-and-after counts carefully. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
10. **Disease Science: From Exposure to Outcome**
   - *Question:* Which biological steps are supported for a named disease?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate established mechanism from hypotheses. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Disease Science: A Disease Rate Changes**
   - *Question:* Does the trend reflect cases, tests, reporting, or risk?
   - *Key Focus:* Use stable definitions and denominators. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
12. **Disease Science: One Condition Across Treatment Eras**
   - *Question:* How did evidence and outcomes change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare populations, care access, and endpoints. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
13. **Disease Science: A Proposed Therapy or Test**
   - *Question:* Which studies would establish benefit and safety?
   - *Key Focus:* State population, comparator, outcome, and follow-up. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
14. **Disease Science: A Symptom Proves a Diagnosis?**
   - *Question:* How many causes can produce it and what evidence distinguishes them?
   - *Key Focus:* Keep the episode educational, not diagnostic. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
15. **Disease Science: A Disease Study's Evidence Trail**
   - *Question:* How did cases, samples, analysis, and review produce a finding?
   - *Key Focus:* Protect identities and trace methods. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Disease Science: From Cases to Mechanism**
   - *Question:* What evidence links a factor to illness?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare confounding, dose, temporality, and biological tests. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
17. **Disease Science: Counting Disease**
   - *Question:* Which case definition and reporting system produced a rate?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish surveillance from clinical diagnosis. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
18. **Disease Science: Individual Illness and Population Trend**
   - *Question:* Why can a population risk fail to predict one person's course?
   - *Key Focus:* Use explicit denominator and time window. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
19. **Disease Science: Lab Finding to Patient Benefit**
   - *Question:* Which cell or animal result has been tested in humans?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate preclinical from clinical evidence. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
20. **Disease Science: Different Disease Courses**
   - *Question:* How do age, context, genetics, and care affect outcomes?
   - *Key Focus:* Report variation without stereotypes. Evidence base: WHO and CDC case-definition guidance, original clinical and epidemiologic studies, laboratory methods, systematic evidence, and consented patient accounts.
