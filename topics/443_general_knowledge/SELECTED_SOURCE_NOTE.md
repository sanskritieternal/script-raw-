# Selected source and boundary note — 443 General Knowledge

Reviewed 2026-10-24. Proposed **BANK-443-046** is an answer-source trail for a common count question, not a claim to define statehood.

## Primary institutional sources

- United Nations, [About Us](https://www.un.org/about-us), reports **193 Member States** as of this review. The [UN member-state list](https://www.un.org/about-us/member-states) provides the institutional roster and admission dates.
- United Nations Dag Hammarskjöld Library, [Observer States & Entities](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/observers), lists **two non-member observer states**, the Holy See and the State of Palestine, and distinguishes observer participation from membership. The UN's [*UN at a glance*](https://www.un.org/en/model-united-nations/un-glance) explains why **195 flags** appear at headquarters: 193 members plus those two observer states. The page has older contextual wording; use current membership/observer lists for present status.

## Limits, jurisdiction and overlap

“193 UN members” and “195 flags” answer different questions. Neither count is a complete, globally agreed definition of how many sovereign countries exist; recognition and territorial status are outside this case. Date any present count and recheck UN rosters before publication. The UN's map/designation disclaimer also cautions against inferring recognition from display. **BANK-218-080** treats how South Sudan's 2011 independence changed country-code mapping; **BANK-236-030** addresses territory versus sovereign state and **BANK-238-040** recognition-related institutional status.
