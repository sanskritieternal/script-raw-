# Brain–computer interfaces primary-source follow-up — 2026-10-24

## BANK-333-042 — 2021 attempted-handwriting study

- Primary paper: Willett et al., *High-performance brain-to-text communication via handwriting*, *Nature* 593 (12 May 2021): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03506-2 . Institutional account by Stanford Medicine, 12 May 2021: https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/05/software-turns-handwriting-thoughts-into-on-screen-text.html .
- The institution describes one participant with paralysis attempting handwriting while implanted recordings were decoded into letters. It reports around 18 words per minute on a defined task and distinguishes sentence copying from free composition.
- Limit: the Nature landing/abstract and Stanford account were available; full paper methods were not independently inspected here. Single-participant laboratory performance does not establish broad clinical effectiveness, stable home use or commercialization. Stanford says the system was investigational. Do not identify the participant beyond study-approved designation or provide medical advice.

## BANK-333-043 — 2023 speech and avatar study

- Primary paper: Metzger et al., *A high-performance neuroprosthesis for speech decoding and avatar control*, *Nature* 620 (23 August 2023): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06443-4 . Public author manuscript: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10826467/ .
- The authors report one clinical-trial participant with severe vocal and limb paralysis, using attempted speech to produce text, synthesized audio and animated facial output under research conditions.
- Limit: the Nature full text redirected to restricted access; the public manuscript/search extract and paper abstract were inspected. This is one participant and task-specific decoding, not arbitrary thought reading, general clinical efficacy or independent daily usability. The paper describes restricted neural data access; respect participant anonymity, voice and likeness consent.
