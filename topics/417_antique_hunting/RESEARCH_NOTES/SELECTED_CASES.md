# Selected antique-hunting case: two Yemeni sculptures at the Met

## BANK-417-043 — title and physical custody

- In its [19 September 2023 announcement](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/press-releases/yemen-agreement-2023-news), the Metropolitan Museum of Art states that provenance research led it to transfer ownership of a sandstone standing female figure and marble rectangular mortar to the Republic of Yemen. It says the statue had been bought in 1998 and the mortar accepted as a gift in 1999; both would remain at the Met on loan at Yemen's request.
- The museum announcement supplies the institution's account and a quoted Yemeni ambassador's agreement; it does not publish the complete underlying provenance file or agreement. The publicly stated transfer of title must not be narrated as physical return to Yemen, and the case does not settle rules for other objects.
- Script gate: inspect the object records, provenance evidence, agreement and current custody status before precise legal or ownership claims. Preserve the difference between an object's acquisition history, title, and location.
- Semantic boundary: BANK-417-035 asks the general disputed-ownership question and BANK-417-038 addresses purchasing checks; this case examines one documented title-versus-custody outcome. Adjacent cultural-heritage and collecting banks should link to this case rather than repeat a generic restitution promise.
