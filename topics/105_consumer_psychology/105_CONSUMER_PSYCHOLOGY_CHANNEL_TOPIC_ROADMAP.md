## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Consumer Psychology across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Cognitive Biases in Purchasing, Anchoring & The Decoy Effect (Scratch / Beginner)** — Daniel Kahneman's System 1 vs. System 2 thinking, price anchoring, the decoy effect (The Economist subscription experiment), loss aversion, and social proof.
- **Tier 2: Behavioral Economics, Nudge Theory & Habit Formation (Intermediate)** — Richard Thaler's Nudge Theory (choice architecture, default options), Nir Eyal's Hook Model (Trigger, Action, Variable Reward, Investment), and the Scarcity Principle (FOMO).
- **Tier 3: Sensory Branding, Neuromarketing & Loyalty Economics (Upper Intermediate)** — Olfactory and acoustic branding (the sound of an opening soda can), fMRI brain scan reactions to Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi, net promoter score (NPS), and the endowment effect (IKEA effect).
- **Tier 4: Dark Patterns, Algorithmic Personalization & The Ethics of Persuasion (Advanced / Expert)** — Deceptive e-commerce dark patterns (roach motels, hidden fees), TikTok dopamine prediction algorithms, surveillance capitalism (Shoshana Zuboff), and designing ethical consumer choices.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Cognitive Biases & Price Anchoring
1. **The Decoy Effect: How Menus and Tech Companies Secretly Force You to Pick the Expensive Option**
   - *Question:* Why did introducing a 'Print Only' subscription for $125 make 84% of readers choose the $125 'Print + Digital' combo over the $59 'Digital Only' option?
   - *Key Focus:* Dan Ariely's famous Economist subscription experiment, Asymmetric Dominance pricing, how cinema popcorn sizes (Small $3, Medium $6.50, Large $7) manipulate choice, and how SaaS software pricing tiers engineer the middle tier as an irresistible target.
2. **System 1 vs. System 2 Thinking: Daniel Kahneman on How Your Brain Actually Shops**
   - *Question:* Why do consumers make 95% of grocery store purchases using automatic, subconscious emotional shortcuts rather than analytical calculation?
   - *Key Focus:* Daniel Kahneman's 'Thinking, Fast and Slow', System 1 (fast, automatic, emotional, low-energy heuristics) vs. System 2 (slow, deliberate, analytical, high-effort), cognitive fatigue at supermarket checkouts, and packaging color psychology.
3. **Price Anchoring: How Steve Jobs Sold a $499 iPad by Starting with $999**
   - *Question:* How did Steve Jobs project a giant '$999' on screen during the 2010 iPad keynote, making $499 feel like the bargain of the century when it dropped?
   - *Key Focus:* Anchoring and Adjustment heuristic, luxury restaurant wine lists featuring an overpriced $300 bottle solely to make the $60 bottle seem reasonable, MSRP crossed-out strike-through pricing, and the psychological relativity of monetary value.
4. **Loss Aversion and The Endowment Effect: Why Losing $100 Hurts Twice as Much as Winning $100**
   - *Question:* Why do car dealerships let you take a car home for a weekend test drive? The psychological trap of instant ownership.
   - *Key Focus:* Kahneman and Tversky's Prospect Theory (losses loom 2 to 2.5 times larger than equivalent gains), Richard Thaler's coffee mug Endowment Effect, free 30-day trials transforming subscriptions from 'buying' to 'not wanting to lose what is already yours', and return policies.
5. **Social Proof and The Bandwagon Effect: Why 'Best Seller' Signs Double Conversion**
   - *Question:* Why did changing hotel bathroom signs from 'Save the environment' to '75% of guests in this room reuse their towels' cause compliance to skyrocket?
   - *Key Focus:* Dr. Robert Cialdini's 'Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion', social proof as an evolutionary survival shortcut in uncertainty, fake tip jars with seed cash, 'Over 1 Billion Served' McDonald's signage, and review star ratings overriding personal judgment.
### Tier 2: Nudge Theory, Habits & Scarcity
6. **Nudge Theory: Richard Thaler on Choice Architecture and The Power of Defaults**
   - *Question:* Why does Austria have a 99% organ donation rate while neighboring Germany has 12%, based purely on whether a box is checked by default?
   - *Key Focus:* Richard Thaler's Nobel Prize-winning Nudge Theory, Libertarian Paternalism, opt-in vs. opt-out default architecture, placing fruit at eye level in school cafeterias, automatic 401(k) retirement enrollment, and the fly painted in Amsterdam airport urinals cutting spillage by 80%.
7. **The Hook Model: How Nir Eyal Dissected Social Media's Habit-Forming Loops**
   - *Question:* How do Instagram, TikTok, and casino slot machines keep users returning 50 times a day using B.F. Skinner's variable reward schedules?
   - *Key Focus:* The 4-phase Hook Cycle: 1) Trigger (external notification moving to internal boredom/loneliness), 2) Action (minimal effort click/scroll), 3) Variable Reward (intermittent dopamine schedule: likes, surprises, novel videos), 4) Investment (user uploading photos or data making switching painful).
8. **The Scarcity Principle: FOMO, Countdown Clocks, and Black Friday Madness**
   - *Question:* Why does seeing 'Only 2 rooms left at this price!' on Booking.com make your heart race and force you to book immediately?
   - *Key Focus:* Cialdini's Scarcity Principle, psychological reactance (perceiving a loss of freedom when options disappear), artificial countdown timers in e-commerce, limited-edition sneaker drops (Supreme/Nike), and Black Friday retail stampedes.
9. **The Paradox of Choice: Barry Schwartz on Why Having 24 Jams Freezes Consumers**
   - *Question:* Why did a supermarket tasting booth with 24 jams attract more lookers, but a booth with only 6 jams sold ten times more actual jars?
   - *Key Focus:* Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper's 2000 Jam Study, Barry Schwartz's 'Paradox of Choice', choice overload causing decision paralysis, buyer's remorse and post-purchase regret, Maximizers vs. Satisficers, and why curated minimalist menus outperform giant phone-book menus.
10. **Charm Pricing: The Magic of $9.99 and Left-Digit Bias**
   - *Question:* Why does changing a price from $34.00 to $39.00 sometimes INCREASE sales, and how does the human brain process numbers from left to right?
   - *Key Focus:* Left-Digit Bias (the brain processes the leading digit before finishing reading the decimal, encoding $2.99 as fundamentally closer to $2.00 than $3.00), Eric Anderson and Duncan Simester's catalog experiments showing $39 outselling $34, and prestige pricing (why luxury brands round up to clean $1,000 figures).
### Tier 3: Sensory Branding & Neuromarketing
11. **Sensory Branding: Martin Lindstrom on Scent, Sound, and Subconscious Purchasing**
   - *Question:* Why does Singapore Airlines spray a patented custom perfume ('Stefan Floridian Waters') on every flight attendant, and why did Harley-Davidson try to patent its engine sound?
   - *Key Focus:* Martin Lindstrom's 'Brand Sense', olfactory marketing bypassing the thalamus straight to the emotional amygdala/hippocampus, Abercrombie & Fitch cologne scent diffusion, Kellogg's patented cereal crunch sound acoustics, and multi-sensory brand anchoring.
12. **The Pepsi Challenge: How fMRI Brain Scans Proved That Brands Override Taste**
   - *Question:* Why did consumers overwhelmingly prefer Pepsi in blind taste tests, but chose Coca-Cola the second they saw the red can inside an fMRI brain scanner?
   - *Key Focus:* Read Montague's 2004 Baylor College of Medicine neuroimaging study: blind tasting activated the sensory ventral putamen (Pepsi wins on sweeter sugar taste), but seeing the Coca-Cola brand logo activated the hippocampus and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (emotional memories overriding physical taste buds).
13. **The IKEA Effect: Why We Love Furniture More Just Because We Built It Ourselves**
   - *Question:* Why did instant cake mixes fail in the 1950s until food manufacturers required housewives to crack their own fresh egg into the batter?
   - *Key Focus:* Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon, and Dan Ariely's 2011 study on the IKEA Effect, cognitive effort justification (labor leading to love), General Mills Betty Crocker egg breakthrough, Build-A-Bear workshops, and why assembling flat-pack furniture creates irrational emotional attachment.
14. **Supermarket Architecture: The Gruen Effect, Eye-Level Shelves, and The Milk in the Back**
   - *Question:* Why is the grocery store produce section immediately at the entrance, while staple milk and eggs are hidden in the furthest back corner of the store?
   - *Key Focus:* The Gruen Effect (deliberate disorienting architecture inducing impulse buying), sensory priming with fresh colorful produce and flowers, slotting fees paid by food conglomerates to secure eye-level 'buy-level' shelves, wider shopping carts increasing purchases by 40%, and slow-tempo background music slowing walking pace.
15. **Luxury Pricing and Veblen Goods: When Making Something Expensive Increases Demand**
   - *Question:* Why does raising the price of a Rolex watch or a bottle of Dom Pérignon champagne by 30% cause sales volume to surge rather than decline?
   - *Key Focus:* Thorstein Veblen's 1899 'Theory of the Leisure Class', Conspicuous Consumption, upward-sloping demand curves violating standard classical economics, luxury goods functioning as positional status signals, manufactured scarcity, and the Snob Effect.
### Tier 4: Dark Patterns, Ethics & Future Persuasion
16. **Dark Patterns in UX: Roach Motels, Sneak into Basket, and Confirmshaming**
   - *Question:* Why is it one click to subscribe to a gym or newspaper online, but requires a 45-minute phone call during business hours to cancel?
   - *Key Focus:* Harry Brignull's 'Dark Patterns' taxonomy: Roach Motel (easy to get in, impossible to get out), Confirmshaming ('No thanks, I hate saving money' opt-out buttons), Sneak into Basket (adding travel insurance automatically), disguised ads, and regulatory crackdowns by the FTC and EU Digital Services Act.
17. **TikTok's Recommendation Algorithm: The Ultimate Hyper-Personalized Dopamine Machine**
   - *Question:* How does TikTok's For You Page decipher your deepest subconscious interests within 40 minutes of download based on split-second watch time and replays?
   - *Key Focus:* Moving from the social graph (who you follow) to the content interest graph, micro-signals (fractional second pauses, re-watches, comment scrolling), natural language processing and computer vision tagging video frames, dynamic exploration vs. exploitation algorithms, and consumer impulse buying on TikTok Shop.
18. **Surveillance Capitalism: Shoshana Zuboff on The Weaponization of Behavioral Surplus**
   - *Question:* If you are not paying for the product, you are not the customer—you are the raw material. How tech giants mine human experience into behavioral futures.
   - *Key Focus:* Harvard Professor Shoshana Zuboff's landmark 2019 book, 'Behavioral Surplus' (collecting digital exhaust far beyond what is needed to improve service), converting human behavior into prediction products sold in behavioral futures markets, micro-targeted psychological nudging, and the erosion of consumer autonomy.
19. **The Gamification of Shopping: Temu, Pinduoduo, and The Slot Machine E-Commerce War**
   - *Question:* Why does shopping on Temu feel like playing a video game at a Vegas casino, with spin-the-wheels, countdown alarms, and free gifts for recruiting friends?
   - *Key Focus:* Colin Huang's Pinduoduo social commerce model (team buying for massive factory discounts), integrating casino gamification loops directly into product browsing, manufacturing extreme variable reward schedules, dopamine-driven impulse consumerism, and air freight logistics.
20. **The Ethical Marketer: Nudging for Good vs. Cognitive Exploitation**
   - *Question:* Where is the moral boundary between persuasive behavioral design that helps people save money and healthy living, versus predatory psychological extraction?
   - *Key Focus:* The Nudge ethics test (is the choice transparent, easy to opt out of, and aligned with the consumer's own stated long-term goals?), Patagonia's 'Don't Buy This Jacket' anti-consumerism campaigns, designing for friction to prevent regret, and empowering conscious, resilient consumers in an age of algorithmic persuasion.
