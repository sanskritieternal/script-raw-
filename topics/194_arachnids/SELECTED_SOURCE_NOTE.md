# Selected source note - niche 194

Checked 2026-10-24. These records support bounded proposed arachnid questions. Species behavior, human health and pet care must remain separate. All linked BANK topics have proposed status and empty verified_sources.

## BANK-194-037 - Goliath birdeater record

Guinness World Records' [largest spider category](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/largest-spider) identifies Theraphosa blondi by mass. A record category and published maximum do not describe a typical individual, captive husbandry or the ethics of collecting wild animals. Confirm whether a cited figure is mass, body length or leg span before comparing species. Do not provide pet-handling advice.

## BANK-194-038 - Peacock-spider courtship

The original Maratus volans study [*Female preference for multi-modal courtship*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26631566/) used mating trials, video and laser vibrometry to analyze visual and vibrational signals. It supports a species- and sample-bounded question about observed courtship and mating success, not a universal story about all peacock spiders or a claim based on TikTok views. Review full methods and ethics before attributing female choice or a causal role to one display feature.

## BANK-194-039 - Diving bell

Seymour and Hetz, [*The diving bell and the spider: the physical gill of Argyroneta aquatica*](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/b88ff19c-7e87-472e-97b3-d872458651ba), Journal of Experimental Biology (2011), measured gas behavior in the diving bell. The bell can take up dissolved oxygen but the spider also brings air from the surface; temperature and demand matter. This does not establish unlimited underwater respiration or that every life stage never surfaces.

## BANK-194-040 - Tick-associated alpha-gal

CDC investigators' [2017-2022 US geographic analysis of suspected alpha-gal syndrome](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7230a2.htm), MMWR (2023), uses laboratory evidence without complete clinical information for all cases. It suggests a geographic association with lone star ticks; it cannot assign every positive result or symptom to a documented bite from that species. The case count is suspected, not a count of clinically confirmed diagnoses. No personal diagnosis, prevention protocol or treatment follows from this note.

## BANK-194-041 - Ballooning

Morley and Robert, [*Electric Fields Elicit Ballooning in Spiders*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6065530/) (Current Biology, 2018), report controlled field manipulations and spider responses. The experiment supports sensitivity and behavior under tested conditions; transport distance, wind and natural field conditions remain separate questions. The archived phrase that electricity lets spiders fly hundreds of miles overstates what a single lab experiment measures.
