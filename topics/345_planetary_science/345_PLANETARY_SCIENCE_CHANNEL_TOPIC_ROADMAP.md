## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Planetary Science across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Planetary Science Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Planetary Science.
- **Tier 2: Planetary Science Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Planetary Science.
- **Tier 3: Planetary Science Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Planetary Science.
- **Tier 4: Planetary Science Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Planetary Science.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Planetary Science: A Planetary Question Emerges**
   - *Question:* Which observation first prompted a testable idea about a world?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated mission, telescope, and study records. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
2. **Planetary Science: A Famous Planetary Discovery Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What did an instrument directly measure and what was inferred?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace data, processing, and independent analysis. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
3. **Planetary Science: People Behind a Planetary Mission**
   - *Question:* Who built instruments, navigated craft, archived data, and interpreted results?
   - *Key Focus:* Credit teams and collaborators. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
4. **Planetary Science: How We Study Another World**
   - *Question:* How do flyby, orbit, lander, telescope, and samples differ?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one planetary question to compare methods. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
5. **Planetary Science: Reconstructing a Planet's Past**
   - *Question:* Which current traces support an ancient process?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare geology, chemistry, chronology, and alternatives. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Planetary Science: An Ambiguous Surface Feature**
   - *Question:* Which explanations fit the image or measurement?
   - *Key Focus:* Use multiple instruments and state remaining uncertainty. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
7. **Planetary Science: A New Planetary Instrument**
   - *Question:* What could it measure that older missions could not?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare resolution, sensitivity, calibration, and constraints. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
8. **Planetary Science: A Mission's Science Decision**
   - *Question:* Who selected a target or measurement and why?
   - *Key Focus:* Read planning records and competing priorities. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
9. **Planetary Science: A Planetary Mission Milestone**
   - *Question:* What was learned at arrival, landing, or sample return?
   - *Key Focus:* Build dated evidence without treating announcement as final result. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
10. **Planetary Science: Orbiter to Scientific Map**
   - *Question:* How are instrument data turned into a planetary claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace acquisition, calibration, mapping, and validation. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Planetary Science: A Planetary Hypothesis Reassessed**
   - *Question:* Which new observation changed confidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated papers and competing mechanisms. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
12. **Planetary Science: One World Across Missions**
   - *Question:* How did later instruments alter a specific conclusion?
   - *Key Focus:* Match location and measurement type. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
13. **Planetary Science: A Proposed Planetary Mission**
   - *Question:* Which observations justify its objectives?
   - *Key Focus:* State target, instrument limits, cost, and alternatives. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
14. **Planetary Science: Habitable Means Life Found?**
   - *Question:* Which conditions for habitability were measured and which were inferred?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate environment from biosignature and life claim. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
15. **Planetary Science: One Measurement Beyond Earth**
   - *Question:* How did a team plan, collect, and interpret the data?
   - *Key Focus:* Use mission archive and documented team work. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Planetary Science: A Planetary Signal Explained**
   - *Question:* Which physical or chemical process best fits observations?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare models with instrument uncertainty. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
17. **Planetary Science: Measuring a Planetary Surface**
   - *Question:* Which sensor, geometry, and calibration produced a value?
   - *Key Focus:* State resolution and units. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
18. **Planetary Science: Surface Today, Past Climate**
   - *Question:* How can a local measurement inform long planetary history?
   - *Key Focus:* Bound spatial coverage and dating uncertainty. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
19. **Planetary Science: Earth Analog and Other World**
   - *Question:* Which lab or field analog matches a planetary condition?
   - *Key Focus:* List differences and limits. Evidence base: NASA and other mission archives, instrument calibration, mapped observations, sample studies, and peer-reviewed competing models.
20. **Planetary Science: A Planetary Landscape's Diversity**
   - *Question:* Does one site represent a whole world?
