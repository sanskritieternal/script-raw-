### Designing original unease

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D01` · foundation.

<a id="bank-011-013"></a>
#### BANK-011-013 — How can a night-shift archive become frightening before anything supernatural appears?

**Individual coverage scope:** Build ordinary routines, document inconsistencies and a protagonist's responsibility; establish what a changed record would cost.

<a id="bank-011-014"></a>
#### BANK-011-014 — How can a village of disagreeing clocks sustain a mystery beyond its opening image?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give different time discrepancies observable rules and consequences; avoid resolving every contradiction through an unexplained twist.

<a id="bank-011-015"></a>
#### BANK-011-015 — What makes a call from a missing ferry passenger emotionally disturbing?

**Individual coverage scope:** Establish relationships, verified details and conflicting choices rather than using the voice only as a jump scare.

<a id="bank-011-016"></a>
#### BANK-011-016 — How can an impossible house remain spatially understandable to the audience?

**Individual coverage scope:** Define navigable rooms, repeated landmarks and the precise rule broken when its interior expands.

<a id="bank-011-049"></a>
#### BANK-011-049 — How can an almost-human character feel uncanny without relying on a presumed universal valley?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare movement, voice, gaze and context in an original design; test audience interpretation rather than declaring a fixed brain response.

### Character and vulnerability

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D02` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-017"></a>
#### BANK-011-017 — How can a cautious protagonist make believable choices inside a horror story?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give information limits, competing obligations and reasonable options; avoid requiring stupidity to advance the plot.

<a id="bank-011-018"></a>
#### BANK-011-018 — What changes when two characters interpret the same disturbing event differently?

**Individual coverage scope:** Build distinct evidence and stakes for each interpretation rather than assigning one character the role of obvious fool.

<a id="bank-011-019"></a>
#### BANK-011-019 — How can grief shape a horror character without becoming a supernatural diagnosis?

**Individual coverage scope:** Treat loss as individual experience and separate emotional interpretation from the invented rules of the threat.

<a id="bank-011-020"></a>
#### BANK-011-020 — How can a character's expertise both help and mislead them?

**Individual coverage scope:** Let a real skill solve one problem while its assumptions fail under the fictional anomaly.

<a id="bank-011-052"></a>
#### BANK-011-052 — How can a sleep-paralysis-inspired scene represent fear without making a diagnosis or supernatural claim?

**Individual coverage scope:** Specify the character?s perspective, sensory uncertainty and aftermath; avoid treating a reported experience as proof of a creature or as a clinical assessment.

<a id="catalog-011-d03"></a>
### Supernatural rules

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D03` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-021"></a>
#### BANK-011-021 — What makes a supernatural rule understandable without an exposition lecture?

**Individual coverage scope:** Reveal it through repeated cause and consequence, then test a boundary that matters to a character.

<a id="bank-011-022"></a>
#### BANK-011-022 — How can a story change the audience's interpretation of a rule without cheating?

**Individual coverage scope:** Plant observable evidence that supports both readings and preserve the established consequences.

<a id="bank-011-023"></a>
#### BANK-011-023 — When does explaining a monster reduce fear rather than deepen the story?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare the information needed for meaningful choices with unnecessary biological or historical completeness.

<a id="bank-011-024"></a>
#### BANK-011-024 — How can a threat remain consistent when it is rarely visible?

**Individual coverage scope:** Track effects, capabilities, limitations and costs in a continuity record instead of adding powers at convenience.

<a id="bank-011-053"></a>
#### BANK-011-053 — What rules let cosmic horror make the unknown consequential without explaining it away?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give the protagonist a bounded goal and observable costs while leaving the phenomenon partly unclassified; distinguish uncertainty from arbitrary plot behavior.

## Category: Clues, atmosphere and story structure

<a id="catalog-011-d04"></a>
### Clues and misdirection

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D04` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-025"></a>
#### BANK-011-025 — How can a sound become a fair clue rather than merely atmosphere?

**Individual coverage scope:** Repeat an identifiable pattern, vary its context and connect the eventual explanation to something viewers could notice.

<a id="bank-011-026"></a>
#### BANK-011-026 — What distinguishes a red herring from information the story later ignores?

**Individual coverage scope:** Give misleading evidence a plausible cause and a payoff that remains relevant after the mystery is solved.

<a id="bank-011-027"></a>
#### BANK-011-027 — How can an unreliable memory be dramatized without invalidating every scene?

**Individual coverage scope:** Establish specific limits and stable external evidence so the audience can still reason about events.

<a id="bank-011-028"></a>
#### BANK-011-028 — How should a horror mystery reveal that a trusted account was incomplete?

**Individual coverage scope:** Expose the missing context through action or records rather than replacing the entire narrative with a late speech.

<a id="bank-011-048"></a>
#### BANK-011-048 — How can a horror story use audience knowledge differently from character knowledge to build suspense?

**Individual coverage scope:** Storyboard one bounded sequence with a disclosed danger, character ignorance and timing of the reveal; contrast suspense with surprise without presenting a filmmaker?s account as a universal audience law.

**Research follow-up:** [Bounded case and source-access note](RESEARCH_NOTES/BANK-011-048.md). This does not change proposal or production status.

### Visual and sonic restraint

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D05` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-029"></a>
#### BANK-011-029 — How can a static camera make a familiar space feel unsafe?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use blocking, duration, offscreen space and a changed background detail while preserving spatial continuity.

<a id="bank-011-030"></a>
#### BANK-011-030 — When is silence more effective than a sudden sound?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare anticipation, sound perspective and the information withheld rather than treating loudness as the only intensity control.

<a id="bank-011-031"></a>
#### BANK-011-031 — How can lighting conceal a threat without making the scene unreadable?

**Individual coverage scope:** Preserve action, geography and meaningful visual anchors; test the image at ordinary screen brightness.

<a id="bank-011-032"></a>
#### BANK-011-032 — What makes an offscreen event consequential enough to frighten viewers?

**Individual coverage scope:** Show credible effects on characters, objects and choices instead of relying on an unexplained noise alone.

<a id="bank-011-056"></a>
#### BANK-011-056 — What makes a found-footage horror camera plausible as a continuing witness?

**Individual coverage scope:** Define why the camera records, who controls it, what its microphone can capture and why footage survives; mark staged material as fiction outside the story frame.

<a id="catalog-011-d07"></a>
### Structure and endings

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D07` · advanced.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-037"></a>
#### BANK-011-037 — How can a horror short resolve its character conflict while leaving the phenomenon unknown?

**Individual coverage scope:** Close a consequential choice and its cost; leave only the supernatural explanation open.

<a id="bank-011-038"></a>
#### BANK-011-038 — When does an ambiguous ending feel earned rather than unfinished?

**Individual coverage scope:** Provide evidence for bounded alternatives and resolve the questions the story explicitly promised to answer.

<a id="bank-011-039"></a>
#### BANK-011-039 — How can a revelation make an earlier ordinary scene frightening in retrospect?

**Individual coverage scope:** Preserve the original scene's observable details and change their meaning through later evidence.

<a id="bank-011-040"></a>
#### BANK-011-040 — What makes a final return of the threat more than a sequel advertisement?

**Individual coverage scope:** Connect it to an established rule, unresolved consequence or meaningful character action.

<a id="bank-011-055"></a>
#### BANK-011-055 — How can a maze-like document narrative be translated into a legible horror video?

**Individual coverage scope:** Adapt page, annotation and spatial-disorientation techniques into a navigation and sound plan; retain a decipherable character arc rather than imitating a named book?s text.

### Folklore and setting

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D06` · intermediate.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-033"></a>
#### BANK-011-033 — How can an original ghost story acknowledge the folklore that inspired it?

**Individual coverage scope:** Name the source tradition, distinguish borrowed motifs from invented events and respect living community knowledge.

<a id="bank-011-034"></a>
#### BANK-011-034 — Why should a local sacred site not automatically become a sinister backdrop?

**Individual coverage scope:** Consider community meaning, consent and fictional alternatives rather than using unfamiliar religious practice as evidence of danger.

<a id="bank-011-035"></a>
#### BANK-011-035 — How can two regional variants enrich a story without being falsely merged?

**Individual coverage scope:** Choose the adapted version deliberately and disclose where the fictional narrative departs from its source.

<a id="bank-011-036"></a>
#### BANK-011-036 — What changes when a story is told by a resident rather than an outsider?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare access, assumptions, stakes and community agency instead of repeating an exotic-location template.

<a id="bank-011-050"></a>
#### BANK-011-050 — How can a Gothic house embody an inherited conflict rather than merely look decayed?

**Individual coverage scope:** Connect architecture, isolation, family choices and a revealed obligation; avoid using decay or disability as an automatic sign of evil.

### Audience and editorial checks

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-D08` · advanced.

Prerequisites: [CATALOG-011-D01](#catalog-011-d01).

<a id="bank-011-041"></a>
#### BANK-011-041 — How can a horror thumbnail signal fiction without pretending to show real evidence?

**Individual coverage scope:** Avoid fabricated surveillance claims, accusations about real people and documentary labels for generated scenes.

<a id="bank-011-042"></a>
#### BANK-011-042 — How can a content warning be specific without spoiling the entire plot?

**Individual coverage scope:** Identify relevant themes and intensity while separating accessibility information from promotional suspense.

<a id="bank-011-043"></a>
#### BANK-011-043 — What should be checked before using a child's perspective in horror?

**Individual coverage scope:** Review age portrayal, consent in production, audience framing and exploitative treatment of vulnerability.

<a id="bank-011-044"></a>
#### BANK-011-044 — How can an editor test whether fear depends on an accidental continuity error?

**Individual coverage scope:** Compare story rules, geography, props and chronology; repair confusion that the narrative does not intentionally establish.

<a id="bank-011-057"></a>
#### BANK-011-057 — How can a fictional story draw on a documented mystery without presenting invention as recovered evidence?

**Individual coverage scope:** Make a source-versus-invention ledger for one historical event, identify the nonfiction niche?s case scope, protect affected people and label the final fiction clearly.

### Documents, formats and recurring worlds

Subcategory `CATALOG-011-R01` · intermediate.

<a id="bank-011-045"></a>
#### BANK-011-045 — How can an original horror story make an ordinary maintenance log become frightening?

**Individual coverage scope:** Design changing entries, gaps, author constraints and competing interpretations; keep every document fictional and avoid imitating a real victim's record.

<a id="bank-011-046"></a>
#### BANK-011-046 — What rules let an anthology revisit the same haunted place without repeating its first twist?

**Individual coverage scope:** Track invariant setting rules, new viewpoints, consequences and revealed limits across a short season outline.

<a id="bank-011-047"></a>
#### BANK-011-047 — How can a mystery told entirely through sound remain understandable without explanatory narration?

**Individual coverage scope:** Plan spatial anchors, recurring acoustic evidence and fair clues; include a transcript pathway and avoid unsupported claims about subliminal effects.

<a id="bank-011-051"></a>
#### BANK-011-051 — How can an original creepypasta use online traces without masquerading as a real missing-person report?

**Individual coverage scope:** Design a forum archive, contradictory posts and creator disclosure; distinguish fictional participation from misinformation about real events.

<a id="bank-011-054"></a>
#### BANK-011-054 — How can analog-horror media artifacts reveal a story through their own constraints?

**Individual coverage scope:** Use one fictional broadcast or tape system with consistent signal, typography, pacing and provenance; disclose fabrication and ensure clues survive accessible viewing.
