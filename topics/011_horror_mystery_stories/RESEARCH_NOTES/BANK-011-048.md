# BANK-011-048 — Hitchcock's information and shot construction

Status: bounded primary-text review for a storytelling proposal, not a film-by-film analysis or a verified script. Inspected 2026-10-24.

## Primary text inspected

- [Alfred Hitchcock, “My own methods,” *Sight and Sound* archive](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/alfred-hitchcock-my-own-methods). BFI presents this as Hitchcock's article for its Summer 1937 issue; its web page also carries later publication and update metadata. Hitchcock describes developing a story pattern before shooting, using the audience's knowledge of the detective and the girl in *Blackmail* to sustain interest, and cutting among the knife, hands, eyes and faces in *Sabotage* to focus attention and build tension.

## Editorial boundary

The archived pitch says Hitchcock “manipulated information.” This source supports a specific creator account of audience knowledge, staging and shot selection; it does not demonstrate a universal psychological mechanism or how viewers of every film reacted. The published article also describes an ending Hitchcock wanted for *Blackmail* but says was changed. A future episode should inspect the actual named film versions, distinguish his retrospective account from finished-film evidence, and compare suspense and surprise without treating one technique as a law. No film viewing or audience research was performed for this note.
