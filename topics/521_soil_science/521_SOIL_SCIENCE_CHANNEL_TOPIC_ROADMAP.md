## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Soil Science across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Soil Science Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Soil Science.
- **Tier 2: Soil Science Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Soil Science.
- **Tier 3: Soil Science Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Soil Science.
- **Tier 4: Soil Science Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Soil Science.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Soil Science: How Soil Science Developed**
   - *Question:* Which observations led scientists to revise a soil concept?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one soil-science concept through field observations, sampling and later revisions; distinguish historical classification from present interpretation. Evidence base: Trace dated origin records and subsequent interpretations. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
2. **Soil Science: A Documented Soil Science Case**
   - *Question:* What can one influential soil study establish beyond its sampled site?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine one influential soil study using site, depth, methods and environmental context; state the limits of transferring its findings. Evidence base: Read the original evaluation and its population, comparator, outcomes and limitations. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
3. **Soil Science: People behind Soil Science**
   - *Question:* Whose field or land-management knowledge shaped a soil investigation?
   - *Key Focus:* Document overlooked field, laboratory or local land-management knowledge in one soil investigation; attribute contributions and obtain consent where needed. Evidence base: Use attributable contribution records and consented participant accounts. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
4. **Soil Science: Understanding Soil Science**
   - *Question:* How do formation, layers and function connect in a soil profile?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain soil formation, layers and functions through a defined profile; distinguish measured properties from broad labels such as healthy soil. Evidence base: Use primary definitions and a documented end-to-end example. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..
5. **Soil Science: Tradeoffs in Soil Science**
   - *Question:* Which sampling and analytical choices change a soil-property estimate?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare soil-analysis or modeling methods for a defined property; examine spatial variability, sampling depth, calibration and uncertainty. Evidence base: Compare original methods, assumptions, measures and uncertainty. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

6. **Soil Science: An Open Soil Science Question**

Evidence base: Compare competing explanations and specify missing discriminating evidence. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Evidence base: Compare validation, deployment and follow-up against a stated baseline. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Evidence base: Align definitions, samples, versions and measurement conditions. Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements. Starting framework: FAO Global Soil Partnership (https://www.fao.org/land-water/soils/global-soil-partnership/en). Select and verify the dated underlying case sources before scripting..

Case records: soil surveys, site histories, depth and season metadata, laboratory methods and repeated field measurements.
