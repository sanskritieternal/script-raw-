# BANK-030-017 — Racetrack Playa moving-stone case

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/7` pitch says ice and wind move “700-pound boulders.” The existing bank already has a question about how moving stones on a dry lakebed were investigated.

## Inspected primary sources

- Norris et al., [“Sliding Rocks on Racetrack Playa, Death Valley National Park: First Observation of Rocks in Motion,” *PLOS ONE* 9 (2014), e105948](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0105948), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0105948. GPS-instrumented rocks, photography, a weather station and time-lapse cameras documented movement. The paper reports an event involving more than 60 rocks on 20 December 2013, thin 3–6 mm ice sheets, light winds around 4–5 m/s, and slow movement around 2–5 m/min. These are observations under specific flooded and freezing conditions, not a rule for every track or rock.
- The [National Park Service 2014 account](https://www.nps.gov/deva/learn/news/racetrack.htm) summarizes the same field observations and the required shallow pond, nighttime ice and daytime break-up sequence.

## Claim disposition

The directly observed mechanism is thin floating ice panels pushed by wind, with water flow also affecting trajectories. The abstract does **not** establish that a 700-pound boulder was among the instrumented moving rocks. A script should inspect the paper's rock inventory and mass measurements before quoting a maximum weight; do not transfer a photographed stone's apparent size into an observed-motion mass. The case also illustrates why a once-unsolved mechanism should no longer be titled as wholly unexplained.
