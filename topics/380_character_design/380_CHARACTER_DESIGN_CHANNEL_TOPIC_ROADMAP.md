## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Character Design across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Character Design Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Character Design.
- **Tier 2: Character Design Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Character Design.
- **Tier 3: Character Design Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Character Design.
- **Tier 4: Character Design Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Character Design.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Character Design: A Character Design Develops**
   - *Question:* Which dated drawing or performance tradition shaped a particular design?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare source sketches and production context. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
2. **Character Design: An Iconic Silhouette Rechecked**
   - *Question:* Which visual choices identify a character at small scale?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare model sheets, poses, and actual screen use. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
3. **Character Design: Designers behind a Character**
   - *Question:* Whose sketches, costume, rig, or paint choices shaped the final figure?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated asset records and credits. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
4. **Character Design: Design for Readability**
   - *Question:* How do shape, color, and pose communicate a character action?
   - *Key Focus:* Test within a specified shot and display size. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
5. **Character Design: Design and Social Meaning**
   - *Question:* Which design choices invite assumptions about role or identity?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare work, creator context, and situated readings. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Character Design: A Disputed Design Credit**
   - *Question:* What do surviving sketches and production records show about authorship?
   - *Key Focus:* Date versions and mark gaps in provenance. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
7. **Character Design: A Rig Changes the Design**
   - *Question:* How did production technology constrain a character's movement or surface?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare concept art, model, rig, and final shots. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
8. **Character Design: A Designer's Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a credited artist change a character's silhouette?
   - *Key Focus:* Use annotated sketches and attributed testimony. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
9. **Character Design: A Redesign for a New Production**
   - *Question:* What changed when a character moved to a different medium or audience?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare approved assets and production briefs. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
10. **Character Design: From Sketch to Screen Character**
   - *Question:* How do exploration, model sheet, rig, and animation connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace one approved version through the pipeline. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Character Design: An Abandoned Character Look**
   - *Question:* Which practical or narrative constraint ended a design?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated tests and notes without equating rejection with failure. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
12. **Character Design: One Character across Versions**
   - *Question:* What traits remain stable and which are reinterpreted?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare identified editions, dates, and audiences. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
13. **Character Design: A New Design Workflow**
   - *Question:* Which released production demonstrates a changed character-design method?
   - *Key Focus:* Document artist control, revision, and credit. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
14. **Character Design: One Artist Designed It All?**
   - *Question:* Which documented collaborators contributed to the finished character?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate concept, model, costume, voice, and performance. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
15. **Character Design: The Character's Visual Evolution**
   - *Question:* How did a design move from first sketch to final shot?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated artifacts and approval decisions. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Character Design: What the Character Must Do**
   - *Question:* Which story and animation needs shaped the design brief?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare requirements with final model and scenes. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
17. **Character Design: The Character Model Sheet**
   - *Question:* What information does a turn-around, expression sheet, or rig provide?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify version, scale, and intended department. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
18. **Character Design: A Design Revision**
   - *Question:* Which change improved readability or movement in a test?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts with actual shot conditions. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
19. **Character Design: Shape and Negative Space**
   - *Question:* How does a character remain legible against a particular background?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare paired layout and final render. Evidence base: dated briefs and sketches, approved model sheets, rigs or costume records, test shots, credits, production notes, and bounded audience feedback.
20. **Character Design: Design across Departments**
   - *Question:* How did story, visual development, modeling, animation, and costume contribute?
