# Library of Congress biography-source cases

Research checked: 2026-10-24. Status: bounded archive-premise research, not script or idea approval.

## BANK-147-045: Frederick Douglass's authored revisions

The [Library of Congress guide to Douglass's three autobiographies](https://www.loc.gov/collections/frederick-douglass-papers/articles-and-essays/in-his-own-words/) links their texts and distinguishes first-publication dates from the digitized editions. The [Douglass Papers collection description](https://www.loc.gov/collections/frederick-douglass-papers/about-this-collection/) records a partial handwritten draft of the third autobiography and warns that earlier writings were lost in an 1872 house fire.

**Limit:** These collection records verify that three authored life accounts and relevant papers exist; they do not, by themselves, prove a particular changed sentence, self-mythologizing motive, or contradiction. Select passages, identify each edition and compare page images before scripting any revision claim. The surviving archive is incomplete.

## BANK-147-046: Rosa Parks beyond one episode

The [Library of Congress Rosa Parks Papers description](https://www.loc.gov/collections/rosa-parks-papers/about-this-collection/) inventories family letters, organizational material, employment and financial records alongside public activism. Its [finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/5833) gives series and access details and reserves copyright in unpublished writings to the Rosa and Raymond Parks Institute for Self Development.

**Limit:** The inventory supports a research path beyond a single bus-ride anecdote; it does not prove the content of an uninspected letter or license reproduction. Select and read particular items, date them, seek context and check image and manuscript permissions before quoting or displaying them.

**Semantic home:** Niche 147 owns how a biographer compares life narratives and archive types. Niche 146 owns a person-centered profile; civil-rights history banks own the movement's chronology. These are method cases, not duplicate full biographies or proof that archives alone resolve every disagreement.
