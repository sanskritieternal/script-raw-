# BANK-038-049 — Four Corners monument claim

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/7` calls the Four Corners monument “actually in the wrong place.”

## Inspected primary authority

- The U.S. National Geodetic Survey's [“Why the Four Corners Monument is in Exactly the Right Place”](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/INFO/fourcorners.shtml), 15 May 2009, explicitly corrects the reported 2.5-mile error. NGS says it did not claim the monument was incorrectly located, and that the monument marks the accepted intersection of Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico and Utah. The release distinguishes the expected theoretical longitude from the legally established surveyed monument.
- The U.S. Bureau of Land Management's [2009 Manual of Surveying Instructions](https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/Manual_Of_Surveying_Instructions_2009.pdf) provides general surveying context for monuments and boundary retracement. The precise Four Corners claim is supported directly by the NGS case explanation, not inferred from the manual alone.

## Claim disposition

The archived “wrong place” headline is false under the authoritative boundary. A useful episode asks why a coordinate calculation and an accepted survey monument can differ while the monument remains the legal four-state intersection. Before scripting exact offset distances, inspect the original survey field notes, statutes and retracement details cited by NGS. Do not invite viewers to move or tamper with the monument.
