## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Hindu Philosophy across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Hindu Philosophy Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Hindu Philosophy.
- **Tier 2: Hindu Philosophy Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Hindu Philosophy.
- **Tier 3: Hindu Philosophy Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Hindu Philosophy.
- **Tier 4: Hindu Philosophy Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Hindu Philosophy.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Hindu Philosophy: A Philosophical School Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which dated texts and debates show one school emerging?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare source chronology, later labels, and rival accounts. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
2. **Hindu Philosophy: A Famous Argument in Context**
   - *Question:* What does one often-quoted Hindu philosophical argument actually claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Read passage, opponent, commentary, and translation together. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
3. **Hindu Philosophy: Teachers Outside the Standard Lineage**
   - *Question:* Whose contribution to one philosophical debate is missing from standard summaries?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated texts, citations, and the limits of surviving records. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
4. **Hindu Philosophy: One Question, Multiple Schools**
   - *Question:* How do two named schools answer a clearly defined philosophical question?
   - *Key Focus:* Present each position in its own terms and avoid a single Hindu answer. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
5. **Hindu Philosophy: Knowledge and Its Sources**
   - *Question:* How does one school justify a specific means of knowing?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace its argument and a named interlocutor's objection. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Hindu Philosophy: An Uncertain Text Attribution**
   - *Question:* What evidence supports or weakens one disputed philosophical attribution?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare manuscripts, citations, language, and later tradition. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
7. **Hindu Philosophy: Digitizing a Philosophical Tradition**
   - *Question:* Can manuscript collation clarify one disputed reading?
   - *Key Focus:* Check witness provenance, transcription choices, and interpretive limits. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
8. **Hindu Philosophy: A Philosopher in Their Debate**
   - *Question:* What can primary texts establish about one thinker's position and influence?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate textual argument from later hagiography. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
9. **Hindu Philosophy: A Debate's Documented Turning Point**
   - *Question:* Which text or institutional event changed one philosophical discussion?
   - *Key Focus:* Date the record and trace later responses without assuming universal adoption. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
10. **Hindu Philosophy: Reconstructing an Argument**
   - *Question:* How do premise, objection, and reply work in one passage?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify technical terms and compare translations. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Hindu Philosophy: A School's Institutional Fortunes**
   - *Question:* How did patronage or teaching institutions affect one school's visibility?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated commentary, records, and source survival. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
12. **Hindu Philosophy: A Classical Idea in Present Discussion**
   - *Question:* How do current interpreters use a named older argument?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish original context, later commentary, and contemporary application. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
13. **Hindu Philosophy: An Open Editorial Question**
   - *Question:* Which source discovery or translation choice could change one interpretation?
   - *Key Focus:* State what evidence is missing without forecasting doctrinal change. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
14. **Hindu Philosophy: One Philosophy for All Hindus?**
   - *Question:* Does a broad claim about Hindu philosophy erase disagreements among schools?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare named texts, lineages, and contemporary voices. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
15. **Hindu Philosophy: One Argument Across Manuscripts**
   - *Question:* How did a philosophical passage travel through editions and commentaries?
   - *Key Focus:* Track witnesses, translations, and contested readings. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Hindu Philosophy: A Passage in Its Work**
   - *Question:* What does one passage mean in the structure of its original text?
   - *Key Focus:* Read preceding argument, later response, and edition notes. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
17. **Hindu Philosophy: Commentators Disagree**
   - *Question:* Where do two named lineages diverge over one proposition?
   - *Key Focus:* Quote short cited passages and attribute each reading. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
18. **Hindu Philosophy: Philosophy in a Practitioner's Life**
   - *Question:* How does one community relate a philosophical idea to practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented accounts and avoid making practice a test of textual correctness. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
19. **Hindu Philosophy: When an Argument Meets Ritual**
   - *Question:* How does a named philosophical tradition interpret one observance?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe the tradition's own account without prescriptive instruction. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
20. **Hindu Philosophy: A Term That Changes the Argument**
   - *Question:* How do alternative translations of one Sanskrit term alter a claim?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare grammatical context and commentarial uses. Evidence base: dated Sanskrit texts and manuscripts, critical editions and translations, named commentaries, institutional records, and consented practitioner accounts.
