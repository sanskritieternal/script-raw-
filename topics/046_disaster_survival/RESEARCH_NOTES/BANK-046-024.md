# BANK-046-024 — 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami warning context

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/8` refers to the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami as a magnitude-9.1 megathrust event with “unwarned coastlines.”

## Inspected institutional records

- The [USGS event record](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/official20041226005853450_30/executive) lists the 26 December 2004 Sumatra–Andaman earthquake as **Mw 9.1**, at 00:58:53 UTC, caused by thrust faulting at the India–Burma plate interface.
- [NOAA's event account](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/2004tsu_max) says there was **no official Indian Ocean tsunami warning system** at the time and discusses limits in public awareness and communication. It gives a regional total of killed or missing and presumed dead; that total must not be assigned to one coastline.
- [UNESCO-IOC's Indian Ocean warning-system history](https://tsunami.ioc.unesco.org/en/iotwms) says the intergovernmental coordinating group was established in **2005** in response to the 2004 disaster.

## Claim disposition

Mw 9.1 is a current USGS event estimate; older sources use other estimates. “Unwarned coastlines” is too uniform for an episode: arrival time, local ground shaking, natural cues, communication and evacuation possibilities varied widely. A selected script needs site-specific tide-gauge/field-survey data and contemporaneous warning and testimony records before describing any particular community's experience or avoidable loss. This historical analysis is not personal survival instruction.
