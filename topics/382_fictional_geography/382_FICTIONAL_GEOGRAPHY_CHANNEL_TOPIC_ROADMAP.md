## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Fictional Geography across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Fictional Geography Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Fictional Geography.
- **Tier 2: Fictional Geography Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Fictional Geography.
- **Tier 3: Fictional Geography Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Fictional Geography.
- **Tier 4: Fictional Geography Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Fictional Geography.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Fictional Geography: The First Map of a Fictional Place**
   - *Question:* Which documented sketch or text established a setting's geography?
   - *Key Focus:* Date the source and distinguish early from final versions. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
2. **Fictional Geography: A Literary Map Put to Work**
   - *Question:* How does a named map guide or complicate a story's journey?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare map, text, edition, and actual route. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
3. **Fictional Geography: Who Drew the World?**
   - *Question:* Which cartographer, illustrator, or designer shaped a familiar fictional place?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credits, archival drafts, and publisher records. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
4. **Fictional Geography: Reading a Fictional Map**
   - *Question:* What do scale, orientation, legend, and omissions allow a reader to infer?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one identified map and its accompanying text. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
5. **Fictional Geography: Geography as Narrative Constraint**
   - *Question:* How do distance, terrain, and borders shape character options?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace a bounded route and decision in the work. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Fictional Geography: An Unmapped Region**
   - *Question:* Does the text leave a place intentionally vague or contradict its map?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare versions and state unresolved gaps. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
7. **Fictional Geography: A Navigable Fictional Map**
   - *Question:* What changes when an audience can explore rather than view a fixed map?
   - *Key Focus:* Test a released interactive version and its limits. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
8. **Fictional Geography: A Mapmaker's Revision**
   - *Question:* What evidence explains a changed coastline, border, or place name?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated drafts and attributed notes. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
9. **Fictional Geography: A New Edition Redraws the World**
   - *Question:* Which publication or adaptation changed spatial information?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare authorized versions and reader access. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
10. **Fictional Geography: Plotting a Journey**
   - *Question:* Can the sequence of travel fit the geography the work establishes?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace locations, times, and stated transport. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Fictional Geography: A Location Removed from Canon**
   - *Question:* Which version dropped or relocated a fictional site?
   - *Key Focus:* Check publication and production chronology. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
12. **Fictional Geography: One World across Two Maps**
   - *Question:* Which places, labels, or boundaries changed between editions?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare identified maps at similar scale. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
13. **Fictional Geography: An Expanding Fictional Atlas**
   - *Question:* Which planned location has documented constraints from existing works?
   - *Key Focus:* Date plans and avoid treating proposals as released canon. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
14. **Fictional Geography: A Map Is the Whole World?**
   - *Question:* What does a map omit despite appearing complete?
   - *Key Focus:* Check text, map purpose, scale, and perspective. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
15. **Fictional Geography: Making a Fictional Map**
   - *Question:* How did a route or settlement move from sketch to release?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow drafts, editorial decisions, and credits. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Fictional Geography: What the Map Must Explain**
   - *Question:* Which story action required a particular spatial relationship?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief, sketch, and final scene. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
17. **Fictional Geography: Sketches, Grids, and Place Lists**
   - *Question:* What do working maps reveal about setting design?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify author, version, scale, and intended use. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
18. **Fictional Geography: Revising a Route**
   - *Question:* Which travel inconsistency or story need prompted a map change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare drafts and resulting narrative. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
19. **Fictional Geography: Map as Storytelling Image**
   - *Question:* How do labels and visual hierarchy direct attention?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze one map and its narrative moment. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
20. **Fictional Geography: Map and Text Made Together**
   - *Question:* How did writer, illustrator, editor, and game designer shape a geography?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credits and asset history. Evidence base: identified texts and maps, dated sketches, publisher and production records, archive catalogs, credited cartographers, and situated reader responses.
