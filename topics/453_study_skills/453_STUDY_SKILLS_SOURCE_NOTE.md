# Study-skills source follow-up - 2026-10-24

## BANK-453-044 - deadline experiment, replication and retraction

- Original study record: Ariely and Wertenbroch, *Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance* (2002), https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/1467-9280.00441/ (inspected 2026-10-24). Its originally published positive claim is historical context only: the journal retracted this paper in 2026 and its findings must not be presented as reliable evidence.
- Primary replication: Hyndman and Bisin, *Replication of Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance* (2026), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976261460772 (inspected 2026-10-24). Their close replication of the proofreading-task study reported no similar performance benefit across its tested deadline conditions. Their research data/materials are linked at https://researchbox.org/3063 (repository index inspected; data and code not rerun).
- Primary editorial action: *Psychological Science* retraction notice, first online September 2, 2026, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976261488042 (inspected 2026-10-24). The editor cites questions about original-data reliability and says both authors agree to retraction. A retraction establishes that the journal no longer stands behind the original findings; it does not prove that all deadline schedules are ineffective.
- Limits: The replication concerns a specific proofreading task and deadlines, not exams, term planning, every student population or clinical procrastination. No individual study or health advice follows from it. Do not infer misconduct beyond the notice's stated grounds, or cite the retracted effect as current evidence.
