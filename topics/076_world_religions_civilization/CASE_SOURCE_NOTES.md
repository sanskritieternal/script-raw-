# Selected religious-history source checks — 24 October 2026

These are bounded checks for three proposed cases. Textual witnesses, institutional accounts and modern research answer different questions; the remaining archived claims are unverified.

## BANK-076-079 — AI dating of Dead Sea Scrolls

- The original [2025 PLOS One study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0323185) trained its Enoch handwriting model on 24 radiocarbon-dated samples and estimated dates for 135 other scrolls. The reported mean absolute errors ranged from 27.9 to 30.7 years and the model's predictions had 79% post-hoc palaeographic agreement. These are manuscript-date estimates, not decoded theological secrets or five separate breakthroughs.
- The [Library of Congress Qumran collection account](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/libr.html) identifies the diverse Jewish manuscript corpus and its broad Second Temple period. A particular scroll's text, hand and dating method must be identified before drawing historical conclusions.
- **Editorial bound:** Give fragment IDs, uncertainty and the distinction between dating, writer identification and text interpretation. The archive's numbered breakthrough claim remains unsupported.

## BANK-076-082 — Gospel of Thomas witnesses

- [Coptic Scriptorium's edition of the Coptic text](https://data.copticscriptorium.org/texts/thomasgospel/gospel-of-thomas/) describes the Nag Hammadi Codex II witness and the editorial use of facsimile images. The edition is a primary-text gateway, with restorations and editorial judgments visible as such.
- The [National Museum of Asian Art's Papyrus Oxyrhynchus 1 object study](https://asia.si.edu/research/essays/the-sayings-of-jesus-logia-jesou/) identifies an earlier Greek fragment that parallels, but is not identical to, parts of the Coptic text. The [Oxford papyrus location list](https://oxyrhynchus.web.ox.ac.uk/location-list) records P.Oxy. I 1 at the Bodleian Library.
- **Editorial bound:** Manuscript date is not composition date. Fragment survival does not establish why a text was omitted from every later Christian canon; that question needs separately dated institutional evidence.

## BANK-076-084 — Vatican Apostolic Archives

- The archive's [institutional history](https://www.archivioapostolicovaticano.va/content/aav/en/l-archivio.html) says the title “Vatican Secret Archives” was replaced with “Vatican Apostolic Archives” in 2019 and describes the collection as records from multiple curial offices. The [2019 apostolic letter](https://www.archivioapostolicovaticano.va/content/aav/en/news/notices/Motu-Proprio-AAV.html) is the official naming act.
- The archive's [access and consultation rules](https://www.archivioapostolicovaticano.va/content/aav/en/consultazione/accesso-e-consultazione.html) currently describe free research access for qualified scholars and consultation through the end of Pius XII's papacy in October 1958.
- **Editorial bound:** Describe rules as of the inspection date and verify them again before publication. Access limits and the old name are not evidence that a particular secret document exists.
