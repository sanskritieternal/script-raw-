## BANK-237-040 — Liechtenstein–Switzerland customs arrangement

- [Liechtenstein National Administration's bilateral-relations note](https://www.llv.li/en/national-administration/diplomatic-representations/geneva-ch-/multilateral-and-bilateral-relations) identifies the customs treaty of 29 March 1923 and explains that Swiss customs law and some related trade arrangements apply, with specified Swiss representation. The [administration's customs guidance](https://www.llv.li/de/privatpersonen/mobilitaet-reisen-und-ausland/grenzuebertritt-und-warenverkehr) likewise describes the common customs territory and division of handling duties.
- These official explanations support a bounded account of delegated customs functions. They do not mean Switzerland governs every Liechtenstein policy or that the treaty alone caused later economic growth. Read the treaty text and subsequent amendments before quoting clauses or claiming present practice in detail.

## BANK-237-041 — San Marino banking contraction

- The [IMF's 2016 Article IV consultation](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/CR/Issues/2016/12/31/Republic-of-San-Marino-2016-Article-IV-Consultation-Press-Release-Staff-Report-and-Statement-43885) describes the offshore banking model's contraction and the loss of roughly a third of output since 2008. The [2013 Article IV release](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2015/09/28/04/53/pn1354) gives an earlier dated estimate and notes nonresident deposit outflows alongside weak external demand and other factors.
- Use one selected report's definitions, base year and data vintage before quoting a percentage. The reports support an historical economic episode, not a claim that small statehood itself caused the banking shock or an up-to-date recovery verdict. Obtain national statistical and bank records, and later IMF series, for any longitudinal script.
