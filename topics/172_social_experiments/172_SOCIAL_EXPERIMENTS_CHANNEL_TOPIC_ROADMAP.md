## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Social Experiments across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Experimental Design in Social Science, Independent Variables & Placebo Controls (Beginner / Scratch)** — Randomized control trial (RCT) design in social sciences, null hypothesis testing, placebo effects and Hawthorne observer effects, and ethical consent boundaries.
- **Tier 2: Conformity and Obedience (Asch line tests, Milgram electric shocks, Bystander intervention) (Intermediate)** — Stanley Milgram's obedience to authority variations, Solomon Asch's perceptual conformity line experiments, Darley and Latané's bystander intervention lab setups, and Albert Bandura's Bobo doll aggression modeling.
- **Tier 3: Unethical Historical Experiments, Institutional Review Boards (IRB) & Belmont Report Ethics (Upper Intermediate)** — The Monster Study on stuttering orphans, the Tuskegee syphilis study atrocities, the Belmont Report core principles (Respect, Beneficence, Justice), and modern IRB oversight mandates.
- **Tier 4: Large-Scale Field Experiments, Algorithmic Social A/B Testing & Digital Civic Sandboxes (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Facebook's 2012 emotional contagion study controversy, nationwide Universal Basic Income random trials, digital civic sandboxes, and evaluating long-term societal interventions.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Milgram Obedience Experiment: The Shocking Reality of Authority and Conscience**
   - *Question:* How did a Yale psychologist prove that 65% of ordinary citizens would administer lethal 450-volt electric shocks to a screaming stranger simply because a man in a lab coat told them to?
   - *Key Focus:* Stanley Milgram's 1961 experiment, the banality of evil (Hannah Arendt link to Eichmann trials), the 'agentic state' shedding personal moral responsibility, variations (proximity of victim, conflicting authorities), and ethical trauma.
2. **The Asch Conformity Experiments: Denying Your Own Eyes to Fit In with the Group**
   - *Question:* Why will 75% of intelligent people look at three lines on a card and publicly agree with an obviously wrong answer because actors in the room gave it first?
   - *Key Focus:* Solomon Asch's 1951 methodology, normative vs informational conformity, the power of a single lone dissenter dropping conformity from 37% to 5%, and the neurobiology of social rejection panic.
3. **The Hawthorne Effect: How Being Watched Radically Alters Human Productivity**
   - *Question:* Why did workers at a Chicago factory work faster when factory lights were made brighter, but also worked faster when the lights were dimmed to near darkness?
   - *Key Focus:* Western Electric's Hawthorne Works experiments (1924-1932), the observer effect in social science, novelty effects, worker feeling of special attention, and controlling for observer bias in modern experiments.
4. **The Bobo Doll Experiment: How Children Learn Violence by Watching Adults**
   - *Question:* What happens when nursery school children watch an adult punch, kick, and yell insults at an inflatable clown doll?
   - *Key Focus:* Albert Bandura's 1961 Social Learning Theory, observational modeling without direct reinforcement, novel aggressive behaviors invented by children, and the 60-year debate over media and video game violence.
5. **The Third Wave: The High School History Class That Accidentally Created a Fascist Movement in 5 Days**
   - *Question:* How did a California high school teacher in 1967 demonstrate how the German public fell under Nazi rule by turning his teenagers into a disciplined, fanatical political cult in under a week?
   - *Key Focus:* Ron Jones' mottoes: 'Strength through Discipline, Strength through Community, Strength through Action', student informant networks turning on classmates, sudden out-of-control momentum, and the auditorium reveal.
### Tier 2: Practical Mechanics & Channels
6. **The Robbers Cave Experiment: How Intergroup Conflict is Created and Healed**
   - *Question:* How did researchers turn 22 well-adjusted boy scouts at an Oklahoma summer camp into bitter, violent tribal enemies in just four days?
   - *Key Focus:* Muzafer Sherif's Realist Conflict Theory, the Eagles vs the Rattlers, zero-sum competition breeding hatred and cabin raids, failure of simple social contact to bring peace, and the power of superordinate goals (fixing the shared water supply) in uniting warring groups.
7. **The Belmont Report: How Unethical Horrors Built the Modern Human Subjects Protection Code**
   - *Question:* How did the 40-year Tuskegee Syphilis Study force the US government to establish the modern ethical rules governing all human medical and social research?
   - *Key Focus:* Withholding penicillin from 400 Black men for decades, whistleblower Peter Buxtun, the 1979 Belmont Report's three core ethical principles: Respect for Persons (informed consent), Beneficence (do no harm), and Justice (fair subject selection), and the creation of Institutional Review Boards (IRBs).
8. **The Monster Study: The Darkest Experiment in Speech Pathology History**
   - *Question:* Why did a renowned speech therapist deliberately induce permanent psychological damage and speech impediments in 22 healthy orphan children in 1939?
   - *Key Focus:* Dr. Wendell Johnson and Mary Tudor's University of Iowa experiment, relentless negative conditioning berating children that their speech was terrible, lifelong psychological withdrawal, decades of institutional coverup, and modern multi-million-dollar state settlements.
9. **The Smoke-Filled Room Experiment: When Pluralistic Ignorance Paralyzes Humans in Real Danger**
   - *Question:* When acrid white smoke begins pouring out of a wall vent, why will people sit calmly in a room for 20 minutes choking on fumes because two actors pretend nothing is wrong?
   - *Key Focus:* Bibb Latané and John Darley's 1968 experiment, 75% of lone subjects reporting smoke in two minutes vs only 10% reporting it when sitting with passive confederates, pluralistic ignorance dynamics, and overcoming social embarrassment during emergencies.
10. **The Rosenhan Experiment: Being Sane in Insane Places**
   - *Question:* What happened when eight completely sane pseudopatients checked into mental asylums claiming to hear the word 'thud', and then acted completely normal?
   - *Key Focus:* David Rosenhan's 1973 Science paper, average 19-day involuntary hospitalization, normal note-taking labeled as pathological 'writing behavior', the reverse experiment catching imaginary pseudopatients, and the overhaul of the DSM diagnostic manual.
### Tier 3: Advanced Methodologies & Frameworks
11. **The Good Samaritan Experiment: When Rushing Seminary Students Stepped Over a Groaning Man**
   - *Question:* Why did theology students on their way to deliver a sermon on the Good Samaritan literally step over a slumping, coughing victim in an alleyway?
   - *Key Focus:* John Darley and Daniel Batson's 1973 Princeton experiment, testing religious orientation vs situational time pressure, 63% stopping when having plenty of time vs only 10% stopping when in a hurry, and situational ethics.
12. **The Facebook Emotional Contagion Study: Manipulating the Feelings of 689,000 Unsuspecting Users**
   - *Question:* How did Facebook secretly alter the news feeds of nearly 700,000 people to prove that suppressing positive or negative words changed what users posted?
   - *Key Focus:* The 2014 PNAS paper by Kramer, Guillory, and Hancock; massive public and scientific backlash; lack of informed consent; emotional manipulation without debriefing; and the unregulated wild west of private corporate algorithmic experimentation.
13. **The AClass Divided (Brown Eyes vs Blue Eyes): Jane Elliott's 1968 Third-Grade Revolution**
   - *Question:* How did an Iowa schoolteacher teach eight-year-old children the visceral reality of racism the day after Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated?
   - *Key Focus:* Jane Elliott dividing her classroom by eye color, giving privileges and scientific praise to blue-eyed kids, watching children turn arrogant and cruel within hours, academic performance collapsing in the stigmatized group, and the enduring PBS Frontline documentary.
14. **The Marshmallow Test: Does 4-Year-Old Delay of Gratification Truly Predict Lifetime Success?**
   - *Question:* Does being able to resist eating one marshmallow for 15 minutes to earn two predict higher SAT scores and career wealth 30 years later?
   - *Key Focus:* Walter Mischel's 1970 Stanford experiment, the famous longitudinal follow-ups, modern 2018 replications revealing the hidden variable of socioeconomic background (wealthy kids trust adults to deliver the second marshmallow), and redefining willpower.
15. **The Invisible Gorilla: Inattentional Blindness and How We Miss What's Right in Front of Us**
   - *Question:* Why do 50% of people counting basketball passes completely fail to notice a person in a full gorilla suit walk into the center of the frame, beat its chest, and walk off?
   - *Key Focus:* Christopher Chabris and Daniel Simons' 1999 experiment, selective attention limits, cognitive load blindness, radiologists missing a matchbox-sized gorilla on lung CT scans, and implications for distracted driving and eyewitness testimony.
16. **The Universal Basic Income Trials: What Happens When You Hand Unconditional Cash to 10,000 People?**
   - *Question:* Do people quit their jobs and buy drugs when given $1,000 a month with no strings attached, or do health, education, and entrepreneurship soar?
   - *Key Focus:* GiveDirectly's 12-year Kenya village trial, Finland's 2,000-unemployed citizen trial, Stockton SEED project, testing work disincentive hypotheses, mental health improvements, and political policy feasibility.
### Tier 4: Enterprise Strategy & Deep Mastery
17. **The Replication Crisis: Why 50% of Psychology's Classic Experiments Can't Be Repeated**
   - *Question:* What happened when 270 researchers attempted to replicate 100 famous psychology papers and discovered that over half failed to produce the original results?
   - *Key Focus:* Brian Nosek's Open Science Collaboration, p-hacking, publication bias ('file drawer problem'), small sample sizes, academic incentives rewarding sensational headlines over replicable rigor, and the pre-registration revolution.
18. **Digital Civic Sandboxes: Large-Scale Social Experiments on Reddit and Twitter**
   - *Question:* How did a blank 1,000x1,000 pixel canvas where each user could place one colored pixel every five minutes spawn global alliances, wars, and art?
   - *Key Focus:* Reddit's r/place social experiment (2017 & 2022), emergent self-organizing communities, defense pacts, diplomatic treaties between subreddits, automated bots vs human labor, and digital sociopolitical simulation.
19. **The Cost of a Clinical Social Trial: The Economics of Field Experimentation**
   - *Question:* Why does a rigorous multi-year randomized social science field trial in 50 villages cost $5 million, and where does every research dollar go?
   - *Key Focus:* Enumerator field staff payroll, biometric tracking hardware, attrition mitigation incentives, survey design translation, data auditing pipelines, and peer review publishing economics.
20. **The Masterclass on Experimental Design: Crafting an Airtight Social Science Experiment**
   - *Question:* How does an experimental sociologist design a study that isolates causal mechanisms while ensuring absolute ethical compliance and high statistical power?
   - *Key Focus:* Balancing internal validity (lab control) vs external validity (real-world generalizability), pre-registration protocols (OSF), power calculations determining sample size, double-blind confederate protocols, and managing post-experiment debriefing ethics.
