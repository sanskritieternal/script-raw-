# Selected social-experiment evidence - 2026-10-24

These notes bound proposal premises. They are neither participant-consent review nor IDEA production approval.

## BANK-172-042 - OpenResearch cash trial

- [OpenResearch's study page](https://www.openresearchlab.org/projects/unconditional-cash-study) describes a 2020-23 U.S. study of adults aged 21-40 in selected Texas and Illinois counties with household income no higher than 300% of the federal poverty level. Its [public data release](https://www.openresearchlab.org/press-announcements/unconditional-cash-study-data-now-open-to-the-public) describes 3,000 participants, with 1,000 assigned $1,000 per month and 2,000 assigned $50 per month for three years.
- A [research-team working paper on employment](https://www.openresearchlab.org/findings/nber-working-paper-employment) reports a modest decrease in labor participation and weekly work hours, while the [consumption paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32784) reports increased measured household expenditures in several categories. These are different outcomes, not a single success/failure score.
- **Claim limit:** The comparison is larger versus smaller unconditional transfers in a selected population and time period; it is not a trial of universal national income, universal withdrawal from work, health cure, or entrepreneurial success. Before scripting numbers, pin the paper revision, outcome definition, uncertainty and public-use dataset version. The 2026 data release follows the original trial and papers.
- **Overlap:** BANK-172-025 owns general unconditional-income questions. BANK-172-042 is the named trial and its design/evidence boundary. Economics and policy consequences can retain their primary home in the appropriate economic-policy niche.

## BANK-172-021 - Facebook news-feed experiment

- The [original 2014 paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320040111) reports a 689,003-person feed manipulation and measures word use in participants' subsequent posts. It reports small effects in expressed language, not directly measured private emotion or proof that users developed clinical symptoms.
- The journal published a [correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4115523/) and an [editorial expression of concern](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24994898/) around the study. A script must inspect those alongside the paper, identify what consent/IRB review actually occurred, and not present the archived claim as an unqualified demonstration that Facebook controlled users' feelings.
- **Overlap:** The study sits in research ethics and design here, while general social-platform effects require their own evidence and population.

## Archived headline boundaries

- The TikTok blind-date stream and NPC livestream are entertainment formats, not identified controlled studies. The no-sleep stunt is not an acceptable experiment to reproduce. A phone-detox brain-scan effect, platform-policy crackdown and $10 million lawsuit need named original protocols, policies or court records before those numerical claims can be used.
