## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Origami across four progressive tiers:

- **Tier 3: Origami Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Origami.
- **Tier 4: Origami Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Origami.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Origami: A Fold in the Record**
   - *Question:* Which dated object or diagram shows a named paper-folding form?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify provenance and distinguish similar traditions. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
2. **Origami: A Model Unfolded**
   - *Question:* Which crease relationships make a named origami model possible?
   - *Key Focus:* Use authorized diagrams and physical example. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
3. **Origami: The Folder behind the Model**
   - *Question:* Whose design, diagramming, testing, or teaching is documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Check publication credits and interviews. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
4. **Origami: Reading a Fold Diagram**
   - *Question:* How do mountain, valley, and orientation marks map to paper?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare diagram and one sample without claiming one notation everywhere. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
5. **Origami: Geometry of a Crease Pattern**
   - *Question:* Which angles or symmetries constrain a foldable pattern?
   - *Key Focus:* State ideal model and physical-paper limits. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Origami: A Model's Disputed Origin**
   - *Question:* What is the earliest documented version and what remains uncertain?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated books, objects, and transmission accounts. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
7. **Origami: Computer-Aided Folding**
   - *Question:* Which design or simulation problem did a program help solve?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare calculated pattern with physical fold. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
8. **Origami: A Designer Revises a Model**
   - *Question:* What documented change improved stability or expression?
   - *Key Focus:* Use diagrams, prototypes, and attributed notes. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
9. **Origami: A Model Spreads through Publication**
   - *Question:* How did a book, class, or exhibition change access?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace editions and creator credit. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
10. **Origami: From Flat Sheet to Form**
   - *Question:* How does one crease sequence create three-dimensional structure?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow an authorized pattern and note thickness effects. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Origami: A Folding Style Reinterpreted**
   - *Question:* Which design goal caused a traditional base to be adapted?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated models and context. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
12. **Origami: One Form across Versions**
   - *Question:* What changes in paper, crease pattern, or display?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare identified designs, not only photographs. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
13. **Origami: A Folding Application**
   - *Question:* Which practical use has been prototyped and what remains untested?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate model demonstration from engineering qualification. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
14. **Origami: One Sheet, No Cuts?**
   - *Question:* Do all paper-folding traditions share the same constraints?
   - *Key Focus:* Define the style or competition rule before judging. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
15. **Origami: Designing a Folded Model**
   - *Question:* How did sketches, crease tests, and diagrams evolve?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated versions and maker testimony. Evidence base: dated folded objects and diagrams, authorized design records, maker interviews, tested prototypes, mathematical proofs, and conservation records.
