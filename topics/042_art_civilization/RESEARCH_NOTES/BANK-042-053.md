# BANK-042-053 — Mona Lisa theft and fame

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `pending_topics/0` says Vincenzo Peruggia's 1911 theft “created the most famous painting on Earth.”

## Inspected museum sources

- The Louvre's [Salle des États history](https://musee.louvre.fr/en/explore/the-palace/from-the-mona-lisa-to-the-wedding-feast-at-cana) dates the disappearance to **21 August 1911** and describes Peruggia's later attempt to sell the painting to an Italian dealer, leading to its recovery.
- The Louvre's [collection record for the portrait](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010062370) says the theft brought worldwide and wider popular fame, especially after recovery in 1913. This supports an increase in fame, not a measurable claim that one person alone made it the world's most famous painting.

## Claim disposition

Distinguish the work's pre-1911 artistic reputation, publicity during its absence, recovery, and later museum and media circulation. “Most famous” needs an explicit measure and comparison; no such ranking was established by these sources. For a selected script, inspect contemporary press and Louvre attendance or reproduction records before attributing a causal share to the theft. Do not dramatize motives or actions beyond cited police and museum records.
