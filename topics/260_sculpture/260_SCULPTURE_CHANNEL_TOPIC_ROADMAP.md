## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Sculpture across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Sculpture Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Sculpture.
- **Tier 2: Sculpture Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Sculpture.
- **Tier 3: Sculpture Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Sculpture.
- **Tier 4: Sculpture Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Sculpture.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Sculpture: How a Sculptural Form Emerged**
   - *Question:* What dated works and tools show one method's development?
   - *Key Focus:* Name place and period; avoid a single invention claim. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
2. **Sculpture: A Celebrated Sculpture Rechecked**
   - *Question:* What does the object show beyond its iconic image?
   - *Key Focus:* Examine scale, material, site, maker, and provenance. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
3. **Sculpture: Fabricators and Installers**
   - *Question:* Whose labor made a sculpture possible?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace studio assistants, foundries, quarries, transport, and care. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
4. **Sculpture: Reading Sculpture in Space**
   - *Question:* How do mass, surface, material, and viewpoint work together?
   - *Key Focus:* Use one object in its actual setting. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
5. **Sculpture: Object, Site, and Audience**
   - *Question:* How does relocating a sculpture change meaning?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare original and current site, access, and interpretation. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Sculpture: An Uncertain Sculptural Attribution**
   - *Question:* Which evidence could distinguish maker, workshop, and later repair?
   - *Key Focus:* Check provenance, tool marks, materials, and archives. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
7. **Sculpture: A New Fabrication Method**
   - *Question:* How did casting, scanning, or printing alter one practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare artist control, labor, finish, and cost. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
8. **Sculpture: A Sculptor's Collaboration**
   - *Question:* Which people influenced one major commission?
   - *Key Focus:* Use contracts, sketches, studio records, and credit. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
9. **Sculpture: A Monument Moved or Contested**
   - *Question:* How did one decision change a sculpture's public role?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace authority, consultation, site, and community response. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
10. **Sculpture: From Model to Installation**
   - *Question:* What stages turn an idea into a stable work?
   - *Key Focus:* Map maquette, material, fabrication, transport, installation, and maintenance. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Sculpture: A Material's Changing Use**
   - *Question:* Why did one sculptural material gain or lose favor?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare supply, price, technique, fashion, and conservation. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
12. **Sculpture: One Work Over Time**
   - *Question:* How have surface, setting, and interpretation changed?
   - *Key Focus:* Use condition reports and dated photographs. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
13. **Sculpture: The Sculpture's Next Home**
   - *Question:* Which choices affect continued access and care?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare funding, conservation, artist intent, and public use. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
14. **Sculpture: Made by One Hand?**
   - *Question:* What did the credited artist and workshop each do?
   - *Key Focus:* Check contracts, process photos, and maker accounts. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
15. **Sculpture: One Sculpture's Full Life**
   - *Question:* How was it made, owned, displayed, and repaired?
   - *Key Focus:* Build a dated provenance and conservation timeline. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Sculpture: A Sculpture for a Site**
   - *Question:* Which spatial, material, and audience needs shaped the commission?
   - *Key Focus:* Read brief, permits, artist proposal, and constraints. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
17. **Sculpture: Why This Material**
   - *Question:* How does stone, metal, wood, or found matter affect form?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare properties, sourcing, labor, and maintenance. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
18. **Sculpture: Fabrication Decisions**
   - *Question:* Which stages changed the design?
   - *Key Focus:* Use models, workshop records, and maker testimony. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
19. **Sculpture: Form from Multiple Viewpoints**
   - *Question:* What changes as a viewer moves around the object?
   - *Key Focus:* Describe measured scale, sightlines, and artist explanation. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
20. **Sculpture: The Studio and Foundry**
   - *Question:* How should fabricators and assistants be credited?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace roles, contracts, and attribution practice. Evidence base: provenanced objects and models, studio and foundry records, commission contracts, site plans, conservation reports, and attributed community accounts.
