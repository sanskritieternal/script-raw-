# Number theory primary-source follow-up — 2026-10-24

## BANK-365-041 — Wiles and Taylor–Wiles, 1995

- Primary journal records: Andrew Wiles, *Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem*, *Annals of Mathematics* 141 (1995): https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1995/141-3/p01 ; Richard Taylor and Andrew Wiles, *Ring-theoretic properties of certain Hecke algebras*, same issue: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1995/141-3/p02 .
- The journal documents the paired published papers, authors and issue. Together they are the established published proof path for Fermat's Last Theorem via modularity results, rather than a large finite search.
- Limit: journal metadata pages do not present a lay-verifiable proof and have no abstracts; full arguments were not independently checked in this review. Script claims about exact theorem dependencies only after a specialist source review. Do not imply the 1993 announcement alone was the final published proof.

## BANK-365-042 — AKS and the correction

- Primary journal paper: Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena, *PRIMES is in P*, *Annals of Mathematics* 160 (2004): https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12 . Primary erratum by the same authors, *Annals of Mathematics* 189 (2019): https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v189-n1-p06-s.pdf .
- The 2004 abstract states an unconditional deterministic polynomial-time primality algorithm. The 2019 erratum explicitly says the proof of Lemma 4.3 was incorrect and supplies a corrected argument.
- Limit: distinguish theorem statement from the originally flawed lemma proof and read the corrected version when discussing proof details. Polynomial time is an asymptotic classification, not evidence this implementation wins on ordinary-sized inputs. A numerical test of examples is not a proof for every integer.
