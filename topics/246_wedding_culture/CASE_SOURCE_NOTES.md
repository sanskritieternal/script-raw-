# Wedding-culture case-source notes (2026-10-24)

Selected institutional records support bounded questions. Neither a UNESCO listing nor a civil rule defines every community’s marriage practice.

| Case | Primary record inspected | Claim and access limit |
|---|---|---|
| Betashar | [UNESCO 2024 Betashar nomination entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/betashar-traditional-wedding-ritual-01746) identifies the rite and lists akyn, relatives and makers; [nomination index](https://ich.unesco.org/en/7b-representative-list-01370) links community-consent materials. | Nomination narrative is submitted heritage documentation, not a survey of all Kazakh weddings or independent proof of a medieval origin. Public entry accessible; individual consent files and contemporary variation need separate review. |
| Outdoor civil ceremonies | [2022 explanatory memorandum to the Marriages and Civil Partnerships approved-premises amendment](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/295/pdfs/uksiem_20220295_en.pdf) describes continuing the option for outdoor areas linked to already-approved premises. | England/Wales civil approved-premises scope and effective date matter; memorandum is explanatory, and the regulations must be checked for any present legal claim. This is not advice for a particular couple or a statement about religious ceremonies. |

No archived superlative, universal custom, livestream or family-consent claim is treated as verified here. Participant privacy and community consent remain production requirements.
