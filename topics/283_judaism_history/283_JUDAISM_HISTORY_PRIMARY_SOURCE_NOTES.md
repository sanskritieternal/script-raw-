Cambridge University Library holds the Taylor-Schechter fragments.

The traveler's identity, origin and circumstance have competing scholarly readings; do not assert one as settled. ## BANK-283-045: trader's letter, 28 October 1103

- Princeton Geniza Project, [letter T-S 12.392](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/9690/), inspected 2026-10-24. Its Judaeo-Arabic transcription and Marina Rustow/Alan Elbaum English translation date it 24 Heshvan 4864 AM (28 October 1103 CE). The writer describes passing through Fustat and Red Sea locations and planning to sail to India while writing to family.
- Cambridge University Library, [Fragment of the Month, April 2026](https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/taylor-schechter-genizah-research-unit/fragment-month/fotm-2026/fragment-1), institution-indexed text inspected 2026-10-24, places this single letter in a wider merchant archive.
- Limit: the writer's name and fate are unknown; the letter does not establish arrival in India, exact historical itinerary beyond the writer's account, or a representative Jewish merchant experience. Some lines are damaged and the prose uses rhetorical puns. Cambridge's article returned 403 on direct fetch; Princeton's primary document and attributed translation are accessible. Keep the Fustat/Red Sea/India context separate from broad Islamic trade history.
