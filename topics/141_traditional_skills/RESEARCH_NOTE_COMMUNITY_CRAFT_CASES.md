# Selected primary-source research: community craft cases

Checked 2026-10-24. These source checks bound two proposed questions. The other archived antiquity, speed, durability and revival claims remain unverified.

## Q’eswachaka bridge renewal — BANK-141-045

- Primary heritage record: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, [knowledge, skills and rituals related to annual renewal of the Q’eswachaka bridge](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/knowledge-skills-and-rituals-related-to-the-annual-renewal-of-the-q-eswachaka-bridge-00594), inscribed in 2013. It names the Quechua-speaking communities of Huinchiri, Chaupibanda, Choccayhua and Ccollana Quehue and describes renewal, family transmission and ritual/social significance.
- Additional state/community reporting lodged with UNESCO: [Peru’s periodic report](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/Signed%20periodic%20report%20-%20Periodic%20report-50250.pdf). It discusses ongoing transmission, participation and uneven distribution of outside support, qualifying a simple rescue narrative.
- Limit: UNESCO inscription records what the submitting state and communities documented; it is not proof that every practitioner uses identical techniques, that the bridge is the only surviving example, or that any individual holder consented to filming or commercializing a restricted practice. Do not turn the descriptions into a structural safety recipe. Seek direct community attribution and consent for any later production.

## Harris Tweed Act and Orb certification — BANK-141-046

- Primary legal text hosted by the statutory body: [Harris Tweed Act 1993](https://www.harristweed.org/wp-content/uploads/Harris-Tweed-Authority-1993-Act-Regulation-and-Annexes.pdf). The [Harris Tweed Authority’s role page](https://www.harristweed.org/our-role/) explains its certification function and summarizes the legal cloth definition: pure virgin wool dyed and spun in the Outer Hebrides, handwoven at islanders’ homes there, and finished there.
- Limit: The Act protects a defined cloth and certification mark; it does not identify the individual weaver of every garment or prove a particular item’s full ownership history. The Outer Hebrides include islands beyond Harris. Do not suggest that every production stage occurs in one cottage, or that a garment is authentic merely because its styling looks traditional. Confirm current label and edition rules before a contemporary authenticity claim.

## Semantic and cultural boundaries

The named bridge practice belongs with its participating communities, beside the broader [craft teaching](../095_handicrafts_traditional_skills/IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-095-039) and [institutional reconstruction](../096_museums_cultural_heritage/IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-096-061) questions. Harris Tweed’s statutory definition differs from [general place attribution in weaving](../265_weaving/IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-265-033) and [material analysis of historical cloth](../264_textile_history/IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-264-039). These records do not authorize replicating protected motifs or representing community knowledge without its holders’ involvement.
