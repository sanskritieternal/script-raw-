## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Sewing across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Sewing Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Sewing.
- **Tier 2: Sewing Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Sewing.
- **Tier 3: Sewing Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Sewing.
- **Tier 4: Sewing Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Sewing.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Sewing: A Sewing Practice in Context**
   - *Question:* Which dated garment or manual documents a named construction method?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify place, material, maker, and later alteration. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
2. **Sewing: A Garment's Construction**
   - *Question:* Which seams, shaping, and finish explain how a surviving garment works?
   - *Key Focus:* Use object study and pattern evidence. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
3. **Sewing: Labor inside a Garment**
   - *Question:* Who cut, stitched, fitted, altered, and cared for it?
   - *Key Focus:* Check labels, records, and maker testimony. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
4. **Sewing: From Pattern to Fit**
   - *Question:* How do body measurements, ease, grain, and seam allowance connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare a named pattern with a documented sample. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
5. **Sewing: Fit across Movement**
   - *Question:* Which garment dimensions change comfort and range of motion?
   - *Key Focus:* Use wearer feedback and measured fit tests. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Sewing: An Unattributed Garment**
   - *Question:* Which stitch or label evidence can identify maker or era?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate provenance, style dating, and speculation. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
7. **Sewing: A Sewing Machine Change**
   - *Question:* Which stitch or production task did a documented machine alter?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated examples and labor process. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
8. **Sewing: A Dressmaker's Revision**
   - *Question:* Why did a maker alter a sleeve, closure, or seam?
   - *Key Focus:* Use fitting notes and attributed accounts. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
9. **Sewing: A Pattern Publication**
   - *Question:* What changed when a pattern became available to home sewers?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace editions, sizing, and distribution. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
10. **Sewing: Building a Wearable Piece**
   - *Question:* How do cutting, assembly, fitting, and finishing interact?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow one object without prescriptive machine instruction. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Sewing: A Construction Method Changes**
   - *Question:* Why did a finishing or fastening method decline?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare materials, labor, and garment use. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
12. **Sewing: One Garment after Alteration**
   - *Question:* Which fit and material decisions differ between original and later version?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated photos and physical evidence. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
13. **Sewing: A New Sewing Material**
   - *Question:* Which performance and repair claims still need testing?
   - *Key Focus:* Define wear, washing, and intended use. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
14. **Sewing: A Single Standard Size?**
   - *Question:* How do body variation and pattern-company blocks change fit?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare size charts and actual measurement. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
15. **Sewing: Making One Garment**
   - *Question:* How did sketch, pattern, fitting, and finish evolve?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow dated artifacts and maker credits. Evidence base: surviving garments, dated patterns and size charts, maker and fitting records, textile tests, conservation reports, and consented wearer accounts.
