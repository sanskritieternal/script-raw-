# Selected primary-source note — credit cards

Inspected 2026-10-24 for `BANK-294-040`. This is a U.S.-specific disclosure and market-report premise check, not advice on an individual account.

- [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Regulation Z §1026.60](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/60/): official U.S. regulatory text says an issuer with a deferred-interest offer may not present a 0% rate as the applicable rate where interest may be owed for the promotional period under some circumstances. Check the effective version and account-specific agreement before production.
- [CFPB, 2015 Consumer Credit Card Market Report to Congress, section 6.7](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201512_cfpb_report-the-consumer-credit-card-market.pdf): distinguishes deferred-interest promotions and discusses payment patterns and risk at expiration using data from the report's stated period. Those aggregate findings do not calculate a specific person's charges or establish the current prevalence of offers.
- [CFPB consumer explanation, reviewed 22 January 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/i-got-a-credit-card-promising-no-interest-for-a-purchase-if-i-pay-in-full-within-12-months-how-does-this-work-en-40/): illustrates conditional offer language for readers; it is guidance, not a substitute for a cardholder agreement.

The sources were accessible. Their legal scope is the United States, regulatory text and issuer terms may change, and promotional rates, deferred interest, fee treatment and minimum-payment allocation must not be conflated. This card-contract case stays in 294 rather than general banking in 293 or historical monetary systems in 295/298.
