## BANK-314-042: English hearth tax, 1662

- UK National Archives, [E179 grant/document notes for the 1662 hearth tax](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/e179/notes.asp?action=3&slctgrantid=352), inspected 2026-10-24. The notes describe a twice-yearly shilling charge per hearth, fire or stove and a poverty exemption requiring local certification; officers requested household returns.
- National Archives, [Taxation before 1689 guide](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/taxation-before-1689/), inspected 2026-10-24. It dates the hearth tax to 1662-1689 and explains that survival and administration differ by period.
- Limits: these are institutional descriptions of the grant and record series, not a transcription of every parish's return. Statutory liability, observed hearths and actual payment differ; missing records and exemption practice prevent treating a surviving list as a complete population census. England/Wales scope and 1662 conditions are specific.

## BANK-314-043: UK window tax debate, 1851

- UK Parliament, [Hansard, Commons, 19 February 1851](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1851-02-19/debates/cfb76df5-d4b0-4aad-824a-b0a5dfae08c1/CommonsChamber), inspected 2026-10-24. A petition asked for unconditional repeal instead of substitution, and the Speaker discussed petition procedure.
- UK Parliament archive, [Ways and Means, 4 April 1851](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1851/apr/04/ways-and-means-the-budget) and [Inhabited House Duty Bill, 18 July 1851](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1851/jul/18/inhabited-house-duty-bill), inspected 2026-10-24, show competing statements about repeal, replacement and revenue.
- Limits: speeches and petitions record positions, not demonstrated incidence or realized revenue; the archive warns of transcription errors. The replacement sequence requires checking enacted statute and accounts before claiming final rates or effects. Do not infer that every house had blocked windows, or generalize to all countries and periods.
