# Selected collectibles source checks — 24 October 2026

These checks support three bounded case proposals. A grader’s standard, an auction house’s rules and its sale record describe their own actions; none independently authenticates an unfamiliar object, establishes title or predicts resale value. Other archived claims require item-specific evidence.

## BANK-084-058 — PSA 9 and 10

- [PSA’s current grading standards](https://www.psacard.com/gradingstandards) define Gem Mint 10 as virtually perfect with stated allowances and Mint 9 as a different condition grade. The page also describes qualifiers and altered or ungradable cards. A numerical label is a service’s condition opinion about a submitted item.
- [PSA’s grading introduction](https://www.psacard.com/info/get-started) illustrates how 9 and 10 differ under that service. It does not imply a constant price spread or prove the total surviving population of an issue.
- **Editorial bound:** A named card needs issue, certification, population date and comparable completed sales. Do not claim a guaranteed $1 million difference or treat a holder as complete provenance.

## BANK-084-070 — reserves and bidding

- [Sotheby’s current buyer guide](https://help.sothebys.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002518078-guide-for-buyers-global) says an auctioneer may bid on a seller’s behalf up to a reserve, including responsive or consecutive bids, with stated limits. The rules are venue and sale dependent.
- [Christie’s Hong Kong 2024 conditions of sale](https://www.christies.com/-/media/conditions-of-sale/ConditionsOfSale-HongKong-English_7MAY24.pdf) also describe auctioneer bids below a reserve. [Christie’s auction glossary](https://www.christies.com/en/help/glossary?add_lang=en) distinguishes hammer price from the buyer’s total purchase price.
- **Editorial bound:** Quote the rules for the relevant sale and jurisdiction, date them, and separate permitted reserve bidding from a claim of fraud or psychological manipulation. Financial outcomes depend on premium, tax and fees.

## BANK-084-073 — conflated ceramic stories

- [Sotheby’s report on its 2017 Hong Kong sale](https://www.sothebys.com/en/slideshows/chinese-ru-ware-washer-shatters-world-record-in-hong-kong) describes a Northern Song Ru guanyao **brush washer** that sold for HK$294.3 million, about US$37.7 million. It does not describe a thrift-store bowl bought for $5.
- The auction house’s [later collecting history](https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/sothebys-legacy-in-asia-the-golden-age-of-collecting) identifies this washer as part of the Le Cong Tang collection. Its documented collection history conflicts with the archive headline’s supposed thrift discovery.
- **Editorial bound:** Treat “$5 thrift-store bowl sold for $37 million” as an unverified conflation. A separate thrift-bowl story needs its own lot, object attribution, purchase evidence, hammer and buyer-inclusive prices before comparison.
