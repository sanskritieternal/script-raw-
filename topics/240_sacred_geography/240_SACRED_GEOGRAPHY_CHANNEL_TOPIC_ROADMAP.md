## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Sacred Geography across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Sacred Geography Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Sacred Geography.
- **Tier 2: Sacred Geography Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Sacred Geography.
- **Tier 3: Sacred Geography Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Sacred Geography.
- **Tier 4: Sacred Geography Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Sacred Geography.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Sacred Geography: A Sacred Landscape in Dated Sources**
   - *Question:* When do named places and their associations appear in the record?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare texts, maps, archaeology, and later community memory. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
2. **Sacred Geography: A Known Sacred Landscape in Context**
   - *Question:* How do distinct communities understand one well-known place?
   - *Key Focus:* Attribute each account and trace dated stewardship. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
3. **Sacred Geography: Custodians of a Sacred Place**
   - *Question:* Whose care work or knowledge is missing from a visitor map?
   - *Key Focus:* Use consented local voices and institutional records. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
4. **Sacred Geography: What Makes a Place Sacred?**
   - *Question:* How does one community define the relationship among land, story, and practice?
   - *Key Focus:* Represent its own terms rather than assigning sacredness from outside. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
5. **Sacred Geography: Overlapping Sacred Geographies**
   - *Question:* How do two communities map meaning onto the same landscape?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare place names, access protocols, history, and coexistence. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Sacred Geography: A Disputed Site Identification**
   - *Question:* Which evidence supports competing locations for one historical place?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate textual description, archaeology, maps, and living tradition. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
7. **Sacred Geography: Mapping a Sensitive Site**
   - *Question:* Can a digital map help stewardship without exposing restricted locations?
   - *Key Focus:* Ask custodians about consent, precision, access, and data control. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
8. **Sacred Geography: A Landscape's Documented Steward**
   - *Question:* What did one named custodian or organizer do for a place?
   - *Key Focus:* Use records and community perspectives beyond a heroic account. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
9. **Sacred Geography: A Sacred Place Changes Access**
   - *Question:* What happened after one dated law, road, or conservation decision?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare access, ritual use, ecology, and local livelihoods. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
10. **Sacred Geography: A Place Network, Not One Point**
   - *Question:* How do named sites, routes, and seasonal practices connect?
   - *Key Focus:* Map community descriptions and distinguish public from restricted knowledge. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Sacred Geography: Changing Use of a Sacred Area**
   - *Question:* Why did visits or observances increase or decline?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare counting methods, policy, mobility, and community choices. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
12. **Sacred Geography: One Landscape Across Generations**
   - *Question:* What changed in names, access, ecology, and stewardship?
   - *Key Focus:* Pair dated maps with consented living accounts. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
13. **Sacred Geography: A Land-Use Proposal at a Sacred Site**
   - *Question:* What would one proposal need to protect community access and ecology?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare plans, rights, impact assessment, and custodial priorities. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
14. **Sacred Geography: A Map as Proof of Sacred History?**
   - *Question:* Does one popular map accurately represent sources and community knowledge?
   - *Key Focus:* Check date, scale, naming, uncertainty, and permissions. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
15. **Sacred Geography: A Sacred Place Through Records and Voices**
   - *Question:* What do maps, legal files, and custodial accounts reveal?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain gaps and attribute sacred meanings. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Sacred Geography: Mapped Boundary and Lived Place**
   - *Question:* Where does an official map diverge from community place knowledge?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare scale, language, access, and field evidence with consent. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
17. **Sacred Geography: Different Paths to Meaningful Sites**
   - *Question:* Why do community members use different routes or sequences?
   - *Key Focus:* Ask practitioners and respect restricted paths. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
18. **Sacred Geography: A Sacred Calendar and the Land**
   - *Question:* How do season and ecology shape one public observance?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare calendars, environmental data, and local interpretation. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
19. **Sacred Geography: Residents and Custodians Speak**
   - *Question:* How do nearby people describe benefits, burdens, and obligations?
   - *Key Focus:* Use diverse consented accounts without assuming one community voice. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
20. **Sacred Geography: Physical Change at a Sacred Site**
   - *Question:* How has erosion, water, vegetation, or development changed one place?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated environmental records and attributed cultural meaning. Evidence base: dated texts and maps, heritage and land records, environmental assessments, access rules, and consented custodian and resident accounts.
