# BANK-119-026 and BANK-119-025: Millennium problem boundaries

Research checked: 2026-10-24. Status: selected premise research; no script or idea approval.

The [Clay Mathematics Institute P vs NP page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/) lists the problem as unsolved and frames it as whether problems with solutions that are easy to check are also easy to solve. Its linked [Stephen Cook official problem description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/MPPc.pdf) provides the formal decision-problem statement. The [Clay Riemann Hypothesis page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/Riemann-Hypothesis/) likewise lists that problem as unsolved and states the claim about nontrivial zeta zeros having real part 1/2. The [prize rules](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/) require publication, two years and broad acceptance before Clay considers a proposed solution.

**Claim boundary:** These sources verify the problem statements and Clay's listed status as of the checked date. They do not establish that solving P vs NP automatically reveals every Bitcoin private key, that a practical algorithm would follow, or that the Riemann Hypothesis directly exposes encrypted traffic. Explain polynomial time, algorithm constants and actual cryptographic assumptions separately before using an application example. Clay's prize is not a shortcut to judging a newly announced proof.

**Semantic home:** Niche 119 owns mathematical statements and proof questions. Niche 296 owns wallet and cryptocurrency systems; niche 326 owns deployed cybersecurity. Niche 121 owns statistical inference applications, while the explicit Bayes arithmetic example in 119 is a mathematical teaching case. Avoid duplicating their application episodes.
