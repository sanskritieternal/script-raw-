## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Real Estate Economics across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Real Estate Economics Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Real Estate Economics.
- **Tier 2: Real Estate Economics Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Real Estate Economics.
- **Tier 3: Real Estate Economics Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Real Estate Economics.
- **Tier 4: Real Estate Economics Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Real Estate Economics.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Real Estate Economics: A Housing Market Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which infrastructure or policy change altered one local housing market?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated land, population, and planning records. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
2. **Real Estate Economics: A Housing Boom Tested**
   - *Question:* What did a frequently cited housing boom actually measure?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare prices, transaction volume, rents, and credit. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
3. **Real Estate Economics: Whose Housing Costs Are Missing?**
   - *Question:* Which renters or informal households are absent from market data?
   - *Key Focus:* Use survey coverage and local accounts. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
4. **Real Estate Economics: Supply, Demand, and Housing Stock**
   - *Question:* Why can a new housing project have a delayed effect on availability?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish stock, flow, vacancy, and household formation. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
5. **Real Estate Economics: The Identification Problem in Housing**
   - *Question:* How can researchers separate a policy effect from simultaneous market change?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare study designs and assumptions for one case. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Real Estate Economics: Why Prices Diverged**
   - *Question:* Which explanations remain plausible for two areas with different price paths?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare jobs, supply rules, credit, and amenities. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
7. **Real Estate Economics: A New Way to Measure Housing**
   - *Question:* Did a data tool improve the accuracy of a housing-market estimate?
   - *Key Focus:* Check sample coverage, listing bias, and validation. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
8. **Real Estate Economics: Actors Behind a Housing Constraint**
   - *Question:* Which owners, lenders, builders, or officials shaped a local outcome?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace incentives and documented decisions. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
9. **Real Estate Economics: A Shock to Housing Supply**
   - *Question:* How did one event affect construction or occupancy?
   - *Key Focus:* Use permits, completions, rents, and migration data. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
10. **Real Estate Economics: From Zoning to Completed Homes**
   - *Question:* Where can housing supply be delayed?
   - *Key Focus:* Map land, approval, finance, construction, and occupancy. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Real Estate Economics: A Housing Cycle Explained**
   - *Question:* What drove a local rise and reversal in activity?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare credit, employment, supply, and prices by date. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
12. **Real Estate Economics: Two Periods of Affordability**
   - *Question:* Did housing become less affordable for the same population?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare prices, rents, income, borrowing costs, and quality. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
13. **Real Estate Economics: A Conditional Supply Scenario**
   - *Question:* What would have to happen for a proposed supply change to affect rents?
   - *Key Focus:* State timing, scale, demand, and location assumptions. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
14. **Real Estate Economics: Testing a Housing Market Claim**
   - *Question:* Does a popular explanation fit the local evidence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare timing and alternative mechanisms. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
15. **Real Estate Economics: One Development and Its Market**
   - *Question:* What did a completed project change in nearby housing indicators?
   - *Key Focus:* Use bounded geography and before-and-after data cautiously. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Real Estate Economics: Loan Terms in a Housing Model**
   - *Question:* How does a change in mortgage terms affect an affordability calculation?
   - *Key Focus:* Use identical property and income inputs. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
17. **Real Estate Economics: Housing Burden Beyond Rent**
   - *Question:* Which costs remain after rent or mortgage payments are counted?
   - *Key Focus:* Use household expenditure data and a defined population. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
18. **Real Estate Economics: Interest Rates and Construction Lag**
   - *Question:* Why can housing respond slowly to a rate change?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace financing, approvals, and project completion times. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
19. **Real Estate Economics: Transaction Costs and Mobility**
   - *Question:* How might taxes and fees discourage a move?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated transaction costs with an illustrative decision. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
20. **Real Estate Economics: Regulation and Market Outcomes**
   - *Question:* Did a consumer or tenant protection alter access or supply?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare intended and measured effects for one jurisdiction. Evidence base: dated price and rent indices, permit and completion records, household surveys, planning rules, and documented study methods.
