# Selected survey-source note — real estate economics

Inspected 2026-10-24 for `BANK-313-041`. This is a New York City survey-definition case, not a current property valuation or personal housing recommendation.

- [NYC Housing Preservation and Development, 2023 NYCHVS selected initial findings](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/about/2023-nychvs-selected-initial-findings.pdf): defines the net rental vacancy numerator as unoccupied units available for rent and move-in at survey time. It separately counts vacant units unavailable for rent or sale for varied reasons. The report's estimates are survey-period and city-specific.
- [NYC HPD, 2023 NYCHVS sample design, weighting and error estimation](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hpd/downloads/pdfs/about/2023-nychvs-sample-design-weighting-error-estimation.pdf): documents sampling and estimation limits. A point estimate should not be treated as an exact count of every unit or a measure of how easily a particular household can find housing.
- [NYC EquityNYC, rental housing vacancy indicator](https://equity.nyc.gov/domains/housing/rental-housing-vacancy): institutional explanation of vacant, habitable and available-for-rent units used in the indicator. Compare definitions before placing this indicator next to total-empty-unit figures.

The city reports and indicator were accessible. The survey does not establish why each unit is unavailable, its legal status, the cost of repair, or a causal estimate of any single housing policy. Avoid transferring 2023 NYC rates to another city or a later year. This is a measurement question in 313; individual property buying/renting in 112 and city-design cases in 111 are distinct.
