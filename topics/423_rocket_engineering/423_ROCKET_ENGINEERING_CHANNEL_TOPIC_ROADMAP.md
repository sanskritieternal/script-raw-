## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Rocket Engineering across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Rocket Engineering Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Rocket Engineering.
- **Tier 2: Rocket Engineering Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Rocket Engineering.
- **Tier 3: Rocket Engineering Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Rocket Engineering.
- **Tier 4: Rocket Engineering Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Rocket Engineering.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Rocket Engineering: A Launch Vehicle Requirement**
   - *Question:* Which payload and mission need drove one rocket design?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated specifications and program decisions. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
2. **Rocket Engineering: A Launch Vehicle Case**
   - *Question:* What do test and mission records show about a celebrated rocket?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify variant, flight, and outcome. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
3. **Rocket Engineering: People behind the Launch**
   - *Question:* Which test operators or manufacturing workers are omitted?
   - *Key Focus:* Use credited, dated program records. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
4. **Rocket Engineering: Propulsion and Staging**
   - *Question:* Why might a rocket use multiple stages for one mission?
   - *Key Focus:* Explain mass flow and mission constraints without build instructions. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
5. **Rocket Engineering: Guidance under Changing Conditions**
   - *Question:* How does a launch vehicle stay within its planned trajectory?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public control and flight-data descriptions. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Rocket Engineering: An Unresolved Anomaly**
   - *Question:* Which telemetry or physical evidence is needed to identify cause?
   - *Key Focus:* Distinguish preliminary hypotheses from final review. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
7. **Rocket Engineering: A New Engine Feature**
   - *Question:* What changed in tested thrust, reliability, or reuse?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare test campaigns and flight conditions. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
8. **Rocket Engineering: A Program Decision**
   - *Question:* How did a named team choose among design alternatives?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated decisions and shared credit. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
9. **Rocket Engineering: A Flight Test Milestone**
   - *Question:* What did one flight demonstrate and leave unproven?
   - *Key Focus:* Read mission objectives and postflight findings. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
10. **Rocket Engineering: Launch Sequence at System Level**
   - *Question:* How do ground, propulsion, guidance, and staging interact?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public sequence and outcome data; omit operational procedures. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Rocket Engineering: A Vehicle Program Ends**
   - *Question:* Which documented cost, technical, or mission factors mattered?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare program records and successor plans. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
12. **Rocket Engineering: Two Rocket Generations**
   - *Question:* Which payload and reliability metrics can be compared?
   - *Key Focus:* Align orbit, configuration, sample size, and era. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
13. **Rocket Engineering: A Proposed Launch Capability**
   - *Question:* What tests and flights would validate its advertised use?
   - *Key Focus:* Mark projections and uncompleted milestones. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
14. **Rocket Engineering: A Payload Headline**
   - *Question:* Is a published payload figure tied to a specific orbit and variant?
   - *Key Focus:* Check mission profile and source. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
15. **Rocket Engineering: From Engine Test to Flight**
   - *Question:* How did one test finding change a launch program?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace dated reports, redesign, and mission results. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Rocket Engineering: Rocket or Launch System?**
   - *Question:* Which outcome includes ground facilities, upper stage, or payload?
   - *Key Focus:* Define boundary and mission. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
17. **Rocket Engineering: Command to Vehicle Response**
   - *Question:* How do guidance commands and vehicle sensors produce a recorded response?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public data, not operational settings. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
18. **Rocket Engineering: Payload and Margin**
   - *Question:* How does a design trade payload against margin or reuse?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public mission-level comparison. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
19. **Rocket Engineering: An Officially Investigated Anomaly**
   - *Question:* What sequence and causal finding does the record support?
   - *Key Focus:* Use public investigation; avoid actionable failure detail. Evidence base: NASA propulsion-test reports, public mission and flight records, launch approvals, official investigations, and environmental reviews.
20. **Rocket Engineering: Ground Test and Flight**
   - *Question:* Which flight conditions cannot be reproduced in a static engine test?
