# BANK-016-049 — What the 10,000-hour shorthand leaves out

**Proposal:** What did the original deliberate-practice studies actually establish about 10,000 hours?  
**State:** bounded primary-study review, 2026-10-24. This is a research note for a proposal, not an IDEA readiness decision or script approval.

## Sources inspected

| Source | Material checked | What it supports | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer (1993), *Psychological Review*](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf) | Original paper, especially printed pp. 367–369 and 378–380, including the practice definition, violinist groups and Figure 9. | The study compared estimated accumulated practice and performance groups. Its best young violinists averaged **7,410 hours by age 18**, versus 5,301 for the good violinists and 3,420 for music teachers. The authors discussed roughly ten years of intense preparation and later used 10,000 hours in a broader argument about long-term adaptation. | The hours came from retrospective weekly estimates, and group averages do not establish an individual threshold. The observed association does not show that any person will become elite after a fixed number of hours. |
| [Ericsson and Harwell (2019), *Frontiers in Psychology*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396/full) | Authors' published review and response, introduction and practice-definition discussion. | The original lead author explicitly distinguishes the popular “10,000-hour rule” from the 1993 paper's evidence and argues that practice quality and definition matter. | This is the authors' later interpretation, not a new experiment or a neutral resolution of all expertise debates. |
| [Macnamara and Maitra (2019), *Royal Society Open Science*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745/) | Published reanalysis abstract and reported conclusion. | The reanalysis reports less variance explained by accumulated practice among expert violinists than the original account and finds that practice amount alone does not account for differences among its most accomplished players. | Its practice definition and measurement are contested by Ericsson and Harwell. A script comparing effect sizes must inspect the full methods and supplementary analysis directly. |

## Bounded editorial finding

The archived local pitch says “10,000-Hour Rule Deconstructed.” The primary 1993 study did **not** test a universal 10,000-hour cutoff. Its group comparison and later theory support investigating sustained, structured practice in a particular performance domain. They do not promise mastery at a fixed hour, establish a dose that transfers to every skill, or prove that effort alone explains success. The 2019 papers disagree about operational definitions and the share of performance variation attributable to practice; neither licenses a universal shortcut.
