# BANK-007-122 — Voice impersonation and family verification

[Return to the main niche bank](../IN_DEPTH_TOPIC_BANK.md#bank-007-122).

This is a source-access note for a proposed public-safety tutorial, not an investigation of a particular call or proof that a rehearsal prevents fraud. The preserved queue's voice-cloning wording should not imply that every emergency scam uses AI.

The [U.S. Federal Trade Commission's family-emergency scam guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money) describes impersonation tactics, including possible AI voice cloning, and urges verification before paying. Its [voice-cloning alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes) recommends calling the person at a number already known to be theirs or contacting another relative or friend, rather than trusting the apparent voice. The [FTC's general scam-signs guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/03/what-are-signs-scam) likewise recommends independently obtained contact information and slowing down under pressure.

An episode could rehearse this decision path with actors, a synthetic example and no real payment or live outbound call. It should test whether a participant remembers to pause, use an independently known number and avoid sending funds until the story is checked. It should not ask viewers to identify cloning by ear, promise a universal “safe word,” or portray a successful role-play as a measured reduction in scam incidence. Recheck current FTC advice and local reporting options before release outside the United States.
