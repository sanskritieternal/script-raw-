## Bonded-leather claim — BANK-136-043

- The [FTC Leather Guides page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/leather-guides) identifies 16 CFR Part 24 and its concern with composition and characteristic claims for specified leather and imitation-leather products. The [FTC's published guide text and amendment history](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/federal_register_notices/guides-select-leather-and-imitation-leather-products-16-cfr-part-24/070523leatherguides.pdf) discuss disclosure of leather and non-leather content in bonded leather.
- The guide's scope includes named product classes such as bags and footwear; do not silently apply its wording to every jacket or jurisdiction. A “genuine leather” label alone does not establish construction, durability, peeling after six months, or that a particular item is plastic. An episode needs the actual product label and material evidence.

## Napleton auto add-ons — BANK-136-044

- The [FTC Napleton case docket](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2023195-napleton-auto) links the 2022 complaint, stipulated order and later case actions. The [FTC and Illinois complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/1%20-%20Joint%20Complaint.pdf) alleges unwanted vehicle add-ons and discriminatory financing or add-on charges; the case page records the $10 million settlement and subsequent consumer refunds.
- Complaint examples are allegations, not findings about every dealer or proof of the archive's specific “four-square” tactic. The docket includes later activity, so a production script must distinguish the 2022 allegations and order from any later modification or current rule. No general refund or negotiation outcome follows for an individual consumer.
