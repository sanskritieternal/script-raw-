# Selected source note — FTC impersonation-scam reports

Reviewed 2026-10-24 for BANK-496-044. Proposed question only; no approved IDEA or individualized fraud-recovery advice.

## Primary regulator records

- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, *Impersonation scams: not what they used to be*, Consumer Protection Data Spotlight (April 2024): https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2024/04/impersonation-scams-not-what-they-used-be . Based on reports directly to the FTC, it describes business and government impersonation patterns, channels, blended impersonation and reported losses; its notes state the reporting base.
- Fixed FTC PDF of the Spotlight: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/imposter-scams-spotlight-2024.pdf . Use its footnotes and definitions before quoting counts or comparisons.
- FTC, *Data Show People Reported Losing $3.5 Billion to Imposter Scams in 2025* (June 2026): https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025 . This later update illustrates why a 2024 snapshot should not be presented as current prevalence.

## Allegation, denominator and remedy limits

FTC reports are unverified submissions and selected contacts, not a census of all scam attempts, proof that any named person committed fraud, or the number of victims in the population. Loss amounts are reported losses, not adjudicated damages or guaranteed recoveries. Channel categories and reporting behavior can change; use the stated reporting period and denominator. A general FTC reporting route is not a promise of repayment. Avoid victim-blaming and identifying complaint details. Paraphrase regulator text and check chart reuse terms.
