# BANK-026-066 — Casgevy approval and outcome boundaries

Reviewed 2026-10-24. The preserved `trending_topics/2` pitch calls Casgevy “the CRISPR sickle cell cure” and “the world's first approved gene-editing therapy.” Both phrases need dated, jurisdiction-specific meaning.

## Inspected regulator records

- The UK MHRA [authorization announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhra-authorises-world-first-gene-therapy-that-aims-to-cure-sickle-cell-disease-and-transfusion-dependent-thalassemia), dated **16 November 2023**, describes Casgevy as a first-of-its-kind gene-editing treatment for sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia. “Aims to cure” is the regulator's characterization, not proof that every treated person is cured.
- The US FDA [8 December 2023 approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies-treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease) says Casgevy and Lyfgenia were the first cell-based gene therapies approved by FDA for sickle cell disease in patients **12 years and older**; Casgevy was the **first FDA-approved treatment using genome editing**. This is a US approval milestone, later than the UK authorization.
- The FDA [product and approval-history page](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/casgevy) shows later indication and age changes. A script must cite the specific current label and its effective date, not silently use the original 2023 eligibility as today's rule.
- The FDA's [2023 summary basis for regulatory action](https://www.fda.gov/media/175179/download) identifies the ex-vivo editing of a patient's hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells at the BCL11A erythroid enhancer, the conditioning and transplant process, and long-term safety concerns including off-target effects. Outcome numbers and adverse-event rates require direct inspection of the appropriate trial tables and follow-up period before quoting.

## Claim disposition

Use “first” only with the named technology, regulator and date. Treatment is a complex transplant pathway, not an injection that edits every sickle cell in the body. “Cure” is too absolute without a defined endpoint and follow-up; compare freedom from severe vaso-occlusive crises, survival, complications and late effects. Access, cost and present eligibility are separate time-sensitive questions. No patient-facing treatment recommendation is made here.
