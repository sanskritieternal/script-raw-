# Selected case: Nanni's letter to Ea-nasir

**Proposed question:** `BANK-545-050` — What can Nanni's complaint tablet to Ea-nasir establish about one Old Babylonian trade dispute?

## Primary evidence inspected

- [Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative object and transliteration, P414985 / UET 5, 81](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/414985). The catalog identifies an Akkadian clay tablet from Ur, in the British Museum as BM 131236 / 1953,0411.71, and displays a line-by-line transliteration. It labels the tablet a complaint to Ea-nasir and records slight damage. The exact dimensions displayed in CDLI appear surprisingly small for the described multiline tablet and must be checked against the museum object before any size claim.
- [British Museum object record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1953-0411-71) and [museum image description](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/image/141562001). The museum's image description summarizes Nanni's complaint about copper quality and a further delayed or misdirected delivery. Direct object-page access returned 403 in this review; the image-description page and CDLI metadata were accessible.

## Bounded premise and gaps

This is one writer's account of a transaction and grievance, preserved in a damaged witness. The tablet does not show a court finding, the recipient's reply, proof that the allegations were true, a complete commercial ledger, or normal practice throughout Mesopotamia. The CDLI transliteration was viewed; a full critical translation against sign photographs, earlier edition and museum record was not completed. Therefore exact commercial terms, dates and monetary quantities need further checking at script stage.

## Coverage and overlap decision

Archived documented-case, merchant-letter and evidence-trail pitches route to this object. `BANK-545-039` asks what merchant letters can show generally; `BANK-545-050` asks how this single complaint can and cannot support a historical account. Household property, administrative language, law collections and archaeology pitches route to their existing scoped IDs. Superlative labels such as the “first customer complaint,” broad commercial failures, tool claims and personal biographies are out of scope without another evidentiary basis.
