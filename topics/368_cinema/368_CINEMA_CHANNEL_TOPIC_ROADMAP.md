## 1. Channel Progression Strategy

This curriculum organizes the core disciplines of Cinema across four progressive tiers:
- **Tier 1: Cinema Foundations & Core Mechanisms (Beginner / Scratch)** — Core physical and conceptual fundamentals, terminology, primary mechanisms, and foundational observation methods in Cinema.
- **Tier 2: Cinema Methodological Deep Dives & Empirical Evidence (Intermediate)** — Detailed instrumentation, measurement metrics, classification systems, and documented case studies of Cinema.
- **Tier 3: Cinema Advanced Systemic Paradigms & Comparative Dynamics (Upper Intermediate)** — Complex systemic interactions, historical shifts, anomalous patterns, and comparative analyses across different contexts in Cinema.
- **Tier 4: Cinema Frontier Technologies, Global Impact & Future Horizons (Advanced / Masterclass)** — Emerging technological breakthroughs, policy frameworks, environmental and human impact, and long-term evolutionary projections for Cinema.

## 3. Topic Catalog (Scratch to Advanced Curriculum)

### Tier 1: Foundational Frameworks
1. **The Architecture of Cinema: A Film Form Takes Shape**
   - *Question:* Which early production or exhibition practice shaped a named film form?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare dated films, trade records, and local exhibition context. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
2. **Cinema: A Landmark Film Revisited**
   - *Question:* What formal choice made a named film influential, and who called it so?
   - *Key Focus:* Analyze the film and dated reception rather than repeat a canon label. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
3. **Cinema: Labor Behind a Film**
   - *Question:* Whose credited and uncredited work shaped a named scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credits, production records, interviews, and competing accounts. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
4. **Cinema: How a Scene Communicates**
   - *Question:* How do framing, sound, performance, and cutting guide one interpretation?
   - *Key Focus:* Show the sequence and distinguish observation from interpretation. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
5. **Cinema: Form and Spectatorship**
   - *Question:* How does a film's viewpoint position its audience across a sequence?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare formal evidence with specific theories and reception studies. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
### Tier 2: Methodological Deep Dives
6. **Cinema: A Lost or Disputed Film**
   - *Question:* Which version, credit, or release history remains uncertain?
   - *Key Focus:* Separate surviving material, catalog records, and speculation. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
7. **Cinema: A Film Tool in Practice**
   - *Question:* How did a camera, sound, or projection change alter a particular production?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace equipment and decisions without claiming technology alone caused an aesthetic shift. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
8. **Cinema: A Filmmaker's Choices**
   - *Question:* Which documented decisions shaped a named film?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare work, drafts, collaborators, and attributed interviews. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
9. **Cinema: A Screening that Changed Circulation**
   - *Question:* How did a festival, censorship decision, or distribution deal affect one film?
   - *Key Focus:* Use dated programs, rulings, contracts, and audience records. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
10. **Cinema: From Shot to Sequence**
   - *Question:* How do shot order and duration change what viewers can infer?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare cuts of the same bounded passage when available. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
### Tier 3: Advanced Analytical Paradigms
11. **Cinema: A Film's Changing Reputation**
   - *Question:* How did critical or public response shift after release?
   - *Key Focus:* Sample dated reviews and access conditions across periods. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
12. **Cinema: One Film Across Formats**
   - *Question:* What changes between an original release and a later restoration or stream?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify source element, aspect ratio, sound, and exhibition conditions. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
13. **Cinema: A Changing Exhibition Practice**
   - *Question:* What evidence supports a proposed shift in film access or presentation?
   - *Key Focus:* Specify place, period, audience, and uncertainty. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
14. **Cinema: A Familiar Cinema Origin Story**
   - *Question:* Which part of a common first-film claim survives archival checking?
   - *Key Focus:* Define the claimed first and compare dated records. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
15. **Cinema: A Film's Production Trail**
   - *Question:* How did a script, shoot, edit, and release shape one surviving film?
   - *Key Focus:* Follow versioned artifacts and testimony with provenance. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
### Tier 4: Frontier Systems & Interdisciplinary Paradigms
16. **Cinema: A Film's Stated Aim**
   - *Question:* What was the production trying to communicate at a documented stage?
   - *Key Focus:* Compare brief or script with finished work without assuming one authorial intent. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
17. **Cinema: Film Stock or Digital Capture**
   - *Question:* How did a chosen medium constrain one visual decision?
   - *Key Focus:* Identify actual stock, sensor, lens, and workflow records. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
18. **Cinema: Making a Scene**
   - *Question:* Which decisions moved a scene from script to final cut?
   - *Key Focus:* Trace drafts, shot plans, takes, and editorial record. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
19. **Cinema: Meaning in a Frame**
   - *Question:* How do placement, movement, and offscreen space shape one scene?
   - *Key Focus:* Use timecoded frame comparisons and alternative readings. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
20. **Cinema: Who Made the Film?**
   - *Question:* Which contributions by writer, performers, crew, and editor are documented?
   - *Key Focus:* Check credits and records; avoid assigning one person every choice. Evidence base: timecoded films and versions, production records, credits, archive catalogs, dated criticism, exhibition records, and participant accounts.
