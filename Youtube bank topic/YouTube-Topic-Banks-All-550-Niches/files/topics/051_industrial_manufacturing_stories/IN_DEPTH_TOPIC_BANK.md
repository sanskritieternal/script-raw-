## 01. From product to production

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|

| BANK-051-014 | Why can a design that works once be difficult to manufacture at scale? | Compare variability, material supply, assembly access and inspection instead of treating replication as automatic. |
| BANK-051-015 | How do factories choose between job production, batches and continuous flow? | Compare demand, changeover, capital and product variety within an identified industry. |
| BANK-051-016 | What does a factory tour omit about the work behind a finished object? | Include procurement, maintenance, quality, logistics and planning rather than showing only visually striking machines. |

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |

| BANK-051-022 | What changes between making yarn, weaving fabric and finishing cloth? | Follow one textile's production chain with distinct machinery, skills and quality decisions at each stage. |
| BANK-051-023 | How does a paper mill balance fiber properties with the intended product? | Compare feedstock, processing and testing without suggesting all paper uses the same recipe. |
| BANK-051-024 | Why can glassmaking require continuous operation? | Explain furnace economics, thermal constraints and documented shutdown planning at a general technical level. |

## 05. Assembly, automation and people

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-051-029 | When does automation move a bottleneck rather than remove it? | Trace throughput, upstream supply, downtime and downstream handling in a documented production line. |
| BANK-051-030 | How do maintenance teams influence a factory's reliable output? | Compare preventive work, fault diagnosis, spares and operating coordination without reducing maintenance to emergency repair. |
| BANK-051-031 | What can workstation design reveal about the relationship between quality and ergonomics? | Use observed task requirements and published studies without diagnosing workers or prescribing medical interventions. |
| BANK-051-032 | How did one factory transition affect workers' skills and bargaining power? | Compare training, job content, employment and testimony rather than equating automation with one inevitable labor outcome. |

## 06. Quality and industrial learning

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-051-033 | What distinguishes process control from inspecting finished products? | Compare sampling, feedback, variation and acceptance decisions through a clearly defined manufacturing example. |
| BANK-051-034 | Why can a process meet an average target while producing unacceptable variation? | Explain distribution, specifications and capability assumptions with an illustrative dataset labeled as such. |
| BANK-051-035 | How does a product recall reveal gaps across manufacturing and supplier systems? | Use official findings to separate identified defects, traceability and corrective action without speculating beyond the record. |
| BANK-051-036 | What makes a continuous-improvement claim measurable? | Compare the baseline, intervention, output quality, throughput and unintended effects rather than relying on a slogan. |

## 07. Supply chains and industrial economics

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use current official terms, filings or macroeconomic data; state jurisdiction, date, fees, taxes, risk, units and assumptions. Use transparent illustrative calculations rather than personal recommendations or return promises.

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-051-037 | How can a cheap component stop an expensive production line? | Trace dependency, lead time, substitution and inventory decisions in a documented disruption. |
| BANK-051-038 | Why do manufacturers cluster in particular regions? | Compare skills, suppliers, infrastructure, institutions and historical investment using a specific industrial cluster. |
| BANK-051-039 | What changes when production is moved closer to customers? | Examine costs, lead times, supplier maturity and actual output rather than treating relocation announcements as completed capacity. |
| BANK-051-040 | How should a factory's claimed economic impact be evaluated? | Separate direct jobs, supplier effects, public subsidy, displacement and forecast assumptions using dated evidence. |

## 08. Environmental and historical legacies

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-051-041 | How can a manufacturing film compare environmental impacts across a product lifecycle? | Define extraction, production, use and end-of-life boundaries and mark unavailable data. |
| BANK-051-042 | What does an industrial brownfield reveal about a factory after closure? | Trace land use, contamination findings, redevelopment and community experience without diagnosing site safety from images. |
| BANK-051-043 | How do repair and remanufacturing differ from recycling materials? | Compare retained function, inspection, replacement and market conditions in a documented product system. |
| BANK-051-044 | Why should industrial heritage preserve workers' accounts as well as machines? | Combine oral histories, production records and physical remains while distinguishing memory from verified chronology. |
