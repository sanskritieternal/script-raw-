## 01. Medical evidence foundations

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Inspect current professional guidance and original studies for the exact population, intervention or measurement. Report comparators, absolute outcomes, uncertainty, harms and limits. Do not diagnose, prescribe or imply that education replaces professional care.

**Source types to locate:** current official clinical or public-health guidance; original trials, cohort studies or diagnostic-validation reports; study registrations, protocols and adverse-event records. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-026-013 | How does a clinical question determine the evidence needed to answer it? | Separate diagnosis, prognosis, prevention and treatment; compare suitable designs rather than one universal hierarchy used without context. |
| BANK-026-014 | What does a diagnostic test result mean before symptoms and baseline risk are considered? | Explain sensitivity, specificity and predictive values with illustrative figures; do not interpret an individual's result. |
| BANK-026-015 | How can two valid studies reach different conclusions about the same intervention? | Compare populations, protocols, comparators, outcomes, timing and uncertainty before claiming a contradiction. |
| BANK-026-016 | Why can a statistically significant result still be clinically unimportant? | Inspect absolute effects, meaningful thresholds, harms and patient priorities rather than the p-value alone. |

## 06. From research to care

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-026-033 | What does randomization accomplish and what does it leave uncontrolled? | Examine allocation, adherence, blinding, attrition and the actual comparison rather than calling every trial equally decisive. |
| BANK-026-034 | How does a surrogate endpoint differ from a patient-centered outcome? | Compare a defined biomarker with symptoms, function or survival and inspect evidence linking them. |
| BANK-026-035 | Why are adverse-event records essential even when an intervention shows benefit? | Compare severity, frequency, exposure and incomplete reporting instead of treating benefit as proof of safety. |
| BANK-026-036 | What can a case report reveal without establishing a treatment effect? | Distinguish hypothesis generation, unusual observations, comparison limits and publication selection. |

## 07. Institutions and ethics

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-026-037 | What does informed consent require beyond a signed form? | Use jurisdiction-appropriate standards to examine understanding, choice, capacity, alternatives and continuing communication. |
| BANK-026-038 | How should allocation of scarce organs be explained without reducing it to one ranking? | Compare official criteria, urgency, compatibility, waiting and ethical debates in a named system. |
| BANK-026-039 | What changes when clinical data is reused for research? | Inspect consent, governance, identifiability and access; do not assume removing a name makes data anonymous. |
| BANK-026-040 | How can a medical documentary film clinical work without compromising patient dignity? | Plan permission, privacy, vulnerability and withdrawal limits before recording care settings. |

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-026-041 | How can a medical animation simplify anatomy without creating a false mechanism? | Identify deliberate abstractions and review the causal sequence against appropriate references. |
| BANK-026-042 | Why should a symptom explainer avoid presenting a checklist as a diagnosis? | Distinguish common symptoms, overlapping conditions and the role of professional assessment. |
| BANK-026-043 | How can an episode explain clinical uncertainty without implying all treatments are guesses? | Separate established benefit, population differences, incomplete evidence and choices under uncertainty. |
| BANK-026-044 | What must be checked before using a patient story as evidence of improvement? | Verify consent, chronology, other interventions and objective records while preserving limits on causal inference. |
