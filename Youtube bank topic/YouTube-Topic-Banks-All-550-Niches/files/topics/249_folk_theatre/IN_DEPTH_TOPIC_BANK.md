## Detailed proposals

### Indian folk theatre

**Research standard:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These source types are not topic-specific sources already inspected.

#### BANK-249-001 — Nautanki performance structures

**Video question:** Nautanki performance structures: what do contemporary records establish, and what remains uncertain?

**Coverage boundary:** Primary context: Folk Theatre; branch: Indian folk theatre. Address Nautanki performance structures through one bounded case, source set or experiment. Exclude an all-purpose survey of the whole niche; distinguish the case's findings from broader claims.

**Deep-dive research outline:**

- Locate Nautanki performance structures in a bounded time and place; build a chronology from identified records.
- Compare at least two independent witnesses or explain why only one survives.
- Analyze institutions, material conditions and overlooked participants rather than attributing everything to a single figure.
- Separate documented conclusions, interpretive debates and gaps that a reconstruction cannot resolve.

### Stage practice

**Research standard:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These source types are not topic-specific sources already inspected.

#### BANK-249-005 — Improvised dialogue

**Video question:** Improvised dialogue: which creative choices change what the audience understands?

**Coverage boundary:** Primary context: Folk Theatre; branch: Stage practice. Address Improvised dialogue through one bounded case, source set or experiment. Exclude an all-purpose survey of the whole niche; distinguish the case's findings from broader claims.

**Deep-dive research outline:**

- Identify the audience, format and communication problem for Improvised dialogue.
- Annotate a specific scene, design or draft to show how the creative choice works.
- Compare a revised version and explain what information, feeling or interpretation changes.
- Check accessibility, rights, consent and possible misreadings before treating the example as a reusable method.

### Continuity

**Research standard:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These source types are not topic-specific sources already inspected.

#### BANK-249-008 — Touring economies

**Video question:** Touring economies: how do participants describe the practice and its differences?

**Coverage boundary:** Primary context: Folk Theatre; branch: Continuity. Address Touring economies through one bounded case, source set or experiment. Exclude an all-purpose survey of the whole niche; distinguish the case's findings from broader claims.

**Deep-dive research outline:**

- Choose a specific community and identify who participates in Touring economies and who may be excluded.
- Document the actual routines, resources and relationships involved through more than one perspective.
- Compare change across generations, locations or life circumstances without assuming uniformity.
- Return to participants for context and consent checks; distinguish their views from the narrator's interpretation.
