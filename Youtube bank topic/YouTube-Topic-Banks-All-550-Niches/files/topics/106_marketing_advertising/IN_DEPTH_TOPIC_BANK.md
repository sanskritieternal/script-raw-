**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

|---|---|---|

| BANK-106-011 | What makes a value proposition assessable rather than merely memorable? | Connect a defined customer need, specific capability and verifiable evidence; avoid implying a slogan proves superiority. |

## 05. Measurement, experiments and attribution

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-106-026 | What does an advertising attribution model assume about credit for a sale? | Read the actual platform methodology, lookback windows and channel visibility; distinguish assigned credit from causal incrementality. |
| BANK-106-027 | How can a holdout test examine whether advertising produced additional demand? | Specify assignment, spillovers, baseline, uncertainty and ethical data handling; avoid invented lift figures or overgeneralizing a small test. |
| BANK-106-028 | Why can acquisition cost look better when the wrong customer population is counted? | Define new versus returning customers, fees, cancellations and time horizon; use transparent illustrative calculations rather than reporting fabricated campaign results. |
| BANK-106-029 | What should an A/B creative test hold constant so the comparison is meaningful? | Compare audience, budget, exposure, timing and outcomes using published methods; distinguish a learning experiment from testing unlimited variants until one appears significant. |

## 06. Economics, retention and operations

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use current official terms, filings or macroeconomic data; state jurisdiction, date, fees, taxes, risk, units and assumptions. Use transparent illustrative calculations rather than personal recommendations or return promises.

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-106-030 | How should customer lifetime value be discussed when future retention is uncertain? | Show assumptions about margin, repeat behavior, discounting and costs in illustrative scenarios; avoid guaranteed payback or personalized investment claims. |
| BANK-106-031 | What changes when a campaign succeeds beyond the company's delivery capacity? | Map inventory, staffing, service promises, returns and complaint handling; distinguish demand generation from fulfilled customer value. |
| BANK-106-032 | Why can retaining customers require product or service change rather than more messages? | Use documented feedback, actual usage and operational evidence; avoid treating every departure as a communication problem. |
| BANK-106-033 | How can marketing budgets compare short-term response with longer-term brand effects? | Inspect original measurement methods, horizon, uncertainty and confounding; avoid claiming one universal budget split. |

## 07. Advertising rules and claim support

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-106-034 | What evidence should exist before an advertisement makes a comparative performance claim? | Use current regulator guidance, actual test conditions and the exact wording; distinguish a documented result from a broader superiority claim. |
| BANK-106-035 | How should health or financial advertising be handled differently from an ordinary product demonstration? | Identify jurisdiction, current official rules and substantiation requirements; exclude treatment, return or personal-benefit promises without appropriate evidence and authorization. |
| BANK-106-036 | What must a testimonial disclose when the featured outcome is unusual or incentivized? | Read current official endorsement guidance and verify the underlying account; avoid implying a selected story is typical. |
| BANK-106-037 | How can environmental marketing avoid a vague claim that hides its measurement boundary? | Specify material, lifecycle stage, baseline and verification; distinguish a narrow improvement from a complete sustainability verdict. |

| BANK-106-039 | What should be checked before presenting a viral campaign as a business success? | Compare actual costs, distribution, sales evidence, backlash and disclosed objectives; distinguish publicity from verified profit. |
| BANK-106-040 | How can a campaign case study separate the agency's contribution from product, price and seasonality? | Use dated primary records and explicit alternatives; avoid assigning every outcome to the most visually memorable advertisement. |
| BANK-106-041 | What does responsible correction look like when an advertisement's central claim changes? | Update prominent messaging, inform affected audiences as required and preserve the dated record; do not rely on a hidden footnote to reverse a misleading impression. |
