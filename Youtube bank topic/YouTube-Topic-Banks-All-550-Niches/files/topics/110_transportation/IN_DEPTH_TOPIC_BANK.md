## 01. Transport as access to activities

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-110-010 | How can a transport system be evaluated by access rather than vehicle movement alone? | Compare journeys to work, care, education and services using official data and consented accounts; distinguish speed from usable opportunity. |
| BANK-110-011 | What changes between measuring a trip and measuring a whole day's travel needs? | Map connected activities, transfers, waiting and care responsibilities; avoid treating one commute as every person's mobility pattern. |
| BANK-110-012 | Why can a short trip still be inaccessible? | Examine crossings, cost, surfaces, assistance, safety and information using user-defined needs; distinguish physical distance from practical access. |

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-110-013 | How does crossing design affect walking time and access? | Use official design guidance, observed conditions and consented user perspectives; avoid engineering conclusions from photographs alone. |
| BANK-110-014 | What makes a cycling network more than disconnected protected segments? | Map continuity, junctions, destinations and maintenance through public plans; distinguish installed length from a demonstrated useful network. |
| BANK-110-015 | How can street space be compared across movement, deliveries and public use? | Use measured dimensions, official policy and varied local accounts; avoid assuming one allocation is best for every place. |
| BANK-110-016 | Why can children and adults experience the same walking route differently? | Compare sightlines, crossings, time and accompaniment with appropriate consent; do not expose children's routines or treat one family as representative. |
