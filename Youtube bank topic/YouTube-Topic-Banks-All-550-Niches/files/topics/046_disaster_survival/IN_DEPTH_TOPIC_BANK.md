**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |

| BANK-046-027 | Why can warnings inside an organization fail to produce action? | Use records and inquiry findings to examine communication, authority and incentives rather than speculation about motives. |

## 06. Survivor-centered storytelling

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-046-033 | How can a survival story preserve the survivor's agency? | Use consented testimony, corroborated chronology and the person's chosen framing while avoiding sensational reenactment. |
| BANK-046-034 | What does survivor bias obscure in celebrated escape stories? | Compare the surviving accounts with broader exposure and loss data without assigning moral superiority to survival. |
| BANK-046-035 | How can a documentary discuss missing people without turning uncertainty into a mystery gimmick? | Use verified records, family preferences and clearly bounded unresolved questions. |
| BANK-046-036 | What can community networks explain about recovery after a disaster? | Compare mutual aid, local institutions and uneven access to assistance without replacing policy analysis with individual heroism. |
