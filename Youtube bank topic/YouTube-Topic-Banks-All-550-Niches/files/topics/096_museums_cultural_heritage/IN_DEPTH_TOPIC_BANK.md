## 01. Objects, collections and public purpose

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-096-013 | How does an object become part of a museum collection? | Trace acquisition, accession, identification and institutional purpose in a documented case; distinguish legal possession from complete provenance or community consent. |
| BANK-096-014 | What can a museum's founding collection reveal about its original priorities? | Compare founding documents, inventories, donors and exclusions; avoid treating the surviving collection as a neutral sample of a civilization. |
| BANK-096-015 | Why can the same object mean different things in a museum and its original setting? | Use provenance, attributed community accounts and display history; separate present interpretation from verified historical use. |
| BANK-096-016 | What distinguishes a museum, archive and living heritage site in their responsibilities? | Compare collection practices, public access and care obligations for named institutions; verify current definitions rather than imposing one global model. |

## 03. Interpretation, labels and exhibition design

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-096-021 | How can an exhibition label distinguish evidence from interpretation in a small space? | Compare original labels with dates, attribution and uncertainty visible; avoid presenting a contested identification as settled through confident wording. |
| BANK-096-022 | What does grouping objects by geography rather than material change about an exhibition? | Develop paired original layouts and explain relationships emphasized or hidden; distinguish curatorial framing from a universal classification. |
| BANK-096-023 | How can lighting and display height change what visitors can inspect? | Compare visibility, access and conservation requirements using institutional guidance; avoid assuming brighter display is always better for fragile materials. |
| BANK-096-024 | Why can a reconstructed room give a misleading impression of historical completeness? | Identify original objects, replicas, conjectural placement and missing contexts in a named exhibit; label reconstruction rather than suggesting untouched survival. |

## 04. Conservation, storage and access

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-096-025 | What happens to museum objects that are not currently displayed? | Trace cataloging, storage, study access and conservation through public institutional records; avoid interpreting nondisplay as deliberate suppression. |
| BANK-096-026 | How do preventive conservation and active treatment address different risks? | Compare monitoring, storage, handling and documented interventions in one material group; exclude household treatment recipes for historic objects. |
| BANK-096-027 | What does a loan agreement need to consider beyond permission to transport an object? | Inspect publicly available policies on condition, environment, insurance and responsibilities; distinguish a policy framework from legal advice for a transaction. |
| BANK-096-028 | How can a museum balance research access with an object's fragility? | Use condition evidence, handling protocols and surrogate images in a documented case; do not assume digitization eliminates all need for physical study. |

## 05. Living heritage and community authority

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-096-029 | What changes when an exhibition is co-curated with the people whose heritage it presents? | Trace decision authority, compensation, review and final wording in a consented case; distinguish consultation from shared control. |
| BANK-096-030 | How should a museum respond when a community identifies an item as restricted or sacred? | Use authorized community guidance and institutional policies on display, access and records; avoid exposing restricted knowledge to explain the restriction. |
| BANK-096-031 | How can oral histories enter a collection without losing the conditions under which they were shared? | Record consent, language, intended uses, access restrictions and withdrawal limits; distinguish preservation permission from unrestricted publication rights. |
| BANK-096-032 | Why can craft demonstrations require a different agreement from displaying historical craft objects? | Compare maker labor, knowledge rights, compensation and audience interaction; avoid treating living practitioners as anonymous illustrations of the past. |

## 07. Digital museums and visitor evidence

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Inspect the actual product version, official documentation, terms and privacy policies. Test claims under stated conditions and distinguish account permissions, data retention, measured functionality and marketing.

**Source types to locate:** official versioned documentation and release notes; dated privacy policies and service terms; reproducible product tests and original validation reports. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-096-036 | What information must accompany a 3D object model to make it useful for research? | Document scale, capture method, missing surfaces, processing and rights; distinguish an accessible visualization from exact material or structural evidence. |
| BANK-096-037 | How can a museum assess a digital tour beyond counting page views? | Compare task completion, accessibility, interpretation and consented visitor feedback; distinguish engagement metrics from demonstrated learning. |
| BANK-096-038 | What should a collection database disclose when an old attribution changes? | Retain dated records, reasons, supporting sources and uncertainty; avoid silently replacing history or treating every previous catalog entry as verified fact. |

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

|---|---|---|
