## 01. Meaning depends on context

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-060-013 | How does a symbol differ from a simple visual resemblance? | Compare established use, audience recognition and contextual evidence before proposing meaning. |
| BANK-060-014 | Why can one symbol carry incompatible meanings in different communities? | Trace specific uses, dates and interpreters rather than treating shape as a universal code. |
| BANK-060-015 | How do icons, indexes and conventional signs help explain visual communication? | Introduce a clearly attributed analytical framework with concrete examples and its limitations. |
| BANK-060-016 | What would establish that a hidden meaning was intentional? | Compare creator statements, contemporary sources, consistent design and plausible alternatives instead of relying on pattern discovery alone. |

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.
