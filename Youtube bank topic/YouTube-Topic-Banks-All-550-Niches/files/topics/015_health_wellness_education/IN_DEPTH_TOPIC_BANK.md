## 01. Understanding evidence

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Inspect current professional guidance and original studies for the exact population, intervention or measurement. Report comparators, absolute outcomes, uncertainty, harms and limits. Do not diagnose, prescribe or imply that education replaces professional care.

**Source types to locate:** current official clinical or public-health guidance; original trials, cohort studies or diagnostic-validation reports; study registrations, protocols and adverse-event records. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-015-013 | What is the difference between a symptom, a diagnosis and a risk factor? | Use a non-diagnostic example and show why the categories lead to different kinds of evidence and assessment. |
| BANK-015-014 | How can relative-risk reduction exaggerate the impression of a benefit? | Compare absolute risks, population, time and uncertainty with clearly illustrative numbers. |
| BANK-015-015 | Why can a screening test produce false positives in people without symptoms? | Explain prevalence, sensitivity, specificity and follow-up; avoid judging an individual's result. |
| BANK-015-016 | What does a normal reference range establish about a laboratory test? | Distinguish statistical ranges, clinical thresholds, variation and the need for context from a qualified professional. |

## 07. Digital health and misinformation

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Inspect the actual product version, official documentation, terms and privacy policies. Test claims under stated conditions and distinguish account permissions, data retention, measured functionality and marketing.

**Source types to locate:** official versioned documentation and release notes; dated privacy policies and service terms; reproducible product tests and original validation reports. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-015-037 | How can a symptom-checking application create both useful prompts and misleading confidence? | Examine validation, intended use, missing context and escalation language without testing it on actual private patient records. |
| BANK-015-038 | What data can a wellness application retain beyond the user's visible diary? | Read dated policies, permissions, integrations and deletion terms rather than relying on a private-mode label. |
| BANK-015-039 | Why does a compelling health testimonial not establish what caused the improvement? | Consider other care, natural variation, selection and missing comparison outcomes. |
| BANK-015-040 | How can an AI-generated health citation look credible while failing verification? | Check existence, relevant passages, population and conclusion before treating an answer as sourced. |

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
