## 01. Finding a place-based question

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-020-013 | What can Chand Baori reveal beyond the spectacle of its geometry? | Compare water access, architecture, patronage and local use with dated evidence and community perspectives. |
| BANK-020-014 | How should Rani ki Vav's iconography be connected to its architecture? | Use documented placement, identified forms and conservation context rather than assigning symbols from a generic chart. |
| BANK-020-015 | What makes Socotra's landscape scientifically distinctive without treating it as an untouched fantasy world? | Compare ecology, settlement, livelihoods and present pressures using local and research sources. |
| BANK-020-016 | How can Matera's cave dwellings be explained without turning former hardship into picturesque scenery? | Trace housing history, policy, conservation and residents' views alongside the visual setting. |

## 02. Research before travel

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use dated official access rules, operator schedules and local information. Separate general planning from time-sensitive availability; include accessibility, local perspectives and changing environmental conditions.

**Source types to locate:** official transport operators and destination authorities; current permit, visa and access rules; consented local accounts and independently verified travel costs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-020-017 | How can a travel film verify access to a lesser-visited site? | Check current official permissions, ownership, seasonal conditions and local arrangements rather than relying on an old itinerary. |
| BANK-020-018 | What should be confirmed before describing a destination as hidden or undiscovered? | Investigate residents, existing tourism and historical knowledge; do not erase people who already know and use the place. |
| BANK-020-019 | How can a place's popular origin story be checked? | Compare local accounts, inscriptions, archives and scholarship while respecting a legend's cultural meaning. |
| BANK-020-020 | What makes a location list misleading when travel times are omitted? | Build actual connections, realistic stops and uncertainty rather than grouping nearby-looking map points into an impossible day. |

## 03. Landscape interpretation

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-020-021 | How can a film explain a waterfall without presenting one season as permanent? | Compare catchment, rainfall, flow variation and current access; avoid turning a filmed moment into a year-round promise. |
| BANK-020-022 | What does a desert settlement reveal about water and seasonal mobility? | Use local hydrology and livelihoods rather than a barren-land stereotype. |
| BANK-020-023 | How can a cave destination be shown without confusing geology and folklore? | Explain formation evidence separately from attributed sacred or local narratives. |
| BANK-020-024 | What makes a mountain viewpoint's apparent isolation misleading? | Include roads, labor, settlements, land use and seasonal movement outside the framed image. |

## 04. Community-led reporting

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-020-025 | How can residents help define what a travel documentary should explain? | Use consented conversations about place, change and visitor behavior rather than asking only for colorful anecdotes. |
| BANK-020-026 | What should change when a community does not want a location publicized? | Consider privacy, sacred restrictions and ecological pressure; choose less identifying coverage where appropriate. |
| BANK-020-027 | How can a guide's expertise be credited beyond a brief introduction? | Attribute interpretation, translation and route knowledge throughout the story and agree on permitted use. |
| BANK-020-028 | How can tourism income be discussed without assuming every resident benefits? | Compare jobs, ownership, prices, access and conflicting local experiences. |

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.
