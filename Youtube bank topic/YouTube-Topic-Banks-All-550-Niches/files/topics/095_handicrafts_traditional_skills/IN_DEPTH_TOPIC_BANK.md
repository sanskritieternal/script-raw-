## 01. Craft knowledge, materials and makers

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-013 | How can a craft be documented as skilled work rather than only an attractive finished object? | Trace decisions, preparation, correction and use through consented maker accounts; distinguish visible hand movements from the full body of knowledge. |
| BANK-095-014 | What does a material's local name reveal and leave uncertain about its identity? | Compare maker terminology, scientific or trade identification and supply records; preserve language while avoiding unverified material or species claims. |
| BANK-095-015 | How can an apprentice's learning path differ from a short public demonstration? | Use consented accounts of repetition, feedback, access and responsibilities; avoid implying a filmed sequence qualifies the viewer to reproduce specialist work. |

## 02. Weaving, embroidery and textile traditions

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-016 | What makes a Banarasi brocade's production depend on design and loom preparation as well as weaving? | Use authorized workshop records, maker accounts and official geographical-indication descriptions; distinguish documented local forms from a universal process for every marketed textile. |
| BANK-095-017 | How can Kutch embroidery traditions be described without collapsing communities into one style? | Attribute names, makers, uses and histories with community permission; avoid publishing restricted patterns or inferring identity solely from a motif. |
| BANK-095-018 | What changes between a block-print design and its repeated impression on cloth? | Compare original drawing, block preparation, alignment and documented printing stages; exclude chemical recipes and preserve the distinction between craft demonstration and industrial manufacture. |
| BANK-095-019 | How can ikat's patterned yarn preparation be explained without treating every regional method as identical? | Use identified textiles, authorized process records and maker terminology; distinguish resist placement, weaving and later surface printing. |

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-020 | How does a pottery form connect the maker's tools with the vessel's intended use? | Compare shape, wall thickness, surface and user requirements through authorized studio examples; exclude kiln procedures and claims of food safety without appropriate testing. |
| BANK-095-021 | What can a terracotta tradition reveal about local clay, seasonal work and ritual use? | Choose a named region with consented makers and attributed sources; keep public craft knowledge separate from restricted religious meaning. |
| BANK-095-022 | How can stone carving be explained through tool marks and staged decisions? | Use professional records and safe documented examples of roughing, refinement and finish; exclude powered-tool instruction or hazardous material processing. |
| BANK-095-023 | Why does a mural tradition need attention to wall preparation and setting as well as imagery? | Compare authorized examples, supports, materials and community context; avoid treating a copied motif on a new surface as identical to the original practice. |

## 05. Fiber, basketry and everyday objects

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|

## 06. Livelihoods, institutions and changing markets

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use current official terms, filings or macroeconomic data; state jurisdiction, date, fees, taxes, risk, units and assumptions. Use transparent illustrative calculations rather than personal recommendations or return promises.

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-032 | How can an artisan's selling price be related to labor and costs without reducing the work to hourly wages alone? | Use consented or illustrative accounts of material, preparation, skill, overhead and selling fees; protect private finances and avoid inventing income claims. |
| BANK-095-033 | What does a craft cooperative change about ownership, bargaining and access to buyers? | Inspect governance, actual services, payments and varied member accounts in one documented case; distinguish collective branding from guaranteed equitable outcomes. |
| BANK-095-034 | How can a geographical-indication label be checked against the actual craft product? | Read the current official registration, authorized-use conditions and traceability records; distinguish geographic recognition from a universal quality or labor guarantee. |
| BANK-095-035 | What changes when craft sales move to online marketplaces? | Compare photography, logistics, returns, fees, imitation and maker control with dated platform terms; avoid promising digital access automatically improves income. |

## 07. Transmission, conservation and design exchange

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-036 | How can a design collaboration credit traditional knowledge and contemporary contributions separately? | Agree attribution, decision authority, compensation and rights with participating makers; do not assume a designer's brief transfers ownership of community-held patterns. |
| BANK-095-037 | What evidence supports a claim that a craft is disappearing? | Compare practitioner numbers, training, production, demand and local accounts with dated methods; avoid declaring extinction from the closure of one workshop. |
| BANK-095-038 | How can a museum support a living craft without freezing it into one historical form? | Compare acquisition, interpretation, maker participation and contemporary commissions in a named programme; distinguish preservation goals from control over current creative choices. |
| BANK-095-039 | Why can teaching a tradition to new participants require more than publishing instructions? | Discuss authority, apprenticeship, context, access and permission through consented accounts; respect restricted knowledge and avoid treating all craft practices as freely transferable. |

## 08. Rights and responsible craft media

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-095-040 | What should a creator obtain before selling images or patterns derived from a workshop visit? | Check maker permission, community authority, intended use and applicable rights; distinguish permission to observe from commercial reproduction rights. |
| BANK-095-041 | How can a craft video make labor visible without staging poverty for audience sympathy? | Center makers' choices, expertise, current work and agreed compensation; avoid exposing private hardship or presenting assistance as a condition of participation. |
| BANK-095-042 | What should an audience be told when a craft sequence combines several makers or days? | Disclose edited stages, substituted objects and different contributors; avoid implying one uninterrupted object history or attributing collective work to one person. |
