## 01. Sources for unheard music

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-013 | How can historians study music that predates sound recording? | Compare notation, instruments, treatises, images and descriptions while identifying what cannot be recovered. |
| BANK-043-014 | What does notation preserve and omit about a performance? | Compare pitch, rhythm, ornamentation, timbre and conventions in one documented tradition. |
| BANK-043-015 | Why can the earliest surviving recording misrepresent a musical tradition? | Examine recording access, duration limits, commercial selection and performer choice. |
| BANK-043-016 | How should oral transmission be documented without assuming it is unchanged? | Compare teacher lineages, remembered variants and contextual evidence with practitioners' accounts. |

## 03. Instruments and technology

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-021 | How can instrument construction influence musical possibilities? | Compare a defined design change, playing technique and documented repertoire rather than assuming technology alone creates style. |
| BANK-043-022 | What can surviving historical instruments tell us about tuning? | Use physical measurements, texts and reproduction experiments while distinguishing original condition from later repair. |
| BANK-043-023 | How did amplification change the balance between singers and ensembles? | Examine microphone practice, venues and arrangement choices in a specific performance tradition. |
| BANK-043-024 | Why did the length of recording media affect song structure? | Compare format constraints, editing and actual release examples without attributing every short song to technology. |

## 04. Patronage and musical labor

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-025 | How did musicians adapt when court patronage weakened? | Trace a bounded set of careers across teaching, recording, theatre and public performance. |
| BANK-043-026 | What does the history of hereditary musical communities reveal about credit? | Compare labor, social status, transmission and later institutional recognition using named perspectives. |
| BANK-043-027 | How did women performers navigate changing ideas of respectability? | Examine documented careers, publicity and institutional access without repeating stigmatizing narratives as fact. |
| BANK-043-028 | How did radio auditions and grading shape professional opportunity? | Investigate published policies, performer accounts and repertory effects in a defined broadcasting institution. |

**Source types to locate:** primary texts in traceable editions; authorized public teaching and ritual descriptions; consented community accounts and critical historical studies. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |

| BANK-043-030 | What changed when a musical form traveled with migrant communities? | Compare instruments, venues, audiences and local collaboration across two documented settings. |
| BANK-043-031 | How can musical borrowing be distinguished from shared older practice? | Compare chronology, contact evidence and musical structure before assigning an origin. |
| BANK-043-032 | What can one song's changing versions reveal about regional identity? | Build a dated version history with credits and context rather than ranking versions by authenticity. |

## 06. Popular music industries

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-033 | How did film studios organize the work behind a song? | Separate composition, lyrics, orchestration, singing, recording and on-screen performance using credited examples. |
| BANK-043-034 | How did playback singing alter the relationship between voice and screen persona? | Compare production practice, publicity and audience recognition in a specific period. |
| BANK-043-035 | Why did cassette circulation create new regional music markets? | Investigate production cost, distribution, local content and surviving sales evidence with uncertainty marked. |
| BANK-043-036 | How do music charts shape the history that later documentaries remember? | Compare eligibility, coverage and measurement methods rather than equating chart position with all listening. |

## 07. Preservation and attribution

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Inspect the actual product version, official documentation, terms and privacy policies. Test claims under stated conditions and distinguish account permissions, data retention, measured functionality and marketing.

**Source types to locate:** official versioned documentation and release notes; dated privacy policies and service terms; reproducible product tests and original validation reports. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-037 | What makes a historical recording restoration faithful to its source? | Compare noise reduction, speed correction, missing information and clearly labeled editorial choices. |
| BANK-043-038 | How should uncertain composer attributions be presented? | Compare earliest documentation, oral attribution, later catalogues and plausible alternatives. |
| BANK-043-039 | Why can an archive contain the sound but lack the performers' names? | Investigate collection records, metadata loss and opportunities for community identification. |
| BANK-043-040 | What does digitization preserve while leaving rights and access unsettled? | Separate copying, cataloguing, consent, ownership claims and actual listener availability. |

## 08. Listening and historical claims

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-043-041 | How can a documentary compare musical systems without ranking their complexity? | Define the feature being compared and explain different analytical vocabularies using short permitted examples. |
| BANK-043-042 | What evidence would support a claim that one genre influenced another? | Combine musician testimony, dated recordings, collaboration and structural comparison rather than resemblance alone. |
| BANK-043-043 | How do nostalgia and reissues change a period's musical reputation? | Compare contemporary reception, later selection and missing material instead of treating memory as a direct popularity measure. |
| BANK-043-044 | How should a music-history film use excerpts responsibly and analytically? | Plan brief illustrative passages, accurate credits and an explanation of each excerpt's evidentiary purpose. |
