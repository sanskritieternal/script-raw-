## 01. Authority, responsibility and leadership claims

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-103-010 | What distinguishes formal authority from influence in a working team? | Compare decision rights, expertise, trust and accountability through consented or fictional cases; avoid treating a job title as proof of leadership quality. |
| BANK-103-011 | How can a leader's stated values be compared with actual decisions? | Trace resource allocation, incentives and documented tradeoffs; distinguish a public slogan from observed practice without guessing private motives. |
| BANK-103-012 | Why can a team's success be difficult to attribute to its leader alone? | Compare selection, resources, inherited conditions and contributors; avoid a single-hero account built from retrospective praise. |

## 02. Goals, delegation and coordination

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-103-013 | How can a manager delegate an outcome without hiding critical constraints? | Use a fictional assignment with authority, dependencies, resources and review points; distinguish delegation from transferring blame without control. |
| BANK-103-014 | What makes a team goal measurable without letting the metric replace the purpose? | Compare objective, indicator, unintended incentives and quality checks in an original project; avoid treating a target's achievement as complete success. |
| BANK-103-015 | Why can unclear decision ownership create repeated meetings? | Map proposal, consultation, approval and escalation in a bounded workflow; distinguish a useful responsibility model from a universal reorganization recipe. |
| BANK-103-016 | How can coordination change when tasks depend tightly on one another? | Compare handoffs, feedback speed, shared information and failure recovery; avoid assuming every team needs the same level of process. |

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-103-021 | What does psychological-safety research measure in actual teams? | Inspect original scales, study designs, outcomes and context; distinguish speaking-up climate from absence of disagreement or accountability. |
| BANK-103-022 | How can disagreement about a task become a personal conflict? | Compare observable communication, roles and resource pressures in a fictional case; avoid diagnosing personalities or inferring malicious intent. |
| BANK-103-023 | What should a leader investigate when team members stop raising problems? | Examine response history, incentives, access and consented accounts; distinguish possible causes from conclusions based on silence alone. |
| BANK-103-024 | How can a team repair a failed handoff without beginning with a search for a culprit? | Reconstruct expectations, information, timing and system conditions; preserve individual accountability where supported rather than assuming either system or person explains everything. |

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-103-029 | How can leadership opportunities be evaluated for unequal access rather than only unequal outcomes? | Inspect assignment criteria, sponsorship, workload and official records with privacy protections; avoid inferring discrimination in a real case without evidence. |
| BANK-103-030 | What should a leader clarify when a conflict of interest may affect a decision? | Use current organizational policies and applicable official rules; distinguish disclosure, recusal and independent review without providing individual legal advice. |
| BANK-103-031 | Why can requesting candor be insufficient when the listener controls promotion or pay? | Examine power, anonymity limits, retaliation protections and consented experiences; do not promise a survey guarantees safety. |
| BANK-103-032 | How can ethical leadership be assessed when commercial targets conflict with service obligations? | Trace documented duties, choices, affected groups and oversight; avoid reducing a real dispute to an inspirational quotation. |

## 08. Measurement and critical leadership literacy

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-103-037 | How can a leadership-training programme be evaluated beyond participant satisfaction? | Compare behavior, team outcomes, selection, controls and follow-up in original evaluations; avoid attributing all later performance to the course. |
| BANK-103-038 | What is missing when a leadership lesson is drawn only from successful executives? | Inspect survivorship, context, inherited advantages and contradictory cases; distinguish a useful anecdote from a tested rule. |
| BANK-103-039 | How can a biography-based episode credit followers and institutions alongside the leader? | Use primary records, multiple perspectives and specific decisions; avoid inventing private dialogue or treating influence as proof of moral virtue. |
