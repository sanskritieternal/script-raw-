## 01. Maps as models

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

|---|---|---|

| BANK-038-015 | What makes a geographic center depend on the question being asked? | Define boundaries, weighting and calculation rather than announcing one universal center. |

## 05. Historical cartography

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |

| BANK-038-031 | Why did early maps include places their makers had never observed? | Trace reports, inherited authorities, speculation and visual convention. |
| BANK-038-032 | How can a mapping error persist across many later maps? | Follow copying, source credibility and the evidence that eventually corrected the feature. |

## 06. Remote observation

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-038-033 | What can satellite imagery establish about a landscape without a field visit? | Specify resolution, bands, dates, processing and ground-truth limits. |
| BANK-038-034 | How can shadows or perspective create a false structure in an aerial image? | Compare lighting, terrain, multiple views and alternative explanations. |
| BANK-038-035 | Why can two elevation models give different heights for the same place? | Check reference surfaces, resolution, interpolation and actual measurement method. |
| BANK-038-036 | What makes a before-and-after map comparison valid? | Align scale, date, definitions and data quality rather than relying on visual contrast alone. |

## 07. Geographic records and claims

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-038-037 | How can a highest-point claim change when definitions change? | Separate summit elevation, prominence, base measurement and agreed reference systems. |
| BANK-038-038 | What does official recognition add to a geographic name? | Distinguish local use, historical names, administrative standards and contested naming. |
| BANK-038-039 | Why can a surprising map fact be true only under a hidden definition? | State the exact categories and test near-counterexamples rather than repeating a trivia slogan. |
| BANK-038-040 | How should a channel investigate a claimed magnetic hill? | Compare measured slope, visual cues, instruments and known physics instead of inferring an exotic force from motion alone. |

## 08. Visual explanation

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-038-041 | How can animation explain a border's history without making change seem inevitable? | Show dated decisions, uncertainty and local consequences rather than a single smooth expansion graphic. |
| BANK-038-042 | What makes a choropleth map misleading when raw counts are used? | Compare population, area, rates, classification and the question the map claims to answer. |
| BANK-038-043 | How should a map distinguish observed data from an interpolated surface? | Label coverage, method and uncertainty so smooth color does not imply dense measurement. |
| BANK-038-044 | What should be corrected when a geographic video uses an outdated boundary or name? | Identify the relevant date, source and changed claim without silently substituting a new map. |
