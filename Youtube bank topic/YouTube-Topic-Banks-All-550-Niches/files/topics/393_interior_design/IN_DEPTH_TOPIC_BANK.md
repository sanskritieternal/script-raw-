It is not exhaustive, and has no fixed topic limit. The suggested research questions and outlines use domain-specific editorial patterns; they are proposals to refine against actual sources, not evidence that the research has been done.

The IDs refer to catalog entries, not episodes produced. Angles are deliberately unassigned until the actual niche angle scope and selected case are reviewed. ### Space planning

**Research standard:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These source types are not topic-specific sources already inspected.

### Use and access

**Research standard:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These source types are not topic-specific sources already inspected.

#### BANK-393-007 — Multigenerational interiors

**Video question:** Multigenerational interiors: how do participants describe the practice and its differences?

**Coverage boundary:** Primary context: Interior Design; branch: Use and access. Address Multigenerational interiors through one bounded case, source set or experiment. Exclude an all-purpose survey of the whole niche; distinguish the case's findings from broader claims.

**Deep-dive research outline:**

- Choose a specific community and identify who participates in Multigenerational interiors and who may be excluded.
- Document the actual routines, resources and relationships involved through more than one perspective.
- Compare change across generations, locations or life circumstances without assuming uniformity.
- Return to participants for context and consent checks; distinguish their views from the narrator's interpretation.
