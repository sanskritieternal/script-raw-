## 01. Organizations and the experience of work

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-010 | What does an organization chart explain and fail to explain about how work gets done? | Compare formal reporting, informal expertise, dependencies and decision paths in a fictional organization; avoid revealing confidential internal structures. |
| BANK-104-011 | How can a workplace culture claim be investigated through routine practices? | Examine meetings, incentives, onboarding, feedback and consented accounts; distinguish published values from actual experience without generalizing one employee's story. |
| BANK-104-012 | Why can two employees experience the same workplace very differently? | Compare role, manager, schedule, access and employment arrangement with ethical accounts; avoid treating one perspective as representative of all staff. |

## 02. Hiring, onboarding and internal mobility

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-013 | What should a fair hiring process document beyond the final selection? | Use current official equality guidance, stated criteria, scoring and records; avoid judging an individual discrimination claim or exposing candidates. |
| BANK-104-014 | How can onboarding reveal the difference between policy access and useful support? | Trace account setup, role expectations, training and help channels in a fictional first month; do not equate a completed checklist with readiness. |
| BANK-104-015 | Why can internal job opportunities remain inaccessible despite being formally open? | Investigate notice, eligibility, manager release, networks and consented experiences; distinguish documented barriers from unsupported intent claims. |
| BANK-104-016 | How should a probation process communicate expectations before the final review? | Inspect current local rules and actual policy on goals, evidence, support and notice; avoid predicting a worker's rights or outcome. |

## 03. Meetings, workflow and organizational friction

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-017 | How can a recurring meeting be evaluated against the decision or coordination it exists to support? | Map inputs, participants, outputs and follow-through in an original example; avoid assuming all meetings are wasteful or replaceable by chat. |
| BANK-104-018 | What can a workflow map reveal about work that repeatedly returns for correction? | Trace handoffs, acceptance criteria, missing information and rework; distinguish a process observation from assigning personal blame. |
| BANK-104-019 | Why can a shared inbox hide responsibility rather than distribute it? | Compare ownership, status, access and escalation in a fictional support team; exclude real private messages from demonstrations. |
| BANK-104-020 | How can documentation remain current when the people doing the work are already busy? | Define ownership, update triggers, actual use and minimal useful detail; avoid treating a document repository's size as evidence of knowledge transfer. |

## 04. Pay, appraisal and workplace rules

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use current official terms, filings or macroeconomic data; state jurisdiction, date, fees, taxes, risk, units and assumptions. Use transparent illustrative calculations rather than personal recommendations or return promises.

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-021 | What does a compensation structure include beyond salary bands? | Compare base pay, variable pay, benefits, progression and eligibility using dated official policy or illustrative figures; avoid individualized tax or negotiation advice. |
| BANK-104-022 | How can performance ratings become inconsistent across managers? | Inspect criteria, evidence, calibration, opportunities and documented disagreement; avoid inferring bias from a single unexplained rating. |
| BANK-104-023 | Why can a bonus target change behavior in unintended ways? | Use original organizational research and a transparent fictional incentive scheme; distinguish plausible gaming from an allegation against real employees. |
| BANK-104-024 | What should employees be able to find in a leave or flexible-work policy? | Read current local law and actual policy for eligibility, procedure and review; do not assume every request has the same legal status. |

## 05. Health, safety and workplace access

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Inspect current professional guidance and original studies for the exact population, intervention or measurement. Report comparators, absolute outcomes, uncertainty, harms and limits. Do not diagnose, prescribe or imply that education replaces professional care.

**Source types to locate:** current official clinical or public-health guidance; original trials, cohort studies or diagnostic-validation reports; study registrations, protocols and adverse-event records. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-025 | How can a workplace-wellbeing programme distinguish support from monitoring? | Compare purpose, data, consent, confidentiality and qualified guidance; avoid collecting real health records or promising a benefit from participation. |
| BANK-104-026 | What does an ergonomic assessment need beyond a photograph of a desk? | Use current occupational-health guidance on tasks, duration, equipment and individual needs; exclude diagnosing pain or prescribing a single posture. |
| BANK-104-027 | How can a workplace episode discuss harassment reporting without promising a particular outcome? | Use current official guidance, policy, confidentiality limits and support routes; protect identities and avoid investigating an individual's allegation through public content. |
| BANK-104-028 | Why can an accessibility policy fail at the level of daily work? | Compare actual tools, facilities, communication and consented user experience; distinguish policy intent from usable access and leave eligibility decisions to appropriate processes. |

## 06. Hybrid work, monitoring and technology

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Inspect the actual product version, official documentation, terms and privacy policies. Test claims under stated conditions and distinguish account permissions, data retention, measured functionality and marketing.

**Source types to locate:** official versioned documentation and release notes; dated privacy policies and service terms; reproducible product tests and original validation reports. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-029 | What should a workplace disclose before introducing productivity-monitoring software? | Inspect actual collection, purpose, access, retention and current official rules; distinguish activity measures from evidence of useful work. |
| BANK-104-030 | How can hybrid attendance rules affect coordination differently across roles? | Compare task dependencies, facilities, travel and documented team needs; avoid claiming one schedule is universally optimal. |
| BANK-104-031 | What should be checked before employees use an AI tool with work material? | Read organizational policy, service terms, data handling and permission scope; use fictional content and exclude confidential uploads. |
| BANK-104-032 | Why can introducing a collaboration tool increase communication burden? | Map duplicated channels, notification rules, search and decision records in a bounded workflow; avoid blaming users before examining system design. |

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-033 | How can a merger's effects be studied beyond its public financial announcement? | Trace role changes, systems, customers and consented worker accounts over time; distinguish announced synergies from measured outcomes. |
| BANK-104-034 | What do layoffs reveal about organizational decisions without defining the worth of affected workers? | Use official notices, stated criteria, applicable rules and consented accounts; avoid rumors or assumptions about individual performance. |

## 08. Workplace storytelling and evidence

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-104-037 | How can a day-in-the-office video represent work without exposing private information? | Use authorized spaces, fictional screens, staff consent and clear staging disclosures; distinguish a promotional visit from an independent workplace audit. |
| BANK-104-038 | What should a company case study disclose about who supplied the evidence? | Identify sponsorship, document access, selected interviewees and missing perspectives; avoid presenting an employer's account as independent verification. |
| BANK-104-039 | How can a workplace series track a policy change over time? | Record baseline, implementation, exceptions and consented experiences at defined points; separate changed opinions from measured operational results. |
