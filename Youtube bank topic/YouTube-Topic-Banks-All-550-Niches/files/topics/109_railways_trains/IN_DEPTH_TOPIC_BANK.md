**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-109-022 | What does an accessible rail journey require beyond a lift at the origin station? | Check current official information on paths, platforms, boarding, assistance and destination access; start from the traveler's stated needs rather than a generic symbol. |
| BANK-109-023 | Why can station design affect how long trains spend at platforms? | Compare passenger flows, doors, level changes and crowd management through official studies; avoid filming sensitive security areas or staging congestion. |
| BANK-109-024 | How can ticketing integration change a journey involving several operators? | Inspect dated official fares, transfer rules, payment and exclusions; distinguish a shared ticket medium from a through-fare guarantee. |
| BANK-109-025 | What can passenger-information systems do when the exact recovery time is unknown? | Compare verified status, uncertainty, alternatives and update frequency using public operator communications; avoid inventing live service advice. |

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-109-026 | How did a named railway corridor change access to markets in its region? | Use dated traffic, maps, economic records and local accounts; distinguish association from proof that rail alone caused growth. |
| BANK-109-027 | What makes rail freight competitive for some loads and difficult for others? | Compare terminals, distance, shipment size, handling and service reliability with official data; avoid universal cost or emissions claims. |
| BANK-109-028 | How can a colonial railway history include labor and land acquisition alongside engineering? | Use primary records, attributed scholarship and affected community accounts; distinguish documented impacts from a single progress-or-exploitation narrative. |
| BANK-109-029 | Why can a preserved locomotive tell a different story from the network it once served? | Compare object history, routes, labor and infrastructure; avoid treating a restored machine as representative of every operating era. |

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-109-034 | How can a new rail project's forecast be compared with its later use? | Align demand, cost, opening scope, fares and measurement period using official appraisals; distinguish a missed forecast from a complete public-value verdict. |
| BANK-109-035 | What does electrification change beyond replacing a train's onboard engine? | Compare infrastructure, power supply, maintenance, operating flexibility and documented costs; avoid assuming every route has the same economics. |
| BANK-109-036 | How should high-speed rail be compared with air travel for a specific corridor? | Define door-to-door time, occupancy, fares, infrastructure and emissions boundaries; verify current services rather than ranking modes universally. |
| BANK-109-037 | Why can rail fares cover only part of a system's public financing? | Read official accounts on operating costs, capital, subsidy and policy goals; avoid treating farebox recovery as the sole performance measure. |
