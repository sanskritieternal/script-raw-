## 01. Food history and evidence

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-013 | How can a dish's history be investigated when several regions claim it? | Compare dated recipes, names, trade and migration evidence without turning cultural attachment into proof of exclusive origin. |
| BANK-024-014 | What changes when a recipe first enters print? | Examine audience, measurements, standardization and earlier oral practice rather than equating publication with invention. |
| BANK-024-015 | How did the Columbian Exchange alter selected Indian regional cuisines? | Trace specific ingredients, adoption periods and local adaptation rather than presenting one uniform national change. |
| BANK-024-016 | Why can a menu be a historical source and a marketing performance at the same time? | Compare pricing, language, audience and other records before inferring everyday eating habits. |

## 03. Household knowledge

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-021 | How can a cook's tacit knowledge be documented without replacing it with false precision? | Record texture, smell, timing and judgment while distinguishing observed measures from a universal recipe. |
| BANK-024-022 | What does intergenerational disagreement about a recipe reveal? | Compare migration, ingredients, memory and values rather than deciding one family member alone holds authenticity. |
| BANK-024-023 | How do seasonal shortages shape traditional preservation practices? | Use one community's materials, climate and livelihood context without converting historical methods into unverified safety advice. |
| BANK-024-024 | How can a film credit domestic labor that a famous dish depends on? | Include preparation, care work and contributors' choices instead of focusing only on the public-serving moment. |

**Source types to locate:** primary texts in traceable editions; authorized public teaching and ritual descriptions; consented community accounts and critical historical studies. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |

| BANK-024-026 | What changes when festival food moves from households to commercial production? | Compare labor, ingredients, scale and meaning using a specific festival and region. |
| BANK-024-027 | How should dietary restrictions be explained within a community's actual diversity? | Distinguish texts, institutions and individual practice without assigning uniform beliefs to all members. |
| BANK-024-028 | How can sacred-food filming respect access and participation rules? | Verify consent, permitted recording and context rather than treating ritual meals as ordinary restaurant footage. |

## 05. Ingredients and techniques

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-029 | What does fermentation change in a named food process? | Separate organism, substrate, conditions and measured outcomes; do not infer universal health benefits from the label. |
| BANK-024-030 | How do millet dishes vary with grain properties and regional technique? | Compare named grains, preparation and texture rather than treating millet as one interchangeable ingredient. |
| BANK-024-031 | Why can two versions of a bread behave differently with the same written quantities? | Inspect flour, hydration, mixing, temperature and measurement before attributing the result to cook skill alone. |
| BANK-024-032 | How can flavor descriptions be grounded in ingredients and process? | Separate sensory impressions, chemical explanation and cultural vocabulary without pretending taste is wholly objective. |

## 06. Restaurants and public food

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use current official terms, filings or macroeconomic data; state jurisdiction, date, fees, taxes, risk, units and assumptions. Use transparent illustrative calculations rather than personal recommendations or return promises.

**Source types to locate:** regulator or official statistical publications; actual contracts, fee schedules and audited filings; original economic studies and disclosed calculation inputs. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-033 | How does a restaurant adapt a regional cuisine for unfamiliar customers? | Compare menu design, ingredient access, pricing and the owners' intentions without assuming adaptation is deception. |
| BANK-024-034 | What lies behind the price of a street-food serving? | Trace ingredients, rent, fuel, labor and waste using a transparent example rather than a generic profit claim. |
| BANK-024-035 | How can tourism change the dishes most visible in a destination? | Compare residents' meals, tourist menus and commercial incentives over a defined period. |
| BANK-024-036 | What can a restaurant's hygiene rating establish and leave uncertain? | Use current jurisdiction-specific criteria and inspection scope rather than judging safety from an attractive kitchen video. |

## 07. Representation and inequality

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Use the current official legal text, policy, procedure or public dataset for a specific jurisdiction and date. Distinguish written rules, implementation and lived access; give no personal legal or financial conclusion.

**Source types to locate:** official laws, rules and administrative guidance; audits, judgments and public service records; consented accounts from affected people and qualified specialists. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-037 | How can food histories acknowledge caste and class without reducing people to categories? | Use specific sources and consented accounts; examine access, labor and exclusion rather than assuming universal experience. |
| BANK-024-038 | When does a food documentary exoticize a familiar meal for its makers? | Compare narration, camera choices, participant agency and the implied outsider norm. |
| BANK-024-039 | How can creators discuss cultural borrowing with attention to credit and power? | Distinguish adaptation, attribution, commercial benefit and relationships instead of offering one universal ownership rule. |
| BANK-024-040 | What should be checked before describing a food as ancient or unchanged? | Trace ingredients, techniques and records; separate continuity of name from an identical recipe over centuries. |

## 08. Research and production

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-024-041 | How should a recipe demonstration separate household tradition from general instruction? | Name the cook, region and variation; verify safety-sensitive steps before presenting them as transferable guidance. |
| BANK-024-042 | What makes an origin claim suitable for a title rather than a discussion of uncertainty? | Require traceable evidence and accurate qualifiers instead of using a disputed superlative as a certainty. |
| BANK-024-043 | How can a cooking scene preserve a contributor's voice through translation? | Retain register, explanations and unresolved terms rather than flattening expertise into generic subtitles. |
| BANK-024-044 | What can a food episode conclude when the historical origin remains uncertain? | Explain documented development, competing evidence and contemporary meaning without manufacturing a winner. |
