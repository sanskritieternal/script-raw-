## 01. Reading clothes as designed objects

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-082-013 | How can a garment be analyzed through cut, material and use rather than a trend label? | Inspect an owned garment's shape, seams, drape and movement; distinguish construction evidence from a guess about the designer's intention. |
| BANK-082-014 | What changes when a clothing silhouette is seen on a moving body? | Compare original static and movement observations with fit, ease and fabric behavior; avoid treating one body as the standard for everyone. |
| BANK-082-015 | Why can the same clothing item communicate different things in different settings? | Use attributed accounts of work, ceremony and leisure in named communities; avoid assigning fixed meanings to colors or garments across cultures. |

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-082-016 | How do fiber identity and fabric structure answer different questions about clothing? | Compare cotton fiber, woven cloth, knitted cloth and finishing using manufacturer or textile references; do not infer fabric performance from fiber percentage alone. |
| BANK-082-017 | What can a seam reveal about a garment's likely repair options? | Examine allowance, stitching, access and lining on an owned example; distinguish observable construction from a guarantee of durability. |
| BANK-082-018 | Why can stretch affect fit even when two garments have the same labeled measurements? | Compare fabric recovery, direction of stretch, cut and wearing ease; use controlled measurements instead of universal size assumptions. |
| BANK-082-019 | How should claims about breathable or moisture-wicking fabric be evaluated? | Inspect the specified test, conditions, finish and intended activity; distinguish laboratory properties from comfort for every wearer. |

## 03. Fit, sizing and clothing access

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use consented participant accounts, local records and the community's own descriptions. Include differences of position, access and power; testimony is not a representative survey or clinical proof.

**Source types to locate:** consented interviews and oral histories; community publications and local records; field notes with method and consent documented. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-082-020 | Why do clothing sizes differ across brands and regions? | Compare current size charts, measurement definitions and target fits; avoid treating a label as a stable body category or a health measure. |
| BANK-082-021 | How can a fitting focus on the wearer's tasks instead of correcting their body? | Use consented feedback about sitting, reaching, fastening and comfort; distinguish alteration choices from judgments about appearance or ability. |
| BANK-082-022 | What design details can make dressing easier for people with different access needs? | Investigate closures, reach, sensory preferences and care requirements with consented users; avoid assuming adaptive labels fit every disability. |
| BANK-082-023 | How can alterations extend a garment's usefulness without promising every fit problem is repairable? | Map seam allowance, fabric, proportion, cost and skilled assessment in an owned example; exclude unsafe machine use and unsupported tailoring shortcuts. |

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| BANK-082-025 | What does a kimono's history reveal about changing production and social use? | Compare dated garments, museum records and attributed Japanese scholarship; distinguish historical periods from a timeless national costume image. |
| BANK-082-026 | How did uniforms negotiate authority, work and identity in one institution? | Trace regulations, surviving garments and wearer accounts; avoid inferring actual compliance or personal belief from the written dress code. |
| BANK-082-027 | How can fashion-history images conceal the clothing of people outside elite archives? | Compare portraiture, work photographs, household records and surviving textiles with their biases; distinguish the most visible examples from typical daily dress. |

## 07. Sustainability, commerce and marketing

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-082-036 | What evidence is required before calling a textile sustainable? | Define lifecycle boundary, durability, care, resource use and end-of-life assumptions; compare actual data rather than relying on a single preferred fiber. |
| BANK-082-037 | How can an environmental certification be read without treating its logo as a complete product verdict? | Review the standard's scope, chain-of-custody and claim rules; distinguish certified attributes from labor, longevity or climate questions left unassessed. |
| BANK-082-038 | What does cost per wear reveal and fail to reveal about a purchase? | Work through clearly illustrative price, use and repair scenarios; acknowledge uncertainty, access and nonfinancial needs rather than prescribing a shopping budget. |
| BANK-082-039 | How should a fashion creator disclose affiliate links and gifted clothing? | Identify the commercial relationship and applicable local rules in concrete content examples; avoid presenting disclosure as evidence that the review is automatically independent. |
