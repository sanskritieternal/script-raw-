## 01. Everyday physical questions

**Level:** foundation. **Evidence direction:** Use original observations or experiments, an appropriate scientific reference and the actual measurement method. Distinguish mechanisms, correlations, model assumptions, uncertainty and limits of generalization.

**Source types to locate:** original research reports and data; research-institution observation records; methods papers and current scientific reference works. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-090-013 | Why does a metal spoon often feel colder than a wooden spoon in the same room? | Compare heat transfer, material properties and initial conditions using a safe observation; distinguish touch sensation from evidence that the objects have different starting temperatures. |
| BANK-090-014 | Why can a puddle disappear on a day that is not especially hot? | Investigate evaporation, airflow, humidity and surface area with recorded conditions; avoid assuming temperature alone explains the rate. |
| BANK-090-015 | Why does a straw appear bent where it enters water? | Explain refraction with a labeled ray diagram and safe original observations; distinguish optical appearance from the straw physically changing shape. |
| BANK-090-016 | Why can a spinning wheel seem to reverse direction on video? | Compare sampling, periodic motion, frame rate and lighting through an original animation; do not infer actual reverse motion from recorded appearance. |
| BANK-090-017 | Why does condensation form on the outside of a cold glass? | Trace water vapor, surface temperature and dew-point conditions; distinguish ambient moisture from liquid passing through the container. |

## 04. Technology and designed systems

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Use the actual system documentation, a worked implementation and measurements. Identify inputs, outputs, constraints, tolerances, failure conditions and the limits of the example. Do not provide hazardous construction or unauthorized access procedures.

**Source types to locate:** original engineering papers; official versioned product or standards documentation; test reports and drawings with clear provenance. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|

| BANK-090-027 | Why can a video look sharp while its file remains relatively small? | Compare compression, redundancy, bitrate, content and viewing conditions using original clips; separate perceived quality from exact recovery of the source. |
| BANK-090-028 | Why can an elevator button light up before the elevator begins to move? | Explain request registration, scheduling and system state through official diagrams; do not infer a malfunction or reveal access to control systems. |
| BANK-090-029 | Why can a microwave heat different parts of the same meal unevenly? | Compare field distribution, food composition, geometry and manufacturer guidance; keep food-safety claims authoritative and exclude appliance modifications. |

## 05. Language, history and institutions

**Level:** intermediate. **Evidence direction:** Compare dated primary records, material evidence and historical scholarship. Distinguish event dates, the dates of surviving witnesses, later retellings and disagreement. Do not invent quotations, intentions or an unbroken lineage.

**Source types to locate:** dated archival records and original publications; inscriptions, artifacts or photographs with provenance; critical editions and historical scholarship. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-090-030 | Why can a place keep an old name after its political boundaries change? | Trace official naming records, language use and local accounts for one named location; distinguish historical explanation from endorsing a contested territorial claim. |
| BANK-090-031 | Why do some languages assign grammatical gender to objects? | Compare documented grammar systems and historical evidence in named languages; avoid treating grammatical categories as proof of speakers' beliefs about real-world gender. |
| BANK-090-032 | Why can the same historical event have different starting dates in different accounts? | Inspect definitions, calendars, geographic scope and primary records; distinguish genuine periodization choices from demonstrable dating errors. |
| BANK-090-033 | Why do public institutions sometimes use procedures that appear slower than a direct personal decision? | Choose one documented process and examine accountability, consistency, access and legal duties; avoid assuming every delay is justified or corrupt. |

## 07. Asking better why questions

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Define the rival claims and their evidence standards. Compare sources on equal terms, separate empirical findings from values or interpretations and do not imply that all positions have equal evidential support.

**Source types to locate:** the original sources behind the compared claims; explicit definitions and measurement methods; independent critiques with traceable evidence. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-090-038 | When does a why question accidentally assume something that has not been established? | Rewrite a false-premise example into observation, verification and explanation stages; distinguish checking the event from debating its possible causes. |
| BANK-090-039 | How can a causal explanation differ from an explanation of purpose? | Compare a designed object, biological trait and social institution using attributed evidence; avoid assuming every feature exists because it was deliberately planned. |
| BANK-090-040 | What makes a popular single-cause explanation inadequate for a complex event? | Map necessary conditions, contributing factors and alternatives in a bounded historical case; avoid replacing it with an equally unsupported all-causes narrative. |
| BANK-090-041 | How can a channel explain uncertainty without making every answer sound equally plausible? | Compare evidence quality, competing predictions and confidence language in an original episode outline; distinguish unknown details from a complete absence of reliable knowledge. |

## 08. Visual proof, sourcing and corrections

**Level:** advanced. **Evidence direction:** Analyze an identifiable work or create a clearly labeled original example. Connect creative decisions to observable audience information; respect rights, attribution, consent and the difference between intent and reception.

**Source types to locate:** the actual work and authorized production materials; creator interviews or published craft explanations; permission-cleared examples and audience feedback. These are requirements, not sources already inspected.

| ID | Specific episode question | Individually written scope and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| BANK-090-042 | What should a why-channel demonstration establish before viewers treat it as proof? | State setup, controlled conditions, measurement and limits for a harmless example; distinguish a visual analogy from an actual test of the explanation. |
| BANK-090-043 | How can an animated explanation avoid smuggling in the conclusion through its graphics? | Identify assumed arrows, hidden forces, scale and timing in an original storyboard; anchor mechanisms in primary sources rather than persuasive motion alone. |
| BANK-090-044 | What should happen when a short explanation's missing exception changes the answer? | Correct the claim, show the relevant conditions and update titles or captions; distinguish an acceptable simplification from a central error. |
| BANK-090-045 | How can a channel choose between a quick answer and a series of linked episodes? | Use a question map with prerequisites, distinct subproblems and evidence needs; avoid multiplying a thin idea into repetitive videos solely to increase output. |
