## Editorial boundary

This niche focuses on **sacred narratives**: episodes, characters, narrative themes, versions, and living retellings. It may draw from epics, Puranic accounts, regional tellings, performance, and visual storytelling, with the particular source named. Neighboring niches remain the primary home for extended treatment of ancient Indian civilization (003), mythological animation as a production format (018), Hindu philosophy (268), individual texts (269–272), Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Shakta traditions (273–275), temple traditions (276), and practice areas (277–280). The boundary describes filing, not a claim that traditions are separable in lived practice.

The generic slots with the greatest risk of misframing were rewritten deliberately. `mysteries_unsolved_cases` addresses real uncertainty in source transmission. `technology_innovation` addresses media and preservation, not unsupported ancient-technology claims. `myths_vs_facts` separates a text's narrative, a practitioner's belief, and historical evidence without treating religious belief as a factual error. `future_developments` looks at documented present-day retellings and open creative choices.

## Source and production practice

The [Oxford Centre for Hindu Studies' Bhāgavata Purāṇa research project](https://ochs.org.uk/bhagavata-purana-research-project/) describes the text's significance across devotional, dance, drama, and vernacular traditions. Its [lecture on Rāmāyaṇa narratives in stone](https://ochs.org.uk/lecture/narratives-in-stone-the-ramayana-in-early-deccan/) shows why images and performance deserve their own questions. [Research on the Rāmāyaṇa's transmission across Asia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/abs/ramayanaan-instrument-of-historical-contact-and-cultural-transmission-between-india-and-asia/F3FC732C9FD0620D118A9F7004BC3DBF) supports treating versions as situated traditions. These readings informed the angle structure; they do not verify the claims of any future episode.

For a production, identify the exact text, edition or translation, performance, and community when relevant. Distinguish narrative setting from the date of a source. Present disputed readings with attribution. Use qualified review for contested religious or historical claims under the existing pipeline gate.
