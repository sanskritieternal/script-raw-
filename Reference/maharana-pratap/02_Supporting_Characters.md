# Supporting Characters: Maharana Pratap

---

## Supporting Character 1: Man Singh I

**Role:** Antagonist — Mughal general, Raja of Amer, led army at Haldighati
**Age Range:** 40s-60s (1550-1614 CE)
**Title:** Raja of Amer, Mansabdar in Mughal court

**Physical Description:**
- Face: Sharp Rajput features, calculating expression
- Eyes: Intelligent, observant
- Hair: Dark, traditional Rajput style
- Beard: Well-groomed Rajput beard
- Body: Strong, military bearing
- Distinguishing: Often seen in Mughal court attire (dual identity)

**Clothing:**
- Battle gear: Mughal-style armor with Rajput elements, turban with kalangi
- Court attire: Rich fabrics, Mughal-style jama with Rajput jewelry
- Weapons: Talwar, shield

**Personality:**
- Brilliant military commander
- Pragmatic politician — chose alliance with Akbar over resistance
- Skilled diplomat
- Personally respected Pratap (both were Rajputs)
- Torn between two worlds — Rajput heritage vs. Mughal service

**Emotional Arc:**
- Leads army against his fellow Rajput with reluctance
- After Haldighati: acknowledges Pratap's valor
- Represents the Rajput dilemma of the era

---

## Supporting Character 2: Rao Chandrasen Rathore

**Role:** Supporting — Raja of Marwar, fellow Rajput resistor
**Age Range:** 20s-40s (born ~1542, died 1581)
**Title:** Rao of Marwar (Jodhpur)

**Significance:**
- Fellow Rajput king who resisted Mughal overlordship
- Allied with Pratap in spirit, though geographically distant
- His resistance in Marwar diverted Mughal resources
- Died in 1581 — left Pratap as sole major resistor

---

## Supporting Character 3: Bhamashah

**Role:** Supporting — Pratap's Finance Minister and closest advisor
**Age Range:** 30s-50s
**Background:** Merchant/administrator from Mewar

**Significance:**
- Managed Mewar's finances during the war years
- Provided crucial financial support when Pratap had no revenue
- His loyalty and sacrifice are legendary in Mewar history
- Represented the civilian backbone of Pratap's resistance

---

## Supporting Character 4: Bhil Tribal Warriors

**Role:** Supporting — Guerrilla fighters from Aravalli forests
**Age Range:** 20s-50s
**Background:** Indigenous Bhil tribe, forest dwellers of Aravalli

**Physical Description:**
- Lean, wiry builds — adapted to mountain terrain
- Dark skin, traditional tribal markings
- Minimal clothing — practical for forest life
- Weapons: Bows, arrows, spears, slings
- Expert knowledge of Aravalli terrain

**Significance:**
- Pratap's most loyal and effective guerrilla fighters
- Knew every pass, cave, and water source in the Aravalli
- Provided intelligence on Mughal movements
- Represented the people's support for Pratap

**Personality:**
- Fiercely loyal to Pratap
- Masters of guerrilla tactics
- United with Pratap against a common enemy

---

## Supporting Character 5: Rao Akheraj Punwar

**Role:** Supporting — Maharani Ajabde's father, Pratap's father-in-law
**Age Range:** 40s-60s
**Title:** Rao of Peepalow

**Significance:**
- Strong ally of Mewar
- His daughter Ajabde was Pratap's most beloved wife
- Provided military support to Pratap

---

## Supporting Character 6: Rai Singh of Bikaner

**Role:** Supporting — Rajput noble in Mughal service
**Significance:**
- Represents Rajputs who chose Mughal alliance
- Contrasts with Pratap's choice
- His forces fought at Haldighati on Mughal side

---

## Supporting Character 7: Jhala Maan (Rawat Jhala)

**Role:** Supporting — Pratap's commander who died at Haldighati
**Age Range:** 30s-40s
**Title:** Rawat (chief) of Jhala clan

**Physical Description:**
- Strong warrior build, battle-hardened
- Wore Pratap's royal turban and insignia at Haldighati
- Sacrificed himself so Pratap could escape

**Significance:**
- Ultimate act of loyalty and sacrifice
- His death is one of the most emotional moments of the documentary
- Represents the warrior code of Mewar

---

## Supporting Character 8: Purohit (Royal Priest)

**Role:** Supporting — Pratap's spiritual advisor
**Age Range:** 50s-70s
**Background:** Brahmin priest at Eklingji temple

**Significance:**
- Guided Pratap's spiritual practices
- Performed coronation rituals
- Represented the religious and cultural foundation of Mewar's resistance

---

## Supporting Character 9: Hakim Sur (Afghan Noble)

**Role:** Supporting — Afghan ally of Pratap
**Age Range:** 40s-60s
**Background:** Afghan noble displaced by Mughal conquest

**Significance:**
- Represents anti-Mughal coalition beyond Rajputs
- His presence at Haldighati shows Pratap's ability to unite diverse forces
- Adds strategic depth to Mewar's resistance

---

## Supporting Character 10: Mewar Soldiers and Civilians

**Role:** Background — Army soldiers, villagers, fort inhabitants
**Significance:**
- Represent the people of Mewar who endured 11 years of war
- Their loyalty and sacrifice sustained Pratap's resistance
- Humanize the documentary — show the cost of war on ordinary people
