# Locations: Maharana Pratap

---

## Location 1: Kumbhalgarh Fort

**Historical Significance:** Birthplace of Maharana Pratap (1540 CE). One of the most impregnable forts in India. Never fell to enemy forces.
**Architectural Description:** Massive fortification wall (36 km long, 15 meters thick at base) — second longest continuous wall in the world after Great Wall of China. Multiple palaces, temples, barracks within. Built on Aravalli ridge at 1,100 meters elevation.
**Landscape Features:** Overlooks the Aravalli mountains, surrounded by dense forests, narrow approach paths.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Golden sunrise from east, dramatic shadows from fort walls, cool blue mountain light.
**Scale and Layout:** Vast complex with multiple levels, winding paths, hidden passages.
**Environmental Storytelling:** Birthplace of Pratap — symbolizes the warrior tradition of Mewar.
**Consistency Requirements:** Massive walls, Rajput architecture style, stone construction, multiple levels.

---

## Location 2: Chittorgarh Fort

**Historical Significance:** Former capital of Mewar. Fell to Akbar in 1568 after heroic resistance. The trauma of its fall shaped Pratap's entire life.
**Architectural Description:** Largest fort in India, spread over 280 hectares. Massive gates (Ram Pol, Padan Pol, etc.), palaces (Rana Kumbha Palace), temples (Samadheshwar), towers (Kirti Stambh, Vijay Stambh).
**Landscape Features:** Hilltop fort overlooking Berach River valley.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Dramatic sunset lighting for siege scenes, somber tones for aftermath.
**Scale and Layout:** Enormous complex, multiple defensive rings, impressive gates.
**Environmental Storytelling:** After the siege — visible destruction, blackened walls, abandoned but defiant.
**Consistency Requirements:** Massive fort walls, Rajput architecture, multiple gates and towers, stone construction.

---

## Location 3: Udaipur

**Historical Significance:** Capital of Mewar under Pratap (though he spent much time in the field). Founded by Pratap's father Udai Singh II around Lake Pichola.
**Architectural Description:** City Palace complex on Lake Pichola, white/cream stone architecture, domes, courtyards, gardens.
**Landscape Features:** Surrounded by Aravalli hills, built around lakes (Pichola, Fateh Sagar).
**Lighting Philosophy:** Soft morning light on lake, warm golden tones for palace scenes.
**Scale and Layout:** Spreading city with palace at center, surrounded by fortifications.
**Environmental Storytelling:** Symbol of Mewar's continuity — Pratap's base even during war years.
**Consistency Requirements:** White stone architecture, lake setting, palace domes, Aravalli backdrop.

---

## Location 4: Gogunda

**Historical Significance:** Site of Pratap's coronation, temporary capital during early reign, location of Haldighati battlefield.
**Architectural Description:** Small palace/fort complex in valley.
**Landscape Features:** Valley surrounded by Aravalli hills, near Haldighati pass.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Dramatic valley lighting, golden sunrise/sunset.
**Scale and Layout:** Modest compared to major forts — reflects war-time conditions.
**Consistency Requirements:** Valley setting, modest palace, mountain backdrop.

---

## Location 5: Chavand

**Historical Significance:** Pratap's final capital (1585-1597), where he died. Here he recovered Mewar's independence and rebuilt his kingdom.
**Architectural Description:** Hill fort, smaller but well-fortified.
**Landscape Features:** Hilltop location, southern Mewar, surrounded by forests.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Peaceful evening light, sense of accomplished sovereignty.
**Scale and Layout:** Well-planned fort, capital functions, surrounded by recovering villages.
**Consistency Requirements:** Hill fort architecture, southern Mewar landscape.

---

## Location 6: Haldighati Pass

**Historical Significance:** Site of the defining Battle of Haldighati (June 18, 1576). Named for turmeric-colored soil.
**Architectural Description:** Natural mountain pass, no man-made structures.
**Landscape Features:** Narrow pass between Aravalli hills, turmeric-yellow soil, rocky terrain, limited visibility.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Dramatic battle lighting — early morning golden light, dust and smoke.
**Scale and Layout:** Confined space — tactical advantage to defenders (Pratap's forces).
**Environmental Storytelling:** Battle-scarred landscape, memorial markers.
**Consistency Requirements:** Narrow mountain pass, yellow soil, rocky terrain, Aravalli backdrop.

---

## Location 7: Panthioli

**Historical Significance:** Mountain refuge where Pratap lived during guerrilla warfare phase (1576-1585).
**Architectural Description:** Cave dwellings, temporary shelters.
**Landscape Features:** Deep Aravalli forest, mountain caves, limited access.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Dim natural light, firelight in caves, shadowy forest scenes.
**Scale and Layout:** Small, hidden, austere.
**Environmental Storytelling:** Extreme hardship — Pratap ate bread made from grass, slept on bare ground.
**Consistency Requirements:** Forest cave setting, austere conditions, mountain backdrop.

---

## Location 8: Dewair

**Historical Significance:** Site of decisive Mewar victory — 36 Mughal outposts captured simultaneously.
**Architectural Description:** Open battlefield/valley.
**Landscape Features:** Open valley, surrounded by hills.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Bright daylight, celebratory atmosphere.
**Scale and Layout:** Wide battlefield.
**Consistency Requirements:** Open terrain, Aravalli backdrop.

---

## Location 9: Battlefield (Generic)

**Historical Significance:** Multiple battle locations across Mewar.
**Architectural Description:** Open terrain — plains, valleys, mountain passes.
**Landscape Features:** Varies by battle — plains, hills, forests.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Dramatic battle lighting — dust, smoke, golden hour.
**Scale and Layout:** Wide enough for cavalry charges.
**Environmental Storytelling:** Dust, smoke, casualties, aftermath.
**Consistency Requirements:** Natural terrain, 16th century landscape, no modern elements.

---

## Location 10: Aravalli Mountains (General)

**Historical Significance:** The mountains that defined Mewar's geography, strategy, and spirit.
**Architectural Description:** Natural landscape — ancient mountain range.
**Landscape Features:** Rocky hills, dense forests, narrow passes, scattered valleys, caves.
**Lighting Philosophy:** Varied — sunrise gold, midday cool, sunset orange, moonlight silver.
**Scale and Layout:** Vast mountain range, thousands of square kilometers.
**Environmental Storytelling:** Nature as Mewar's greatest defender.
**Consistency Requirements:** Aravalli geology — rocky, ancient, green vegetation, wildlife.
