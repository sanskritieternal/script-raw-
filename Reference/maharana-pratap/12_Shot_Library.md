# Shot Library: Maharana Pratap

**Purpose:** Catalog of recurring shot types for this documentary. Ensures visual variety while maintaining consistency.

---

## Establishing Shots

**1. Aravalli Mountain Range**
- Shot: Wide drone aerial shot
- Movement: Slow pan across mountains
- Purpose: Establish geography, scale, Mewar's natural fortress
- Lighting: Golden sunrise/sunset
- Used in: Opening, chapter transitions, guerrilla warfare sequences

**2. Fort Establishing Shot**
- Shot: Wide drone shot slowly approaching fort
- Movement: Slow push forward toward fort
- Purpose: Introduce forts — Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Udaipur, Chavand
- Lighting: Golden hour, dramatic clouds
- Used in: All fort introductions

**3. Battlefield Establishing**
- Shot: Wide static shot from elevated position
- Movement: None — static
- Purpose: Show battle scale and terrain
- Lighting: Harsh midday or golden hour
- Used in: Haldighati, Dewair, other battle scenes

---

## Character Shots

**4. Pratap — Royal Audience**
- Shot: Medium shot, Pratap seated on throne
- Movement: Slow push forward
- Purpose: Royal authority, decision-making
- Lighting: Warm interior light, window glow
- Used in: Court scenes, diplomatic scenes

**5. Pratap — Battle Ready**
- Shot: Medium wide shot, Pratap on horseback
- Movement: Slow tracking shot following movement
- Purpose: Warrior king, leading his army
- Lighting: Golden hour, dust particles
- Used in: Battle preparation, army reviews

**6. Pratap — Alone in Mountains**
- Shot: Wide shot, Pratap small in frame against vast mountains
- Movement: Static or very slow
- Purpose: Isolation, determination, connection to land
- Lighting: Cool morning light, vast sky
- Used in: Guerrilla phase, reflective moments

**7. Pratap — Close-Up Emotional**
- Shot: Close-up, Pratap's face
- Movement: Static
- Purpose: Grief, determination, resolve
- Lighting: Side light for dramatic shadows
- Used in: Chittorgarh aftermath, Haldighati wound, deathbed

**8. Pratap and Ajabde**
- Shot: Medium shot, both in frame
- Movement: Slow pull back
- Purpose: Royal couple, partnership
- Lighting: Warm, intimate
- Used in: Palace scenes, private moments

---

## Battle Shots

**9. Cavalry Charge**
- Shot: Wide tracking shot following cavalry
- Movement: Tracking forward with horses
- Purpose: Scale and power of battle
- Lighting: Dusty, golden, chaotic
- Used in: Haldighati, major battles

**10. Hand-to-Hand Combat**
- Shot: Medium close-up, dynamic angle
- Movement: Handheld documentary feel
- Purpose: Intensity of close combat
- Lighting: Dusty, firelight, chaotic
- Used in: Haldighati key moments

**11. Artillery Barrage**
- Shot: Wide shot from behind Mughal lines
- Movement: Static
- Purpose: Show Mughal firepower
- Lighting: Smoke, dust, orange explosions
- Used in: Haldighati — Mughal cannon fire

**12. Archery**
- Shot: Medium shot, mounted archer
- Movement: Slow push forward
- Purpose: Rajput/Bhil guerrilla tactics
- Lighting: Forest dappled light, battlefield dust
- Used in: Guerrilla warfare scenes

**13. Battle Aftermath**
- Shot: Wide static, showing casualties and destruction
- Movement: None
- Purpose: Cost of war, tragedy
- Lighting: Dim, somber, evening light
- Used in: After Haldighati, after sieges

## Detail Shots

**20. Weapon Close-Up**
- Shot: Extreme close-up of weapon (sword, shield)
- Movement: Static or slow rotation
- Purpose: Symbolic, detailed craftsmanship
- Lighting: Dramatic side light
- Used in: Before battles, coronation

**21. Jewelry/Armor Detail**
- Shot: Close-up of royal jewelry or armor
- Movement: Slow push
- Purpose: Royal status, craftsmanship
- Lighting: Warm, reflective
- Used in: Coronation, court scenes

**22. Temple Bell**

## Veo Compliance Notes

- Every shot: Maximum 3 named characters with visible faces
- Background figures: Indistinct, blurred, no faces
- Composition: Simple, readable, one focal point
- Camera: One movement per scene
- Duration: Always 8 seconds
