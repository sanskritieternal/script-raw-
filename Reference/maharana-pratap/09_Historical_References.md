## Primary Sources

### Abul Fazl — Akbarnama (1590 CE)
- **Type:** Imperial Mughal chronicle
- **Coverage:** Akbar's reign, including Haldighati campaign
- **Book III:** Details the 1576 campaign against Mewar
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Written under Akbar's supervision, detailed military accounts
- **Bias Note:** Mughal perspective — portrays events from Akbar's viewpoint. May minimize Mughal casualties, emphasize Mewar's defeat.
- **Key Facts:** Confirms Haldighati battle date (June 18, 1576), Man Singh I as commander, Pratap's escape

### Abul Fazl — Ain-i-Akbari (1590s CE)
- **Type:** Administrative encyclopedia of Mughal Empire
- **Coverage:** Mughal administration, military organization, revenue system
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Detailed and systematic
- **Key Facts:** Mughal army structure, mansabdari system, military organization

## Academic Research

### Dr. R.C. Majumdar — "The History and Culture of the Indian People" (Series)
- **Type:** Comprehensive Indian history series
- **Volume on Mughal Period:** Covers Akbar, Rajput relations, Mewar resistance
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Leading Indian historian, widely cited
- **Key Facts:** Rajput-Mughal relations, political context of Mewar's resistance

### Jadunath Sarkar — "A History of Jaipur"
- **Type:** Regional history
- **Coverage:** Rajput kingdoms including Mewar
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Renowned historian of medieval India
- **Key Facts:** Political dynamics between Rajput kingdoms and Mughals

### Satish Chandra — "Medieval India"
- **Type:** Comprehensive medieval Indian history
- **Coverage:** Mughal period, Rajput resistance
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Leading academic historian
- **Key Facts:** Mughal policy toward Rajputs, Mewar's unique position

- **Type:** Regional history of Rajasthan
- **Coverage:** Complete history of Rajasthan including Mewar
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Most comprehensive modern work on Rajasthan

- **Coverage:** All major battles involving Rajput kingdoms
- **Reliability:** LIKELY — Well-researched but some details debated

### B.N. Puri — "History of the Gurjara-Pratiharas"
- **Type:** Academic monograph

- **Reliability:** LIKELY — Academic work, some details specific to Pratap era limited

### Mohammad Habib — "Akbar and India"
- **Type:** Historical analysis

## Recognized Historians and Their Works

### James Tod — "Annals and Antiquities of Rajasthan" (1829-1832)
- **Type:** Historical narrative (based on Rajput bardic traditions)
- **Coverage:** Complete history of Rajasthan and its princely states
- **Reliability:** TRADITIONAL — Used Rajput bards (Charan and Bhat poets) as sources. Rich in detail but contains legendary elements. Western perspective of colonial era.
- **Key Facts:** Detailed accounts of Pratap's life, Haldighati battle, guerrilla warfare
- **Bias Note:** Romanticized Rajput heroism. Some events embellished. Essential reading but needs cross-verification.
- **Usage:** Use for narrative detail and cultural context, verify major events against primary sources

### G.S. Sardesai — "New History of the Marathas"
- **Type:** Historical work
- **Reliability:** LIKELY — Academic historian, references Mewar in broader context

## Archaeological Evidence

### Kumbhalgarh Fort Inscriptions
- **Type:** Stone inscriptions
- **Coverage:** Pratap's birth, early life, genealogy
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Physical evidence, dated inscriptions
- **Key Facts:** Confirms Pratap's birth at Kumbhalgarh, Sisodia dynasty lineage

### Chittorgarh Fort Ruins
- **Type:** Architectural evidence
- **Coverage:** Siege of 1568, Pratap-era repairs
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Visible architectural evidence
- **Key Facts:** Burn marks, structural damage consistent with siege

### Udaipur Records
- **Type:** City and temple records
- **Coverage:** Udai Singh II's reign, Pratap's coronation
- **Reliability:** LIKELY — Temple records generally reliable for this period

### Coins Minted During Pratap's Reign
- **Type:** Numismatic evidence
- **Coverage:** Pratap's sovereignty claims
- **Reliability:** VERIFIED — Physical coins survive
- **Key Facts:** Pratap issued his own currency — symbol of independence

### Temple Records at Eklingji
- **Type:** Religious and royal records
- **Coverage:** Pratap's spiritual practices, donations, coronation rituals
- **Reliability:** LIKELY — Temple records generally reliable

- **Type:** Oral and written poetic traditions
- **Coverage:** Complete narrative of Pratap's life, battles, heroism
- **Reliability:** TRADITIONAL — Passed down through generations, contains legendary elements
- **Key Facts:** Detailed accounts of battles, Pratap's character, warrior code

- **Coverage:** Pratap's heroism, Chetak's loyalty, guerrilla warfare
- **Reliability:** TRADITIONAL — Oral tradition, varies by region

- **Reliability:** TRADITIONAL — Local knowledge, contains family/generational memories
- **Key Facts:** Local knowledge of hideouts, battles, community sacrifices
- **Usage:** Use for local color and detail; cross-reference for major events

## Source Conflict Summary

### Conflicts Between Sources:

1. **Army sizes at Haldighati:**
   - Tod/folk tradition: 100,000+ Mughal army
   - Academic historians (Majumdar, Chandra): 8,000-10,000
   - Resolution: Use academic consensus, note traditional accounts as TRADITIONAL

2. **Battle outcome:**
   - Some historians (Tod): Moral victory for Mewar
   - Others: Tactical defeat with strategic recovery
   - Resolution: Present both views, note as DEBATED

3. **Pratap's death:**
   - Some sources: Illness/old age
   - Popular tradition: Tiger attack during hunting
   - Resolution: Illness is LIKELY, tiger story is TRADITIONAL

4. **Number of diplomatic missions:**
   - Sources differ on exact number (4, 6, or "several")
   - Resolution: Use "six" from most commonly cited sources, note as LIKELY
